1. Experimental Procedures 
1.1 Chemicals and general methods 
Lysing enzymes from Trichoderma harzianum were purchased from Sigma Aldrich (USA). 
Yatalase and restriction enzymes were purchased from Takara Biotech Co. Ltd. (Dalian, China). 
pEASY-Basic Seamless Cloning and Assembly Kit and Trans2-Blue was purchased from TransGen 
Biotech Co. Ltd. (Beijing, China). Trelief 5α Chemically Competent Cell was purchased from 
Tsingke Biotech Company (Beijing, China). KOD-Plus DNA Polymerase was purchased from 
Toyobo Biotech Co., Ltd (Shanghai, China). Primer synthesis was conducted at Generay Company 
(Shanghai, China), and DNA sequencing was performed at Tsingke Biotech Company (Beijing, 
China). Aglycon A1 was prepared from compound 24 catalyzed by OleD.1 Aglycon A9 was 
obtained by heterologous expression of FusA from Fusarium sp. MMA4 in Aspergillus nidulans. 
Sugar donors, aglycons A2, A3 and A6−A8 were purchased from Beijing BG Biotech Co. Ltd 
(Beijing, China), Sigma Aldrich (USA), and InnoChem Science & Technology Co., Ltd (Beijing, 
China). Compounds A4 and A5 were isolated from Talaromyces cellulolyticus CBS136886. HPLC-
MS analyses were performed on an Agilent 1260 series HPLC system (Agilent Technologies, USA) 
coupled with a LCQ Fleet ion trap mass spectrometer (Thermo Electron Corp., Germany) equipped 
with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source. HR-ESI-MS data were measured using a SHIMADZU 
LCMS-9030 spectrometer (SHIMADZU, Japan). NMR spectra were recorded on Bruker AVIIIHD-
400 spectrometer (Bruker BioSpin GmbH, Rheinstetten, Germany) at 400 MHz for 1H NMR and 
100 MHz for 13C NMR or an Agilent VNMRS 600 MHz for 1H NMR (Palo Alto, CA, USA). 
Chemical shifts (δ) were referenced to internal solvent resonances and given in parts per million 
(ppm), and coupling constants (J) were given in hertz (Hz). Optical rotations were measured on a 
Perkin-Elmer Model-343 digital polarimeter (PerkinElmer Inc., Waltham, MA, USA). IR spectra 
were acquired on a Nicolet 5700 FT-IR microscope spectrometer. 
1.2 Strains and culture conditions 
The fungi strains A. ustus CGMCC3.3904 and T. pinophilus CGMCC3.1433 were purchased 
from China General Microbiological Culture Collection Center (CGMCC, Beijing, China). The 
fungi strain Verticillium dahliae CICC2534 was purchased from China Center of Industrial Culture 
Collection (CICC, Beijing, China). The fungi strains T. amestolkiae CBS274.95, T. cellulolyticus 
CBS136886, Phaeomoniella chlamydospora CBS239.74, and Chalara longipes CBS411.76 were 
purchased from Centraalbureau voor Schimmelcultures (CBS, Utrecht, Netherlands). The fungi 
strain Fusarium sp. MMA4 was identified and collected in our lab. These fungi were cultivated on

B1 10 mg/L, agar 15.0 g/L, pH 6.0) at 28 °C and stocked in 4 °C. For the production of secondary 
metabolites and genomic DNA extraction, these fungi were grown in PDB at 28 °C for 12 days and 
3 days, respectively.

1.3 Genomic DNA and RNA preparation and reverse transcription-PCR 
The mycelia of these above fungi and knockout mutants were inoculated in PDB liquid medium 
culture at 28 °C, 150 rpm for 3 days and were collected with filter paper, respectively. The DNA 
and RNA were prepared using the Ezup Column Fungi Genomic DNA Purification Kit (Sangon 
Biotech, China) and E.Z.N.A.TM Fungus RNA Kit (Omega Bio-Tek, USA), respectively. RNA 
integrity was confirmed by electrophoresis on the TBE agarose gel, and the concentration was 
determined by spectrometer (Metash, Shanghai). Then the reverse-transcribed to cDNA with 
SmartScribe reverse transcriptase (Clontech, USA) following the manufacturer’s instructions. The 
genomic DNA and cDNA were stored at −20 °C and used as templates for gene knockout or 
heterogeneous expression, respectively. 
1.4 Transcriptome sequencing 
The mycelia of A. ustus strain was inoculated in PDB liquid medium culture at 28 °C, 150 rpm 
for 9 days and was collected with filter paper, respectively. Transcriptome sequencing was 
performed by Beijing Genomics Institute (Beijing, China) using BGISEQ-500 platform. The RNA-
seq data was utilized to analyze the expression level of cluster stm (Supplementary Table S1).

downstream the target gene stmA were amplified from genomic DNA of A. ustus using designated 
primer pairs containing 15 bp overlapping regions (Supplementary Table S2). The hph fragment 
was amplified from pCSN44 plasmid. The above three fragments and linearized pCE-Zero vector 
were assembled by ClonExpress® Multis One Step Cloning Kit C113 (Vazyme, China) according

1.6 Genetic manipulation in A. ustus and cultivation of deletion mutants 
The protoplast preparation and transformation of A. ustus were performed with modification 
according to the literature.3 Fresh spores were inoculated into 50 mL PDB medium in 250 mL flask 
and incubated at 150 rpm and 28 °C for germination. The mycelia were harvested after 24 h by 
filtration through Miracloth (Calbiochem, USA) and washed with 1.2 M MgSO4. The mycelia were 
then transferred into a 100 mL flask with 10 mL of osmotic buffer (1.2 M MgSO4, 10 mM sodium 
phosphate, pH 5.8) containing 60 mg lysing enzymes and 40 mg yatalase. After shaking at 85 rpm 
and 35 °C for 6 h, the mixture was transferred into a sterile 50 mL tube through Miracloth and gently 
added with 10 mL trapping buffer (0.1 M Tris-HCl, 0.6 M sorbitol, pH 7.0). After centrifugation at 
6,000 rpm and 4 °C for 15 min, the protoplasts were collected in the supernatant and transferred to 
a new sterile tube. An equal volume of STC buffer (1.2 M sorbitol, 10 mM CaCl2, 10 mM Tris-HCl, 
pH 7.5) was added and centrifuged at 4 °C, 3000 rpm for 10 min. After twice washing with STC 
buffer, protoplasts were resuspended in 100 μL STC buffer and gently mixed with 10 μg DNA 
fragments for 50 minutes on ice. 1.25 mL PEG solution (60% PEG4000, 50 mM CaCl2 and 10 mM 
Tris-HCl with pH 7.5) were added and gently mixed. After incubation at room temperature for 30 
min, the mixture was spread on the regeneration selection medium (1.2 M sorbitol and 200 µg/L 
hygromycin B in PDA).  
After culturing at 28 °C for 3−4 days, the transformants were transferred onto fresh PDA plates 
containing 250 µg/mL hygromycin B for selection. The obtained transformants were inoculated in 
PDB medium for isolation of genomic DNA to verify the genotype via PCR amplification using 
four pairs of designated primers. After cultivation in PDB liquid medium at 150 rpm and 28 °C for 
12 days, the mycelia of mutants were harvested by filtration, dried and extracted with MeOH, the 
medium was extracted with EtOAc, respectively. These extracts were evaporated to dryness, and 
then redissolved in 1 mL MeOH and centrifuged at 15,000 g for 30 min for HPLC–MS analysis. 
The supernatants were performed on a Capcell pak CR 1:4 (250 mm×4.6 mm I.D., 5 µm, Shiseido 
Co., Ltd., Japan) at a flow rate of 1 mL/ min at 30 °C. The mobile phase was a gradient elution of 
solvents A (ACN) and B (0.1% formic acid aqueous solution). The gradient programs were used for 
the analyses of the reactions (Supplementary Table S3). 
To prepare compound 2, a 10 L scale of fermentation of ΔstmC mutant was performed in PDB 
liquid medium. For preparative purification of the product, the extract was fractionated by silica gel 
column chromatography (CC) eluting with petroleum ether, petroleum ether/EtOAc, EtOAc and 
MeOH. The desired product 2 was in the fraction of EtOAc, and was further purified by Sephadex 
LH-20 (MeOH). Structure of 2 was characterized by UV, IR, HR-ESI-MS, 1D and 2D NMR analysis 
as follows (Table S4; Figures S27−S36). The molecular formula of compound 2 was C32H38O7 (m/z

1.7 Heterologous expression of clusters in A. nidulans 
The protoplast preparation and transformation of A. nidulans were performed as described in 
the literature.2 Genes stmABC from the stm cluster with their terminators (~300 bp) were amplified 
from genomic DNA of A. ustus with overhang primers (Supplementary Table S2). Similarly, genes 
ver1835 and ver1834 from V. dahliae and genes FusA and FusB from Fusarium sp. MMA4 were 
amplified with overhang primers, respectively (Supplementary Table S2). Plasmids pYT-U and 
pYT-P were digested with Pac I, and pYT-R was digested with BamH I. The digested vector and 
overlapping DNA fragments were assembled in S. cerevisiae BJ5464-NpgA by yeast homologous 
recombination. The positive colonies were checked by colony-PCR. The plasmids were extracted 
from yeast with Zymoprep™ Yeast Plasmid Miniprep I Kit (Zymo Research, Irvine, CA, USA), and 
were transformed into E. coli 5α or trans2-Blue for propagation. The positive E. coli transformants 
were confirmed by colony-PCR and sequencing. The recombinant plasmids pYT-U-stmA, pYT-P-
stmB, pYT-R-stmC, pYT-U-1835 and pYT-P-1834, pYT-U-FusA and pYT-P-FusB were extracted 
for transformation of A. nidulans, respectively. 
To obtain the protoplasts of A. nidulans, the spores were inoculated into 50 mL liquid GMM 
medium (NaNO3 6 g/L, KCl 0.52 g/L, MgSO4·7H2O 0.52 g/L, KH2PO4 1.52 g/L, trace elements 1 
mL/L, glucose10 g/L, yeast extract10 g/L) supplemented with 0.56 g/L uracil, 1.26 g/L uridine, 0.5 
mg/L pyridoxine, and 2.5 mg/L riboflavin, and germinated at 37 °C, 220 rpm for 5 h. The germlings 
were harvested by centrifugation at 3500 rpm, 4 °C for 10 min, and washed with 10 mL mycelium 
wash buffer (0.6 M MgSO4, 10 mM sodium phosphate buffer, pH 7.0). Then, the mycelia were 
resuspended in 12 mL osmotic buffer (1.2 M MgSO4, 10 mM sodium phosphate buffer, pH 7.0), 
which containing 36 mg lysing enzymes and 24 mg yatalase, and incubated at 80 rpm for 8–9 h at 
30 °C for enzymatic digestion. The mixture was transferred to a 50 mL tube and slowly added of 12 
mL trapping buffer (0.1 M Tris-HCl, 0.6 M sorbitol, pH 7.0). After centrifugation at 3750 rpm, 4 °C 
for 15 min, the protoplasts at the middle layer were transferred to a new tube and washed twice with 
equal volume of STC buffer (1.2 M sorbitol, 10 mM Tris-HCl, 10 mM CaCl2, pH 7.5). the 
protoplasts were obtained by centrifugation at 3750 rpm, 4 °C for 5 min, and then resuspended in a 
suitable volume of STC buffer for transformation. The recombinant plasmids were added into 100 
μL of protoplasts and incubated on ice for 60 min. The empty plasmids were transferred into the 
protoplasts, which were used as the control. Then the mixture was added to 600 μL PEG solution

(10 mM Tris-HCl, 60% PEG4000 and 50 mM CaCl2, pH 7.5) and lightly mixed, incubated 20 min

1.9 Heterologous expression of truncated AuCGT-1 in Escherichia coli and activity assay 
The coding region of truncated AuCGT-1 lacked the predicted transmembrane domain (amino 
acid residues L509−A535) was amplified with the primer pairs harboring BamH I restriction sites. 
The PCR product was purified, and cloned in pET-28a vector. The recombinant plasmid pET-28a-
AuCGT-1 was transformed into BL21 (DE3) E. coli (TransGen Biotech, China) for heterologous 
expression. The truncated enzyme was inducted and purified according to our previous work.4 The 
assays for the activity of truncated AuCGT-1 were performed as described above.  
1.10 Effects of temperature, pH, and divalent metal ions on AuCGT 
To assay the optimal reaction temperature, the reactions were incubated at different 
temperatures (25−60 °C). To determine the optimal pH, the enzymatic reaction was performed at 
30 °C in various reaction buffers with pH values in the ranges of 5.0−6.0 (citric acid–sodium citrate 
buffer), 6.0−8.0 (Na2HPO4−NaH2PO4 buffer), 7.0−10.0 (Tris-HCl buffer) and 9.0–11.0 (Na2CO3–
NaHCO3 buffer). To test the effects of divalent metal ions on AuCGT, MnCl2, CoCl2, FeCl2, CaCl2, 
BaCl2, SrCl2, NiCl2, MgCl2, ZnCl2, CuCl2 and EDTA were used individually at a final concentration 
of 5 mM. All determations were performed with 200 μg recombinant yeast microsome, UDP-
glucose as a donor and 2 as an acceptor. Aliquots were quenched with 200 µL ice cold MeOH and 
centrifuged at 15,000 g for 30 min for HPLC analysis. The supernatants were performed on a 
Capcell Pak CR 1:4 (250 mm×4.6 mm I.D., 5 µm, Shiseido Co., Ltd., Japan) at a flow rate of 1 mL/ 
min at 30 °C. The mobile phase was a gradient elution of solvents A (ACN) and B (0.1% formic 
acid aqueous solution). The gradient programs were used for the analyses of the reactions (Table 
S3). For quantification, three parallel assays were routinely carried out.  
1.11 Determination of kinetic parameters of AuCGT 
For kinetic studies of AuCGT, a typical assay contained 50 mM Tris-HCl (pH 8.0), 200 μg of 
yeast microsomal protein, UDP-glucose (1000 µM) and varying concentration of 2 (50−800 µM) or 
2 (400 µM) and varying concentration of UDP-glucose (100−1600 µM) at 30 °C in a total volume 
of 100 μL. Aliquots were quenched with 200 µL ice cold MeOH and centrifuged at 15,000 g for 30 
min. Supernatants were analyzed by HPLC as described above. All experiments were performed in 
triplicate. Kinetic parameters, including the Michaelis–Menten constant (Km) and the turnover 
number (kcat), were calculated by nonlinear regression analysis using GraphPad Prism 5 software. 
1.12 Substrate specificity of fungal C-glycosyltransferase AuCGT 
The reaction mixture contained 0.8 mM UDP-sugar, 0.4 mM aglycon and 200 μg recombinant 
enzymes in a final volume of 100 µL were incubated at 30 °C for 12 h. The reactions were terminated 
by the addition of 200 µL ice cold MeOH. Subsequently, samples were centrifuged at 15,000 g for 
30 min and analyzed by HPLC-MS. For quantification, three parallel assays were routinely carried 
out. HPLC analyses were performed as described above.

1.17 Modeling, docking and molecular dynamics simulation of AuCGT and PcOGT1 
The protein structures of AuCGT and PcOGT1 were constructed utilizing the AlphaFold2 
(Highly accurate protein structure prediction with AlphaFold), respectively.10 Molecular docking 
was performed by AutoDock Vina with default parameters.11 The obtained AuCGT or PcOGT1 
complex was simulated via canonical molecular dynamics (MD) simulation with AmberTools 18 
for the conformation relaxations. The protein was subjected to the Amber ff14SB force field, while 
the water molecules were modeled using the TIP3P model. The ligands, on the other hand, were 
governed by the GAFF force field. Counterions were added to neutralize the system and then the 
system was solvated by a rectangular water box with the 10.0 Å buffer distance in each dimension. 
The system's overall energy was subjected to minimization, subsequently elevated to 310 K (NVT 
ensemble) over a span of 1000 ps, wherein both the protein and ligands were restrained. This was 
followed by equilibration at 310 K (NPT ensemble) for a duration of 4000 ps, during which 
restraints were applied solely to the protein's Cα atoms. Lastly, all restraints were released, and the 
system was simulated at 310 K (NPT ensemble) for an extended 80 ns duration. All structural 
pictures were rendered in PyMOL (www.pymol.org).

1.20 Bioactivity assays of fungal glycosides  
1.20.1 Anti-HIV assay  
SupT1 cells (2 × 105) were cotransfected with 0.6 mg of pNL-Luc-E and 0.4 mg of pHIT/G. 
Then the VSV-G pseudo typed viral supernatant (HIV-1) was harvested by filtration through a 0.45 
μm filter after 48 h and the concentration of viral capsid protein was determined by p24 antigen 
capture ELISA (Biomerieux, Lyon, France). SupT1 cells were exposed to VSV-G pseudotyped HIV-
1 (MOI = 1) at 37.8 °C for 48 h in the absence or presence of test compounds. A Luciferase Assay 
System (Promega, Madison, USA) was used to determine the inhibition rate. The cytotoxicity was 
measured by the CCK-8 method. SupT1 cells (5 × 105) were seeded into a 96-well microtiter plate 
in the absence or presence of test compounds (negative control, DMSO) in triplicate and incubated 
at 37 °C. After 48 h incubation, cell viability was measured by the CCK-8 method. The dilutions of 
the tested compounds were independently performed three times. The IC50 values were calculated 
by GraphPad Prism 5.0.

1.20.2 Anti-IAV Activity Assay 
293T-GlUC cells (5 × 105 cells/ml) were seeded in the wells of 96-well plate at 100 µL well-1. 
After 24 h incubation at 37 ºC and 5% CO2, cells were cultured to 90% confluence. For evaluation 
assay of antivirals, 1 µL of each tested compound (DMSO and ribavirin were used as negative and 
positive controls, respectively) was added to cells and incubated for 2 h prior to infection, after 
which cells were infected with influenza A virus (IAV) at an MOI of 0.3. After a further incubation 
for 24 h at 37 ºC, the cell supernatant was collected and measured for 293T-GlUC activity. 
1.20.3 Anti-bacterial activity 
Meticillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) was used in the antimicrobial assay 
according to the Antimicrobial Susceptibility Testing Standards outlined by the Clinical and 
Laboratory Standards Institute document. The assay was carried out in flat bottomed 96-well 
microtiter plate. A loopful of glycerol stock was streaked on an LB-agar plate, which was incubated 
overnight at 37 °C. A single bacterial colony was picked and suspended in Mueller–Hinton Broth to 
approximately 1 × 104 CFU/ml. A twofold serial dilution of each compound to be tested was 
prepared and an aliquot of each dilution (2 μL) was added to a 96-well flat-bottom microtiter plate 
(Greiner). An aliquot (78 μL) of bacterial suspension was then added to each well and the plate was 
incubated at 37 °C aerobically for 16 h. Finally, the optical density of each well at 600 nm was 
measured with an EnVision 2103 Multilabel Plate Reader (Perkin-Elmer Life Sciences). MIC values 
were defined as the minimum concentration of compound that inhibited visible bacterial growth. 
1.20.4 α-glucosidase inhibitory assay 
Fungal glycosides (10 µM) and the positive control (Acar, 10 µM) were preincubated with 0.1 
U mL−1 α-glucosidase in phosphate buffer at 37 °C for 5 min, respectively. Then each buffer was 
added substrate p-nitrophenyl-α-d-glucopyranoside to continuously incubated at 37 °C for 15 min 
and terminated by the addition of 0.4 M Na2CO3. Meanwhile, a similar assay buffer without α-
glucosidase was used as a blank. The absorbance was determined at 400 nm. The IC50 values were 
calculated by GraphPad Prism 5.0. 
1.20.5 PTP1B inhibitory activity 
In vitro enzymatic activity of PTP1B was measured by using 4-nitrophenyl phosphate 
disodium salt (pNPP) as substrate as described. Briefly, the glycosides and recombinant human His-
PTP1B protein were pre-incubated at room temperature for 5 min. Assays were performed in 96-
well plates with a final volume of 100 μL reaction buffer containing 50 mM HEPES, 5 mM DTT, 
150 mM NaCl, 2 mM EDTA and 2 mM pNPP (pH 7.0), incubated at 30 °C for 10 min, stopped by 
addition of 50 μL 3 M NaOH. The absorbance density of p-nitrophenol was monitored at 405 nm. 
A similar system without His-PTP1B protein was used as blank. Each concentration of the 
compounds was tested in three parallels. The IC50 value was calculated with GraphPad Prism 5.0.

HepG2, PC9 and PANC-1) was measured using the MTT assay as described in the literature.7 Taxol 
was used as a positive control. After incubation for 24 h, the cells were treated with various

stock solution (2 mg mL−1, Sigma Chemical) was added into each well, and the plates were 
incubated at 37 °C for an additional 4 h. All the incubation process was conducted in the dark. The 
solution was then removed from each well, and 150 μL DMSO was added. Following gentle 
agitation, the absorbance was measured using an ELISA reader (Bio-Rad, USA) at 570 nm. Three

obtained for vehicle-treated cells to determine the percentage of surviving cells. Three parallel 
samples were measured each time. The IC50 values were calculated by GraphPad Prism 5.0.
