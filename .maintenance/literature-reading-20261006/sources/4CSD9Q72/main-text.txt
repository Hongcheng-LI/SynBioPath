        1.  Introduction

         Ever since the discovery of spongouridine and spongothymi-
         dine from the Caribbean marine sponge Tectitethya crypta
          (Cryptotethya crypta) in the early 1950s,1,2 marine natural prod-
          ucts have been in the spotlight of the drug discovery commu-
           nity. The diverse chemical features and the wide range of
          biological activities possessed by marine natural products have
          established them as a source of highly desirable chemodiversity
          to support drug discovery and pharmacology applications. Thus
            far, almost 30 000 publications have been listed in the major
         database of marine natural products, MarinLit,3 indicating the
        unquenchable enthusiasm of the scientic community towards
         the discovery of new drug leads from marine habitats. Accord-
         ing to the latest review by Blunt et al., 1378 new compounds
         were isolated from marine organisms4 in 2014 compared with
         332 in 1984.5,6 Following the example of pioneers in the eld7,8
