Figure 1. Key points in the reconstitution of the biosynthetic pathway of baccatin III. (A) Catalytic functions of taxane oxetanase (TOT)-1 and chemicals extracted
from Taxus mairei, namely taxadiene hexa-acetate, 1-dehydroxubaccatin IV, and baccatin I. (B) Biosynthesis of baccatin III from geranylgeranyl pyrophosphate (GGPP)
involves 13 enzymes, including recently discovered taxane 9α hydroxylase (T9αH) and TOT (red), as well as the as yet unidentiﬁed taxane 1β hydroxylase (T1βH) and
taxane 9α oxidase (T9O; green). The intermediate substrates are also shown. (C) Tobacco leaves genetically engineered to produce baccatin III. The mimic biosynthetic
pathway contains a chloroplast-localized taxadiene synthase (TXS), six endoplasmic reticulum (ER)-localized cytochrome P450s [i.e., taxane 5α hydroxylase (T5αH),
taxane 9α hydroxylase (T9αH), taxane 2α hydroxylase (T2αH), taxane 7β hydroxylase (T7βH), taxane 13α hydroxylase (T13αH), and TOT), and two cytoplasm-localized
enzymes [i.e., taxadien-5α-ol-O-acetyl transferase (TAT) and taxane 2α-O-benzoyl transferase (TBT)]. Abbreviations: DBAT, 10-deacetylbaccatin III 10β-O-acetyl
transferase; T10βH, taxane 10β hydroxylase.
