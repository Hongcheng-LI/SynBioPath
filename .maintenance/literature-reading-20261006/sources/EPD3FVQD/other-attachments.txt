II. Protein Expression and Purification 
 
General Considerations. All E. coli growth media and buffers for protein purification were 
prepared with water filtered with a Milli-Q purification system. Proteins were purified using a Cytiva 
ÄKTA Pure 25 L1 fast protein liquid chromatography (FPLC) system equipped with an F9-C 
fraction collector and S9 sample pump controlled by Unicorn v7 software. All buffers were vacuum 
filtered with 0.2 µm nylon filters (Millipore Sigma). 5 mL HisTrap FF columns (Cytiva) were used 
for affinity chromatography. Large cultures (>500 mL) were harvested using a Beckman Coulter 
Avanti JXN-26 centrifuge. Cells were lysed by sonication with a QSonica Q500 ultrasonic 
processor. Protein samples were analyzed with Tris SDS-PAGE gels (BioRad) and Quick 
Coomassie stain (Anatrace). All purification steps were performed at 4 °C unless otherwise 
indicated.  
 
Expression Protocol for AetF, JamD, PhmJ, and Co-VBPO. pET-28a(+) plasmids containing 
codon-optimized aetF, jamD, phmJ, or Co-VBPO sequences were transformed into chemically 
competent E. coli BL21(DE3) cells using standard heat shock procedures and plated onto LB 
agar supplemented with 50 µg/mL kanamycin. Single colonies were used to inoculate 10 mL LB 
media containing 50 µg/mL kanamycin for precultures incubated at 37 °C, 200 rpm overnight. The 
precultures were used to inoculate 1 L TB media supplemented with 0.4% glycerol and 50 µg/mL 
kanamycin in 2.4 L Fernbach flasks. Cultures were incubated at 37 °C and 190 rpm until the 
optical density at 600 nm (OD600) reached 0.6-0.8. The incubator was set to 18 °C and the cultures 
were incubated for 1 h to equilibrate to temperature. Expression was induced by the addition of 
0.1 mM isopropyl-b-D-1-thiogalactopyranoside (IPTG, 100 µL of 1 M stock). The induced cultures 
were further incubated at 18 °C and 190 rpm overnight (approx. 18 h) and harvested by 
centrifugation (5,000 x g, 10 min). 
 
Purification Protocol for AetF, JamD, PhmJ, and Co-VBPO. The described procedure is 
identical for the each of the proteins with exceptions as indicated. Pellet from 1 L TB culture was 
resuspended in ~100 mL cold lysis buffer (50 mM Tris-HCl pH 8, 300 mM NaCl, 10% glycerol, 10 
mM imidazole) and sonicated in a glass beaker on ice, 5 min total “on” time, alternating cycles of  
3 s on, 7 s off. Lysed cells were centrifuged at 40,000 x g for 30 min at 4 °C. The supernatant was 
sonicated for 3 x 10 s on ice and filtered with 0.45 µm syringe filters (CellTreat). The clarified 
lysate was purified by affinity chromatography by FPLC using a 5 mL HisTrap FF column. Buffer 
A was the lysis buffer as described above and Buffer B was composed of 50 mM Tris-HCl pH 8, 
300 mM NaCl, 10% glycerol, and 500 mM imidazole. The column was equilibrated with 5 column 
volumes (CV) of 0% Buffer B at 5 mL/min and the sample was loaded at 2 mL/min via the sample 
pump. The sample pump was washed with 10 mL Buffer A and the loaded column was washed 
with 3% Buffer B for 10 CV at 2.5 mL/min. The column was washed with a linear gradient of 3-
100% B over 15 CV at 2 mL/min to elute protein. Fractions were analyzed using Tris SDS-PAGE 
gels and fractions containing the desired protein were pooled and concentrated to 2.5 mL using 
50 kDa Amicon centrifugal molecular weight cutoff filters. Concentrated proteins were desalted 
using PD-10 desalting columns (Cytiva) equilibrated with storage buffer (50 mM Tris-HCl pH 8, 
100 mM NaCl, 10% glycerol) following the manufacturer’s directions. For JamD only, a storage 
buffer containing an increased amount of NaCl (300 mM) was used to prevent precipitation. Yields 
were determined by standard Bradford Assay. AetF and PhmJ purified as bright yellow proteins, 
JamD was pale yellow, and Co-VBPO was colorless. 
 
Flavin Quantification in JamD and PhmJ. The absorbance spectra of 1 mL of 10 µM JamD and 
PhmJ were measured from 300 nm to 700 nm in 2 nm increments in polystyrene cuvette 
(BrandTech) using a Cary 60 UV-vis spectrophotometer (Agilent Technologies). The protein was

transferred to a microcentrifuge tube containing 20 µL of 10% sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS). The 
sample was mixed, incubated at room temperature for 10 min, and the UV absorbance spectrum

III. Phylogenetic Analysis 
 
Maximum Likelihood Phylogenetic Tree Construction. Protein sequences were selected from 
the FAD-NAD(P)-binding enzyme family (Pfam PF13454 and Interpro IPR036188) and the 
Pyr_redox_2 enzyme family (Pfam PF07992) based on the enzyme families associated with the 
AetF protein sequence. Representative sequences from both families were compiled at the 50% 
sequence identity cutoff (Uniref50 on the UniProt database). Duplicates were removed and the 
sequence lengths outside of the 125-1100 amino acid range were removed. The relatively large 
number of sequences returned (>150,000) were further clustered with CD-HIT1 at low percent 
identity (30%) to yield an initial dataset. Known flavin-dependent enzyme sequences (in bold in 
Table S1) were seeded into the dataset to identify relevant groupings. Multiple sequence 
alignments were generated with kalign2 and preliminary trees were generated with FastTree.2,3 
Iterative rounds of manual selection based on groupings of seeded sequences within the diverse 
dataset and higher percentage identity clustering with CD-HIT yielded a final dataset of 269 
sequences that are representative of a large diversity of flavin-dependent enzymes, as listed in 
Table S1. The final maximum likelihood tree was prepared with IQ-TREE v2.1.4 and the 
automatically selected WAG+F+G4 substitution model with bootstrapping (1000).4 In addition to 
known flavin-dependent enzyme sequences, the top BLAST hits for AetF were also included in 
the analysis. All trees were visualized and annotated in iTOL.5

General Considerations. Solvents used for extraction, purification, and LC-MS/MS analysis 
were purchased from Fisher Chemical. Deuterated solvents were purchased from Cambridge 
Isotope Laboratories. NMR spectra were recorded using a JEOL ECZ 500 MHz NMR 
spectrometer (Akishima, Tokyo, Japan). Data for NMR spectra are reported as follows: shift (δ) 
in ppm; s, singlet; d, doublet; t, triplet; q, quartet; m, multiplet or unresolved; brs, broad signal; J, 
coupling constant(s) in Hz. NMR spectra were analyzed using MestreNova v. 14.3.0-30573 
(Mestrelab, Santiago de Compostela, Spain). Mass spectrometry data were analyzed using 
Xcalibur Qual Browser v. 1.4 SR1 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Inc.). 
 
Microbial Strain and Culture Conditions. Moorena producens JHB was collected from Hector 
Bay, Jamaica in 1996 and has been maintained as a unialgal culture in the Gerwick laboratory in 
SWBG11 media at 26 oC with a 16 h light/8 h dark regimen.6 
  
Extraction and LC-MS of Crude Extract. A fresh culture sample of M. producens JHB grown in 
laboratory culture as described above was harvested through vacuum filtration resulting in 129.79 
g of biomass (wet weight). The harvested sample was then extracted using 2:1 
dichloromethane:methanol (DCM:MeOH) to afford 2.35 g crude extract. 1 mg of extract was 
subjected to filtration using a C18 SPE column, dried under nitrogen, and redissolved in LC-MS 
grade MeOH to a final concentration of 1 mg/mL. Samples of the extract and a blank of MeOH 
were analyzed on a Thermo Finnigan Surveyor Autosampler-Plus/LC/PDA-Plus system coupled 
to a Thermo Finnigan LCQ Advantage Max MS (San Jose, CA, USA). A Phenomenex Kinetex 5 
μm C18 100 Å 100 x 4.60 mm column was used for reversed phase separation at a flow rate of 
0.6 mL/min. Mobile phase solvent A, water + 0.1% formic acid (FA), and solvent B, acetonitrile 
(MeCN), were used. The separation was performed using a 32-minute method starting with 
equilibration at 30% solvent B for 2 minutes, followed by a linear gradient to 99% solvent B over 
22 minutes, followed by a 4-minute washout phase at 99% solvent B, and a 4-minute re-
equilibration period at 30% solvent B. Data dependent acquisition of MS/MS spectral data was 
performed in positive ionization mode. 
 
Purification. The remaining 2.35 g of extract was dissolved in 4 mL of 2:1 DCM:MeOH and mixed 
in a 500 mL round bottom flask with 11.75 g of TLC grade silica. The mixture was dried and loaded 
onto the column for vacuum liquid chromatography (VLC). Fixed volumes of progressively 
increased polar mixtures of hexanes, ethyl acetate (EtOAc), and MeOH were used for initial 
separation: (A) 100% hexanes, (B) 10% EtOAc/hexanes, (C) 20% EtOAc/hexanes (D) 40% 
EtOAc/hexanes, (E) 60% EtOAc/hexanes, (F) 80% EtOAc/hexanes, (G)100% EtOAc, (H) 25% 
MeOH/EtOAc, and (I) 100% MeOH. VLC Subfractions F and G containing jamaicamide A (4) and 
jamaicamide B (7) were combined to afford 90.5 mg. The combined fractions F and G were 
purified further by HPLC using a Thermo Scientific Dionex Ultimate 3000 Pump/RS 
Autosampler/RS Diode Array Detector/Automated Fraction Collector which yielded 6 
subfractions. A Phenomenex Kinetex 5 μm C18 100 Å LC 100 x 4.60 mm column was used for 
reverse phase separation at a flow rate of 9 mL/min. Mobile phase solvent A, water + 0.1% FA, 
and solvent B, MeCN, were used. A 32-minute method was used starting with equilibration at 
30% solvent B for 5 minutes, followed by a linear 20-minute gradient to 99% solvent, followed by 
a 3-minute washout phase at 99% solvent B, and a 4-minute re-equilibration period at 30% solvent 
B. Fraction FG #1 contained jamaicamide B (7) and Fraction FG #2 contained jamaicamide A (4). 
These fractions were purified further by HPLC using a Phenomenex Kinetex 5 μm C18 100 Å LC 
100 x 4.60 mm column at a flow rate of 1 mL/min. A 32-minute method was used starting with

General Conditions for JamD Reactions for Compound Isolation. Reactions containing 5 
mol% (25 µM) JamD, 2 µM PtdH, 5 mM sodium phosphite, 1 mM NADP+, 500 µM substrate, 10 
mM KBr, 100 µM FAD, and 50 mM Tris-HCl pH 7 were prepared in 50 mL Erlenmeyer flasks and 
incubated at 30 °C and 100 rpm for 18 h. 2 mg of each substrate was used from a 50 mM stock 
in methanol (final concentration 1% v/v) with the exception of carmabin A (16) where 1.5 mg of a 
50 mM stock in DMSO (final concentration 1% v/v) was used due to material limitations. The 
isolation and NMR characterization of compound 168 are reported previously. Reactions were 
quenched by the addition of 3 volumes methanol, vortexed to mix, and centrifuged at 8,000 rpm 
for 10 min. Supernatants were transferred to round-bottom flasks and the pellets were washed 2 
additional times with methanol, separating supernatant by centrifugation as previously described. 
Extracts were concentrated using a SpeedVac SPD140DDA vacuum concentrator (ThermoFisher 
Scientific). The residues were resuspended in 600 µL methanol and filtered into HPLC vials. 
Products were purified by semi-preparative HPLC as described above and concentrated by 
SpeedVac evaporation. All products were characterized by 1H and 13C NMR where disappearance 
of the alkynyl proton (typically present at ~1.95 ppm in CDCl3) is diagnostic. Fully annotated NMR 
spectra are available in Section VII (NMR Spectra).

VI. Synthesis 
 
General Considerations. All chemicals and solvents were used as received from commercial 
suppliers (Millipore Sigma, Fisher Scientific) without further modifications. Thin layer 
chromatography was performed using silica plates from Merck (silica gel 60 F254, glass plates). 
Preparative-scale flash column chromatography was performed using a Teledyne ISCO 
CombiFlash® Rf+ Lumen™ system. NMR spectra were recorded using a JEOL ECZ 
spectrometer (500 MHz). Data for NMR spectra are reported as follows: shift (δ) in ppm; s, singlet; 
d, doublet; t, triplet; q, quartet; m, multiplet or unresolved; brs, broad signal; J, coupling constant(s) 
in Hz. NMR spectra were analyzed using MestreNova 12.0.0.
