ABSTRACT: The growing demand for natural products in
pharmaceutical applications has prompted a focus on more
sustainable methods to produce high-value terpenoids using
microbial cell factories. Due to the characteristics of high
abundance, renewable, and low cost, methane has emerged as a
promising feedstock for biomanufacturing. In this study, the
methanotrophic bacterium Methylotuvimicrobium buryatenese
5GB1C, known for its industrial potential, was metabolically
engineered to synthesize taxadiene, a crucial precursor in paclitaxel
production. The biosynthesis of taxadiene from methane was first
established by employing an endogenous strong promoter to
enhance the expression of heterologous taxadiene synthase in M. buryatenese 5GB1C. To further optimize the metabolic flux, rate-
limiting enzymes (Dxs and IspA) were upregulated in the methylerythritol phosphate pathway while complementing the native
pathway with the essential idi gene that was originally deficient. Coupled with a 2.1-fold improvement in the NADPH/NADP+ ratio,
these modifications collectively boosted taxadiene production from 2.58 to 22.97 mg/L in serum vials. Ultimately, a temperature-
controlled two-stage cultivation was implemented in 3 L bioreactors, which achieved a remarkable titer of 104.88 mg/L, representing
the highest reported titer for diterpenoid biosynthesis from methane. This work demonstrates the potential of utilizing methane for
the sustainable production of advanced terpenoids with reduced environmental impact.
KEYWORDS: taxadiene, methane, methanotrophic bacteria, promoter engineering, flux enhancement, culture optimization
■INTRODUCTION
Natural terpenoids have garnered attention in clinical practice
due to their pharmacological activities in the treatment of
various diseases.1,2 Paclitaxel is a prominent terpenoid
compound recognized for its anticancer properties. The
conventional method of procuring paclitaxel involves extrac-
tion from the bark of Pacific yew trees. Nevertheless, this
methodology is marked by inefficiencies and raises consid-
erable concerns regarding environmental sustainability.3,4 This
is attributed to the prolonged growth duration and inadequate
regeneration capabilities of the yew. The anticipated swift
expansion of the global market for paclitaxel has led to an
increased interest in identifying sustainable methods for this
important compound. Currently, microbial manufacturing5
offers innovative approaches for the synthesis of terpenoid
natural products while also relieving environmental degrada-
tion, owing to its abbreviated production cycle and moderate
production conditions. Taxadiene, which constitutes the
carbon backbone of paclitaxel, serves as a crucial intermediate
compound in the biosynthetic pathway of paclitaxel.
Researchers have successfully accomplished the heterologous
synthesis of taxadiene, marking a significant advancement in
this field.6,7 Simultaneously, there exists an opportunity to
develop a backup raw material in production processes,
thereby further promoting the tenets of sustainable manu-
facturing.
Methane (CH4), the second-most-abundant greenhouse gas
generated as a byproduct from anthropogenic sources and a
primary constituent of natural gas, has drawn particular
attention as an abundant and inexpensive alternative carbon
feedstock for sustainable manufacturing.8,9 The conversion of
methane derived from waste into taxadiene presents a
promising opportunity for resource reutilization and the
creation of an efficient circular economy, which also plays a
crucial role in decreasing carbon emissions and addressing
climate change.10,11 Methanotrophs possess the ability to
utilize methane as their exclusive carbon source for the
biosynthesis of a range of chemical compounds, such as 4-
hydroxybutyrate, isobutyraldehyde, and ectoine.12−14 For type

I methanotrophs, methane is initially oxidized into methanol
by methane monooxygenase (MMO), which is further
converted to formaldehyde by methanol dehydrogenase.
Subsequently, formaldehyde reacts with ribulose-5-phosphate
(Ru5P) via 3-hexulose-6-phosphate synthase to form 3-
hexulose-6-phosphate (H6P), which finally enters the RuMP
cycle. Extensive research has been focused on the elongation of
carbon chains derived from methane. Recently, the develop-
ment of an orthogonal platform based on formyl-CoA
elongation (FORCE) reactions has demonstrated the potential
to bioupcycle one-carbon substrates into various multicarbon
compounds, albeit less complex than taxadiene.15,16 The
biosynthesis of long-chain terpenoids from methane neces-
sitates an efficient methylerythritol phosphate (MEP) pathway.
While the native MEP pathway has been characterized in type I
methanotrophs, there remains a need to enhance the carbon
yield, productivity, and terpenoid product diversity. For
instance, different sesquiterpenoids have been successfully
produced by Methylotuvimicrobium alcaliphilum 20Z using
methane, yielding titers ranging from 0.83 to 91.55 mg/L.17,18
Squalene, a triterpene, was synthesized by Methylomonas sp.
DH-1 strain, with a titer of 8.4 mg/L and a volumetric
productivity of 1.4 mg/L·d.19 Methylotuvimicrobium buryatense
5GB1C, renowned for its efficient methane oxidation, rapid
growth rate, and high cell density cultivation, holds promise as
an industrially viable methanotrophic host.20,21 Despite the
successful production of various bioproducts through engineer-
ing M. buryatense 5GB1C, biosynthesis of diterpenes such as
taxadiene from methane remains unexplored and poses
challenges. These obstacles include the uncertain capacity of
methanotrophic bacteria to produce the diterpenoid precursor
geranylgeranyl pyrophosphate (GGPP), low cyclization
efficiency of taxadiene synthase (TXS),22,23 a scarcity of
genetic components necessary for the regulation of intricate
metabolic pathways, and ambiguities surrounding the rate-
limiting steps involved in the process. Collectively, these
factors hinder the efficient biosynthesis of taxadiene from
methane.
The advanced development of metabolic engineering
techniques offers valuable insights for overcoming the
mentioned challenges. The application of effective gene
expression elements to modulate the expression of terpenoid
synthase represents a significant approach to enhancing the
capacity for terpenoid synthesis. Zhang et al. reported
genetically engineered a sugar-utilizing microbe with a
promoter of puc operon, which resulted in enhanced farnesyl
diphosphate (FPP) cyclization to bisabolene, leading to a 2.2-
fold increase in bisabolene titer.24 The precise identification
and resolution of bottlenecks in product synthesis can
significantly enhance production efficiency. A previous study
demonstrated the production of α-farnesene at a titer of 42.61
mg/L with a productivity of 6.09 mg/L·d by overexpressing a
single ispA gene.17 It is worth mentioning that optimum
culture conditions may also promote the biosynthesis of
terpenoids. For example, the production of glycyrrhetinic acid,
α-amyrin, and β-caryophyllene in yeast was successfully
improved by changing culture temperature, which presumably
augmented the stability and accumulation of key enzymes.25
These metabolic engineering approaches outlined herein offer
insights into the effective production of taxadiene in M.
buryatense 5GB1C.
In this study, we constructed and optimized a methano-
trophic cell factory for the first time to bioupcycle methane
into taxadiene. Based on transcriptomics data, several
endogenous promoters of M. buryatense 5GB1C were
identified and utilized for efficiently regulating gene expression
to enhance the activity of key enzymes. Subsequently, a dozen
genes involved in the MEP pathway were analyzed to address
bottlenecks in carbon flux toward taxadiene synthesis. To
correct the imbalances of intracellular reducing power needed
for synthesizing MEP-based intermediates, various NADPH-
producing enzymes were subjected to experimental validation,
and the observed variations in enzyme activity were elucidated
through the application of computer-aided methods (Figure
1). Ultimately, to maximize the performance of TXS, the
engineered strain attained an unprecedented taxadiene titer of
104.88 mg/L, with maximum volumetric productivity of 26.22
mg/L·d, utilizing methane as a sole carbon source through a
temperature-induced two-stage cultivation process.

■EXPERIMENTAL SECTION
Strains and Culture Conditions. M. buryatense 5GB1C
obtained from Prof. Mary Lidstrom of the University of
Washington and all engineered strains (Table 1) were cultured
in a 250 mL serum vial containing 50 mL of nitrate mineral
salts (NMS) medium20 at 30 °C and 200 rpm. Five mL of n-
dodecane was added into the vials at the start of culture.
Methane was supplied to a final concentration of 20% (v/v) by
gas substitution using a gastight syringe, and the headspace was
refreshed daily. Preliminary exploration was conducted for
scale-up production using a 500 mL spinner bottle. 300 mL of
NMS medium and 30 mL of n-dodecane were added to the
spinner bottle, and a magnetic rotor was added. A heated
magnetic stirrer was used to control the temperature and stir at
a speed of 400 rpm. The composition of the NMS culture
medium in the spinner bottle is the same as that in the serum
vial. Inoculate the strain into the medium to achieve an OD600
of 0.5. After inoculation, a mixture gas of air and methane with
a ratio of 4:1 was introduced, with a ventilation rate of 300
mL/min. Cell growth in serum vials and spinner bottles was
assessed by optical density at 600 nm (OD600) using an
ultraviolet−visible spectrophotometer (T6, Beijing Purkinje
GENERAL Instrument Co., China).
Promoter Screening. Promoter screening was based on
transcriptome data26 using the online software Softberry
(www.softberry.com) to predict core promoter regions.
Upstream sequences of genes were extracted to ensure the
entire core region and its components. Promoters were
amplified by PCR with corresponding primers, using the M.
buryatense 5GB1C genome as a template (Table S2). GFP
served as the reporter gene and fluorescence density was
measured using a microplate reader (Spark, Tecan Co.,
Switzerland). One mL of culture was collected at each
fermentation time point, centrifuged, and resuspended in
PBS buffer before diluting to a final OD600 of 0.5. A two
hundred μL resuspended solution was placed in a 96-well
microplate, and fluorescence intensity was measured using
excitation and emission wavelengths of 395/509 nm. Relative
fluorescence intensity was calculated by subtracting the control
strain (PG11) fluorescence from each engineered strain’s
fluorescence.
Plasmids Construction. pAWP89 was used for gene
expression.20 Specifically, we validated the overexpression
effects of genes in MEP and PPP pathways of the taxadiene
synthesis using the pAWP89 plasmid. After the above
validation experiments, to ensure a stable inheritance, genes
with significant production-enhancing effects in MEP pathway
and PPP pathway were introduced into the genome using
mined promoters. pUC19 was used as a backbone to assemble
DNA fragments inserted into the genome, and the DNA
fragments were inserted into BamH I and SalI restriction sites.
pK18mobsacB mediated traceless insertion method27 was
utilized to insert zwf1 into the genome when constructing the
chassis strain. pAWP89, pUC19, and pK18mobsacB vectors
were used to construct the recombinant plasmids using the
ClonExpress Ultra One-Step Cloning Kit (Vazyme Biotech
Co., China) (Table S1). Plasmid generation was carried out
using E. coli DH5α cells. The primers and procedures used to
construct expression vectors in this study can be found in
Table S2.
Construction of Recombinant Strains. The pAWP89-
donor plasmids were introduced into competent cells of M.
buryatense 5GB1C as previously reported.20 The genome of M.
buryatense 5GB1C was edited using an electro-transformation
method,13 and the DNA fragments used for transformation
were amplified from the pUC19-donor plasmid by using DNA
polymerase 2 × Phanta Max Master Mix (Vazyme Biotech Co.,
China). The traceless genome insertion refers to the method of

Evaluation of Intracellular Cofactor Regeneration.
Cells cultured for 48 h were used for the determination of
NADPH and NADP+ using the Coenzyme II NADP(H)
Content Assay Kit (Beijing Solarbio Science & Technology
Co., China) following the manufacturers’ instructions.
The structure of the enzyme was modeled through online
Alphafold2 (https://colab.research.google.com/github/
deepmind/alphafold/blob/main/notebooks/AlphaFold.ipynb)
and optimized through energy minimization using GROMACS
2023. Subsequently, the structure of glucose-6-phosphate
dehydrogenase (GDH) (PDB ID: 7XHP)29 was aligned as a
template to identify the active pockets of GDHs encoded by
zwf. Our analysis involved docking the cofactor NADP+ and
G6P into the corresponding active pocket positions using
Autodock4. Five conformations with the lowest binding energy
were identified as the most likely binding modes.
Culture Optimization with Temperature Control. In
the serum vial experiment, four temperature points (18 °C, 22
°C, 26 °C, and 30 °C) were investigated for optimal taxadiene
production. Subsequently, a preliminary exploration was
conducted for scale-up production in a 500 mL spinner bottle,
containing 300 mL of NMS medium and 30 mL of n-
dodecane, agitated at 400 rpm using a heated magnetic stirrer
at specified temperatures. The inoculum was adjusted to an
initial OD600 of 0.5, and a gas mixture of air and methane (4:1)
was introduced at 300 mL/min. The temperature was then
lowered from 30 to 22 °C at 48 h. After the preliminary
experiment, the scale-up cultivation was carried out in a 3-L
automatic bioreactor (Baoxing Bio-Equipment Engineering
Co., China) with 2-L high-density medium (0.2 g/L MgSO4·
7H2O, 0.02 g/L CaCl2·6H2O, 8 g/L KNO3, 7.5 g/L NaCl, 1 ×
carbonate buffer, 4 × phosphate buffer and 8 × trace
elements).20 A mixture of air and methane with a ratio of
4:1 was introduced, with a ventilation rate of 2000 mL/min. By
adjusting the speed of the stirring blade, the dissolved oxygen
(DO) was controlled above 20%, and the maximum speed of
the stirring blade was 1000 rpm. The pH value is controlled at
9.0 ± 0.1. The pH and dissolved oxygen (DO) were monitored
by using polarographic probes (Mettler Toledo Corp., Zurich,
Switzerland). To control the foam level, 50−100% sterile
antifoam (DF-104, Dow Chemicals (China) Co., Ltd.,
Shanghai, China) was added manually. The two-stage
cultivation was initiated at a temperature of 30 °C for the
first 48 h, after which the temperature was controlled at 22 °C
to the end of cultures. At 48 h, 200 mL of n-dodecane was
introduced into the 2-L fermentation broth. Cell growth in the
3-L bioreactor was assessed by dry cell weight (DCW) after
processing with a freeze-drying instrument (LGJ-10T, Beijing
Songyuan Huaxing Technology Develop Co., China).
Analysis of Products and Off Gases. Qualitative and
quantitative analysis of taxadiene and α-farnesene were
performed using GCMS (GCMS-QP2020NX, Shimazu Co.,
Japan). The chromatographic column was SH-Rxi-5Sil MS
(Shimazu Co., Japan). Helium was used as the carrier gas at a
flow rate of 2 mL/min. The oven temperature was set at 80 °C
for 1 min, increased at a rate of 10 °C/min to 250 °C, and held
for 1 min. The MS source, MS quad, interface, and inlet were
maintained at 230, 150, 280, and 250 °C, respectively. The
internal standard method is used for quantitative analysis of
terpenes.6,30,31 For taxadiene quantification, nonadecane
(purchased from Shanghai Aladdin Biochemical Technology
Co., Ltd.) was added to the fermentation product to achieve a
final concentration of 50 mg/L. Taxadiene levels were
quantified by comparing the total ion count peak to that of
nonadecane.6 For α-farnesene, β-farnesene standard (purchase
from Shanghai Aladdin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd.)
was added as an internal standard to the fermentation
product,31 with its final concentration reaching 50 mg/L. α-
Farnesene was quantified by determination of its total ion
count peak area in comparison to that of the internal standard
β-farnesene.
Headspace methane and CO2 in the serum vials were
analyzed using an Agilent 7890A Gas Chromatograph (GC)
equipped with a thermal conductivity detector (TCD) and
TDX-01 column (2 m, 1/8) (Agilent Technologies Co., USA).
Argon gas was used as the carrier gas at 45 mL/min. The
injector temperature was at 150 °C and detector temperature
was at 250 °C, and the oven temperature was maintained at
120 °C. Methane and CO2 were removed from the system
after the detection through ventilation for 30 min. The
chromatographic peaks of the samples are compared with the
standard gas mixture (20.2% CH4, 2.98% CO2, 2.01% O2,
1.98% H2, and N2, balance) (China Haohua (Dalian) Research
& Design Institute of Chemical Industry Co. Ltd.) to
determine the relative concentrations of methane and CO2
in the samples. The relative concentration was multiplied by
the headspace volume and divided by the molar volume to
calculate its corresponding molar quantity. CH4, O2, and CO2
in the off-gas stream from the 3-L bioreactors were real-time
monitored and analyzed by gas sensors (Fuji Electric (China)
Co., China) to calculate the gas uptake/evolution rates.

■RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Mining of Endogenous Promoters for Taxadiene
Biosynthesis. The elongation of isoprene units is essential
for the biosynthesis of diterpenes. Notably, the ispA gene
encoding farnesyl pyrophosphate synthase and geranylgeranyl
diphosphate synthase is involved in the extension of isoprene
units within the MEP pathway, thereby contributing to the
synthesis of precursors for various terpenoids (Figure 2A). In
most microorganisms, ispA is limited to the synthesis of the
sesquiterpene precursor farnesyl pyrophosphate (FPP), only a
few variants can directly synthesize geranylgeranyl diphosphate
(GGPP).33−37 Fortunately, the ispA gene in M. buryatense
5GB1C encodes type II GGPP synthase, which not only
catalyzes the synthesis of FPP but also extends the carbon
chain to GGPP. To assess the terpenoid skeleton synthesis
capability of M. buryatense 5GB1C, strains TD00 and TD01
were constructed, incorporating TXS-TcGGPPS and TXS,
respectively (Figure 2B). Following 120 h cultivation period in
serum vials, taxadiene was detected in the cultures of both
constructs. This finding also represents the inaugural successful
bioconversion of methane to taxadiene (Figure 2C). However,
the overexpression of TcGGPPS did not result in a significant
increase in taxadiene production (Figure 2D). Previous
research has indicated that the cyclization reaction catalyzed
by TXS may constitute a potential bottleneck in the efficient
synthesis of taxadiene,38,39 compounded by the limited carbon
chain elongation capabilities of C1 microorganisms,15,40,41
which may explain the lack of a significant impact on taxadiene
production in M. buryatense 5GB1C from a single variable
modification. To address this limitation, it is essential to
identify a high-yield characterization compound to facilitate
the single-factor assessment of the terpene synthesis
capabilities of M. buryatense 5GB1C. α-farnesene, a terpene
with a synthesis pathway that largely overlaps with that of

effectively drive the overexpression of TXS, leading to the

modification.

clarity regarding the content and distribution ratio of various
compounds within the MEP pathway makes it challenging to
accurately predict the bottlenecks. Therefore, it is essential to
elucidate the influence of individual key genes involved in
diterpene biosynthesis in methanotrophic bacteria, to enhance

synthesis rate of pathway intermediates and subsequently

alleviate feedback inhibition caused by the product.50 The gene
ispA in M. buryatense 5GB1C encodes type II geranylgeranyl

carbon chains is essential for the synthesis of diterpenes, and
the expression level of GGPPS directly affects the production
of GGPP, providing sufficient substrates for TXS. Furthermore,
this process encompasses divergent pathways and utilizes
intermediates such as DMAPP and GPP, such as the in vivo
synthesis of prenylated FMNH2 and ubiquinol.51,52 Con-
sequently, the elevated expression of ispA is essential for
steering the carbon flux toward the biosynthesis of long-chain
terpenes.

Revealing the Influence of Reducing Power Supply
on Taxadiene Biosynthesis. In addition to the carbon flow
involved in the biosynthesis of terpenes, the supply of reducing
power required for redox reactions in the MEP pathway poses
a significant challenge for taxadiene production.46 For each
molecule of taxadiene synthesized, the enzyme DXR will
directly utilize four molecules of NADPH. Furthermore, the
(E)-4-hydroxy-3-methylbut-2-enyl-diphosphate synthase
(IspG) and 4-hydroxy-3-methylbut-2-enyl diphosphate reduc-
tase (IspH) will indirectly utilize a combined total of eight
molecules of NADPH via ferredoxin. These metabolic
dependencies are likely to disturb the equilibrium of reducing
power within M. buryatense 5GB1C. As shown in Figure S2, a
noticeable decrease in NADPH levels in terpene synthesis
strains underlines the need for NADPH supplementation in
cells.
The irreversible oxidation stage in the pentose phosphate
pathway (PPP), which can generate a substantial amount of
reducing power, was prioritized for enhancement (Figure 5A-
5B). This stage involves three NADPH-regenerating enzymes:
two glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenases (GDH1 and GDH2)
encoded by zwf1 and zwf2, respectively, and one phospho-
gluconate dehydrogenase (PGDH) encoded by gnd. Over-
expression of GDH1 led to significant enhancement in terpene
production (Figure 5C) and restored the NADPH/NADP+
ratio to the same level as that of wild-type strain (Figure 5D).
Interestingly, GDH2, an isoenzyme of GDH1, did not lead to a
considerable improvement in NADPH supply or terpene
production.

using Alphafold2. The structural refinement of the models was

Based on the above conclusions, we reconstructed TD07
(Figure 6A), which overexpressed zwf1 on the genome
compared to TD06. As shown in Figure 6B, a 2.1-fold increase
in the NADPH/NADP+ ratio was observed. This elevated
reducing power led to a taxadiene titer of 22.97 mg/L in strain
TD07, which is 1.4-fold higher than strain TD06 (Figure 6C).
It is noteworthy that strain TD06 experienced growth delay
and obstruction, while the growth of TD07 closely resembled
that of the wild type (Figure 6D). Initially, it was speculated
that the reinforcement of the C1-compound assimilation
pathway through zwf1 overexpression could improve methane
utilization efficiency and accelerate the cell growth rate, as PPP
is also involved in the RuMP cycle (Figure 1). However, TD06
and TD07 exhibited similar methane consumption rates
(Figure 6E) and CO2 emission rates (Figure 6F), indicating
that the restoration of the growth rate in TD07 was not due to
increased methane uptake, but rather a replenishment of the
reducing power required for the cell growth and central
metabolisms of M. buryatense 5GB1C. Despite the potential
competition between the PPP and MEP pathway for the
common substrate G6P (Figure 1), the significant increase in
taxadiene titer in TD07 demonstrated that the limiting factor
in TD06 was the availability of reducing power rather than
carbon flux through the MEP pathway.
Temperature-Induced Two-Stage Cultivation for
Taxadiene Production. The thermostability of TXS had a
significant impact on taxadiene production as evidenced by
molecular dynamics simulations, which showed higher root-
mean-square fluctuations (RMSF) in multiple loops of this
enzyme (Figure S3). Similar findings have been reported in
model strains such as yeast and E. coli,25,39,57 but no mutants
targeting these temperature-sensitive regions have been
explored in methanotrophs. However, low temperatures may
disrupt normal metabolic processes in vivo and inhibit the
growth of the strain. Therefore, it is crucial to investigate the
optimal culture temperature for taxadiene production by M.
buryatense 5GB1C, to balance the growth of strain and the
stability of TXS. Four temperature gradients ranging from 18
to 30 °C were assessed, and strain TD07 showed a higher
taxadiene titer under 22 °C cultivation conditions (Figure 7A).
Additionally, the growth of the strain was markedly suppressed
at temperatures below 22 °C. These findings may be attributed
to the differences in temperature preferences of enzymes
related to central metabolism and cyclization reactions, as well
as the strong stability and low volatility of terpenes at lower
temperatures. Furthermore, taxadiene accumulation was
observed after 48 h of fermentation, coinciding with the
bacteria entering the late growth phase (Figure S4). The
properties of taxadiene synthase, along with the occurrence of
growth decoupling, encourage the exploration of innovative
fermentation techniques aimed at maximizing taxadiene titer.
To enhance taxadiene production in high cell density
cultures of M. buryatense 5GB1C, a temperature-induced two-
stage culture strategy was developed in this study. Initially, the
culture temperature was maintained at 30 °C to promote cell
growth. Once the growth reached the late log-growth phase at
48 h, the temperature was lowered to 22 °C to stimulate
taxadiene biosynthesis. The effectiveness of this two-stage
cultivation was validated in spinner bottles, resulting in
increased taxadiene titer and reduced growth inhibition
(Figure S4). This approach successfully allowed for the
accumulation of sufficient cells and enzymes at specific-
controlled culture temperatures, ensuring high metabolic flux
and catalytic activity of TXS. Finally, the implementation of
this two-stage strategy in 3-L bioreactors yielded the highest
taxadiene titer of 104.88 mg/L (Figure 7B) and reached
maximum volumetric productivity of 26.22 mg/L·d in the
second stage (Table S5), demonstrating the advancement of
our fermentation strategy for producing taxadiene.
Additionally, off-gas analysis revealed that stain TD07
primarily utilized methane and oxygen for assimilation and
respiration to support cell growth in the first-stage culture, as
indicated by measurements of methane uptake rate (CUR),

demonstrates that the taxadiene titer (104.88 mg/L) achieved

environmental friendliness and circular economy. This
transition toward CH4-based biomanufacturing aligns with
sustainability efforts and is expected to address critical issues
related to resource depletion and the limited availability of
arable land. Substantial improvements in titer, yield, and
carbon conversion efficiency are essential to match or exceed
the efficiency of traditional sugar-based methods. Future
research efforts focus on overcoming these challenges through
targeted optimization of carbon flow, enhanced carbon
utilization efficiency, and refined metabolic engineering
strategies could be helpful to establish a more competitive
and sustainable alternative for current taxadiene production
methodologies.
Additionally, the production of taxadiene from methane
achieves a negative carbon footprint (Figure 7C). Conven-
tional management practices for waste-derived methane may
contribute to an increase in greenhouse gas emissions. In
contrast, our biomanufacturing process effectively captures
methane and transforms it into taxadiene. According to the
calculations, producing 1 kg of taxadiene could potentially
offset over 1,900 kg of CO2 equivalent emissions (Figure 7C),
demonstrating significant promise in reducing overall carbon
emissions and combating climate change. Our study introduces
a proof-of-concept for biosynthesizing diterpene from CH4 by
methanotrophic bacteria, which offers potential environmental
advantages.
■CONCLUSIONS
Microbial cell factories have been extensively studied for their
potential in synthesizing key natural product compounds. The
methane-based biomanufacturing has raised considerable
concern due to the promising future sustainability and
economic and environmental benefits. In this study, metabol-
ically engineered strains of M. buryatense 5GB1C were
developed to enable the utilization of methane for taxadiene
production. To overcome the challenges of inadequate genetic
components, unclear rate-limiting steps, and insufficient
reducing power in M. buryatense 5GB1C, we employed a
comprehensive approach involving the identification of
multiple promoters, enhancement of carbon and energy flux
in the MEP pathway, and the implementation of a temper-
ature-induced two-stage cultivation. These strategies resulted
in a substantial increase in taxadiene production, reaching a
taxadiene titer of 104.88 mg/L and productivity of 26.22 mg/
L·d. Moreover, the promoter mining discussed in this article
will expand the repertoire of accessible gene elements for M.
buryatense 5GB1C, thereby enhancing its potential for
industrial applications. The proposed strategy to overcome
the limitations in terpene synthesis is expected to not only
substantially enhance the production of taxadiene but also
facilitate the synthesis of other derivatives from the MEP
pathway, including sesquiterpenes and triterpenes. Addition-
ally, the investigation into the performance variations of
NADPH regenerating enzymes offers valuable insights into
their enzymatic engineering modifications. In conclusion, this
study establishes a promising platform for utilizing methane in
the biosynthesis of valuable natural products, thereby
decreasing the reliance on edible substrates and reducing
greenhouse gas emissions.
