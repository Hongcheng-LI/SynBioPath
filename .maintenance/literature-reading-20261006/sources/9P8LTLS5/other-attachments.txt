Materials and Methods 
General 
Bacterial strains and plasmids used in this study are summarized in Table S2. All primers used 
in this study were listed in Table S3 and S4. Plasmid extraction, DNA purification and gel extraction 
were carried out using commercial kits (Omega Biotech Co., Ltd., Beijing, China). Genes synthesis 
and DNA sequencing were performed at Tsingke Biotech Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). PCR reactions 
were carried out using Phanta DNA polymerase (Vazyme Biotech Co., Ltd., Nanjing, China) or Taq 
DNA polymerase (TIANGEN Biotech Co., Ltd., Beijing, China). Catalase from bovine liver was 
obtained from Millipore Sigma (2,000-5,000 U/mg, stock solutions were prepared assuming 2,000 
U/mg. L-Glutathione reduced was obtained from Sangon Biotech Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). 
General chemical reagents were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich and Sangon Biotech Co., Ltd.. 
1000 x kanamycin was prepared at 50 mg/mL. Stock solutions of 20 mM NADP+/NAD+ and 10 mM 
FAD were prepared in 50 mM HEPES, pH 7.4 (reaction buffer) and stored at -20 °C until use. 
High-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) analysis was performed on an Agilent 1260 
Infinity system equipped with a diode array detector (DAD). HPLC-MS experiments were 
performed on an Agilent 1290 HPLC system coupled with a Thermo Electron LTQ-Orbitrap XL 
mass spectrometer. All chemicals and solvents were of analytical or chromatographic grade. The 
resuspended cells were lysed by an ultrasonic processors VCX750 (Sonics and Materials Inc, PA, 
USA). 
Bioinformatic tools 
Enzyme Function Initiative-Genome Neighborhood Tool (EFI-GNT) was used for mining gene 
clusters containing PhzB homologous protein from public databases (https://efi.igb.illinois.edu/efi-
gnt/). 1 
Comparative analysis of the identified gene clusters was performed using CAGECAT2 
(https://cagecat.bioinformatics.nl/). 
The protein abbreviation, grouping and Uniprot number of representative flavin-dependent 
monooxygenases (FMOs) from each categorical group are listed in Table S13. These sequences 
were employed to construct a limited sequence similarity network (SSN) using the EFI-EST server 
at an E-value of 5 and an alignment score of 15. The resulting network was visualized and 
annotated in Cytoscape v3.10.4

(b): Protein expression and purification 
The codon-optimized pezW, prnF and GDH were each amplified using the primer pairs in 
Table S3 and digested by NdeI and XhoI, and then respectively cloned into the same sites of 
pET30a (+) to generate pET30a-pezW, pET30a-prnF and pET30a-GDH. After confirmation by 
sequencing, they were introduced into E. coli BL21 (DE3). Single colonies were used to inoculate 
10 mL LB media containing 50 µg/mL kanamycin and then incubated at 37 °C, 200 rpm overnight. 
The overnight cultures were used to inoculate 1 L LB media supplemented with 50 µg/mL 
kanamycin. Cultures were incubated at 37 °C, 180 rpm until the optical density at 600 nm (OD600) 
reached 0.8-1.0. The cultures were chilled on ice and cooled to 16 °C, and then expression of each 
protein was induced by the addition of isopropyl-β-D-1-thiogalactopyranoside (IPTG, 0.2 mM final 
concentration), and cultivation was continued for additional 12 h at 16 °C. 
Cells were harvested by centrifugation at 10,000 g, washed twice and resuspended in Buffer 
A (50 mM Tris-HCl pH 7.54, 500 mM NaCl) and sonicated in an ice-water bath with an ultrasonic 
processors VCX750 (Sonics & Materials Inc, PA, USA), and centrifuged at 10,000 g for 30 min at 
4 °C. The supernatant was filtered through 0.22 μm membrane and applied to a HisTrap HP column 
(1 mL, GE Healthcare) and the C-(His)6-tagged protein was eluted with a linear gradient of 
imidazole (20–500 mM) in Buffer A using an ÄKTA Purifier system. The proteins were desalted 
using Ultrafree ®-4 Centrifugal Filter Unit (Millipore, Bedford, MA, USA), and concentrated. The 
purified proteins were stored in Buffer B (20 mM NaCl, 25 mM Tris-HCl, pH 7.5) at -80ºC until use 
and the protein concentrations were determined by the Bradford method using bovine serum 
albumin (BSA) as a standard.

HPLC and LC-MS analysis 
General considerations: HPLC separations were performed using an Agilent Technologies 
1260 series HPLC equipped with a degasser, quaternary pump, autosampler, and diode array 
detector. Analytical-scale separations were performed with an YMC pack ODS-AQ (5 µm, 4.6 × 
150 mm) column fitted with a guard cartridge, and semi-preparative separations were performed 
with an HPLC YMC-Pack ODS-AA C18 (5 µm, 250 × 10.0 mm) column. LC-MS analysis was 
performed with an Agilent Technologies 1290 Infinity series HPLC equipped with a degasser, 
quaternary pump, autosampler, and diode array detector coupled to a Thermo LTQ-Orbitrap XL 
mass spectrometer. Separations were performed with a YMC pack ODS-AQ column 5 µm, 4.6 × 
150 mm column. For both HPLC and LC-MS analysis, solvent A was water + 0.1% trifluoroacetic 
acid and solvent B was methanol + 0.1% trifluoroacetic acid. 
HPLC Method 1 (analytical scale PezW reaction): 1 mL min-1 flow rate; gradient program: 0-
5 min 30% B, 5-35 min 30-100% B, 35-40 min 100% B, 40-41 min 100-30% B, 41-46 min 30% B. 
HPLC Method 2 (semi-preparative scale PezW reaction): 2 mL min-1 flow rate; gradient 
program: 0-25 min 62% B. 
LC-MS Method 3: 0.3 mL min-1 flow rate; gradient program: 0-5 min 5% B, 5-15 min 5-95% 
B, 15-18 min 95-100% B, 18-20 min 100-5% B, 20-25 min 5% B. ESI ion source, positive polarity.

Enzymatic assays 
Analytical-scale halogenation reaction of PezW 
For the halogenation catalyzed by PezW, a typical reaction (100 µL) consists of 5 µM PezW, 
100 µM FAD, 500 µM compound 1/2, 1 mM NAD(P)H and 10 mM NaX (X = F-, Cl-, Br-, I-) in HEPES 
buffer (50 mM, pH 7.4). The reaction mixtures were incubated at 30 °C (metal bath) for 4 h and 
were quenched by the addition of equal volume of acetonitrile. To increase the production of 
halogenated molecules and decrease the amount of NADH required, the above in vitro assay was 
optimized by using an additional enzyme, GDH. Under the newly designed reaction system, 100 
µL reactions mixtures contained 5 µM PezW, 0.5 µM GDH, 100 µM FAD, 500 µM substrate 1-8, 1 
mM NAD+, 10 mM NaX (X=F-, Cl-, Br-, I-), 25 mM glucose and 50 mM HEPES (pH 7.4) buffer were 
prepared in 1.5 mL centrifuge tubes and incubated at 30 °C (metal bath) for 4 h. Reactions were 
quenched by the addition of 100 µL methanol and centrifuged at 14,000 rpm for 10 min prior to 
remove the denatured proteins. The reactions were monitored by HPLC analysis using a C18, 
YMC pack ODS-AQ column (5 μm, 4.6 ×150 mm) with UV detection at 280 nm. 50 µL of the filtered 
sample was injected on a YMC pack ODS-AQ column 5 µm, 4.6 × 150 mm column using HPLC 
Method 1. Conversion is defined as the percentage of the HPLC-UV peak area corresponding to 
the product(s) relative to the sum of the peak areas for the starting material and all product(s). 
Preparative-scale PezW reactions 
For purification of the PezW enzymatic chlorination product, a 50 mL scale reaction was set 
up using 30 mg of 1. The reaction mixture contained the following components: 0.05% PezW, 0.5 
µM GDH, 100 µM FAD, 0.5 mM substrate 1, 1.0 mM NAD+, 10 mM NaCl, 25 mM glucose, 1 mM 
glutathione, 70 U/mL catalase and 50 mM HEPES buffer (pH 7.4). After incubation for 12 h at 30 ℃ 
(metal bath), the reaction mixture was extracted with ethyl acetate (50 mL) and filtered through an 
0.22 µm syringe filter. The aqueous layer was further extracted with ethyl acetate (2 × 50 mL). The 
combined extracts were concentrated under reduced pressure on a rotary evaporator (water bath 
temperature < 35 °C). Analysis of the crude products by HPLC (quantified by peak area comparison) 
suggested 75% conversion to 1a-1d prior to further purification. Products 1a-1d were separated 
and purified using HPLC method 2, then dried in vacuo, affording 1a as a dark blue powder (0.42 
mg, 1.4% yield), 1b as a yellow powder (6.32 mg, 21.07% yield), 1c as a dark green powder (8.85 
mg, 29.5% yield), 1d as a yellow powder (5.42 mg, 18.07% yield). The identity of 1a was confirmed 
by matching 1H-NMR with published data 8 (Tables S4 and S5). New compounds 1c-1d were 
identified by 1H-NMR, 13C-NMR, and 2D spectroscopy (1H-1H COSY, 1H-13C HSQC, and 1H-13C 
HMBC). 
For purification of the PezW enzymatic bromination product, a 50 mL scale reaction was 
conducted with 30 mg of 2. The reaction mixture contained the following components: 0.05% PezW, 
0.5 µM GDH, 100 µM FAD, 0.5 mM substrate 2, 1.0 mM NAD+, 10 mM NaBr, 25 mM glucose 1

Preparative-scale PezW variants reactions 
To evaluate the scalability of PezW variants (Q121A, S414A and T418A), preparative-scale 
reactions (10 mL) were conducted using 5.0 mg of substrate 1 per variant. Each reaction mixture 
contained: 0.05% PezW variant, 0.5 µM GDH, 100 µM FAD, 0.5 mM substrate 1, 1.0 mM NAD+, 
10 mM NaCl, 25 mM glucose, 1 mM glutathione, 70 U/mL catalase and 50 mM HEPES buffer (pH 
7.4). After incubation for 12 h at 30 ℃ (metal bath), the reaction mixture (~10 mL) was extracted 
with ethyl acetate (10 mL) and filtered through an 0.22 µm syringe filter. HPLC analysis of the crude 
products (quantified by peak area comparison) indicated conversions of 78% (Q121A), 84% 
(S414A), and 89% (T418A) to 1a-1d prior to purification (Fig S22B). Products 1a-1d from each 
variant were separated and purified by HPLC method 2, then dried in vacuo. The isolated yields 
are summarized in Fig S22B. For Q121A: 1b (0.32 mg, 6.45% yield), 1c (2.06 mg, 41.31% yield), 
1d (1.98 mg, 39.98% yield). For S414A: 1a (0.25 mg, 5.08% yield), 1b (1.39 mg, 27.72% yield), 
1c (2.00 mg, 40.05% yield), 1d (0.96 mg, 19.27% yield). For T418A: 1a (0.10 mg, 2.08% yield), 1b 
(1.16 mg, 23.19% yield), 1c (1.55 mg, 31.07% yield), 1d (1.11 mg, 22.18% yield). 
To evaluate variants I54A and I54A/T57A, preparative-scale reactions (10 mL) were 
conducted using 5.0 mg of substrate 2 per variant. Each reaction mixture contained: 0.05% PezW 
variant, 0.5 µM GDH, 100 µM FAD, 0.5 mM substrate 2, 1.0 mM NAD+, 10 mM NaBr, 25 mM 
glucose, 1 mM glutathione, 70 U/mL catalase and 50 mM HEPES buffer (pH 7.4). After incubation 
for 12 h at 30 ℃ (metal bath), the reaction mixture was extracted with ethyl acetate (10 mL) and 
filtered through an 0.22 µm syringe filter. HPLC analysis of the crude products (quantified by peak 
area comparison) indicated conversions of 36% (I54A) and 59% (I54A/T57A) to 2d-2f prior to 
purification (Fig S22B). Products 2d-2f were separated and purified by HPLC method 2, and dried 
in vacuo. Detailed isolated yields are shown in Fig S22B. For I54A: 2d (0.35 mg, 7.10% yield), 2e 
(0.21 mg, 4.22% yield), 2f (0.07 mg, 1.38% yield). For I54A/T57A: 2d (1.16 mg, 23.14% yield), 2e 
(0.34 mg, 6.82% yield), 2f (0.05 mg, 1.04% yield). 
Isolated yield is defined as the amount of the desired products obtained after completion of 
the enzymatic reaction and subsequent isolation and purification (using HPLC method 2), 
expressed as a percentage of the theoretical maximum amount.
