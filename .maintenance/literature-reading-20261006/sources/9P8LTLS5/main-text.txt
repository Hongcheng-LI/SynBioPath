ABSTRACT: Halogenated phenazines hold promise as antimi-
crobial and antibiofilm agents, yet are mainly accessed via chemical
synthesis. Herein, we report PezW, a novel single-component
flavin-dependent halogenase (FDH) that halogenates phenazine
scaffolds, notably enabling enzymatic synthesis of bioactive 2-
bromo-1-hydroxyphenazine (2d) and 2,4-bromo-1-hydroxyphena-
zine (2f). Structural modeling and mutagenesis revealed key
residues critical for substrate binding and catalysis. These findings
highlight PezW’s potential as a biocatalyst for the synthesis of
halogenated phenazines with pharmaceutical relevance.
H
alogenated compounds play a pivotal role in pharma-
ceutical and agricultural fields, contributing to their
enhanced bioactivity and stability.1 78 of the 200 top-selling
small molecules in 2023 are halogenated compounds (https://
sites.arizona.edu/njardarson-lab/), and around 96% of recently
launched herbicides and insecticides contain halogens.2
Efficient production of halogenated compounds has thus
attracted significant attention. Given the environmentally
friendly reaction conditions and high stereo- or regioselectivity
of biocatalysis, exploiting enzymes for halogenation is of great
value for biocatalytic applications.
Flavin-dependent halogenases (FDHs) catalyze the selective
electrophilic halogenation of electron-rich aromatic rings,3
conjugated systems, terminal alkynes and 1,3-diketones. They
functionalize C−H bonds by installing halogen atoms (Cl−,
Br−, I−), generating a diverse array of halogenated compounds
in a highly selective manner.4 Majority of FDHs are known as
“two-component” halogenases (i.e., phenolic halogenase
PltM,5 7-Trp halogenase RebH,6 and 6-Trp halogenase
KtzR,7 naphthacemycin halogenase FasV,8 et al.), which
require an exogenous flavin reductase (FR) to initiate the
catalytic cycle. Differently, “single-component” FDHs (i.e.,
AoiQ,9 AetF,10 JamD11) eliminated the requirement for a
separate flavin reductase component, thus simplifying reaction
systems for biocatalytic applications.12
Phenazines are heterocyclic nitrogenous aromatics featuring
a dibenzopyrazine core (Figure 1A,B), mainly isolated from
Gram-positive (i.e., Streptomyces) and Gram-negative (i.e.,
Pseudomonas) bacteria.15 These compounds display a wide
range of bioactivities, including antimicrobial, antibiofilm,
antitumor, antifungal, and antimalarial effects.16 For example,
phenazine-1-carboxylic acid (PCA) has been commercialized
as the biopesticide “Shenqinmycin” for agricultural antifungal
applications.17 Noticeably, halogenated derivatives exhibit
potent inhibition against multidrug-resistant pathogens includ-
ing Mycobacterium tuberculosis and Staphylococcus aureus,
highlighting their therapeutic potential.18 However, to date,
halogenated phenazines are mainly chemically synthesized
(Figure S1A),14 underscoring the need to identify dedicated
phenazine halogenases for enzymatic biosynthesis.
Up to now, only eight naturally occurring halogenated
phenazines have been reported (Figure S1B), including
halogenated terpenylated phenazines (azulocyanin,19 marinoo-
cyanins A-F13) and 2-bromo-1-hydroxyphenazine (2d).20 Very
recently, a vanadium-dependent chloroperoxidase LvcH from
Streptomyces sp. CNZ-289 was reported, which could
incorporate Cl−and Br−into the terpenylated phenazine
lavanducyanin to generate azulocyanin (WS-9658B).21 Nota-
bly, 2d displays potent antibiofilm activities against S. aureus
and Staphylococcus epidermidis (MIC = 6.25 μM),22 yet its
biosynthetic halogenase remains unknown.
Inspired by this fact, we hypothesized that there is probably
a halogenase responsible for the biosynthesis of halogenated
phenazines somewhere adjacent to phenazine biosynthetic
gene clusters (BGCs), which typically contain six or seven
highly conserved core genes, phzA(B)CDEFG.23 To test this
hypothesis, herein we first performed an Enzyme Function
Initiative-Genome Neighborhood Tool (EFI-GNT) analysis
using PhzB (ID: AAC64490.1 from P. aeruginosa PAO1) as a
probe to extract phenazine BGCs in the public databases.24 A

As PezW and PezW1 are highly similar, PezW was chosen
for further enzymatic study. The pezW gene was codon-
optimized, synthesized, and cloned into pET30a (+). Soluble
expression of PezW was achieved as described in the Materials
and Methods section (Figures S4 and S5, Tables S2 and S3),
followed by in vitro enzymatic reactions. Delightedly, PezW
was able to recognize both 1,6-dihydroxyphenazine (1) and 1-
hydroxyphenazine (2), when using NaCl as a halide donor in
the presence of NADH/NADPH (Figure 3A). High resolution
liquid chromatography−mass spectrometry (LC-MS) analysis
confirmed the generation of mono- and dichlorinated
products: Cl-1 (1a, 1b, [M + H]−= 247.02, Figures S6B
(iii), S6C), di-Cl-1 (1c, 1d, [M + H]−= 280.99, Figures S6B
(iii), Figure S6C), Cl-2 (2a, 2b, [M + H]−= 231.03, Figures
S7B (ii), S7C) and di-Cl-2 (2c, [M + H]−= 264.99, Figures
S7B (ii), S7C). Compunds 1a-1d were isolated and structurally
characterized via NMR as ortho-substituent 2-chloro-1,6-
dihydroxyphenazine (1a), para-substituent 4-chloro-1,6-dihy-
droxyphenazine (1b), ortho- and para-disubstituent 2,9-
dichloro-1,6-dihydroxyphenazine (1c), and ortho-disubstituent
2,7-dichloro-1,6-dihydroxyphenazine (1d) (Tables S4, S5;
Figures S24−S39). Based on the structure of 1c and 1d, the
second chlorination should happen on 1a rather than 1b,
which was supported by further in vitro conversion (Figure
S8A). Importantly, the PezW-catalyed reaction proceeded
without an exogenous FR (Figure 3B, ii, (iii), supporting PezW
to be a single-component halogenase. Judging from the
conversion rates, PezW was more inclined to 1 than 2 with a
preference for NADH over NADPH as coenzyme.

We then evaluated the ability of PezW to utilize halogen
donor (F−, Cl−, Br−, I−). As shown in Figures 3C, S9 and
Table S6, when using 1 as an acceptor, PezW can utilize Cl−,
Br−, and I−but not F−, with the highest preference for Cl−, to
generate corresponding halogenated phenazines (1a-1h). LC-
MS analysis identified the products as mono- (1e, 1f, [M +
H]−= 290.98) and dibrominated (1g, [M + H]−= 370.89),
and iodinated (1h, [M + H]−= 338.96) derivatives (Figure
S5). To confirm the iodination activity, catalase and
L-
glutathione were added to eliminate the possibility of
iodination by HOI generated from H2O2 with I−. As shown
in Figure S10, iodinated phenazine 1h formation persisted,
demonstrating PezW’s intrinsic iodination activity. So far, only
a few FDHs have been reported to catalyze iodination,
including two component halogenases PltM5 and VirX126 and
single-component halogenases Bmp527 and AetF.11 Therefore,
PezW serves as another valuable FDH with iodination activity.
PezW can also transfer Cl−, Br−, and I−onto 2, generating
2a-2g (Figure 3D). LC-MS analysis identified Cl-2 (2a, 2b, [M
+ H]−= 231.03), diCl-2 (2c, [M + H]−= 264.99), Br-2 (2d,
2e, [M + H]−= 274.98), diBr-2 (2f, [M + H]−= 352.89), and
I-2 (2g, [M + H]−= 322.97) (Figure S7). 2d-2f were purified
and identified via NMR (Table S5 and Figures S40−42) as
ortho-2-bromo-1-hydroxyphenazine (2d),28 para-substituent 4-
bromo-1-hydroxyphenazine (2e)29 and ortho- and para-
disubstituent 2,4-bromo-1-hydroxyphenazine (2f).28 To de-
termine the order of the successive bromination, the catalytic
activity of PezW toward 2d and 2e was each tested. The results
revealed that bromination occurs initially at the ortho-position,
followed by the para-position (Figure S8B). Interestingly, the
preferences of PezW toward the halogen donors were not that
much different for 2. In this way, 2-bromo-1-hydroxyphenazine
(2d) and of note the synthetic biofilm-eradicating agent 2,4-
bromo-1-hydroxyphenazine (2f) were successfully reconsti-
tuted using enzymatic approach. We further explored the
activity of PezW toward selected aromatic compounds (Figure
S11). While the generation of corresponding halogenated
products could be confirmed by LC-MS for all the tested
compounds, their assay yields were far below those for
compounds 1 and 2 (Figures S5−S6, S12−S17). To
understand the molecular basis of the PezW-catalyzed reaction,
sequence alignment (Figure S18) was performed, revealing the
following features in PezW: (i) conserved FAD-binding motifs
G17AGMAG22 and W257LWVIP262, consistent with PltM5 and
VemK;25 (ii) an NAD(P)+-binding motif G310TGRLQYSA318,
similar to VemK;25 (iii) a highly conserved catalytic residue
(K93). A structure model of PezW was then generated by
using AlphaFold2,30 which was subjected to molecular docking
with FAD, NAD+ and substrate (1/2) using AutoDock vina
(Figure 4A). PezW shares the highest homology with the two-

To investigate the catalytic mechanism of PezW, residues
within 5 Å of the substrate were analyzed (Figure S21 and
Figure 4B). All these residues were subjected to alanine-
scanning mutagenesis followed by in vitro assays (Figure 4B,
Table S7). As shown in Figure 4C and Table S8, when the
resulting variants were each incubated with 1 in the presence of
NaCl, K93A completely lost activity, confirming its catalytic
role. Mutations in other residues underscore the importance of
C-terminal region in substrate recognition, consistent with that
observed for PltM.5 Given the intriguing bioactivities of mono-
and dibromized 2,29 we then carried out bromination assays
with 2. Except for K93A and W410A, all the other variants
showed altered profiles compared to chlorination with 1
(Figure 4D and Table S9). Delightedly, I54A displayed
significantly increased dibromination ability, leading to
conversion improvement of the biofilm-eradicating agent 2,4-
bromo-1-hydroxyphenazine (2f) by ∼2.6 fold compared to
wild-type PezW. T57A specifically brominates at the ortho-
position and not para-position any more. Therefore, double
mutant I54A/T57A was constructed, and the assay yield of 2d
was successfully achieved a ∼9.7-fold increase (Figure 4E and
Table S10).
To evaluate the synthetic utility of PezW and its variants,
preparative-scale reactions were performed. Using 0.05 mol %
wild-type PezW (30 mg 1), total isolated yield of chlorinated
products (1a-1d) was 70% (Figure S22). Variants Q121A,
S414A, and T418A (0.05 mol %, 5 mg 1) gave yields of 87%,
92%, and 78%, respectively (Figure S23). Q121A increased the
isolated yield of dichlorinated products (1c, 1d) from 47.6% to
81%. For bromination of 2 (0.05 mol % enzyme), total isolated
yields of brominated products (2d-2f) were 14% (wild-type),
13% (I54A), and 31% (I54A/T57A). Of note I54A and I54A/
T57A respectively increased the yields of 2f (from 2% to 4%)
and 2d (from 6% to 23%).
In summary, this study identified and characterized PezW as
a novel single-component FDH capable of regioselective
halogenation (Cl−, Br−, I−) of phenazine derivatives, shedding
light on the biosynthesis of naturally occurring 2-bromo-1-
hydroxyphenazine (2d). Structural insights from homology
modeling and mutagenesis provide a foundation for engineer-
ing PezW to enhance bioconversion and specificity. Prepara-
tive-scale reactions demonstrate the feasibility of enzymatic
synthesis of chlorinated and brominated phenazines. Of note
I54A and I54AT57A (0.05 mol %) respectively achieved
isolated yields of 4% (for 2f) and 23% (for 2d). These findings
set the stage for the enzymatic synthesis of halogenated
phenazines with pharmaceutical and industrial relevance.
