2. Results and discussion

2.1. The phylogenetic analysis of GTC2 and E2E2

  We previously collected 85 characterized terpene synthases (TPSs)
and 48 TPSs from 14 in-house sequenced Streptomyces islates [6]. Based
on phylogenetic analysis, we found that A0A848GTC2 (UniProt ID;
abbreviated as GTC2) from C. fulva and A0A1N6E2E2 (abbreviated as
E2E2) from C. niabensis clustered with known diterpene synthases, such
as chryseodiene synthase [11] and polychrichastrene synthase [10]
(Fig. S1). Moreover, E2E2 shares less than 30 % amino acid identity with
the collected TPSs, whereas GTC2 shows 42 % identity with chrys­
eodiene synthase. These analyses suggest that E2E2 is likely a novel
diterpene synthase, while GTC2 may possess a catalytic function similar
to that of chryseodiene synthase.

2.2. Functional characterization of GTC2

   Codon optimized gtc2 gene was synthesized and cloned into the
expression vector pET-28a(+) for expression. The recombinant protein
was heterologously expressed and purified to homogeneity (Fig. S2).
Enzymatic assays using GPP, FPP, GGPP, and GFPP as substrates
revealed that GTC2 could utilize both FPP and GGPP (Fig. 1). To obtain
sufficient quantities of products for structural elucidation, GTC2 was co-
expressed in an Escherichia coli chassis [22] equipped with either an FPP
or a GGPP synthase. After fermentation and purification, eight com­
pounds (1–8) were isolated. Compound 1 ([α]D20 = +20.8, c 0.25,
CH2Cl2) was identified as (R)-hedycaryol by comparison of  its MS
spectrum (Fig. S3) and 1H NMR data (Fig. S4) with those reported in the
literature [23]. Compounds 2–4 were identified as trans-β-farnesene,
nerolidol and farnesol, respectively, by comparison with authentic
standards. 3 ([α]D20 = +2.7, c 0.23, CH2Cl2) was further confirmed to
