Reshaping the Diversity of Oxidized Polyquinane Sesquiterpenoids
by Cytochrome P450s

ABSTRACT: Polyquinane sesquiterpenoids (PQSTs) are com-
plex compounds with two or three fused cabocyclopentane ring
systems, and the biocatalysts for direct C−H bond oxidation on
these scaffolds have rarely been discovered. In this study, we
discovered two versatile fungal CYP450s capable of performing
diverse oxidations on seven PQST scaffolds, resulting in the
generation of 20 unique products. Our findings significantly expand
the diversity of oxidized PQST scaffolds and provide important
biocatalysts for the selective oxidation of inert carbons of
terpenoids in future research.
S
elective C−H bond oxidation of inert carbons is a crucial
strategy for creating the oxidized diversity of hydrocarbon
skeletons, particularly for cyclized terpenoids and steroids, and
linear fatty acids.1,2 This modification step is commonly
accomplished by metal-containing enzymes such as α-
ketoglutarate-dependent dioxygenases (α-KGDs) or cyto-
chrome P450s (CYP450s).3,4 An example of such an oxidized
transformation is significantly represented in the biosynthesis
of two important drugs, sesquiterpenoid artemisinin and
diterpenoid paclitaxel (Figure 1A).5,6 Oxidation catalyzed by
CYP450s at sp2 or sp3 carbon atoms (1) improves the polarity
of terpenoid scaffolds, (2) is convenient for the construction of
more complex core backbones, and (3) provides additional
optional sites for further postmodification conversions, such as
esterification and glycosylation.7−9 Apart from these native
pathways, utilization of CYP450s as the biocatalysts on non-
native substrates, combination of chemical synthesis, and
divergent synthesis of highly oxidized terpenoids could be
achieved in a concise manner.10 Therefore, the discovery of
larger amounts of versatile CYP450s and application of them as
the biocatalytic reagents in a chemoenzymatic platform make
up a core method for accessing the unexplored oxidative space
of terpenoid scaffolds.
Our research focuses on the polyquinane sesquiterpenoid
(PQST) scaffolds, and the natural products derived from them
usually feature an intriguing two- or three-fused cabocyclo-
pentane ring system and exhibit a wide range of important
biological activities.11 To date, three biosynthetic examples of
PQST scaffolds have been well-identified, namely, angular
diquinane (−)-presilphiperfolan-8β-ol (1), angular triquinane
silphinene (2) from fungi, and angular triquinane α-isocomene
(3) from plants.12−15 The biosynthetic routes for these three
scaffolds involve the generation of a universal presilphiperfo-
lan-8-yl cation precursor from farnesyl pyrophosphate (FPP)
via a caryophyllenyl cation and a presilphiperfolan-9-yl cation
intermediate by various sesquiterpenoid cyclases (STCs)
(Figure 1B and Figure S1). Subsequently, the precursor
undergoes different transformations. (1) STC BcBOT2
catalyzes the quenching of the C-8 carbocation by water to
synthesize 1.12 (2) STC PeniA mediates Wagner−Meerwein
rearrangement, resulting in ring contraction and deprotonation
to yield 2.13,14 (3) STC MrTPS2 sequentially catalyzes
Wagner−Meerwein rearrangement, 1,3-H shift, 1,2-methyl
shift, and deprotonation to synthesize 3.16 It is noteworthy
that the instability of 1 leads to its non-enzymatic conversion
to other PQST scaffolds such as cameroonan-7α-ol (4),
silphiperfol-ene (5), and presilphiperfol-7(8)-ene (6).17,18
Despite the extensive knowledge of the biosynthetic
pathways of PQST scaffolds catalyzed by various STCs, the
identification of enzymes that directly oxidize C−H bonds on
these scaffolds remains limited. Among the few identified
oxidases, CYP450 BcBOT4 and PeniB are representative
examples (Figure 1C).13,19 BcBOT4 catalyzes the hydrox-
ylation of position C-11 of 1,19 while PeniB performs a six-
electron oxidation of C-15, a radical addition of C-15
carboxylate to the C-1−C-2 olefin, and a dehydrogenation of
the C-2 hydroxyl group to produce the unusual γ-lactone-2-
hydroxy[5.5.5.5]fenestrane in 2 (Figure S2).13 These two

CYP450-catalyzed reactions are crucial tailoring steps in the

multifunctionality and can catalyze different types of oxidation
reactions on the scaffold of 2.
Although 1−6 are structurally distinct, however, considering
they all originated from the presilphiperfolan-8-yl cation, we
hypothesized that CYP450s such as BcBOT4 and PeniB may
possess broader oxidizing capabilities with respect to various
PQST scaffolds. In this work, (1) we utilized yeast as a
platform to investigate the multifunctional abilities of these
CYP450s through combinatorial biosynthesis, (2) we con-
structed a CYP450 library from Fusarium sp. and identified
CYP450-4710, which exhibited exceptional oxidizing activity
on different PQST scaffolds, and (3) we employed genome
mining to discover a new STC, Coll34TC, responsible for the
biosynthesis of presilphiperfol-1(2)-ene (16) and (−)-pre-
silphiperfolan-1β-ol (17), and examined its compatibility with
CYP450s. Our findings not only expand the repertoire of
oxidized PQST scaffolds but also provide versatile CYP450
biocatalysts for the chemoenzymatic synthesis of highly
oxidized terpenoids.
We first investigated the feasibility of pairing BcBOT4 with
PeniA and MrTPS2. The cDNA of each gene was cloned
under the ADH2 promoter and transformed into Saccha-
romyces cerevisiae RC01 (see the Supporting Information), an
engineered strain containing an integrated copy of CYP450
reductase (CPR) from Aspergillus terreus.20 Unfortunately, the
expected oxidized products were not detected in S.c-peniA-
bcbot4 or S.c-mrtps2-bcbot4 (Figure S4). These results suggest
that BcBOT4 may not be suitable for oxidizing triquinane
scaffolds 2 and 3.
We next investigated the oxidizing ability of PeniB on
different PQST scaffolds by combining peniB and bcbot2 in S.
cerevisiae RC01. Fortunately, a variety of products were
produced by strain S.c-bcbot2-peniB (Figures S5). Seven of
them (7−13) were isolated from the large-scale cultivation,
and their structures were subsequently confirmed by HR-MS
and NMR analyses (Tables S4−S10 and Figures S13−S61 and
S178−S184). As shown in Figure 2A, (1) for all scaffolds,
PeniB catalyzes the hydroxylation on C-15 to generate 7, 9, 12,
and 13 and (2) for scaffold 1, PeniB could catalyze C-2
hydroxylation to yield 8 and for scaffold 4, apart from the C-2
hydroxylation for 10 formation, it could catalyze hydroxylation

at C-13 to form 11. Although PeniB does not exhibit its
multiple-step oxidation ability on other PQST scaffolds to
construct more complex structures, it has a broad tolerance for
substrates and could accept various PQST scaffolds to catalyze
oxidations on inert carbons. It should be mentioned that, for
plant-derived PQST scaffold 3, the current identified fungal
CYP450s PeniB and BcBOT4 are not the ideal candidates for
its oxidative modification (Figure S4).
To identify additional CYP450s capable of performing
oxidative modifications on PQST scaffolds, particularly scaffold
3, we constructed a fungal CYP450 library. We chose to focus
on Fusarium fungi as they have previously been reported to
catalyze oxidation reactions on inert carbons of steroid
skeletons.21,22 A total of 77 CYP450 family genes (Table
S31, GenBank entries OQ850184−OQ850260) were cloned
under the ADH2 promoter and integrated into the rDNA sites
of S. cerevisiae RC01 to generate the CYP450 library (see the
Supporting Information). Following a comprehensive screen-
ing process, we identified one CYP450 (CYP450-4710) from
Fusarium proliferatum that possessed the ability to catalyze
oxidation of 1, 4, and 3 (Figure S7). As shown in Figure 2A,
CYP450-4710 installed a hydroxyl group at C-5 of 1 to yield
14 [with the X-ray structure (Table S28, Figure S200, Table
S11, Figure S62−S68, and S185)] and catalyzed C-2
hydroxylation of 4 to form 10. These findings indicate that,
unlike PeniB, CYP450-4710 can catalyze oxidation at various
inert carbons depending on the PQST scaffold under
investigation.
In addition to the PQST scaffolds derived from fungi,
CYP450-4710 was also found to catalyze oxidations on plant-
derived PQST scaffold 3 to generate 15, which contains an
unexpected ether bond linkage between C-2 and C-6 and a
hydroxyl group at C-5 (Figure 2B, Table S12, and Figures
S69−S75 and S186). The transformation of 3 into 15
highlights the multifunctionality of CYP450-4710, which may
first catalyze hydroxylation at C-2 and C-5C-6 epoxidation.
The C-2 hydroxyl group was proposed to trigger C-6 of the
epoxide, an intermolecular 6-exo-tet cyclization leading to the
epoxide opening and forming a new oxygen bridge.9 The
successful construction of an artificial pathway through the
pairing of CYP450-4710 and 3 demonstrates that fungal
CYP450s could be utilized as potential oxidative catalyst
sources for the derivation of plant sesquiterpenoids. Indeed,
when CYP450-4710 was combined with amorpha-4,11-diene
synthase, it could catalyze successive dihydroxylations and
double bond isomerization on the amorpha-4,11-diene scaffold
(Table S27, Figures S8, S172−S177, and S199).
Encouraged by the combination of results presented above,
we attempted to discover more PQST synthases and CYP450s
by genome mining. With the help of sequence similarity
network (SSN) analysis (Figure S9),23 a gene cluster
containing one sesquiterpene cyclase Coll34TC and one
Coll34P450 from Colletotrichum fructicola was found (Figure
3A). Coll34TC is 66% similar to and 48% identical to
BcBOT2, which indicates its catalytic ability to synthesize the
PQST scaffold. To validate this hypothesis, the cDNA of
Coll34TC was chemically synthesized, cloned under the
control of the ADH2 promoter, and transformed into S.
cerevisiae (see the Supporting Information). GC-MS analysis
showed that two compounds (16 and 17) were produced by
S.c-coll34TC (Figure S10), which were purified from the large-
scale cultivation, and their structures are confirmed via NMR
analyses (Tables S13 and S14 and Figures S76−S87).
Compounds 16 and 17 are presilphiperfol-1-ene and
presilphiperfolan-1-ol (Figure 3 and Figure 1B), which were
previously isolated from the liverwort Concocephalum conicum
and the fern Anemia tomentosa var. anthriscifolia, respec-
tively.24,25 These results confirm that Coll34TC was indeed a
PQST synthase; via the presilphiperfolan-9-yl cation, it may
mediate the 1,2-H shift to generate the presilphiperfolan-1-yl
cation, which could be undergo deprotonation to yield 16 or
quenched by water to form 17 (Figure 1B). When coll34P450
was coexpressed with coll34TC, compound 18 was produced
by S.c-coll34TC-coll34P450 and was identified as C-3 hydroxyl
16 (Figure 3A, Table S15, and Figures S12A, S88−S94, and
S187). These results demonstrate that Coll34P450 is the
previously unidentified C-3 hydroxylase toward PQST
scaffolds and that it catalyzes oxidation only on the
silphiperfol-1-ene scaffold, while presilphiperfolan-1-ol may
be a shunt product of Coll34TC.
When Coll34P450 was combined with PeinA, BcBOT2, and
MrTPS2, the corresponding oxidized PQST products were not
observed (Figure S11). However, when PeniB was coexpressed
with Coll34TC in S. cerevisiae RC01, three major products
(19−21) were produced by S.c-coll34TC-peniB (Figure S12B)
and subsequently isolated from large-scale cultivation. Their
structures were characterized by HR-MS and NMR analyses
(Tables S16−S18 and Figures S95−S115 and S188−S190). As

be further dehydrogenated at C-2−C-3 by CYP450-4710 to

structure (Table S29 and Figure S201)] is possibly formed
from 17 via CYP450-4710-catalyzed successive dihydroxyla-
tion reactions at C-2 and C-3.
In summary, we developed a yeast-based platform for the
oxidative derivatization of PQST scaffolds using various
strategies such as combination biosynthesis, CYP450 library
construction, and genome mining. We mainly identified two
versatile CYP450s, PeniB and CYP450-4710, that can catalyze
a wide range of oxidation reactions on seven PQST scaffolds
resulting in a total of 20 products with diverse oxidative
degrees, including (di)hydroxylation, epoxidation, carboxyla-
tion, and ether ring formation. Our work significantly reshapes
the diversity of oxidized PQST scaffolds and provides
important biocatalysts for the selective oxidation of inert
carbons in terpenoids in the future.
