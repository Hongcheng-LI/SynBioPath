ABSTRACT: Ten undocumented carbazole derivatives (2−11)
along with the reported analogue (1) were isolated from the
mangrove-derived Streptomyces sp. OUCMDZ-5511, cultured with
NaBr-supplemented liquid medium. Compounds 1−7 are bromi-
nated carbazoles, and 8, 10, and 11 feature an additional thiazole or
2,3-dihydro-1,4-oxathiine rings, respectively. Their structures were
identified through spectroscopic techniques, computational chem-
istry, and X-ray crystallography. Notably, compounds 6 and 8
effectively inhibited immune cell migration, indicating anti-
inflammatory activity in vivo, potentially via Myd88/Nf-κB
pathways, as suggested for compound 6.
I
nflammation is the immune system’s initial reaction to
stimuli and contributes to the pathology and progression of
chronic diseases like atherosclerosis, arthritis, cancer, and
neurodegenerative disorders.1 Current anti-inflammatory treat-
ments, predominantly steroids and NSAIDs, are effective but
may cause serious adverse effects like hypertension, osteopo-
rosis, immunosuppression, or digestive ulcers with long-term
use.2,3 Thus, identifying nontoxic, efficacious anti-inflammatory
agents is imperative. Moreover, mangrove ecosystems are
emerging as rich sources for novel bioactive natural
products,4,5 particularly those from sediment-associated
microbes,6 with mangrove actinomycetes yielding compounds
of unique structures and significant biological activities.7,8
To further investigate the influence of halide salt stress on
microbial secondary metabolites,9−13 the halotolerant strain
Streptomyces sp. OUCMDZ-5511, originally isolated from
mangrove sediments and known for metabolizing chlorinated
carbazole derivatives with anti-quorum sensing (QS) activity
under NaF stress,14 was cultured in media containing NaBr.
Ethyl acetate (EtOAc) extracts of the fermentation media were
tested for anti-inflammatory properties using a CuSO4-induced
zebrafish model.15 Preliminary results indicated that extracts
from a medium enhanced with 5% NaBr exhibited notable
anti-inflammatory effects (Figure S1). Subsequent large-scale
fermentation and chromatographic separation uncovered 10
new carbazole derivatives (2−11) as well as brocarbazomycin
A (1).16 The anti-inflammatory potential of these compounds
(1−11) was then evaluated using the established CuSO4-
induced zebrafish model.15

Compound 10 was isolated as a gray amorphous powder
with the molecular formula C14H11NO2S, as indicated by
HRESIMS with m/z 256.0426 ([M −H]−) (Figure S78). The
NMR data (Figures S81−S85, Table 4) were highly similar to
those of 9, suggesting that compound 10 is also a carbazole.
The NMR also exhibited additional 1H−1H COSY correlations
connecting OH-2′ (δH 7.39) to H2-3′ (δH 3.30/3.11) via H-2′
(δH 5.61) (Figures 1 and S83) and HMBC correlations from
H-2′ (δH 5.61) to C-3 (δC 142.8) and from H-3′ (δH 5.61) to
C-4 (δC 111.2) (Figures 1 and S85), suggesting a 2,3-dihydro-
1,4-oxathiine ring fused to the carbazole core at C-3 and C-4
via 1′-O and 4′-S linkages. Compound 10 was identified as 2,3-
dihydro-7H-[1,4]oxathiino[2,3-c]carbazol-3-ol and given the
trivial name streptomycarbazole B. Chiral separation of
racemic 10 produced enantiomers 10a and 10b (Figure
S86), but 10a and 10b rapidly reverted to the racemate due to
hemiacetal instability in water (Figure S87),17 resulting in no
optical rotation or ECD Cotton effect.
Compound 11 was isolated as a gray crystal with the
molecular formula of C14H10NO3S, as identified by HRESIMS
with m/z 272.0381 (calcd for C14H10NO3S, 272.0376) [M −
H]−(Figure S88). Its 1D NMR data closely resembled those
of 10 (Figures S91 and S92, Table 4), with a notable difference
being an increase in the chemical shift of C-3′ (δC 48.8) (Δδ
+19.1) due to oxygenation of the 4′-S. Furthermore,
compound 11 exhibited COSY and HMBC correlations
analogous to those of compound 10 (Figures 1, S93, and
S95). An additional oxygen atom in the structure suggested
that compound 11 is the sulfoxide analog of 10. Consequently,
compound 11 was elucidated as 3-hydroxy-2,3-dihydro-7H-
[1,4]oxathiino[2,3-c]carbazole-1-oxide and given the trivial
name streptomycarbazole C. Compound 11 featured C-2′ and
4′-S as chiral centers, consistent with compounds 10 and 9,
respectively. Chiral separation of 11 yielded four stereoisomers
(Figure S96), two pairs of which rapidly interconverted in
water at hemiacetal C-2′, as observed for 10 (Figure S97).
Similarly, the absolute configuration of 4′-S was determined
using ECD calculations, as depicted in Figure S98. It is worth
noting that compound 11 may potentially be an artifact
resulting from oxidation during the isolation process.18
Compounds 1−11 were assessed for anti-inflammatory
activity using a CuSO4-induced zebrafish inflammation
model.15 Treatment with CuSO4 increases immune cell
migration compared to the untreated control. Among the 11
compounds, compound 7 caused malformation, disqualifying it
from further analysis. Treatment with compounds 6 and 8
significantly reduced CuSO4-induced immune cell migration,
highlighting their anti-inflammatory potential (Figure S2). We
chose compound 6 for further investigation due to its
exceptional ability to reduce the number of immune cells.

zebrafish mortality across 14 concentrations (Figure 2A),

The inflammation caused by CuSO4 may be due to Myd88/

compound 6 (Figure S3A−3G).
Additionally, CuSO4 treatment downregulated the expres-
sion of pparγ, an inhibitor of inflammatory mediators, a
reduction that was notably mitigated by compound 6
treatment, as shown in Figure 3F.26 These findings suggest
that the anti-inflammatory effects of compound 6 may be
associated with the suppression of pro-inflammatory factors
and the enhancement of anti-inflammatory factors within the
Myd88/Nf-κB pathway.27−29
In conclusion, this research presents eight 4-bromo
carbazole derivatives (1−8) and three 4-sulfur carbazole
derivatives (9−11). Notably, the 4-bromo-8-hydroxy derivative
(6) and the 4-Br-8-N thiazole derivative (8) demonstrate
potent anti-inflammatory effects in a zebrafish model. The
mechanism of action for the most active compound (6) likely
involves suppressing pro-inflammatory and enhancing anti-
inflammatory cytokines via Myd88/Nf-κB pathways. Con-
sequently, compound 6 emerges as a promising anti-
inflammatory agent, potentially addressing the limitations of
current treatments for inflammatory diseases.
■EXPERIMENTAL SECTION
General Experimental Procedures. Ultraviolet (UV) spectra
were recorded on a Thermo Fisher Scientific NanoDrop One
microspectrophotometer. IR spectra were taken on a Nicolet Nexus
470 spectrophotometer as KBr disks. Optical rotations were measured
with a JASCO P-1020 digital polarimeter. ECD spectra were
measured on a JASCO-815 spectrometer. NMR spectra of
compounds 1−11 were recorded on a Bruker Avance 500 MHz
spectrometer or a JEOL JNM-ECP 600 spectrometer in DMSO-d6
solution and were referenced to the corresponding residual solvent
signals (δH/C 2.50/39.5 for DMSO-d6) with TMS as an internal
standard. Chemical shift (δ) was expressed in ppm. HRESIMS spectra
were collected using a Q-TOF Ultima Global GAA076 LC mass
spectrometer. ESIMS data were measured using a Waters ACQUITY
SQD 2 UPLC/MS system with a reversed-phase C18 column
(ACQUITY UPLC BEH C18, 2.1 × 50 mm, 1.7 μm) at a flow rate of
0.4 mL/min. Column chromatography (CC) was performed on silica
gel (100−200 mesh, 200−300 mesh, 300−400 mesh, Qingdao
Marine Chemical Ltd.); vacuum−liquid chromatography (VLC)
utilized silica gel H (Qingdao Marine Chemical Factory) and
Sephadex LH-20 (Amersham Biosciences). Semipreparative HPLC
was performed using an octadecyl silica (ODS) column (YMC-pack
ODS-A, 10 × 250 mm, 5 μm, 4 mL/min) or an octadecyl and phenyl
co-bonded (fully porous) silica column (C18/AR, 10 × 250 mm, 5
μm, 4 mL/min).
Actinobacterial Strain. The actinobacterial strain OUCMDZ-
5511 was isolated from Thai mangrove soil and identified as
Streptomyces sp. by 16S rRNA gene sequence and morphological
characteristics.14
Cultivation and Extraction. Spores were seeded into 150 mL of
A1 medium containing soluble starch (10 g), yeast extract (4 g),
peptone (2 g), Fe2(SO4)3·7H2O (0.04 g), and distilled water (1 L) in
500 mL flasks, incubated at 180 rpm and 28 °C for 3 days, and then
transferred to 240 flasks with 100 g of corn, 80 mL of A1 medium,
and 5.0 g of NaBr. After static culture at 28 °C for 30 days, each flask’s
medium was extracted thrice with 600 mL of EtOAc for 24 h. The
combined EtOAc solutions were concentrated under vacuum to yield
25.5 g of extracts.
Purification. The EtOAc extract (25.5 g) was divided into 10
fractions (Fr.1−Fr.10) via silica gel VLC using a dichloromethane
(DCM)−MeOH gradient (1:0−0:1). Fr.1 and Fr.2 (4.8 g combined,
3 L each, v/v 1:0 and 100:1) were further split into 10 subfractions
(Fr.1.1−Fr.1.10) utilizing petroleum ether (PE)−DCM (1:0−0:1) on
silica gel VLC. Subfraction Fr.1.6 (875 mg, v/v 10:1, 0.9 L) was then
separated into 12 subfractions (Fr.1.6.1−Fr.1.6.12) using a Sephadex
LH-20 column with MeOH elution. Compounds 1 (18.8 mg, tR 9.2
min) and 2 (3.2 mg, tR 8.5 min) were isolated from Fr.1.6.9 (38 mg)
using semipreparative HPLC on an ODS column with 80% MeCN−
H2O plus 0.5% trifluoroacetic acid (TFA). Compound 6 (2.0 mg, tR
8.0 min) was purified from Fr.1.6.10 (29 mg) similarly with 90%
MeCN−H2O containing 0.5% TFA. Fr.1.3 (1.1 g, v/v 50:1, 0.9 L)
was separated into 11 subfractions (Fr.1.3.1−Fr.1.3.11) using a
Sephadex LH-20 column and MeOH. Fr.1.3.7 (46 mg) was purified
by semipreparative HPLC with 90% MeCN−H2O and 0.5% TFA on
an ODS column to yield compound 3 (2.5 mg, tR 8.1 min).
Compound 7 (5.6 mg, tR 12.1 min) was isolated from Fr.1.3.8 (65
mg) under similar conditions but with 80% MeCN−H2O containing
0.5% TFA. Fr.3 (5.6 g, v/v 95:1, 3 L) was chromatographed on
Sephadex LH-20 with MeOH, yielding 13 subfractions (Fr.3.1−
Fr.3.13). Fr.3.11 (458 mg) underwent semipreparative HPLC on a
C18/AR column with 55% MeCN−H2O and 0.5% TFA, producing
compounds 4 (15.0 mg, tR 15.5 min), 5 (8.7 mg, tR 10.5 min), and 10
(3.6 mg, tR 15.0 min). Compound 8 (5.1 mg, tR 10.5 min) was
isolated from Fr.3.13 (121 mg) using semipreparative HPLC with a
C18/AR column and 65% MeCN−H2O plus 0.5% TFA. Compounds
9 (1.4 mg, tR 11.6 min) and 11 (4.5 mg, tR 5.7 min) were obtained
from Fr.3.7 (198 mg) through semipreparative HPLC on a C18/AR
column with 50% MeCN−H2O and 0.5% TFA. Enantiomers 9a (0.6
mg, tR 4.5 min) and 9b (0.5 mg, tR 6.0 min) and diastereomeric
mixtures 11a (1.1 mg, tR 4.5 min) and 11b (2.7 mg, tR 6.5 min) were
each purified via chiral separation on a Chiral INA analytical column
using 55% MeCN−H2O as the eluent.
