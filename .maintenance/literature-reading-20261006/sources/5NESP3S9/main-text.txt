ABSTRACT: The bridged polycyclic sesquiterpenoids derived from
sativene, isosativene, and longifolene have unique structures, and many
chemical synthesis approaches with at least 10 steps have been reported.
However, their biosynthetic pathway remains undescribed. A minimal
biosynthetic gene cluster (BGC), named bip, encoding a sesquiterpene
cyclase (BipA) and a cytochrome P450 (BipB) is characterized to produce
such complex sesquiterpenoids with multiple carbon skeletons based on
enzymatic assays, heterologous expression, and precursor experiments.
BipA is demonstrated as a versatile cyclase with (−)-sativene as the
dominant product and (−)-isosativene and (−)-longifolene as minor ones.
BipB is capable of hydroxylating different enantiomeric sesquiterpenes,
such as (−)-longifolene and (+)-longifolene, at C-15 and C-14 in turn.
The C-15- or both C-15- and C-14-hydroxylated products are then further
oxidized by unclustered oxidases, resulting in a structurally diverse array of sesquiterpenoids. Bioinformatic analysis reveals the BipB
homologues as a discrete clade of fungal sesquiterpene P450s. These findings elucidate the concise and divergent biosynthesis of
such intricate bridged polycyclic sesquiterpenoids, offer valuable biocatalysts for biotransformation, and highlight the distinct
biosynthetic strategy employed by nature compared to chemical synthesis.
B
ridged polycyclic terpenoids remain fascinating targets for
chemists, owing to their distinctive synthetic challenges
and significant biological values posed by their unique
topological frameworks.1 Sesquiterpenoids with the sativene-,
isosativene-, and longifolene-related skeletons are emblematic
of this type of molecule (Figure 1A). They feature remarkable
tricyclo[4,4,0,0]decane-, tricyclo[5,4,0,0]undecane-, and
tricyclo[5,2,1,0]decane-bridged ring systems, respectively. For
over half a century, longifolene and related sesquiterpenoids
have been the subject of numerous attempts at total synthesis,
employing many visionary synthetic strategies since the first
example was reported in 1961.2−13 The most recent chemical
synthesis, reported in 2022, utilized functionalized camphor as
a precursor to introduce topological complexity in the
synthesis of (−)-longiborneol and its oxidative congeners
through late-stage C−H functionalization.10 Eventually,
(−)-longifolene was derived from (−)-longiborneol via a
Wagner−Meerwein shift. The shortest total synthesis of
(−)-longifolene was achieved in 10 steps from available
commercial materials (Figure 1B).10
Biosynthetically, the diverse and intricate structures of
terpenoids arise from specialized and versatile biosynthetic
enzymes.14,15 Their biosynthesis can be broadly characterized
as a “two-phase” process (Figure 1C). In the first phase,
terpene cyclases (TCs) play a pivotal role in catalyzing the
stereoselective formation of scaffolds using linear and achiral
oligomeric isoprenoid diphosphate precursors, employing a
carbocation-based mechanism.16 In the second phase, various
tailoring reactions take place on the terpene skeletons,
primarily through the oxidation of alkenes and C−H
bonds.14,15 Notably, cytochrome P450 enzyme-facilitated
regioselective and stereoselective hydroxylation, epoxidation,
and other modifications are among the most common tailoring
reactions.17 P450 enzymes, acting as biocatalysts for C−H
functionalization, have found widespread application in the
chemoenzymatic synthesis of structurally complex terpenoids.
Their use significantly enhances the efficiency of synthetic
processes in this context.18−22
Despite extensive efforts invested in the discovery, biological
evaluation, and total synthesis of sativene-,23−27 isosativene-,28
and longifolene-related sesquiterpenoids,2−13 the genetic and
enzymatic mechanisms responsible for their biosynthesis have
remained elusive.26 In this study, we have identified 24 such
sesquiterpenoids from the endophytic fungus Bipolaris sp. L1-2

Discovery and Identification of Bridged Polycyclic

These sesquiterpenoids were categorized into 11 different
carbon skeletons and proposed to derive from (−)-sativene,
(−)-isosativene, and (−)-longifolene scaffolds through oxida-
tive C−C bond cleavage and conjugation (Figure 1D).
Compounds 1−3 retained the intact sativene scaffold with
varying oxidation state, while 4−15 and 16/17 featured 14,15-
seco-sativene and 1,15-seco-sativene skeletons, respectively.
Compounds 14 and 15 contained three additional carbon
atoms at C-14 and C-15, forming the 14,15-seco-14-
trihomosativene and 14,15-seco-15-trihomosativene skeletons,
respectively. A similar skeleton-forging strategy was applied to
isosativene- and longifolene-derived compounds 18−21 and
22−24. Compounds 18/19 and 22 were oxidized isosativene
and longifolene, while 20/21, 23, and 24 had 1,14-seco-
isosativene, 14,15-seco-longifolene, and 1,15-seco-longifolene
skeletons, respectively. The oxidized carbon atoms, ranging
from 3 to 8 sites, as well as the C−C bonds that underwent
oxidative cleavage, were illustrated on the precursor scaffolds
(Figure 1A), signifying the occurrence of multistep oxidative
events during the biosynthesis of these complex sesquiterpe-
noids.
Subsequent to characterizing the chemical structures, we
evaluated the biological activity of purified compounds. None
of them exhibited inhibitory activity at 20 μM against tumor
cell lines HepG2 and LoVo. We also assessed their anti-
inflammatory activities by measuring their inhibitory effects on
lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-induced NO release in RAW264.7
macrophages. Compound 15 displayed notable bioactivity with
an IC50 value of 3.9 ± 0.1 μM (Figure 1E).
The intriguingly diverse structures of these compounds
prompted us to investigate their biosynthetic basis. We
sequenced and analyzed the genome of this strain and
identified seven potential terpene BGCs using the fungal
antiSMASH online tool (Figures 1F and S86),31,32 These
BGCs encoded three sesquiterpene synthases/cyclases, one
bifunctional sesterterpene synthase, one squalene cyclase, and
two phytoene synthases. To pinpoint the target BGC among
the three candidate sesquiterpenoid BGCs, we collected
mycelia at two time points, 2 and 6 days, corresponding to
minimal and high levels of production of 15, 19, and 22, for
transcriptomic sequencing (Figure S87A). Transcriptomic
analysis indicated that the expression of only one sesquiterpe-
noid BGC from contig 4 matched the production profile
(Figure S87B), which was named bip, and encoded only one
putative TC and one P450 (Figure 1F and Table S1). Since
longiborneol possesses a related bridged polycyclic skeleton,
we compared the three identified sesquiterpene synthases/
cyclases with the longiborneol synthase FgCLM1.33 The TC
BipA exhibited 33.5% sequence identity with FgCLM1,
whereas no detectable sequence identity was observed for
the other two sesquiterpene synthases/cyclases from contigs 2
and 5. This homology search further supports the bip BGC as
the biosynthetic origin of the sesquiterpenoids discovered in
this study. Considering the highly oxidized and diverse
structures of the sesquiterpenoids discovered in this study,
the bip BGC was unexpectedly simple.

Characterization of the TC BipA in Vitro and in E. coli.
To validate the proposed functions of bip BGC, we conducted
in vitro reconstitution of the TC BipA (Tables S14 and S15).
We purified the soluble and homogeneous protein by
expressing in E. coli BL21(DE3). Enzymatic cascade reactions
were adopted to produce farnesyl diphosphates (FPP) through
the combination of two kinases (EcTHIM and MjIPK) and
FPP synthase (GsFDPS),34 as confirmed by mass analysis
(Figure S88). The addition of BipA in the enzymatic reaction
system resulted in the formation of three sesquiterpene
products, as shown by the analysis of the GC-MS and 1H
NMR spectra (Figure 2A and B). All sesquiterpenes showed
the same molecular weight of 204 Da. The major peak with a
retention time of 9.41 min accounted for over 90% of the total
sesquiterpene products. Its structure was unambiguously
determined as (−)-sativene through 1D and 2D NMR data,
as well as CD spectrum analysis (Table S16 and Figures S89−
S94). Its mass spectrum perfectly matched that of standard
(−)-sativene (Figure S95). Another noticeable peak eluting at
9.67 min was determined to be (−)-longifolene, based on the
comparison of the GC-MS data and elution profile with
commercial (+)-longifolene (Figures 2A and S96). The
signature signals for the terminal double bond of (−)-sativene
and (−)-longifolene were also identified in the
1H NMR
spectrum as shown in Figure 2B. Additionally, a third pair of
signals for a terminal double bond was observed, and the
corresponding sample was enriched through the guidance of
1H NMR detection. GC-MS analysis of this sample showed a
peak of 204 Da with identical retention time to (−)-sativene
and similar mass spectrum to (−)-longifolene. Thus, the third
product of BipA was assigned as (−)-isosativene (Figures 2B
and S97).
When geranyl diphosphate (GPP) or geranylgeranyl
diphosphate (GGPP) was provided for BipA reaction using
similar enzymatic cascade reactions, no corresponding
monoterpenes or diterpenes were detected by GC-MS analysis
(Figure S98A).
The function of BipA was also characterized in E. coli, where
it was co-transformed with the pBbA5c-MevT(CO)-MBIS
(CO, ispA) plasmid (pMev), encoding a heterologous MEV
pathway consisting of eight genes controlled by the lacUV5
promoter.35,36 The hexane-extraction products were analyzed
by GC-MS and 1H NMR, yielding results consistent with those
from in vitro enzymatic reactions (Figure S98B). These data
clearly demonstrated that BipA is a specific and versatile
sesquiterpene cyclase responsible for the production of three
sesquiterpenes, with preference for (−)-sativene.

and mPW1PW91) for the stationary points were calculated
and are shown in Figure 2D. The overall reactions were
exothermic, and the energetic barriers required for the
formation of the three sesquiterpenes through proposed
transition states (TS) were low, demonstrating the feasibility
of this pathway. The largest barriers were associated with the
first macrocyclization step starting from the linear carbocation
(RC in Figure 2D) after the detachment of the diphosphate
group. The calculated activation energy barrier for the 1,10-
closure was 16.0 kcal/mol, which was 1.5 kcal/mol lower than
that for 1,11-closure. This difference accounts for the fact that
the isolated (−)-sativene- and (−)-isosativene-derived sesqui-
terpenoids outnumbered the (−)-longifolene-derived ones
(Figure 1D). Additionally, in the final step of (−)-longifolene
formation, it needed to overcome a barrier from intermediate 8
(INT8) to INT9 through TS9, which was larger than that of
(−)-sativene and (−)-isosativene by 6.8 kcal/mol, from INT3
to INT4 through TS4.
The production of (−)-sativene was achieved by losing a
proton from INT4 with the lowest energy. It is proposed to
undergo a rearrangement process through TS5 to give INT5,
which, after proton loss, produced (−)-isosativene. The
calculated barrier between INT4 and TS5 was 14.0 kcal/mol,
corresponding to the extra energy required for (−)-isosativene
production. These computational data were consistent with the
observation that (−)-isosativene-derived sesquiterpenoids were
much less common than (−)-sativene-derived ones (Figure
1D).

These results enable us to propose the biosynthetic pathway
for these structurally diverse sesquiterpenoids: (1) BipA-like
TCs cyclize FPP to generate the (−)-sativene, (−)-isosativene,
and (−)-longifolene scaffolds, (2) BipB-like P450s catalyze the
hydroxylation at C-15 and C-14 in turn, and (3) unclustered
oxidases recognize the C-15- or both C-14- and C-15-
hydroxylated product for further oxidation (Figure 4F). The
BipB-catalyzed hydroxylation at C-15 serves as an initiator for
the oxidation by the unclustered oxidases.
To further evaluate the substrate promiscuity of BipB,
similar chemical supplementation experiments were conducted
for several additional commercially available sesquiterpenes,
including α-caryophyllene, β-caryophyllene, α-cedrene, (+)-ce-
drol, santalol, and guaiazulene. Analysis of the metabolites
showed that the AO-bipB strain produced no additional peaks
compared to the AO strain, suggesting that BipB is specific to
sativene, isosativene, and longifolene.
Bioinformatic Analysis of bip BGC. The versatility of
BipB as a key point in the divergent biosynthesis of these
polycyclic sesquiterpenoids inspired us to look into the
sequenced homologues, which might provide insights into
the evolution of fungal sesquiterpene P450s. A sequence
similarity network (SSN) was generated with BipB as seed
using the UniProt database (version 2023_04).44 A 51%
sequence identity threshold was applied to separate the BipB-
containing cluster in the SSN, which consisted of 12 putative
P450 sequences (nine unique) (Figure S176 and Tables S1
and S27). Each of these 12 BipB homologues was individually
co-located with a sesquiterpene cyclase, which exhibited high
sequence identity with BipA (Table S1). BipA and BipB shared
identical sequences with ones from Bipolaris sorokiniana
(Sacc.) Shoemaker (BsBipA and BsBipB, UniProt ID:
A0A8H5Z7W4 and A0A8H5Z7T4) and Cochliobolus sativus
ATCC 201652 (CsBipA2 and CsBipB2, UniProt ID: M2SPA3
and M2TK17). Several strains of C. sativus were reported to
produce (−)-sativene-derived sesquiterpenoids.27 This analysis
suggested the involvement of these 12 newly identified
minimal BGCs in the biosynthesis of (−)-sativene-, (−)-iso-
sativene-, and (−)-longifolene-related sesquiterpenoids, further
supporting our conclusions.
Given that many fungal sesquiterpenoid biosynthetic P450s
have been characterized, the evolutionary relationships of
BipB-like P450s and others were investigated through
phylogenetic analysis. A maximum-likelihood tree was
constructed with 31 P450s involved in fungal sesquiterpenoid
biosynthesis (Figure 5A and Table S27), which clearly showed
the presence of BipB-like P450s in a separate clade.
Compilation of the reaction steps catalyzed by these P450s
revealed the multifunctionality of several reported P450s,
including BcBOT1,45−47 AneG,48 FlvD,49 Tri4/FgTri4,50
AstB,51 PeniB,52 and Fma-P45053 (Figure 5B). The
phylogenetic distinction between BipB-like P450s and other
fungal P450s also corresponds with their substrate specificity.
■CONCLUSION
The biosynthesis of sativene-, isosativene-, and longifolene-
derived sesquiterpenoids is unexpectedly concise and pro-
gressively divergent, which leads to extremely diverse
structures using a sophisticated biosynthetic machinery with
only two genes in cluster and unclustered genes that seem
widespread in different fungi. This study offers a clear picture
of the biosynthetic pathway of these complex polycyclic
sesquiterpenoids, sets up a platform for the characterization of
the unclustered oxidases, provides valuable biocatalysts for
biotransformation, and highlights the distinct biosynthetic
strategy employed by nature compared to chemical synthesis.

Bioinformatics. The BipB sequence was used as a seed for SSN
construction using the “Sequence BLAST” option of the EFI-EST
tools (UniProt version 2023_04).44,55,56 Then an alignment score
corresponding to 51% sequence identity was used for final SSN
generation. The 100% representative node network was opened and
analyzed by Cytoscape.57 Nodes were shown in different colors based
on the size of each cluster.
All P450 sequences, together with BipB-like ones, were inputted to
Mega11 to construct the maximum-likelihood tree.58
Cytotoxic Activity. Cytotoxicity of 2, 14, 15, 20, 21, 32−36, and
39−43 was evaluated against HepG2, LoVo, and RAW264.7
macrophage cells by the CCK8 assay following our previously
published protocol.59
Measurement of Nitric Oxide Content. NO production was
determined for the same set of compounds by the level of nitrite in
the culture medium using the Griess assay.60 A previously published
protocol was employed.59
