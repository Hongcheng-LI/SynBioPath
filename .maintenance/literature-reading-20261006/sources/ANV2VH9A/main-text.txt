                                                       for the highly efficient, economical production of physcion and a representative example for microbial synthetic

3.1. Activation of geodin biosynthesis in A. terreus

    It is worth noting that a substantial number of BGCs for microbial
SMs are either silent or expressed at extremely low levels. The expres­
sion of these BGCs could be activated or further enhanced by engi­
neering  pathway-specific  DNA-binding  transcription   factors  or
modifying global regulatory factors via chromatin modulation (Mache­
leidt et al., 2016; Meng et al., 2022). Initially, geodin biosynthesis was
shown to be strongly inhibited in the statin-hyperproducing A. terreus
strain HXN301, which was used as the parent strain in this study, and
negligible amounts of suspected emodin (16) and its O-methylated de­
rivative questinol (4) were detected, along with a variety of statins (data
not shown)  (Fig. 2A, trace  i). To activate geodin biosynthesis, a
promoter-exchange strategy was applied to generate an OEgedR variant
from HXN301-ΔpyrG (a uracil auxotrophic variant of HXN301), in
which another copy of the transcriptional activator gedR gene was
overexpressed under  the  control  of  the  constitutive promoter  of
glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase from A. terreus (PgpdAt)
(Huang et al., 2014) (Fig. 2B and S1). As expected, the colonies of the
OEgedR variant cultured on PDA plates were darker than those of the
parent strain HXN301 (Fig. S2), indicating that this mutant produced
additional SMs. Further intensive analysis revealed that the OEgedR
variant showed many more chromatographic peaks in the HPLC profile
