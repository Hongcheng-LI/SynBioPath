ABSTRACT: Nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) chemical shift calcu-
lations are powerful tools for structure elucidation and have been extensively
employed in both natural product and synthetic chemistry. However, density
functional theory (DFT) NMR chemical shift calculations are usually time-
consuming, while fast data-driven methods often lack reliability, making it
challenging to apply them to computationally intensive tasks with a high
requirement on quality. Herein, we have constructed a 54-layer-deep graph
convolutional network for
13C NMR chemical shift calculations, which
achieved high accuracy with low time-cost and performed competitively with
DFT NMR chemical shift calculations on structure assignment benchmarks.
Our model utilizes a semiempirical method, GFN2-xTB, and is compatible
with a broad variety of organic systems, including those composed of
hundreds of atoms or elements ranging from H to Rn. We used this model to
resolve the controversial J/K ring junction problem of maitotoxin, which is the largest whole molecule assigned by NMR calculations
to date. This model has been developed into user-friendly software, providing a useful tool for routine rapid structure validation and
assignation as well as a new approach to elucidate the large structures that were previously unsuitable for NMR calculations.

natural organic compounds is crucial for further chemical
and biologic research. Density functional theory (DFT) NMR
chemical shift calculations, particularly for 13C NMR, play an
essential role in validating and elucidating structures, providing
high accuracy and reliability in numerous practices.1−6
However, these accurate and reliable DFT-based method-
ologies are often time-consuming and computationally
intensive, making it challenging to apply them to systems
with hundreds of heavy atoms or many low-energy conformers.
On the other hand, the fast empirical or data-driven
approaches, including empirical additivity rules, similarity-to-
database analysis, or machine learning models,7−20 have not
been widely proven for elucidating complex structures, such as
new natural products with multiple stereogenic centers.7,8,21−23
For instance, CASCADE,8 the recent state-of-the-art NMR
calculator with DFT level accuracy, still has weaknesses in
distinguishing stereoisomers.22 Additionally, because of the
limitation of methods (e.g., MMFF94) or algorithms used to
calculate molecular features, they cannot support or provide
correct results for some organic structures. For example,
CASCADE is not compatible with molecules consisting of
elements other than C, H, N, O, S, P, F, and Cl or having
formal/net charges (nitro compounds, amine oxides, anions,
cations, zwitterions, etc.), as well as the structures that cannot
be properly calculated by MMFF94.8
There are challenging tasks that remain unsolved due to the
limitations of DFT or data-driven approaches. An example is
the controversy over the relative configuration at the J/K ring
junction of the largest known nonpolymeric natural product,
maitotoxin (1, MTX) (Figure 1).24−27 Although the fragments
of the GHIJK (2a)26 and the GHIJKLMNO (3a)27 ring
systems have been successfully synthesized, supporting the
originally proposed structure (1a) based on comparison
between their 13C NMR chemical shift data, the possibility
of 1b cannot be fully excluded. Assigning relative configuration
to a single diastereoisomer with one set of experimental data is
supposed to be the exact application scenario of DFT NMR
chemical calculations, for which sophisticated statistical
approaches, including DP4, DP5, DP4+, and Prel, have been
designed.28−31 Due to the computational cost, however, DFT

It is well-known that feed-forward neural networks with
sufficient depth and width are universal function approxima-
tors,32 which has been widely verified in the numerous
practices of successful architectures, such as graph convolu-
tional networks (GCNs),33,34 recurrent neural networks,35

this understanding, it is plausible that a sophisticated neural
network model can accurately approximate the process of
converting the chemical environments of atoms into chemical
shifts.
In this paper, we introduce a very deep GCN model for 13C
NMR chemical shift calculations that combines the efficiency
of machine learning methods and the performance of DFT
level methods. We will elaborate on our work from five
aspects: data collection, feature engineering, details of model,
performance in benchmarks, and application in elucidating the
J/K ring junction of MTX.
■RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Data Collection. While DFT is based on fundamental
principles of physics, data-driven approaches rely highly on
large amounts of correct experimental data. However, flaws of
13C NMR chemical shift data, such as mislabeling and wrong
or ambiguous structures, are pervasive in the literature and
databases, making it challenging to collect sufficient correct
data.8 To address this issue, Paton’s group cleaned the
NMR8K data set to generate Exp5K by DFT
13C NMR
calculations, which they generously made available for open
access.8 These precious data have been included in our data
set. However, without further correction, their DFT 13C NMR
calculation method fails for a considerable number of chemical
environments (e.g., heavy atom bearing carbons, some sp2
hybrid carbons),1−6,31,38 potentially excluding some correctly
assigned data. Besides, some wrong configuration assignments,
which cause less significant error in DFT
13C NMR
calculations, may not be excluded. Moreover, this set lacks
some elements (such as Br and I) or functional groups (such as
ionized groups) that frequently occurred in organic com-
pounds, which will limit the application area of our model.
Therefore, we have collected and fully labeled another over
4000 compounds from the accessible literature or the AIST
database.39 These data from the literature were validated by
GIAO DFT calculations at the ωB97x-D4/TZVP//r2scan-3c
level of theory40 or manual checking of their 1D/2D NMR
data, and any suspicious structures or data were excluded.
Additionally, the energetically favorable structures of com-
pounds with tautomerism were also determined by DFT free
energy calculations at the B3LYP/TZVP level of theory. As a
result, we obtained over 9000 compounds with over 110,000
carbons labeled by experimental 13C NMR chemical shifts
(Exp9K). In addition, the elements of these compounds
included H, B, C, N, O, F, P, S, Cl, Br, Si, Ge, Se, Sn, and I
(Table S43), covering most types of organic compounds.
Moreover, the structure coverage can be further extended to
other elements in the range of H to Rn or other moieties
supported by GFN2-xTB,41 if there are experimental data
available for training.
For most compounds with multiple stereogenic centers,
however, only a few diastereomers have experimental data.
Because determining the relative configuration is a major
anticipated capacity of our model, the incompleteness of the
experimental data may hinder its ability to analyze the general

Feature Engineering. To address configurational prob-
lems, obtaining accurate 3D molecular geometry is critical,
particularly for some unique structures.8 To date, GFN2-xTB
is the most accurate semiempirical method with the highest
compatibility with most organic structures.41 Although slightly
more time-consuming than force field methods for small
organic molecules, GFN2-xTB provides robustness for as many
organic compounds as possible, making it the optimal choice
for 3D geometry optimization and molecular property
calculation.41 Herein, we used GFN2-xTB to optimize
geometries and calculate the coordination number (CN),
atomic partial charge (q), and Wiberg bond order (WBO).
For flexible compounds, however, the accuracy of semi-
empirical methods for conformational energy calculation is too
rough to correctly order the population of low-energy
conformers, unlike DFT-based methods.42,43 Such a problem
may lead to misguiding results in configuration determination,
because important conformers are missing or minor con-
formers are erroneously emphasized after Boltzmann averag-
ing.44 In a previous study, to deal with inappropriate
description of the conformational amplitudes, Zanardi et al.
explored an approach that completely neglected the relative
energies given by DFT calculations and alternatively created
and evaluated different ensembles with random conformational
amplitudes.44 As we are proposing a fast method, obtaining
more accurate conformational energy by more time-consuming
methods (e.g., DFT) is not a feasible option. Herein, to
balance between removing high-energy conformers and
reducing the influence of inaccurate conformational weights,
we equally weighted all the conformers within an energy
window of 3 kcal/mol, referring to the mean absolute deviation
range of GFN2-xTB for conformational energy.43

In the edges and edge features, a comprehensive integration
of molecular internal coordinates for atoms takes precedence.
It involves the incorporation of bond-length (r, in angströms),
bond-angle (α, within the interval [0, π]), and dihedral angle
(θ, within the interval [0, 2π]) measurements between atoms.
These coordinates holistically encode the intricate 3D
molecular characteristics, thereby bestowing our molecular
graph with both translational and rotational invariance (Figure
2).
For a pair of atoms separated by one, two, or three bonds
(WBO > 0.52), they are assigned with an undirected edge with
11 edge features. For atoms directly bonded, the first two and
the last two edge features are WBO, the reciprocal of r2, WBO
divided by r2, and squared WBO, respectively, and the other
features are set to zero (Figure 2). The last two edge features
also feature expansion terms. The third edge feature records
the bond angle of the atoms separated by two bonds. To
differentiate the zero value of angle from those edges without
angle information, the value of α is increased by one (Figure
2).
Edge features calculated from bond orders, bond lengths,
and bond angles do not vary significantly between conformers
of flexible compounds. Therefore, these edge features of
flexible compounds can be directly averaged from all low-
energy conformers. However, the dihedral angles involving
rotatable bonds are the major differences between the
conformers for the atoms separated by three bonds. If the
dihedral angles of conformers have a large deviation (e.g., 0 for
one conformer and π for another one), the averaged
intermediate value may not represent the dihedral angles of
any conformers. Thus, we divide the range of dihedral angle [0,
2π] into six intervals, corresponding to the staggered
conformation of ethane, namely, [0, π/6] ∪[11π/6, 2π],
(π/6, π/2], (π/2, 5π/6], (5π/6, 7π/6], (7π/6, 3π/2], and (3π/
2, 11π/6) (Figure 2). We use the fourth to ninth features to
record θ values, which correspond one-to-one to the six
intervals. If θ falls within one of the six intervals, the value of θn
+ 1 is recorded in the corresponding position (Figure 2). For
flexible compounds, nonzero values of these six features are
averaged, respectively. Therefore, the multiple patterns of
dihedral angles at flexible moieties are all recorded and passed
to our model.

molecules.33,34 However, stacking many GCN layers leads to a
vanishing gradient problem and hinders the ability of the
network to distinguish between different nodes because the
“oversmoothing” issue eliminates or minimizes their feature
differences.45−47 Consequently, most state-of-the-art GCNs are
shallow.33,34 The numbers of GCN layers in the recent NMR
predicting models developed by Paton’s group,8 Choi’s group,9
Scheidt’s group,10 and White’s group19 were three, five, three,
and four, respectively. The limitation of depth in those models
may cause a significant loss of their power.45−47
Herein, we have developed a GCN model named
GFN2NMR, which overcomes the training difficulty and
oversmoothing problem associated with much deeper GCN
models, using residual connections and a deep adaptive
strategy.45−47 Our optimized model comprises 54 GCN layers,
which are organized into 18 stacked residual blocks with
shortcut connections (Figure 3). Each residual block consists
of 3 stacked GCN units with a shortcut connection. The
message passing and propagation unit is a NNConv48 module
that uses an attention-augmented multilayer perceptron to map
the edge features. The node features output from each residual
block are concatenated, multiplied with two-head self-attention
weights, and then passed through a fully connected layer to
output final chemical shift values (Figure 3).
To ensure the model’s generalizability, we divided the
Exp9K data set into a training set (7455 molecules) and test
set (1792 molecules). We also anticipate a model that has high
performance on previously unseen structures, which frequently
occurs in natural product research. Therefore, we collected 92
molecules with previously undescribed ring systems and
complex stereogenic centers as an external validation set to
challenge the generalizing power of our model as well as other
data-driven methods (Set A, Figure S1, X-ray diffraction-
analyzed neutral natural products published in 2022 in the
journals Organic Chemistry Frontiers, Organic Letters, and
Chinese Chemical Letters).
First, the molecules in the test set and the training set were
selected randomly from Exp9K, respectively (Figure 4).
Besides, we also clustered all of the Murcko scaffolds of
molecules in Exp9K and randomly selected 1792 molecules
with unique scaffolds to compose another test set, and the rest
of the molecules were used as another training set (Figure 4).
In model training, the losses of training, test, and validation
sets tended to converge within 400 epochs (Figure 4). With a
randomly composed training set, the best root-mean-square
(RMS) value of the validation sets was less than 3.00 ppm.
When we selectively allocated a considerable number of
unique molecules to the test set, we observed only a marginal
increase of approximately 0.33 ppm in the best RMS value for
validation sets and a similarly modest increase of approximately
0.49 ppm for the test set. These results underscore the robust
generalizability of our model, even when confronted with
previously unseen scaffolds.
Moreover, we also analyzed the deep adaptive weights
associated with each carbon for the 92 molecules in the
validation set. Notably, our findings revealed a strong
correlation between the depth of graph node representations
and the specific chemical environments of these carbons
(Figure 5). Deeper graph node representations gather more
global information from larger receptive fields. The observed
hierarchy of depth across different carbon types emerged as
follows: H/C-connected sp3 carbons < heteroatom-connected
sp3 carbons < H/C-connected sp2 carbons < heteroatom-
connected sp2 carbons, which generally aligns with the

Comparison with State-of-the-Art Data-Driven Meth-
ods. To assess the performance of GFN2NMR, we selected
the recent state-of-the-art methods, namely, the online version
of CASCADE8 and Ensemble NMR Prediction49 consisting of
Mestrelab and Modgraph (Neural Network + HOSE), for
comparison. Our evaluation utilized compounds in Set A,
which were unlikely to have been part of the training sets or
databases employed by the two methods. We evaluated the
performance of each method using several metrics, namely,
coefficient of determination (R2), unscaled MAE, RMS, max
error, and kernel density estimation (KDE). The results
indicated that both GFN2NMR-Exp9K and GFN2NMR-full
outperformed the other two tools (Figure 6), which was likely
attributed to a synergistic interplay between the deeper model
architecture and an increased volume of training data.
Moreover, GFN2NMR-full exhibited a particularly superior
performance compared to GFN2NMR-Exp9K. This observa-
tion suggests that the incorporation of configurational effects
learned from the coverage of diastereomers substantially
contributed to enhancing the performance on those previously
unseen scaffolds.
Comparison with DFT-Based Methods. First, we tested
GFN2NMR on the compound set used to test the regular
GIAO DFT 13C NMR chemical shift calculation method with
global scaling correction (GSC) and the STS protocol.31 The
accuracy of GFN2NMR-full (R2 = 0.9975, unscaled MAE =
1.85 ppm, RMS = 2.56 ppm) fell between the regular GSC
method and the STS protocol (Table 1). CASCADE was not
tested for this set, because some molecules in the STS set are
in its training set or have formal charges.

DFT 13C NMR chemical shift calculations. Although Paton’s
group achieved some positive results with CASCADE8 in

Therefore, we can confidently assign the configuration of the
J/K ring in MTX to the originally proposed structure (1a).
Discussion. One of the key advantages of GFN2NMR is
the deep architecture that ensures a wide receptive field on the
molecular graph and the extraction of high-level features of
graph data. Notably, configurational information encoded in
edge features can be directly perceived by every GCN layer,
which probably reinforces the learning of configurational
effects. Furthermore, GFN2-xTB, a semiempirical method
utilized in this model, provides high-quality geometric data and
introduces nonempirical features that may also contribute to its
enhanced performance.
Like all other data-driven approaches, however, GFN2NMR
is still limited by the data used for training. Although we made
efforts to collect the data of chemically diverse compounds,
and GFN2-xTB supports a broad range of elements, the results
for some unique chemical systems still need further
verification. In addition, GFN2NMR cannot deal with the
conformational energy accurately. For these cases sensitive to
conformational population (e.g., nivariol54), the users should
always be cautious and combine other methods including NOE
or J-based analysis to draw correct conclusions.
■CONCLUSION
In summary, we present a very deep GCN model
(GFN2NMR) for 13C NMR chemical shift calculations that
unprecedently offers both low time-cost and DFT level
performance in structure assignment. Based on GFN2-xTB,
this model is compatible with a broad variety of organic
structures. Therefore, it can be an ideal tool for assigning large
or flexible organic molecules and rapidly validating proposed
structures. We demonstrated the utility of our model by
resolving the J−K ring junction of MTX and its synthetic
moieties. Our results illustrated that the power of NMR
chemical shift calculations can extend to much larger systems
than previously feasible using this model. Furthermore, we
believe that GFN2NMR has the potential to be applied to the
structure determination of biomacromolecules, which would
open a fascinating avenue for future research.
