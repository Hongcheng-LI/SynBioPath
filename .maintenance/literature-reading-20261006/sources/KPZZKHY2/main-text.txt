Unexpected Noremestrin with a Sulfur-Bearing 15-Membered
Macrocyclic Lactone from Emericella sp. 1454

ABSTRACT: Two previous unreported epipolythiodioxopipera-
zines of the emestrin family, namely, noremestrin A (1) and
secoemestrin E (2), were successfully isolated from the fungal
source Emericella sp. 1454. Employing comprehensive spectro-
scopic techniques, such as high-resolution electrospray ionization
mass spectrometry, infrared, and nuclear magnetic resonance
(NMR), along with NMR and electronic circular dichroism
calculations, the chemical structures of compounds 1 and 2 were
elucidated. Particularly noteworthy is the distinctive nature of noremestrin A, representing the inaugural instance of a noremestrin
variant incorporating a sulfur-bearing 15-membered macrocyclic lactone moiety. Compounds 1 and 2 exhibited weak cytotoxic
activities against the human chronic myelocytic leukemia cell lines MEG-01 and K562.
M
icrobial secondary metabolites constitute a significant
component of natural compounds, serving as prominent
reservoirs for potential candidates or established pharmaceut-
icals.1−4 Following the discovery of penicillin, the exploration
of fungal secondary metabolites have attracted the attention of
the scientific community as a result of their novel structural
frameworks and diverse bioactivities.5−9 Epipolythiodioxopi-
perazines (ETPs), characterized by a sulfur-bridged 2,5-
diketopiperazine ring, represent an intriguing class of bioactive
natural products.10−15 Emestrins, a distinctive subset of ETPs,
showcase a seven-membered dihydrooxepine ring along with a
15-membered macrocyclic lactone, characterized by an ester
linkage between C-7 and C-1′ and an ether linkage between C-
3′ and C-3″ (Figure 1).12,16−18 Emestrin (Figure 1), the
pioneering compound of the emestrin type, was first isolated
from the fungus Emericella striata in 1986.17 Subsequent to this
discovery, numerous emestrin derivatives, numbering around
30, have been characterized from several filamentous
fungi.16,19−24 The emestrin compounds exhibited diverse
biological activities, encompassing antibacterial,25−27 antifun-
gal,27 antitumor cell proliferation,26,28,29 and other miscella-
neous activities.30
In our previous study,19,31 two unusual emestrins with a
thioethanothio bridge and secoemestrin C have been isolated
from the extract of the fungus Emericella sp. 1454. In our
ongoing research of the new metabolites derived from the
Emericella sp. 1454, two novel emestrin-type epipolythiodiox-
opiperazines, namely, noremestrin A (1) and secoemestrin E
(2) (Figure 1), were isolated from the EtOH extract by
ultraviolet (UV)-guided analysis. Notably, compound 1 is the
first example of a noremestrin with a sulfur-containing 15-
membered macrocyclic lactone. Our investigation also revealed
that both compounds 1 and 2 showed weak cytotoxic activities
against human chronic myelocytic leukemia cell lines MEG-01
and K562. This report thus encompasses the isolation process,
identification, and subsequent biological evaluation of
noremestrin A and secoemestrin E from Emericella sp. 1454.

presence of a coupling system among the hydrogen atoms: H-
6/H-7/H-8/H-9. HMBCs from H-6 to C-11 and C-12 and

The relative configuration of compound 1 was ascertained
through analysis of the coupling constant and rotating-frame
nuclear Overhauser effect correlation spectroscopy (ROESY)
data (Figure 2). The prominent coupling constant of 8.4 Hz
observed between H-6 and H-7 provided evidence for their
anti relationship, which mirrored the configuration seen in
other emestrins. The ROE correlations of S-methyl protons
with H-7 and H-13 indicated that S-methyl, H-13, and C-7
occupied the same face of the pyrrolidine ring. Likewise, the
correlation of S-methyl protons with H-2″ implied that the S-
methyl and 3″-oxygen-4′′-hydroxybenzenethio units were
positioned on the same side of the dioxopiperazine ring.
This orientation was supported by the 13C NMR calculation of
compound 1.33 Two possible models, (3S*,6S*,7S*,13R*,-
14R*)-1 (1a) and (3R*,6S*,7S*,13R*,14R*)-1 (1b), under-
went computational analysis (Figure S1 of the Supporting
Information). The calculated 13C NMR data of structure 1b
aligned more closely with the experimental data, showcasing a
higher correlation coefficient (R2 for structure 1a, 0.9917; R2
for structure 1b, 0.9945; Figures S2 and S3 of the Supporting
Information). In addition, the DP4+ analysis34 revealed a
definitive 100% probability (Table S20 of the Supporting
Information) for structure 1b based on the 13C NMR data,
indicating 3R*,6S*,7S*,13R*,14R* configuration for com-
pound 1. To firmly establish the absolute configuration of
compound 1, we performed the electronic circular dichroism
(ECD) calculation using the time-dependent density func-
tional theory method.35,36 The calculated ECD curve for
(3R,6S,7S,13R,14R)-1 demonstrated a strong alignment with
the experimental CD spectrum of compound 1 (Figure 3).
Thus, the absolute configuration of compound 1 was
ascertained to be 3R,6S,7S,13R,14R, leading to its designation
as noremestrin A.
The molecular composition of compound 2 was established
as C28H24N2O11S on the basis of HR-ESI-MS analysis,
corresponding to a total of 18 degrees of molecular
unsaturation. Comparative analysis of NMR data between
compound 2 (Table 1) and a secoemestrin analogue,
MPC1001F,22 revealed substantial similarities. Notably, a
contrastive assessment of the 1H and 13C NMR data of both
compounds suggested the substitution of methoxy at position
C-4″ in MPC1001F with hydroxy, which was validated by
observing correlations from OH-4′′ to C-3′′, C-4′′, and C-5′′
(Figure 2). The relative configuration was determined by
employing ROESY correlations (Figure 2). Specifically, the
correlation of H-7 with H-13 and S-methyl protons provided
evidence for the co-localization of H-13, C-7, and S-methyl on
the face of the pyrrolidine ring. Conversely, the correlation of
H-6 with OH-13 implied placement on the opposing face of
the pyrrolidine ring. Finally, using the calculated and
experimental ECD data, the (6S,7S,13R,14R) configuration
was established for compound 2 (Figure S6 of the Supporting
Information).
In our previous research, emestrin and secoemestrin C
exhibited significant cytotoxicities against human chronic
myelocytic leukemia cell lines MEG-01 and K562, manifesting
IC50 values ranging from 0.2 to 1.3 μM.19 In the preliminary
bioactivity test, compounds 1 and 2 displayed weak cytotoxic
activities against the cell line K562, with IC50 values at 63.6 and
15.3 μM, respectively. Similarly, in relation to the cell line
MEG-01, their IC50 values were determined as 71.1 and 23.0
μM, respectively (Table S25 of the Supporting Information).
The results further corroborated that the polysulfide bridge is a
vital pharmacophore for cytotoxic activities.
