ABSTRACT: Isoprenoids are essential metabolites whose biosyn-
thesis originates from two five-carbon (C5) isomers, isopentenyl
pyrophosphate (IPP) and dimethylallyl pyrophosphate (DMAPP).
Although these isomers serve as the core substrates for isoprenoid
biosynthesis, tracking their independent incorporation into down-
stream metabolites is difficult due to the lack of available chemical
tools. To address this issue, we have developed a cell-permeant,
stable isotope-labeled analog of DMAPP that employs self-
immolating esters to mask the β-phosphate, allowing for efficient
cellular uptake and direct forward isotopic labeling of downstream
products of DMAPP in Bacillus subtilis. We demonstrate that 13C-
labeled, ester-protected DMAPP (13C3 SIE-DMAPP) achieves
significant incorporation into menaquinone-7 (MK-7), regardless
of inhibition of endogenous production of IPP and DMAPP. By knocking out isopentenyl pyrophosphate isomerase (IPPI)
expression, we achieve specific 13C isotopic labeling exclusively at DMAPP-derived positions of MK-7 and demonstrate that IPPI
overexpression enables B. subtilis to utilize DMAPP as its sole isoprenoid source. Finally, we tracked DMAPP incorporation during
the transition from vegetative growth to sporulation, revealing compartment-specific isotope labeling patterns that underscore the
metabolic independence of isoprenoid metabolism in the mother cell and endospore during sporulation in B. subtilis. The
introduction of this stable isotope-labeled DMAPP facilitates the tracking of DMAPP-derived metabolites in native biological
contexts and opens new avenues for studying prenyl metabolism, terpene biosynthetic pathways, and the regulatory mechanisms
governing cellular isoprenoid pools.
■INTRODUCTION
The biosynthesis of all isoprenoids, an essential class of lipids
across all organisms, is derived from the five-carbon (C5)
isomers isopentenyl pyrophosphate (IPP) and dimethylallyl
pyrophosphate (DMAPP).1,2 IPP and DMAPP are the
precursors for longer-chain isoprenoids, such as geranyl-
(C10), farnesyl- (C15), and geranylgeranyl- (C20) pyrophos-
phate, which are intermediates for biologically important and
complex molecules involved in protein and small molecule
prenylation, cell to cell signaling, and small molecule
defense.3,4 In bacteria, IPP and DMAPP are primarily
biosynthesized through the methylerythritol phosphate
(MEP) pathway, wherein pyruvate and glyceraldehyde-3-
phosphate are converted to IPP and DMAPP (Figure 1A).2,5
To maintain equilibrium between IPP and DMAPP in
organisms relying on the MEP pathway, two enzymes are
involved: 4-hydroxy-3-methylbut-2-enyl diphosphate reductase
(HDR) and isopentenyl pyrophosphate isomerase (IPPI).6
HDR produces IPP and DMAPP, while IPPI interconverts the
C5 substrate pool.7,8 Maintaining the correct balance of IPP
and DMAPP is crucial for cellular physiology, ensuring
sufficient availability for chain elongation and the production
of isomer-specific metabolites.9
In bacteria, IPP and DMAPP can then be used to
biosynthesize several essential and distinctive molecules.10
Notable examples include N6-tuberculosinyladenosine, unique
to the pathogenic Mycobacterium tuberculosis, and lipid II, a key
component of the bacterial cell wall that is a frequent target for
antibiotics (Figure 1B).11−13 Beyond acting as precursors for
longer-chain isoprenoids, IPP and DMAPP also perform
distinct, isomer-specific roles in various metabolic pathways.
For example, the bacterium Bacillus subtilis uses an unidentified
isoprene synthase to produce isoprene exclusively from
DMAPP.14,15 Additionally, in actinomycete Lentzea spp.,
DMAPP is required for the biosynthesis of lentztrehaloses,

To investigate the distinct metabolic roles of IPP and
DMAPP, we developed cell-permeant analogs that allow for
the precise exogenous delivery of IPP or DMAPP.19,20 These
analogs mask the β-phosphate of IPP or DMAPP with self-
immolating esters (SIE-IPP or SIE-DMAPP, respectively),
which enable diffusion across the cellular membrane and
subsequent release of active IPP or DMAPP by native esterase
activity (Figure 1C). This overcomes the inability of the
negatively charged IPP or DMAPP to cross the cellular
membrane. Furthermore, it offers an alternative to the need for
genetic engineering of non-native kinases to convert
exogenously delivered isoprenol into IPP or prenol into
DMAPP.21−23
While we can deliver each isomer specifically, tracking their
downstream metabolic incorporation remains challenging due
to the lack of chemically distinct probes.24 Furthermore, the
interconversion of IPP to DMAPP and vice versa by IPPI
complicates efforts to distinguish the metabolic fates of these
two isomers. Independently tracking IPP and DMAPP would
provide direct evidence of their incorporation into downstream
metabolic pathways and a clearer understanding of their
distinct biological roles. Beyond tracking IPP or DMAPP-
derived metabolites, the ability to label only the DMAPP-
derived position of isoprenoids allows straightforward tracking
of known and unknown isoprenoids (Figure 1D).25 Addition-
ally, isomer-specific labeling could enable more facile
elucidation of the function of prenyltransferases involved in
forming prenylated amino acids, saccharides, and other unique
natural products.26−28
In response to these challenges, we have synthesized carbon
stable isotope labeled [3,4,5-13C3] self-immolating ester-
protected DMAPP (13C3 SIE-DMAPP) to track DMAPP
incorporation in B. subtilis (Figure 1E). First, we demonstrate
that irrespective of pathway inhibition, our approach enables
robust isoprenoid labeling. Next, we achieve selective and
uniform 1-13C3 stable isotope labeling in menaquinone-7 (MK-
7) in B. subtilis by knocking out the fni gene, which encodes
IPPI. Then, we created an IPPI-overexpression strain of B.
subtilis and, by feeding 13C3 SIE-DMAPP only, demonstrated
complete isoprenoid labeling regardless of the IPP- or
DMAPP-derived C5 unit (Figure 1F,G). Finally, we leveraged
the temporal incorporation of stable isotope-labeled DMAPP
to elucidate the metabolic independence of the mother cell and
endospore within the isoprenoid pathway during sporulation in
B. subtilis. Overall, we show that our stable isotope-labeled
DMAPP analog is a powerful tool for tracking DMAPP-derived
metabolites in native biological contexts, opening new avenues
for investigating prenyl metabolism, biosynthesis, and the
regulatory role of IPPI in cellular metabolism.
■EXPERIMENTAL SECTION
Supplies and Reagents. All chemicals were purchased from
Fisher Scientific (Waltham, MA) or Millipore Sigma (Burlington,
MA) and used without purification. Dry solvents were prepared by
distillation and storage over 3 Å molecular sieves. All reactions, unless
indicated, were carried out in flame-dried glassware under argon.
Argon was dried by passing it through calcium sulfate. Thin-layer
chromatography (TLC) was performed using either Baker-flex
disposable TLC plates (J.T. Baker, Phillipsburg, NJ) or TLC Silica
gel 60 F245 glass-backed TLC plates (Millipore Sigma). Visualization
was achieved using UV light (254 nm) or staining in KMnO4 (1 g
KMnO4, 6.67 g K2CO3, 1.67 mL 5% NaOH in 100 mL of H2O).
Flash chromatography of crude products was performed using a Buchi
Pure C-850 FlashPrep chromatography system with prepacked silica
or C18 cartridges where indicated (Buchi - Flawil, Switzerland).
General Culturing Conditions. B. subtilis 168 and B. subtilis
trpC2 Δfni::erm were obtained from the Bacillus Genetic Stock
Center (Columbus, OH). Cells were isolated from single colonies
grown on Luria−Bertani (LB) agar plates and incubated at 37 °C for
all experiments. Liquid cultures were maintained in LB broth and
incubated in a shaking incubator at 250 rpm and 37 °C. The
Δfni::erm strain was maintained with erythromycin at 10 μg/mL,
while the fni overexpressing strain was maintained with chloramphe-
nicol at 5 μg/mL.
Growth Curve Analyses with B. subtilis. To measure the
growth of B. subtilis across a range of concentrations of SIE-IPP and
SIE-DMAPP, we monitored growth curves in a 96-well format, which
was prepared as follows: In a transparent 96-well round-bottom plate
(ThermoFisher, Waltham, MA), 93 μL of LB media was added,
followed by 1 μL of SIE-IPP or SIE-DMAPP from a 100× stock (1, 5,
10, 25, 50, 75, 100, 125 mM) of either SIE-IPP or SIE-DMAPP
prepared in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO). To ensure that the 1−2 μL
of DMSO from the SIE-IPP or SIE-DMAPP solutions did not
interfere with the assay, separate wells containing the DMSO vehicle
alone were used as controls. After all compounds were added, an
exponentially growing culture of B. subtilis at an OD600 of 1 was added
to each well at a 20× dilution.
Following the addition of B. subtilis, the 96-well plate was placed in
a Cytation 1 cell imaging multimode microplate reader (Agilent, Santa
Clara, CA), and OD600 measurements were taken every 10 min for 10
h at 37 °C with orbital shaking at 528 cycles per minute (cpm). After
10 h of incubation, the growth curves were baseline-corrected relative
to the average value of the OD600 at 0, 10, and 20 min for each growth
curve. The growth curves for each triplicate were combined, and the
area under the growth curve was determined using a custom R script,
which can be found at the following DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.14889738.
Stable Isotope Labeling of MK-7 in B. subtilis. Into a 17 mm ×
100 mm culture tube (VWR, Radnor, PA) containing 5 mL of LB
broth, SIE-IPP, and 13C3 SIE-DMAPP were added at their specified
concentrations from a 100 mM DMSO stock solution. If
fosmidomycin was required, it was added simultaneously from a
100 mM stock in water. After adding all compounds, a B. subtilis
culture at an OD600 of 1 was added to each 5 mL culture at a 20×
dilution. Cultures were incubated in a shaking incubator (New
Brunswick Scientific, Edison, NJ) at 37 °C, 250 rpm, for 8 h.
Following incubation, cultures were transferred to 15 mL conical
tubes (VWR, Radnor, PA) and centrifuged at 4000g for 6 min. The
supernatant was discarded, and the cell pellets were placed on ice.
Each pellet was resuspended in 1 mL of 50 mM Tris buffer (pH 7.2).
Subsequently, 5 μL of T4 lysozyme (New England Biolabs, Ipswich,
MA) was added, and the samples were incubated on ice for 30 min.
Cell suspensions were then flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen and
lyophilized overnight using a benchtop freeze-dryer (Labconco,
Kansas City, MO). The next day, 10 mL of a 7:2 acetone: methanol
mixture was added to each lyophilized sample. Samples were bath-
sonicated (Branson Ultrasonics Corporation, Danbury, CT) at room
temperature for 30 min and placed on a rotator (Fisher Scientific,
Waltham, MA) at 25 rpm for 2 h. After rotation, samples were
centrifuged at 4000g for 6 min, and the supernatants were transferred
to new 15 mL conical tubes. The extracts were dried using a Reacti-
Vap (Thermofisher, Waltham, MA) nitrogen evaporator. Dried
extracts were reconstituted in 100 μL of isopropanol and transferred
to 1.5 mL microcentrifuge tubes. Clarification was performed by
centrifugation at 10,000g for 5 min, and the resulting supernatants
were transferred to LC/MS vials (Thermo Scientific, Waltham, MA).
Additional Methods. Detailed methods for the creation of the B.
subtilis OE::fni, endospore staining, LC-HRMS conditions, and media
formulations are provided in the Supporting Information.

of IPP or DMAPP incorporation into downstream metabolites
with single-isomer resolution. We sought to overcome this by
synthesizing stable isotope-labeled analogs of our SIE-
protected DMAPP. This would not only facilitate the
identification of natural product biosynthetic pathways linked
to their specific prenyl isomers but also allow isotopically
labeled time-resolved experiments to track IPP, DMAPP, or
defined mixtures of both isomers independently.
We leveraged our established SIE delivery system for
DMAPP to address this limitation and synthesized SIE-
protected DMAPP with three 13C labels. The synthesis of the
isotopolog began with the conversion of acetone-13C3 into
[3,4,5-13C3] prenol through a three-step synthesis based on
methodology from the Poulter lab (SI Appendix, Scheme
S1).29 Following the synthesis of [3,4,5-13C3] prenol, the
alcohol was converted to the monophosphate, followed by the
coupling of bis(4-hexanoyloxybenzyl)-N,N-diisopropylphos-
phoramidite and subsequent oxidation to yield the SIE
protected [3,4,5-13C3] DMAPP isotopolog (13C3 SIE-
DMAPP) (Figure 2A, and SI Scheme S2).20 Following the
synthesis of
13C3 SIE-DMAPP, we aimed to track its
incorporation into the isoprenoids produced by B. subtilis
and demonstrate DMAPP-specific isotopic labeling.
To achieve robust isotopic labeling, we administered 25 μM
13C3 SIE-DMAPP alongside 50 μM natural carbon abundance
SIE-IPP. These concentrations were based on our previous
work, where we found that B. subtilis cannot grow on DMAPP
alone.19 To feed the labeled cultures, B. subtilis was grown for 8
h, a period chosen to ensure adequate incorporation of 13C3
SIE-DMAPP and to produce sufficient material for the reliable
detection of downstream metabolites. To monitor
13C3
DMAPP incorporation, we focused on its integration into
MK-7, an essential isoprenoid produced by B. subtilis (Figure
2B). We utilized liquid chromatography high-resolution mass
spectrometry (LC-HRMS) to measure the incorporation of
13C3 SIE-DMAPP into MK-7, which allowed us to precisely
track the number of 13C3 prenyl units incorporated into the
seven-prenyl unit side chain of MK-7 by the mass to charge
(m/z) ratio. To facilitate a straightforward comparison
between different 13C3 labeling patterns, we compared the
area of the extracted-ion-chromatogram (EICs) of the MK-7
parent mass [M + H]+ and the parent masses corresponding to
each 13C3 label. We then summed the total areas of each EIC
and compared the area of each labeled parent mass to the total
area of all MK-7 parent masses, allowing us to quantify the
proportion of MK-7 molecules containing each specific 13C3
label (Figure 3A).
We began by administering 13C3 SIE-DMAPP to B. subtilis
168 (B. subtilis WT) and observed a 35% incorporation rate of
13C3 DMAPP into MK-7 biosynthesis without MEP pathway
inhibition (Figure 3A−C). Even without inhibiting the MEP
pathway, this demonstrates that enough
13C3 DMAPP is
incorporated into downstream isoprenoids for reliable
detection by LC-HRMS. Furthermore, we observed that only
2% of MK-7 was 2-13C3 labeled and only 1% was 3-13C3
labeled, demonstrating that, in the background of native IPPI
expression, B. subtilis WT does not significantly contribute to
label interconversion between DMAPP and IPP when the
MEP pathway remains active.
To increase labeling efficiency, we inhibited the native
production of IPP and DMAPP using 100 μM fosmidomycin,
which we demonstrated can inhibit the growth of B. subtilis
WT in a liquid medium (SI Figure S1). We observed a
substantial increase in total 13C3 labeling, with 76% of all MK-7
containing a single 13C3 label, 14% remaining unlabeled, and
10% containing two 13C3 labels (Figure 3A,D). While this
result showcased the potential of our SIE-DMAPP labeling
approach, with three-quarters of all MK-7 labeled with a single
13C3 unit, it did not result in a homogeneous single 13C3 MK-7
labeling due to the activity of IPPI. To address the challenge of
specific DMAPP-derived labeling, we wanted to repress the
expression of IPPI, thereby allowing DMAPP-specific labeling,
even with the addition of SIE-IPP.

Growth with Either IPP or DMAPP Is Dependent on
IPPI Activity in B. subtilis. While we successfully
demonstrated 13C3 stable isotope labeling at DMAPP-derived
positions in MK-7 using the IPPI deletion strain, our goal was
to extend beyond positional labeling to achieve uniform
labeling of the entire isoprenoid tail. To accomplish this, we
needed to eliminate exogenous IPP supplementation during
isoprenoid inhibition. However, before doing so, we first
needed to understand why B. subtilis fails to grow when
supplied with DMAPP alone, a limitation we hypothesized was
due to insufficient IPPI activity.19 We first sought to confirm
that MEP pathway inhibition with fosmidomycin, which may
inhibit growth beyond its effect on DXR, did not interfere with
the potential for DMAPP alone to rescue growth.
To decouple inhibition from IPP- and DMAPP-specific
rescue, we bypassed chemical inhibition by employing a
CRISPRi-based knockdown of HDR (B. subtilis dCas9-ispH),
the final enzyme of the MEP pathway.31 In this strain, dCas9 is
expressed under the Pxyl promoter and binds to the locus,
repressing HDR expression. Without xylose, low basal
expression of dCas9 partially inhibits HDR, leading to reduced
isoprenoid biosynthesis. Upon xylose induction, dCas9
expression is significantly enhanced, resulting in stronger
repression of ispH and further inhibition of isoprenoid
biosynthesis, thereby inhibiting growth. To assess the growth
of B. subtilis dCas9-ispH, we measured OD600 over 10 h and
calculated the area under the growth curve (AUGC) to
compare growth across conditions (Figure 5). When B. subtilis
dCas9-ispH was grown without expression of dCas9 but with
supplementation of SIE-IPP at concentrations below 100 μM,
growth was demonstrated to be 50% above that of the B.
subtilis dCas9-ispH strain alone, suggesting that exogenous IPP
can overcome background repression of ispH (Figure 6A,B).
Upon induction, the B. subtilis dCas9-ispH achieved 83% of
uninduced B. subtilis dCas9-ispH growth. In contrast, SIE-
DMAPP failed to rescue growth in B. subtilis dCas9-ispH
(Figure 6A−D, and SI Figure S3). Given that SIE-DMAPP
failed to rescue growth in the HDR knockdown strain, we next
sought to determine whether increasing IPPI expression could
overcome this limitation and enable growth on DMAPP alone.
Specifically, we investigated whether elevated IPPI levels
could enhance DMAPP to IPP conversion, enabling growth in
conditions where DMAPP alone was previously ineffective. To
test this, we first confirmed that IPPI activity is required for

grow on IPP alone depended on IPPI activity. We subjected B.
subtilis Δfni to the same growth rescue assay conditions as the
WT strain and, as anticipated, no growth in wells containing
only SIE-IPP or SIE-DMAPP was observed (Figure 7B and SI

growth in the Δfni strain. Similar to the WT strain, no growth
was observed when the ratio of DMAPP to IPP exceeded a
ratio of 1:1 over the 10 h of incubation.
After establishing the optimal ratios of SIE-IPP and SIE-
DMAPP that demonstrate rescue in both B. subtilis WT and
Δfni strains, we performed the matrix-based growth rescue
assay on the IPPI-overexpressing strain, B. subtilis OE::fni
(Figure 7C and SI Figure S7). In contrast to the WT and Δfni
strains, which required both isomers for growth, we observed a
rescue of growth with SIE-DMAPP alone in the OE::fni strain
(Figure 7A−C). Additionally, fni overexpression eliminated
the previously observed growth limitation of a DMAPP to IPP
ratio greater than 1:1. This demonstrated that overexpressing
IPPI enhances the cellular rate of DMAPP to IPP isomer-
ization, ensuring sufficient IPP availability to produce essential
isoprenoids, such as lipid II and MK-7. As a result, B. subtilis
OE::fni no longer depended on externally supplied IPP,
enabling growth with DMAPP alone.
Effects of IPPI Overexpression on 13C3 SIE-DMAPP
Isoprenoid Labeling Patterns. While labeling with either
IPP or DMAPP allows for tracking isomer-specific metabolites,
uniformly labeling all carbon atoms using a single isomer
provides a more straightforward method for determining the
number of prenyl units in a natural product. This approach
enables the efficient discovery of isoprenoids, particularly in
metabolites containing multiple nonprenyl-derived carbon
atoms. Before proceeding with stable isotope labeling using
13C3 SIE-DMAPP without the addition of SIE-IPP, we
repeated the stable isotope DMAPP feeding in B. subtilis
OE::fni, as previously conducted in the WT and Δfni strains
(Figures 3 and 4). Repeating these experiments allowed us to
compare 13C3 SIE-DMAPP label scrambling under identical
conditions across the WT, Δfni, and OE::fni strains, ensuring
consistency in labeling patterns before advancing to metabolic
tracking with 13C3 SIE-DMAPP only. When the OE::fni strain

Next, we repeated the feeding experiment with coadminis-

strains with elevated isomerization activity (Figure 8B−D).

concentration of 13C3 SIE-DMAPP to 50 μM, sufficient to

culture (Figure 8E,F). This demonstrated that isoprenoid
labeling of IPP and DMAPP-derived positions is achievable
using 13C3 SIE-DMAPP alone.

■DISCUSSION
Although IPP and DMAPP are typically studied for their role
in the biosynthesis of longer-chain isoprenoids, each isomer
has distinct and independent metabolic functions crucial for
cellular physiology. To decouple the independent metabolism
of IPP and DMAPP, we previously developed cell-permeant
analogs by masking the β-phosphate of IPP and DMAPP with
ester-protected self-immolating groups, enabling exogenous
delivery of these cell-impermeant metabolites into cells. To
track the specific incorporation of DMAPP, here we created
the first stable isotope labeled DMAPP cell-permeant analog,
13C3 SIE-DMAPP, thereby providing cellular delivery of 13C3-
DMAPP without the need for genetic engineering of kinases.
With this chemical tool in hand, we set out to validate 13C3
SIE-DMAPP’s ability to isotopically label DMAPP-specific
positions in isoprenoids within a living bacterial cell.
We delivered
13C3 SIE-DMAPP along with natural
abundance SIE-IPP to B. subtilis WT, and observed by LC-
HRMS that over one-third of the produced MK-7 had
incorporated 1-13C3 label. This marks the first documented
instance of DMAPP-derived labeling in live cells without
requiring heterologous prenol kinase expression. Moreover,
this demonstrates that when native isoprenoid biosynthesis
remains unimpeded, nearly one-third of the downstream
isoprenoids incorporate DMAPP obtained from SIE-DMAPP,
indicating that inhibition of native isoprenoid pathways is
unnecessary for achieving substantial isoprenoid labeling in B.
subtilis with these analogs. To increase the labeling efficiency of
13C3 SIE-DMAPP in B. subtilis isoprenoid biosynthesis, we
inhibited the MEP pathway with a lethal dose of
fosmidomycin. We observed an increase in
13C3 labeling,
albeit with a scrambling of the number of 13C3 labels detected
in MK-7.
The incorporation of 13C3, both with and without MEP
pathway inhibition by fosmidomycin, confirmed that the
growth restoration seen with SIE-DMAPP was due to its
incorporation into native isoprenoid biosynthesis rather than
potential off-target effects attributed to fosmidomycin. Further
supporting the specific isoprenoid-related rescue, the resur-
gence of normal growth in the ispH knockdown strain by both
SIE-IPP and SIE-DMAPP demonstrated that the effect was
specifically due to isoprenoid replenishment. Furthermore,
these experiments demonstrate that SIE-DMAPP is depro-
tected to DMAPP by native esterases at a biorelevant rate in B.
subtilis. While esterase activity and isoprenoid pathway flux
may not be directly coupled in B. subtilis, native esterases
release DMAPP at a biologically relevant rate, slow enough to
prevent rapid depletion yet sustained enough to support
continuous incorporation.
To eliminate
13C3 DMAPP isomerization and achieve
uniform DMAPP-specific labeling, we utilized an IPPI
knockout strain (B. subtilis Δfni). When supplied with both
SIE-IPP and 13C3 SIE-DMAPP, labeling was highly specific,
resulting exclusively in the incorporation of a single 13C3 unit
into the MK-7 tail. Comparing 13C3 label scrambling between
the WT and Δfni strains, we observed low, but detectable
isomerization of DMAPP to IPP. This finding aligns with
previous kinetic studies, which reported that the Km for
DMAPP to IPP conversion is 2.7 times higher than that for
IPP to DMAPP conversion.37,38 Although IPPI naturally favors
IPP to DMAPP conversion, we hypothesized that over-
expressing IPPI allows sufficient DMAPP to IPP conversion
to support growth on DMAPP alone. If successful, this would
enable full prenyl chain labeling using only 13C3 SIE-DMAPP.
To determine whether IPPI overexpression is sufficient to
support growth on DMAPP alone, we examined how IPPI
expression levels influence growth when either IPP or DMAPP
was the sole isoprenoid precursor and tested this across the
WT, Δfni, and OE::fni strains. To assess the effect of IPPI
activity on DMAPP utilization, we systematically varied the
DMAPP to IPP ratio, making this the first study to administer
controlled DMAPP to IPP ratios to live cells. Growth was only
observed in WT and Δfni when the DMAPP to IPP ratio
remained below 1:1, suggesting that excess DMAPP disrupts
some aspect of isoprenoid biosynthesis, or has other toxic
effects, ultimately limiting viability. One possible explanation is
that DMAPP may compete with IPP for FPPS binding, thereby
impairing the synthesis of longer-chain isoprenoids.39 Alter-
natively, excess DMAPP may act as an inhibitor of key MEP
pathway enzymes, such as an allosteric inhibitor of 1-deoxy-D-
xylulose-5-phosphate synthase (DXS) or an uncompetitive
inhibitor, preventing dimerization of 2-C-methyl-D-erythritol 4-
phosphate cytidylyltransferase (CMS).40,41 Since these en-
zymes are essential to the MEP pathway, their inhibition would
reduce IPP synthesis, thereby restricting the supply of prenyl
precursors needed for longer-chain isoprenoid biosynthesis.
While our previous studies demonstrated that high concen-
trations of SIE-IPP or SIE-DMAPP led to similar toxicity,
those experiments were not conducted under MEP pathway
inhibition.19 This distinction is crucial, as our findings indicate
that native IPP production via the MEP pathway is sufficient to
maintain a DMAPP to IPP ratio below 1:1 to support B. subtilis
growth.
When we repeated the matrix feeding assay in the OE::fni
strain, we found that overexpressing IPPI eliminated the 1:1
DMAPP to IPP ratio limitation on growth. This finding is
significant as it demonstrates that B. subtilis can grow
exclusively on DMAPP. This represents the first documented
case of B. subtilis growing with DMAPP without endogenously
produced IPP. To further confirm that OE::fni-derived IPP
supported growth, we fed
13C3 SIE-DMAPP to B. subtilis
OE::fni and observed 13C3 labeling at all IPP- or DMAPP-
derived positions in MK-7. However, the majority of MK-7
remained unlabeled, suggesting that much of it originated from
the starter culture. This indicates that, while OE::fni can
generate sufficient IPP from DMAPP to support growth,
overall isoprenoid biosynthesis remains reduced, likely due to a
limited IPP supply, even with increased IPPI expression.
After confirming the efficacy of
13C3 SIE-DMAPP for
labeling isoprenoids in B. subtilis during vegetative growth, we
next investigated isoprenoid metabolism during sporulation.
Given that the mother cell and developing endospore undergo
distinct genetic regulation, we sought to determine whether
they also exhibit independent isoprenoid biosynthesis.42,43 To
test this, we delivered
13C3 SIE-DMAPP at the start of
sporulation in B. subtilis to track isoprenoid incorporation into
both the endospore and mother cell. Our analysis revealed a
striking difference in labeling between the two fractions where
65% of MK-7 in the mother cell contained a 13C label, while
only 21% of MK-7 in the endospore was labeled. This
differential labeling suggests that the endospore’s isoprenoid
pool may originate primarily from precursors shared early in
sporulation or from limited biosynthesis within the forespore.
Meanwhile, the mother cell continues to synthesize its own
distinct isoprenoid pool even after sporulation has been

■CONCLUSIONS
Here, we introduce 13C3 SIE-DMAPP as a powerful chemical
tool for tracking both DMAPP-specific and general isoprenoid
biosynthesis in live cells without requiring kinase engineering.
Our findings demonstrate the role of controlled IPPI
expression in regulating isoprenoid metabolism, revealing
how DMAPP to IPP balance dictates both growth and labeling
efficiency. By enabling precise, high-resolution tracking of
DMAPP-derived metabolites, this approach provides insight
into the functional roles of isoprenoid metabolism across
diverse biological contexts, such as prenyl isomer-specific
growth and isoprenoid biosynthesis during sporulation.
Beyond B. subtilis, this strategy has the potential to expand
to other organisms and applied to a range of metabolic studies,
including those involving complex cellular differentiation,
stress responses, and secondary metabolite production. By
improving the ability to track compartmentalized and dynamic
metabolic processes, 13C3 SIE-DMAPP serves as a valuable tool
for studying isoprenoid metabolism and its regulation in living
systems.
