ABSTRACT: Two new (1 and 2) along with six known (3−8)
dixiamycins were isolated from the culture broth of a cold-seep-
derived actinomycete, Streptomyces olivaceus OUCLQ19-3. Struc-
tures of the isolated compounds were elucidated based on
extensive MS and NMR spectroscopic analyses together with
ECD calculations. In the antibacterial test, compounds 1−8
exhibited notable growth inhibitions against a panel of multi-drug-
resistant (MDR) strains with MIC values of 0.78−6.25 μg/mL,
among which 1, 2, and 5−7 were more potent than the positive
control tetracycline.
A
ntibiotics represent one of the greatest discoveries of
mankind in the twentieth century, which greatly
promoted the development of the medical ﬁeld and human
society.1 However, with the widespread and excessive use of
antibiotics, multi-drug-resistant (MDR) pathogens have
continued to appear, and the problems caused by MDR
bacterial infections are getting worse. Being faced with this
global healthcare crisis,2 development of new antibacterial
drugs with novel mechanism of actions is urgently required.3,4
Therefore, we are committed to seeking and developing new
antibiotics.
In the search for antibacterial drug lead compounds, we
focused on underinvestigated habitats to collect chemically and
physiologically unique microbes as a source of novel
antibiotics. Cold-seeps are the areas of the deep-sea ﬂoor
where hydrogen sulﬁde, methane, and other hydrocarbon-rich
ﬂuid seepage occur.5 The temperature of cold-seeps is 2−4 °C,
basically the same as the temperature around the seaﬂoor.
Since the ﬁrst discovery of cold-seep resources in 1983,6
people have paid attention to the development and utilization
of abundant natural gas, oil, and other energies;7 however,
relatively few studies on microbial resources have been
conducted. In 2019, Sun and co-workers ﬁrst isolated
antifungal surfactin homologues CS30-1 and CS30-2 from
the cold-seep-derived bacterium Bacillus sp. CS30 as potential
promising agents to biocontrol plant diseases caused by
Magnaporthe grisea.8 In 2020, Wang and co-workers isolated
ophiobolin sesterterpenoids and farnesylated phthalide de-
rivatives from the cold-seep-derived fungus Aspergillus insuetus
SD-512, which showed broad-spectrum antibacterial activities.9
They also reported the antimicrobial asperoxide A from the
cold-seep-derived fungus Aspergilllus nidulans SD-531.10 Ji and
co-workers isolated penoxamydrazones A−C and penoxazo-
lones A and B from the cold-seep-derived fungus Penicillium
oxalicum 13-37, which exhibited inhibition against several
marine phytoplankton and bacteria species.11 Thus, cold-seep
microbes are an emerging source of structurally novel and
biologically active natural products.
In this study, Streptomyces olivaceus OUCLQ19-3 was
isolated from a mud sample collected from the cold-seep
area of the South China Sea (Figure S1). This strain showed
notable growth inhibition against a panel of MDR bacterial
strains (Table S1). Under bioassay guidance, two new (1 and
2) and six known (3−8) dixiamycins were obtained from S.
olivaceus OUCLQ19-3. Dixiamycins are dimers of xiamycin
A,12 which displayed better antibacterial activity than xiamycin
monomers.13 Up to now, N-1/N-1′, N-1/C-6′, and N-1′/C-21
coupled and sulfonyl-bridged dixiamycins have been re-
ported,13−16 while 1 and 2 show a new N-1/C-8′ coupling.
Herein, we describe the isolation, structure identiﬁcation, and
anti-MDR bacterial activity evaluation of these dixiamycins.
A 1530-bp 16S rRNA gene sequence (Figure S2) of the
OUCLQ19-3 strain was determined and compared against the
EzTaxon-e server Database.17 The result showed it is highly
similar to Streptomyces 16S rRNA with 99.93% identity to that
of Streptomyces olivaceus NRRL B-3009 (GenBank ID:
MT543222.1). Therefore, this strain was named S. olivaceus
OUCLQ19-3. Phylogenetic analysis of OUCLQ19-3 with its
closely related Streptomyces type strains is shown in Figure S3.

S. olivaceus OUCLQ19-3 was cultivated in large scale (19 L),
and the culture broth was extracted with EtOAc and monitored
by HPLC (Figure S4). Then, the extract (1.68 g) was
subsequently subjected to reversed-phase open column
fractionation followed by HPLC puriﬁcation to aﬀord
compounds 1−8.
Compound 1 was obtained as a white powder. The
molecular formula of 1 was determined to be C46H48N2O6
based on the HRESIMS data. In detail, the NMR data of 1
showed two sets of signals. The COSY correlations of H-5 (δH
8.15)/H-6 (δH 7.21)/H-7 (δH 7.27)/H-8 (δH 7.03) and the
HMBC correlations from H-5 (δH 8.15) to C-7 (δC 126.3) and
C-9 (δC 142.8) and from H-6 (δH 7.21) to C-4 (δC 125.1) and
C-8 (δC 110.6) revealed the connection from C-4 to C-9,
which conﬁrmed the presence of a 4,9-ortho-substituted
benzene ring (Figure 1). In the
1H NMR spectrum, two
singlet aromatic protons (δH 8.12, 6.71) were observed, and
the HMBC correlations from H-5 (δH 8.15) to C-3 (δC 123.3)
and from H-10 (δH 8.12) to C-4 (δC 125.1) substantiated the
structure of the carbazole ring. In addition, the aliphatic region
of the
1H NMR spectrum presented the peaks for one
oxygenated methine H-15 (δH 4.11) and two singlet methyl
groups H3-22 (δH 1.31) and H3-23 (δH 1.24). The COSY
spectrum displayed the connections for H2-13 (δH 2.68, 1.79)/
H-14 (δH 1.92)/H-15 (δH 4.11) and H-17 (δH 2.17)/H2-18
(δH 1.97, 1.52)/H-19 (δH 2.90). Moreover, based on the
HMBC correlations from H-14 to C-12 (δC 38.3) and C-16
(δC 54.8), from H-17 to C-22 (δC 26.2), C-23 (δC 11.4), and
carboxyl carbon C-24 (δC 181.7), from H-19 to C-11 (δC

compound 1 contains a xiamycin A unit.12 Moreover, the
remaining part of 1 was also assigned to be xiamycin A on the
basis of the COSY and HMBC correlations (Figure 1),

COSY correlations of H-6′ (δH 7.28) with H-5′ (δH 8.17) and

only H-8 was visible, whereas the counterpart H-8′ was missing
in the
1H NMR spectrum, and together with the non-
protonated oleﬁnic carbon C-8′ (δC 121.8), an N−C coupling
between N-1 and C-8′ was conﬁrmed. Thus, compound 1 was
determined to be a new N−C coupled dixiamycin with
xiamycin A as its monomeric unit. Compound 2, being isolated
as a white powder, has the same molecular formula of
C46H48N2O6 as that of 1. Interestingly, the detailed NMR
assignment of 2 in DMSO-d6 (Figure 1) demonstrated it
shares the same planar structure as 1 (Figure 1), suggestive of
them being stereoisomers. In a stability experiment, compound
1 was partially converted into 2 in a ratio of 26:1 when placed
in MeOH for 12 h at room temperature (Figure S5).
The relative conﬁgurations of 1 and 2 were inferred from
their NOESY spectra. The NOESY correlation of H-15/H-17
in 1 suggested a syn-conﬁguration between these two protons
(Figure 2). The NOEs of H3-22/H3-23, H3-22/H-13a, H3-23/
H-13a, and H-15/H-13b in 2 supported that H3-22, H3-23, and
H-13a were on the same side, while H-15 and H-13b were on
the opposite side (Figure 2). All of these relative conﬁgurations
were in accordance with those of reported xiamycin A,12
indicating that 1 and 2 appeared to be atropo-diastereomers at
the N-1/C-8′ coupling bond. Furthermore, the N−C axial
chirality of 1 and 2 was determined by ECD calculations with
Gaussian 09. The geometry optimization was performed at the
B97D/TZVP level, and the TD calculations were performed at
the CAM-B3LYP/6-31G(d) level of theory. The atropisomeric
conformers of 1 and 2 used in the calculations were selected
from the low-energy conformers built with a random search
procedure implemented in Sybyl 2.0 that supported the
absolute conﬁgurations of the xiamycin A unit.12 The
calculated ECD curve of the M-conﬁguration was in good
agreement with the experimental ECD data of 1, and the
calculated P-conﬁguration curve was in general a good match
with the experimental ECD curve for 2 in spite of opposite
maxima near 240 nm (Figure 3). Thus, compounds 1 and 2
were ﬁnally identiﬁed as a pair of new atropo-diastereomers of
N-1/C-8′ coupled xiamycin A dimers, named dixiamycin 12a
and dixiamycin 12b, respectively. The

8 with reported literature data,16 it was identiﬁed as
sulfadixiamycin C.
The antibacterial activities of 1−8 were tested against a
panel of MDR bacteria including four Gram-positive and three
Gram-negative strains (Table 2). Compounds 1, 2, and 5−7
exhibited stronger growth inhibition than the positive control
tetracycline against S. aureus CCARM 3090, E. faecalis
CCARM 5172, and P. aeruginosa 15690 with MIC values in
the range of 0.78−6.25 μg/mL, and these compounds also
displayed comparable activities (MICs = 1.56−6.25 μg/mL)
with tetracycline against E. faecium CCARM 5203 and M.
luteus ML01, while all compounds were inactive against E. coli
CCARM 1009 and S. typhimurium CCARM 8250 up to 12.5
μg/mL.

Xiamycins, representing one of the ﬁrst examples of
pentacyclic indolosesquiterpenes produced by prokaryotes,
were isolated from several diﬀerent Streptomyces spe-
cies.12,13,19,20 Up to now, eight dixiamycins16 have been
identiﬁed, including three N−C coupled dimers, two N−N
coupled
dimers,
and
three
unique
sulfonyl-linked
dimers.13,15,21 In terms of biosynthesis, Zhang et al.
demonstrated that the ﬂavoenzyme XiaK functions as an N-
hydroxylase catalyzing N-hydroxylation of xiamycin, and the
resultant N-hydroxyxiamycin spontaneously generates a nitroxy
radical. The nitroxy radical undergoes dimerization accom-
panied by deoxygenation to yield a nitrogen radical species,
which serves as a key intermediate leading to the formation of
dixiamycins.16,18,22 Herein, we isolated another two dixiamy-
cins with a stereogenic N-1/C-8′ coupling bond, which could
be synthesized nonenzymatically from N-hydroxyxiamycin as
well. Of note, compounds 1 and 2 exhibit potent anti-MDR
bacterial activity, providing candidate molecules for the
development of new antibiotics.
■EXPERIMENTAL SECTION
General Experimental Procedures. Optical rotations were
recorded with a JASCO P-1020 digital polarimeter (JASCO
Corporation, Tokyo, Japan). The UV spectra were measured on a
Beckman DU640 spectrophotometer. The ECD spectra were
recorded on a JASCO J-715 spectropolarimeter, using MeOH as a
solvent. The IR spectra were measured on a Nicolet NEXUE470 FT-
IR. The NMR spectra were recorded on a Bruker Avance III 600
spectrometer at 298 K, and chemical shifts were reported with
reference to the respective solvent peaks and residual solvent peaks
(δH 4.87 and δc 49.2 for CD3OD, δH 2.50 and δC 39.5 for DMSO-d6).
The HRESIMS data were obtained on a Q-TOF Ultima Global
GAA076 MS spectrometer. High-performance liquid chromatography
(HPLC) was performed on an Agilent 1260 Inﬁnity equipped with a
diode array detector. The optical density measurements were
recorded on a Biotech Epoch2 microplate reader.
Bacterial Isolation and Identiﬁcation. The OUCLQ19-3 strain
was isolated from a cold-seep sea mud sample from the South China
Sea, which was stored at −80 °C before being processed. The sea mud
sample was suspended in distilled H2O and diluted into three
gradients, 10−1, 10−2, and 10−3 g/mL. Then, the suspension
containing diverse microorganisms was spread evenly on the surface
of starch casein agar culture medium plates (starch 10 g, K2HPO4 2 g,
KNO3 2 g, casein 0.3 g, MgSO4·7H2O 0.05 g, CaCO3 0.02 g, FeSO4·
7H2O 0.01 g, agar 15 g, natural sea salt 30 g, and distilled H2O 1000
mL, pH = 7.0) and then incubated at 30 °C for 7 days. A single
colony of OUCLQ19-3 was selected and shifted onto another plate.
After incubation for another 7 days at 30 °C, the pure colony of
OUCLQ19-3 was then transferred onto an MS agar medium plate
(soya ﬂour 30 g, mannitol 20 g, agar 20 g, and tap water 1000 mL)
and stored at 4 °C for further study. In a radial diﬀusion antibacterial
assay, the EtOAc extract (100 μg) of OUCLQ19-3 showed inhibitory
activities against MDR bacterial strains (Table S1). The sequence of
16S rDNA revealed that the OUCLQ19-3 strain had 99.93% identity
with Streptomyces olivaceus (NRRL B-3009).
Isolation and Puriﬁcation of the Compounds. The
OUCLQ19-3 strain was grown and maintained on MS agar medium
plates for sporulation. For each fermentation, a pure colony of the
strain was inoculated into 250 mL Erlenmeyer ﬂasks containing 50
mL of AF/MS liquid medium (glucose 20 g, yeast extract 2 g, soya
ﬂour 8 g, CaCO3 4 g, NaCl 1 g, distilled H2O 1000 mL, pH = 7.3)
and incubated at 30 °C on a rotary shaker at 220 rpm for 7 days. The
combined culture broth (19 L) was centrifuged to yield the
supernatant. Then, the supernatant was extracted with double
volumes of EtOAc three times. The combined organic extracts were
concentrated (1.68 g) and then partitioned between 90% MeOH and
n-hexane to yield two residues. The aqueous MeOH layer (1.53 g)
was subjected to a reversed-phase (C18) open column chromatog-
raphy with 10−100% MeOH to aﬀord 16 fractions (Fr.). Fr.11 was
further puriﬁed by C18 (YMC ODS-A column, 250 × 10 mm, 5 μm)
semipreparative HPLC with an isocratic elution (85% MeOH, 3 mL/
min) to yield compound 3 (2.3 mg). Fr.12 (90% MeOH, 3 mL/min)
yielded compounds 1 (1.2 mg), 4 (1.1 mg), 5 (3.5 mg), and 8 (1.7
mg). Fr.13 (88% MeOH, 3 mL/min) yielded compound 7 (3.7 mg).
Fr.14 (97% MeOH, 3 mL/min) yielded compounds 2 (1.1 mg) and 6
(5.7 mg).
