ABSTRACT: Seven undescribed terpestacin-type sesterterpenoids,
maydistacins A−G (1−7), along with two known congeners (8 and
9), were isolated from the phytopathogenic fungus Bipolaris maydis
collected from the leaves of Hypericum longistylum. The structures of
1−7 were elucidated based on extensive spectroscopic analysis,
chemical methods, NMR calculations with DP4+ probability analysis,
and comparison of experimental and calculated electronic circular
dichroism (ECD) calculations. In vitro anti-inflammatory effects of
these compounds were tested in lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-stimulated
RAW264.7 macrophages. Compound 1 exhibited inhibition of the
production of nitric oxide in LPS-induced macrophages, with an IC50
value of 19 ± 2 μM. A dexamethasone control displayed an IC50 value of 6.7 ± 0.6 μM. Compound 1 is the first terpestacin-type
sesterterpenoid reported to display anti-inflammatory activity and may provide a novel chemical scaffold for the discovery of new
anti-inflammatory drugs.
I
nflammation is a protective reaction of a host to infection or
injury.1−4 In this complex process, nitric oxide (NO) is
recognized as a crucial regulator,4 and the overproduction of
NO is implicated in various inflammatory disorders, such as
arthritis, asthma, and cardiovascular disease.5 The use of
pharmaceuticals to inhibit the production of NO is a significant
therapeutic method to slow or reverse the pathogenesis of
those inflammatory disorders,6−8 and there is still an urgent
need for the development of novel anti-inflammatory drugs
with NO production inhibitory activity.9,10
Sesterterpenoids, with frameworks derive from five isoprene
units, are a group of natural products containing 25 carbons,
which have attracted extensive attention due to their diversified
structural skeletons and interesting biological activities,
including anticancer, immunosuppressive, antimicrobial, and
antibiofilm activity.11−13 Terpestacin-type metabolites belong
to a minority of sesterterpenoids that possess a unique 5−15
fused
bicyclo
ring
system,
bearing
an
octadeca-
hydrocyclopenta[15]annulene fragment.14 After the first report
of a terpestacin-type sesterterpenoid in 1993,14 only about 20
members of this subclass have been reported.15−20 In our
continuing search for bioactive secondary metabolites from
fungi, seven undescribed terpestacin-type sesterterpenoids,
namely, maydistacins A−G (1−7), as well as two known
congeners, fusaproliferin (8)16 and terpestacin (9),21 were
isolated from the phytopathogenic fungus Bipolaris maydis
collected from Hypericum longistylum. More significantly,
compound 1 exhibited excellent inhibitory activity against
lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-induced NO production in RAW
264.7 macrophage cells, with an IC50 value of 19 ± 2 μM
(dexamethasone was used as positive control and exhibited an
IC50 value of 6.9 ± 0.7 μM). Herein, the detailed isolation,
structural elucidation, and bioactivity evaluation of 1−9 have
been reported.

The planar structure of 1 was deduced based on the two-

the five-membered ring B of 1. Thus, the planar structure of

The relative configuration of compound 1 was assigned
based on the NOESY correlations. The cross-peaks of H3-20/
H-2a, H-7/H-9b, and H3-22/H-14a established the (3E)-,
(7E)-, and (12E)-configurations in ring A. The NOESY
correlations of H-15/H-2b, H-15/H-23, and H3-19/H-2a
indicated the β-orientation of H-2b, H-15, and H-23 and the
α-orientation of H-2a and H3-19. According to previous
reports of terpestacin-type sesterterpenoids,15−21 we expected
compound 1 to possess trans-fused A/B rings, which were
confirmed by the NOESY correlation of H3-19/H-2a, as well as
the lack of NOESY correlations between H3-19 and H-15
(Figure 2). In order to determine the absolute configuration of
1, electronic circular dichroism (ECD) calculations were
performed using a time-dependent density functional theory
(TDDFT) method at the PBE0/def2-TZVP level in the
CPCM model.22−24 The calculated ECD data of (1S,15R,23S)-
1 showed close similarities to the experimental ECD spectra of
1, indicating that the absolute structure of 1 should be assigned
as 1S,15R,23S (Figure 3A).
Maydistacin B (2) was isolated as a colorless oil. Its
molecular formula, C25H36O4, was deduced by the HRESIMS
ion peak at m/z 423.2517 [M + Na]+ (calcd for C25H36O4Na,
423.2511), indicating 8 degrees of unsaturation. The 1D NMR
data of 2 were extremely similar to those of 1, suggesting they
shared the same terpestacin framework. The key
1H−1H
COSY correlations of H2-2/H-3, H2-5/H-6, H-6/H-7, H2-9/
H2-10/H-11, and H-13/H2-14/H-15 as well as the HMBC
correlations from H3-19 to C-1, C-2, and C-15, from H3-20 to
C-3, C-4, and C-5, from H3-21 to C-7, C-8, and C-9, and from
H3-22 to C-11, C-12, and C-13 indicated the presence of a Δ6-
double bond in 2, rather than a Δ7-double bond in 1. In
addition, the degrees of unsaturation of 2 and the deshielded
carbons C-8−C-11 (δC 84.5, 36.6, 32.0, and 83.8) of 2 (Table
2) indicated there was an oxygen bridge between C-8 and C-11
in 2. Subsequently, the planar structure of 2 was confirmed
based on careful examination of its NMR data.
The NOESY correlations between H3-20/H-2a and H3-22/
H-14a indicated that the C-3/C-4 and C-12/C-13 double
bonds have E-configurations. Moreover, the large coupling
constant (J = 15.6 Hz) of H-6 and H-7 indicated that the C-6/
C-7 double bond was also E-configuration (Table 1). The
NOESY correlations between H-15/H-2b, H-15/H-23, H-15/
H-13, and H-13/H-9a indicated that these protons were

consistent with literature precedent.15 In order to determine
the absolute configuration of 2, ECD calculations were
performed, and the results indicated that the calculated curve
of (1S,8R,11S,15R,23S)-2 agreed well with the experimental
ECD curve of 2, allowing assignment of the absolute
configuration of compound 2 as 1S,8R,11S,15R,23S (Figure
3B).
Maydistacin C (3) was obtained as a colorless oil with the
formula C27H40O6, which was determined by the HRESIMS
ion at m/z 483.2724 [M + Na]+ (calcd for C27H40O6Na,
483.2723), indicating an index of hydrogen deficiency of 8.
Comparison of 1D NMR data of 3 with 2 revealed that they
were highly similar, with the main difference being the
presence of an acetyl group at C-24 in 3, rather than a hydroxy
in 2. This hypothesis was further confirmed by the HMBC
correlations from H-24 and H3-27 to C-26 (Figure 1).
Moreover, the lower chemical shift of C-8 (δC 74.3) and C-
11 (δC 80.4) (Table 2), coupled with the reduced unsaturation
of 3, suggested hydroxy groups located at C-8 and C-11 in 3, in
place of the oxygen bridge between those two carbons in 2.
The C-3/C-4, C-6/C-7, and C-12/C-13 double bonds could
easily be assigned as E-configuration on the basis of the large
coupling constant between H-6 and H-7 (J = 15.5) and the
NOESY data, which were similar to those of 1 and 2.
Furthermore, NOESY cross-peaks between H-15/H-2b, H-15/
H-13, H-13/H-11, H-15/H-23, and H3-19/H-2a indicated that
H-15, H-13, H-11, H-23, and H-2a are all located on the same
face, while H3-19 and H-2a were on the opposite face.
Nevertheless, the relative configuration of C-8 remained
uncertain due to the absence of significant NOESY
correlations. In order to determine the relative configuration
of C-8 of 3, we subjected the two possible isomers

Anti-inflammatory Activities. All isolates (1−9) were
evaluated for their inhibitory activity against NO production in
LPS-activated RAW 264.7 macrophages, with dexamethasone
as a positive control. Cell viability was determined using the
MTT assay to establish whether any observed inhibition of
NO production was caused by cytotoxicity. All compounds
showed no or weak cytotoxic effects (over 90% cell survival) at
a concentration of 50 μM on RAW 264.7 cells. Compound 1
exhibited significant inhibition of NO production, with an IC50
value of 19 ± 2 μM. The dexamethasone positive control
showed an IC50 value of 6.9 ± 0.7 μM, while the other
metabolites were inactive (<40% inhibition at 50 μM).
In summary, this work presents the discovery of seven
undescribed terpestacin-type sesterterpenoids, maydistacins
A−G (1−7), from the phytopathogenic fungus B. maydis
collected from H. longistylum leaves. The planar structures and
absolute configurations of all undescribed compounds were
unambiguously established by a combination of HRESIMS,
NMR spectroscopic analysis, comparison of calculated and
experimental ECD spectra, and chemical methods. Signifi-
cantly, 1 inhibited NO production in LPS-activated RAW
264.7 macrophages, showing potential ability in anti-inflam-
matory drug development.
■EXPERIMENTAL SECTION
General Experimental Procedures. Optical rotations were
measured on a PerkinElmer 341 polarimeter in MeOH. UV spectra
were recorded on a Varian Cary 50 UV/vis spectrophotometer. A
JASCO J-810 spectrometer was used to obtain ECD spectra. IR
spectra were recorded on a Bruker Vertex 70 FT-IR instrument. The
NMR spectra were recorded in CDCl3 and CD3OD on Bruker AM-
400 and AM-600 equipment. Tetramethylsilane was employed as an
internal standard (0 ppm) for the 1H and 13C spectra. HRESIMS data
were obtained on a Thermo Fisher LTQ XL LC/MS. Column
chromatography (CC) was performed with silica gel, Sephadex LH-20
(GE Healthcare Bio-Sciences AB), and ODS (50 μm, YMC). Thin-
layer chromatography was performed using silica gel 60 F254 and RP-
C18 F254 plates. Semipreparative HPLC was performed on a Dionex
Ultimate 3000 HPLC with a UV detector and an Ultimate XB-C18 (10
× 250 mm, 5 μm) column.
Fungal Material. The fungus was isolated from Hypericum
longistylum leaves collected from Enshi in Hubei Province, P. R.
China, in 2017, and identified as Bipolaris maydis based on its ITS
gene sequences with GenBank accession no. KT363890.1. A voucher
specimen of B. maydis was deposited in the culture collection center
of Tongji Medical College (Wuhan, China).
Fermentation, Extraction, and Isolation. The fungus B. maydis
was incubated on potato dextrose agar (PDA) at 25 °C for 5 days, and
the agar medium cut into small pieces (approximately 0.5 × 0.5 × 0.5

200 g of rice and 200 mL of distilled water. After incubation at 30 °C
for 24 days, the solid rice medium was extracted with 90% aqueous
ethanol (3 × 50 L) at room temperature. The combined ethanol
extract was evaporated under vacuum to afford a crude extract (25
kg), which was suspended in H2O and then extracted three times with
ethyl acetate. The ethyl acetate extract (250 g) was subjected to silica

obtain five fractions (A1−A5). Fr. A1 was further separated by silica
