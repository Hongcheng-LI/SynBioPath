Facile biosynthesis of taxadiene by a newly constructed Escherichia coli
strain fusing enzymes taxadiene synthase and geranylgeranyl
pyrophosphate synthase
Jin-Yi Wang , Zheng-Yu Huang , Qing-Yang Wu , Jiang Pan , Chun-Xiu Li , Jian-He Xu *
Laboratory of Biocatalysis and Synthetic Biotechnology, State Key Laboratory of Bioreactor Engineering, Shanghai Collaborative Innovation Centre for Biomanufacturing,

A R T I C L E  I N F O                  A B S T R A C T

Keywords:                                      Taxadiene has received extensive attention as the hydrocarbon backbone of a well-known anti-cancer drug,
Escherichia coli                                         Paclitaxel. In this study, a de novo pathway for taxadiene biosynthesis from cheap carbon sources was newly
MEP pathway                                     constructed using Escherichia coli BL21 (DE3) as a chassis cell. The total biosynthesis pathway of taxadiene was
Taxadiene                                                divided into upstream and downstream modules for respective optimization. To facilitate the cyclization of thedo novo Biosynthesis                                                 diterpene substrate geranylgeranyl pyrophosphate, two of the key downstream module enzymes, taxadieneMacroporous adsorption resin
Separation engineering                             synthase and geranylgeranyl pyrophosphate synthase, were genetically expressed in the form of fusion protein.
                                                    After a series of optimization, the best recombinant strain, designated as E. coli strain ECU5008, achieved a titer
                                                     of 93.5 mg/L taxadiene in shake flask cultivation. In addition, a commercially available macroporous adsorption
                                                     resin was employed for the extraction and purification of taxadiene, in which each gram of resin Hz-816 could
                                              adsorb about 13.2 mg of taxadiene from the methanol extract of the cultured E. coli cells. The application of
                                            macroporous adsorption resin effecctively improved the isolation yield of taxadiene from fermentation broth.
