                          Further, JA stimulates PTX biosynthesis in TmLOX13-silenced cell suspension
                                           It is known that JA can activate the biosynthesis of certain secondary metabolites in plant and more particularly
                              those implicated in the plant defence mechanisms. To experimentally assess this point, JA levels in TmLOX13-
                               silenced cells were measured and compared to those in control (wild-type) cells. As shown in Fig. 8A, JA levels
                               in TmLOX13-silenced cells were drastically reduced—by approximately 74%—relative to wild-type cells. This
                              reduction was accompanied by a significant decrease in PTX production, which dropped by about 2.4-fold
                          compared to wild-type cells. Furthermore, the JA-deficient phenotype of TmLOX13-silenced cells was partially
                             rescued by exogenous JA treatment, restoring PTX production to approximately 58% of the level observed in
                              wild-type cells (Fig. 8B). This was apparently due a concurrent augmentation in the transcripts levels of TXS,
                     DBAT and DBTNBT, genes in the PTX biosynthesis pathway. Quantitatively, the heat map in Fig. 8C and Table
                                S5, showed that the transcripts levels of target genes peaked on day 16, reaching about of 21-, 15- and 30-fold,
                                  respectively, after treatment with 100 µM JA. Moreover, although JA treatment positively affected the transcripts
                                   level of the TmLOX13 gene, this level still remained lower than those in the wild type. Our results demonstrate
                                 that silencing the TmLOX13 gene directly reduces cellular JA levels and that exogenous JA treatment partially
                                 restores PTX production in TmLOX13-silenced cell suspensions.
