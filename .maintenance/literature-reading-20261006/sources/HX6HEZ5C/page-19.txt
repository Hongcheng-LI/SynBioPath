                                cultures treated with MeJA81,82.
                            The involvement of caleosin/peroxygenase (CLO/PXG) in the biosynthesis and secretion of PTX was recently
                               highlighted at two complementary levels: the first one is based on the fatty acid hydroperoxide (FA-OOH)-
                              reductase activity of CLO/PXG, leading to the formation of their corresponding alcohols (FA-OH), that impact
                              the plant growth, development and its responses to stress43,44,58,83,84. The second level is based on the structural
                                 roles of CLO/PXG in cellular lipid droplet (LD) assembly85–87. This is consistent with recent data indicating
                                 that treatment of T. media cells with 13-HPOT-with induced expression of CLO/PXG, thus increasing the LD
                            production and activating the secretion of PTX58,63. Similar observations were also reported in plants exposed
                                to hydrophobic environmental contaminants such as dioxins44,47,86–89.
                                Further data on the role of TmLOX13 in the biosynthesis of PTX were gained by siRNA-based silencing
                                of TmLOX13 gene in T. media cell suspension. Silencing of TmLOX13 reduced the production of PTX with
                        no significant effects on cell growth. This decline is probably due to down-regulation of some key genes of
                              the PTX biosynthesis pathway, most notably TXS, DBAT and DBTNBT. In line with this, silencing of 13-
                    LOX led to decreased tolerance of cotton against Verticillium dahliae through a JA-mediated pathway90. Also,
                           OsHI-LOX-silenced rice became more susceptible to chewing herbivores, but more resistant to a phloem
                                    feeder91,92. Moreover, silencing of LOX H1 in potato plants resulted in a marked reduction in the production
                                of volatile aliphatic C6 aldehydes, acting as either signalling molecules for wound-induced gene expression or
                                as antimicrobial substances93. A recent report showed that silencing of CLO/PXG-encoding genes in T. media
                                      cell cultures reduces production of PTX58. This implies that the TmLOX13-silencing phenotype is due to the
                              deprivation of 13-LOX/PXG pathway-derivative metabolites.
                          Our data indicate that exogenous application of JA to TmLOX13-silenced cell suspensions restored the PTX-
                                 deficient phenotype by upregulating PTX biosynthetic genes. Consistent with these findings, the direct role of JA
                               in activating PTX biosynthesis has been shown to be mediated by MYC2, a key JA-responsive transcription factor
                             belonging to the basic helix–loop–helix (bHLH) family94, MYC2 regulates the expression of critical genes involved
                               in secondary metabolite biosynthesis in plants95. Specifically, MYC2 directly modulates PTX biosynthesis by
                             binding to cis-regulatory elements, such as the G-box, within the promoters of its target genes96. In this context,
                   MYC2 has been reported to activate several key genes in the PTX biosynthetic pathway, notably TXS (taxadiene
                               synthase, following JA treatment95,97. Moreover, our results also indicate that exogenous treatment with 13-LOX/
                             PXG-derivative oxylipins restored the PTX-deficient phenotype of T. media cells resulting from the silencing of
                       TmLOX13. However, PTX secretion by TmLOX13-silenced cells was re-established up to normal level compared
                                to wild type-cells after treatment with 13-HOT, Mix-EOE and Mix-HOE, respectively. Intriguingly, similar
                                    effects of such oxylipins have been reported in fungal cells silenced of AfPXG gene, a fungal CLO/PXG with roles
                               in fungal growth, development and secondary metabolite production57,98.

                      Conclusions
                             In conclusion, our work emphasizes the pivotal role of 13-Lipoxygenase (13-LOX) in enhancing PTX
                                biosynthesis, thereby contributing to the advancement of production strategies. Through the identification and
                                characterization of a novel ortholog, TmLOX13, in Taxus media, the study provides compelling evidence for the
                                 direct involvement of the 13-LOX pathway in PTX production. The observed correlation between TmLOX13
                              expression and the activation of PTX biosynthesis in T. media cell cultures underscores the biological significance
                                of this enzyme in the process. Most notably, the ability of exogenous treatment with 13-LOX/PXG-derivative
                                oxylipins to rescue the PTX-deficient phenotype of T. media cells further emphasizes the potential applications
                                of harnessing 13-LOX for improving PTX production. These results suggest that manipulating the 13-LOX
                           pathway could serve as a viable strategy to enhance PTX yield in Taxus sp. This newfound understanding of the
                              interconnectedness between 13-LOX and PTX biosynthesis provides a foundation for targeted biotechnological
                               interventions aimed at optimizing PTX production, which holds significance for pharmaceutical and medicinal
                                 applications.
