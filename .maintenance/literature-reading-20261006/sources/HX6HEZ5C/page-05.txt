                           Inhibition of 13-LOX activity
                          The inhibitory effect of nordihydroguaiaretic acid (NDGA) against TmLOX13 activity60 was firstly determined
                               in vitro using the purified recombinant TmLOX13. The enzyme was pre-incubated with 1 mM of NDGA for
                           5 min at 25 °C. Then, the enzymatic activity was assayed as described before using 0.5 mM of linolenic acid
                              (C18:3) acids in optimal reaction conditions for the enzyme activity. Subsequently, the inhibition of LOX by
                  NDGA was assayed in T. media cell suspension culture. In this experiment, the inhibitor was added to the
                                  T. media cell culture at 5 mM and the production of PTX as well as the transcripts levels of TXS, DBAT and
                     DBTNBT were evaluated at 4, 8, 12, 16, 20 and 24 days after inoculation (DAI).

                       JA-exogenous treatment of TmLOX13-silenced cells
                                  Elicitation of both TmLOX13-silenced and wild-type T. media cells was carried out using jasmonic acid (purity
                       > 98%, Larodan, Inc., Sweden) at the onset of the cell growth stage and maintained for 24 days19. For JA-treated
                                  cultures, 1 mg of jasmonic acid was initially dissolved in ethanol, then evaporated to dryness under a nitrogen
                              stream, and subsequently reconstituted in 1 mL of dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) to prepare a stock solution at
                             a concentration of 1 mg mL− 1. Cell cultures were elicited with 100 µM JA for treated samples, while control
                                cultures received an equal volume of DMSO alone. PTX was extracted and quantified at 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, and 24
                                days. Additionally, cells from both treated and control cultures were harvested by filtration at the specified time
                               points for gene expression analysis.

                      Complementation of TmLOX13-silenced cells with 13-LOX/PXG-pathway Oxylipins
                              Oxylipins derived from the 13-LOX/PXG pathway were synthesized in three successive steps as described
                                 previously57. In  brief, commercial 13-HPOT was incubated overnight at 26 °C with 50 µg of purified
                            recombinant AfPXG, a fungal peroxygenase with well characterized hydroperoxide-reductase activity57,61. The
                                respective oxylipin derivatives (13-HOT, 9,10-epoxy-octadecadienoic acid, 12,13-epoxy-octadecenoic acid,
                              9,10-dihydroxy-octadecadienoic acid, 12,13-octadecadienoic acid, 9,10,18-trihydroxy-octadecadienoic acid
                         and 12,13,18-trihydroxy-octadecadienoic acid) were separated and determined according to Hanano et al., 44.
                     An ethanol solution of 100 µM of 13-HOT or a mix of epoxides (Mix-EOE) or dihydroxys (Mix-DHOE) was
                          added to cultures of (+) siRNTmLOX13 cell line. Thereafter, the biomass, PTX production and the expression of
                          TXS, DBAT and DBTNBT were determined at 4, 8, 12, 16, 20 and 24 days after inoculation (DAI). Six separated
                              treatments were examined for their responses to each group of oxylipins. In parallel, a control experiment was
                               carried out using ethanol only.

                         Extraction and analysis of JA
                          JA was extracted according to Liu et al.62,. Briefly, 500 mg of harvested frozen cells was ground to fine powder
                             with a mortar and pestle in the presence of liquid N2. JA was extracted with 600 µL of methanol at 4 °C overnight.
                          The samples were subsequently centrifuged at 4800 × g for 10 min. The supernatant was transferred to a new 5
                   mL glass tube, evaporated under N2 flow until dryness, then the residue was re-taken with 200 µL of methanol.
                           3000 µL of distillated H2O was added to the extract and the mixture was applied onto the Waters Sep-pak C18
                                 cartridge. The cartridge was washed with 200 µL of 20% methanol and 250 µL of 30% methanol. Finally, the JA
                                extract was eluted with 300 µL of methanol for further analysis. JA concentration was determined using a Sykam
                    HPLC System, Germany, with an UV-Vis Diode Array Detector (DAD) following the method described by Liu
                                   et al.62,. The mobile phase was composed of acetonitrile and triethylamine buffer (3 mL L− 1, pH 2.6). The C18
                          was used as the stationary phase (4.6 × 250 mm; 5 μm) at 35 ˚C with a flow rate of 1 mL min- 1. The detection
                            wavelength was 195 nm and the injection volume was 10 µL.

                             Statistics
                                All data presented were expressed as means ± standard deviation (SD). Statistical analysis was performed using
                     IBM SPSS statistics version 23.0. Statistical significance between control and treatments was evaluated by
                     ANOVA.

                       Results
                   TmLOX13 encodes a typical 13-lipoxygenase
                   A gene sequence homology approach coupled with 5’-end Rapid Amplification cDNA end, identified a full-length
                    cDNA of 2676 bp. This clone was referred to as TmLOX13 and its complete coding sequence (CDS) was deposited
                               in GenBank database under the accession number (MW030627). This CDS predictably encodes a protein of 891
                         amino acids that shares a high identity with other plant 13-lipoxygenases. Table S2 (supplementary information)
                               presents the identity scores between the TmLOX13 amino acids sequence and the top 20 representative plant
                          LOXs. We note that the highest three scores of identities (55.53, 55.41 and 54.82%) were detected between
                     TmLOX13 and 13 S-LOX from Olea europaea var. sylvestris and with two 13-LOXs isoforms from Olea europaea
                               subsp. europaea, respectively. The complete alignment of proteins sequences is presented in Fig. 1. Basing on
                              the identity score between protein sequences, the evolutionary history was inferred using the Neighbor-Joining
                           method. The optimal tree is shown for the top 20 plant LOXs orthologs in Fig. 2A. We note that the TmLOX13
                          was separately clustered with a well characterized LOX from Taxus chinesis (AEU04517). In addition to this, the
