Experimental procedures section 
Strains and culture conditions  
Wild type Paecilomyces variotii strain CBS101075 was acquired as a gift from A/Prof. 
Alexander Idnurm. The A. nidulans LO7890 (a gift from Prof. Berl Oakley, University of Kansas) 
was used as the heterologous host for expression experiments in this work. The characteristics 
of A. nidulans LO7890 was as described previously.1, 2 Saccharomyces cerevisiae strain BJ5464 
was used for yeast-mediated homologous recombination. E. coli BL21(DE3) was used for 
protein purification 
Czapek Yeast Extract Agar (CYA, 30 g Sucrose, 5g Yeast Extract, 1g K2HPO4, 0.3 g NaNO3, 
0.05 g KCl, 0.05 g MgSO4, 0.01 g ZnSO4, 0.01 g FeSO4, 0.5 mg CuSO4, 15 g Agar in 1 L), Malt 
Extract Agar (MEA, 20 g malt extract, 20 g D-glucose, 1 g Bacto-peptone, 15 g Agar in 1 L), YES 
media (4 g yeast extract, 20 g sucrose,1 g KH2PO4, 0.5 g MgSO4, 15 g Agar in 1 L, pH 6.2±0.2) 
were tested for culturing of P. variotii CBS101075 for accumulation of metabolites. For 
metabolic profile analysis and compound isolation, stationary culture was performed with CYA 
plates at 30 oC for 14 days for accumulation of secondary metabolites.  
Glucose minimal medium (GMM, 10 g Glucose, 6g NaNO3, 1.52 g K2HPO4, 0.52 g KCl, 0.52 g 
MgSO4·7H2O, 22 mg ZnSO4·7H2O, 11 mg H3BO3, 5 mg MnCl2·4H2O, 1.6 mg FeSO4·7H2O, 1.6 mg 
CoCl2·5H2O, 1.6 mg CuSO4·5H2O, 1.1 mg (NH4)6Mo7O24·4H2O, 50 mg Na4EDTA in 1L, adjusted to 
pH 6.5) was used for culturing of A. nidulans. Uracil/Uridine, riboflavin, and pyridoxine with 
suggested concentration are supplemented if necessary. In terms of A. nidulans protoplast 
transformation, Stabilized minimal medium (SMM, GMM supplemented with 1.2 M sorbitol) 
was used. 
For small-scale metabolic profile analysis of A. nidulans culture, spores with a 
concentration of ~108/L were inoculated into 50ml GMM media. Cultures were grown at 37 
oC/180rpm for 18h. 2.5ml/L of cyclopentanone was added to induce the expression of Palc 
promoters.3 Cultures were incubated at 25 oC/180rpm for another 4 days for the accumulation 
of secondary metabolites. For each combination, three separate transformants were peaked as 
replicates. For isolation of target compounds, scaled-up cultures were performed under the 
same conditions.

Construction of Yeast-Fungal Artificial Chromosomes (pYFACs) for heterologous 
expression in A. nidulans 
A. nidulans heterologous expression vectors (pYFAC-CH1/2/4/5) were constructed in 
previous work.7 Yeast homologous recombination was used for the construction of YFACs in 
this work. Full-length genomic fragments of vdt1-6 were amplified with primers designed 
(Table S1) and cloned into corresponding vectors (Table S2) as previously described.7 Yeast

Sample preparation and Metabolic profile analysis 
For A. nidulans cultures, extracts from both mycelium and media were analyzed. Mycelium 
was collected by filtration and extracted with acetone. Media was extracted with either ethyl 
acetate/methanol/acetic acid (89:10:1). Crude extracts were dried down and redissolved in 
methanol for LC-DAD-MS analysis. For P. variotii cultures, agar culture was chopped into small 
pieces and extracted with methanol under sonication condition. Crude extracts were filtered, 
dried and redissolved in methanol for LC-DAD-MS analysis. 
LC-DAD-MS analysis was performed with Agilent 1260 liquid chromatography (LC) 
system coupled to a diode array detector (DAD) and an Agilent 6130 quadrupole mass 
spectrum (MS) with an ESI source. For analytical purpose, a Kinetex C18 column (2.6 µm, 2.1 
mm i.d. x 100 mm; Phenomenex) was used. The mobile phase gradient of eluent B (acetonitrile 
with 0.1% formic acid) started at 5% and gradually increased to 95% over 10 mins at a flow 
rate of 0.75 ml/min. 
Compound isolation and structural characterization. 
Ethyl acetate/methanol/acetic acid (89:10:1) extraction method was used for isolation of 
4-13 from A. nidulans culture medium. Ethyl acetate/methanol (90:10) was used for 
extraction from P. variotii CYA culture. Crude extracts were dried in vacuo and then 
fractionated on a Reveleris flash chromatography system (Grace) using a dichloromethane 
/methanol gradient on a Reveleris HP silica flash cartridge. Fractions containing the target 
compound were combined for further purification using a semi-prep HPLC with a C18 column 
(Agilent, 5 μm, 21.2 × 150 mm). 
For structural characterization, nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectra were 
collected on Bruker Avance IIIHD 500MHz/600MHz NMR spectrometer. Chloroform-d, 
DMSO-d6, acetonitrile-d3, and acetone-d6 were used as solvents. Electronic circular dichroism 
(ECD) spectra were recorded on a JASCO J-810 spectropolarimeter, with acetonitrile as solvent. 
The axial chirality of dimeric compounds isolated was determined by comparing with both the 
ECD spectrum of (M)-viriditoxin standard purchased from sapphire bioscience and published 
data.8  
Purification of recombinant VdtE and VdtD form E. coli 
Total RNA of VdtD and VdtE was prepared from A. nidulans heterologous expression 
strains. Briefly A. nidulans mycelium from 50 ml culture was harvested, frozen with liquid 
nitrogen and grinded into a fine powder with pestle and mortar. The powder was immediately 
transferred to hot phenol buffer (10 ml phenol, 20 ml homogenisation buffer (100 mM 
Tris-HCl, pH 8.0, 5 mM EDTA pH 8.0, 100 mM NaCl, 0.5% SDS), 0.2 ml -mercaptoethanol) and 
shaked for 15 mins. 10 ml chloroform was then added and shaken for further 15 mins. The 
mixture was spined at room temperature for 10 mins. The aqueous phase was extracted twice 
with phenol-chloroform mixture. 0.1 volume of 3M sodium acetate and 0.8 volume of 
isopropanol was then added to the aqueous phase and precipitated at -20 oC for 15 mins. 
Precipitant was collected by centrifugation at 4 oC for 30 mins. Pellet was dried and 
resuspended with 500 µl MilliQ water. An equal volume of 4M LiCl was added and incubated at 
4 oC over-night. Precipitant was collected by centrifugation at 4 oC for 30 mins. Pellet was

washed with 80% ethanol once, dried and resuspended in 100 µl MilliQ water. 
cDNA was synthesized with ProtoScript® II First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (New England 
Biolabs, Inc (NEB)) following the manual provided. pETM-41 was used for protein purification 
in this research. The vector carries both N-terminal 6xHis-tag and N-terminal maltose binding 
protein (MBP)-tag for ease of purification. cDNA fragments of vdtD and vdtE were cloned into 
NcoI/NotI site of pETM-41.  
We applied 6xHis-tag for purification of VdtE and VdtD. The purification procedure was 
carried out as previously described.9 Briefly, an overnight culture of E. coli BL21(DE3) carrying 
corresponding expression construct was inoculated into fresh LB medium to OD600 of 0.02. 
The culture was grown at 37oC/180rpm until OD600 reach to 0.4. 400 µM IPTG was added to 
the culture for over-night induction at 16 oC. For purification of Vdt5, 0.0025% riboflavin was 
supplemented during induction. The cells were harvest by centrifugation at 5000g for 15 mins. 
The pellet was washed with MiliQ water and resuspended in Lysis buffer (50mM NaH2PO4, 
300mM NaCl, 10mM imidazole, 2 mM DTT, pH8.0). The cell suspension was lysed by 
sonication on ice. Cellular debris was removed by centrifugation at 17000 rpm, 4 oC for 40 min, 
and ProfinityTM IMAC Ni-charged resin (Bio-Rad) was then added to the supernatant (2 mL per 
liter of culture). The suspension was swirled slowly at 4 oC over-night and loaded into a gravity 
column. The protein-bound resin was washed with 2 column volume of washing buffer (50mM 
NaH2PO4, 300mM NaCl, 50mM imidazole, 2 mM DTT, pH8.0). The protein was then eluted with 
elution buffer (50mM NaH2PO4, 300mM NaCl, 250mM imidazole, 2 mM DTT, pH8.0) . Purified 
Vdt4/Vdt5 was concentrated and exchanged into storage buffer (50 mM Tris-HCl, pH 7.9, 2 mM 
EDTA, 2 mM DTT, 10% glycerol) with the centriprep filters (Amicon), aliquoted, and flash 
frozen using liquid nitrogen. The concentration was measured with Thermo Scientific 
NanoDrop 2000 Spectrophotometer. 
Preparation of cell-free extracts for VdtB and VdtD 
pYFAC-2 expressing vdtB was transformed into A. nidulans. Mycelium was harvested after 
3-days induction, frozen with liquid nitrogen and grinded into a fine powder with pestle and 
mortar. The powder was resuspended in 50 mM citrate buffer pH 5.0 supplemented with 2 
mM DTT and lysed by sonication on ice. Cellular debris was removed by centrifugation at 
17000 rpm, 4 oC for 40 min and the supernatant was used directly for in vitro assays.  
in vitro enzymatic reaction assay for VdtB, VdtD and VdtE 
All in vitro reactions were performed at 100 µl scale. For VdtE, the reaction mixture 
contains 50 mM Tris-HCl pH 8.0, 5 mM NADPH or NADH, 0.5 mM FAD, 0.5 mM 5, 10 µM VdtE.10 
The reaction was carried out at 30 oC, 400rpm and incubated over-night. For VdtB and VdtD, 
the reaction was performed in 50 mM citrate buffer, pH 5.0.11 40 µl VdtB cell-free 
lysate/purified fraction and an equal volume of VdtD cell-free lysate/purified fraction was 
added together with 2 mM 10 or 1 and 1’. 0.1% Triton X-100 was also added to increase 
compound solubility. The reaction was carried out at 30 oC, 400 rpm and incubated for 5 hours. 
The reactions were quenched with 200 µl of ethyl acetate/methanol/acetic acid (89:10:1). The 
organic phase was separated, dried, redissolved in 50 µl of methanol, and analyzed with 
LC-DAD-MS as previously described.
