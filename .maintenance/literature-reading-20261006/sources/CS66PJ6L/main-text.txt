Biosynthesis of Viridicatol in Penicillium palitans Implies a
Cytochrome P450-Mediated meta Hydroxylation at a Monoalkylated
Benzene Ring

ABSTRACT: Cyclopenol (1) and viridicatol (6) with m-hydroxyl
groups were isolated from a culture of Penicillium palitans. Genome
mining and heterologous expression in Aspergillus nidulans led to
the identiﬁcation of their biosynthetic gene cluster and the
cytochrome P450 enzyme VdoD responsible for the meta
hydroxylation. Precursor feeding experiments into vdoD trans-
formant proved the conversion of cyclopenin (2) to 1, which then
undergoes a spontaneous or VdoA-catalyzed rearrangement to 6. A
direct conversion of viridicatin (5) to 6 by VdoD was not detected.
T
he oxyfunctionalization of carbon−hydrogen (C−H)
bonds is one of the important reactions for increasing
the structural complexity of natural products and provides a
directing group for further diversiﬁcations.1,2 In nature,
hydroxylation can be achieved by diﬀerent enzyme classes
such as cytochrome P450s,3 2-oxoglutarate-dependent oxy-
genases,4 and ﬂavin-dependent oxidoreductases.5 Hydroxyla-
tion at a monoalkylated benzene ring usually takes places at the
para or ortho position of the substituent.3−5 An enzyme-
catalyzed meta hydroxylation rarely occurs in nature.
The viridicatin-type alkaloids with a 4-aryl-2-quinoline
skeleton comprise only a few species and are widely distributed
in the fungal genera of Penicillium and Aspergillus. For example,
viridicatin (5) with a 4-phenyl residue and its meta-
hydroxylated congener viridicatol (6) (Figure 1A) have been
isolated from Penicillium polonicum6 and Penicillium sclerotio-
rum.7 Together with its 4′-methoxylated derivative, viridicatin
was also identiﬁed in Aspergillus nidulans (A. nidulans).8 These
compounds possess various biological activities such as
antitumor,9 anti-inﬂammatory,10 and antifungal activities.11
Genome mining led to the identiﬁcation of a nonribosomal
peptide synthetase (NRPS) containing biosynthetic gene
cluster asq for 5 and its 4′-methoxylated derivative in A.
nidulans FGSC A4.8 A very similar pen cluster from Penicillium
thymicola IBT 5891 is responsible for the biosynthesis of 4′-
methoxyviridicatin (Figure 1).12 Molecular biological and
biochemical investigations revealed that viridicatin and its 4′-
methoxylated derivative are rearrangement products of the aryl
benzodiazepinediones cyclopenin (2) and 4′-methoxycyclope-
nin, respectively (Figure 1A).8,12 This conversion was
catalyzed by the hemocyanin-like enzyme AsqI or PenL
(Figure 1A). It has also been reported that 2 is derived from
anthranilic acid and L-phenylalanine. Accordingly, 4′-methox-
ycyclopenin originates from anthranilic acid and 4′-methox-
yltyrosine. L-Phenylalanine was not used by the NRPS PenN
for cyclodipeptide formation (Figure 1A).8,12 On the basis of
these results, it can be speculated that 6 with an additional m-
hydroxyl group in comparison to 5 could be the rearrangement
product of cyclopenol (1). Together with 6, 1 was also isolated
from fungal strains such as Penicillium viridicatum and
Penicillium cyclopium.13 This hypothesis raised the question
about the origin of the aryl residue in 6. It could also be
speculated, in analogy to 4′-methoxyviridicatin, that the
hydroxyl groups in 1 and 6 are from m-tyrosine (Figure 1A).
The formation of m-tyrosine catalyzed by a phenylalanine m-
hydroxylase was reported in Streptomyces coeruleorubidus.14
However, a meta hydroxylation of cyclopenin to cyclopenol
was demonstrated in the later 1970s with a crude enzyme
extract from Penicillum cyclopium.15,16 This controversial
ambiguity encouraged us to investigate the biosynthesis of 6
and especially the hydroxylation step, because a meta
hydroxylation at a monoalkylated benzene ring has rarely

palitans, syn Penicillium commune) NRRL 203317−20 for our
study, because 1 and 6 were previously isolated from
Penicillium commune.21,22
Cultivation of P. palitans in modiﬁed Czapek−Dox
(mCDH) medium at 25 °C for 15 days and LC-MS analysis
(Figure 2 and Figure S1) revealed the presence of the two
dominant products 1 and 6 with respective [M + H]+ ions at
m/z 311.1031 and 254.0815. These values correspond very
well to those of cyclopenol and viridicatol, respectively. To
conﬁrm the integrity of 1 and 6, a large-scale fermentation was
performed. Fractionation of the crude extract on a silica gel
column and further puriﬁcation by HPLC led to the isolation
and identiﬁcation of six pathway-related metabolites 1−6
(Figure 2). High-resolution electrospray ionization (HRESI)
and 1H NMR (Tables S5−S7 and Figures S2−S7) analyses
conﬁrmed them to be cyclopenol (1),23 cyclopenin (2),23
cyclopeptin (3),24 dehydrocyclopeptin (4),24 viridicatin (5),25
and virdicatol (6).26 Among them are two pairs with and
without a m-hydroxyl group: i.e., 1 and 2 as well as 5 and 6. An
interpretation of the 1H NMR spectrum of cyclopeptin (3)
(Figure S4) also revealed the presence of the two conformers
3a and 3b in a ratio of 2:1, as reported previously.8
For biosynthetic studies on 1 and 6, the genome of P.
palitans NRRL 2033 was sequenced and the draft genome
sequence was used for prediction of putative gene clusters by
using AntiSMASH.27 Genome mining and a Blastp search
using enzymes from the asq and pen clusters (Table S1 and
Figure 1B) led to the identiﬁcation of a putative biosynthetic
gene cluster for viridicatol, termed the vdo cluster hereafter.
The candidate cluster shares high sequence identities of 53−
79% on the amino acid level with three genes of the
aforementioned asq and pen clusters (Table S1 and Figure
1B), coding for a hemocyanin-like protein (VdoA, homologue
of AsqI/PenL), NRPS (VdoB, AsqK/PenN) and an FeII/2-
oxoglutarate-dependent oxygenase (VdoC, AsqJ/PenM). As
illustrated in Figure 1A, these enzymes are responsible for the
formation of the viridicatin scaﬀold. In the vdo cluster, the gene
vdoD coding for a cytochrome P450 enzyme is located
downstream from vdoC and shares only 27% amino acid
sequence identity with the cytochrome P450 asqL in the asq
cluster, indicating diﬀerent functions in the biosynthesis. The
function of AsqL was neither reported nor speculated. On the
basis of its high sequence identities with putative benzoate 4-
monooxygenases from diﬀerent fungi, e.g. GAQ45310.1 from
Aspergillus niger (63%), PYH35076.1 from Aspergillus neoniger
(62%), and RAK89448.1 from Aspergillus costaricaensis (62%),
we speculated that VdoD could be responsible for the meta
hydroxylation in 1 and 6.
Attempts to prove the cluster function by gene deletion
failed, because P. palitans NRRL 2033 is diﬃcult to genetically
manipulate. We therefore changed our strategy to heterologous
expression in A. nidulans LO8030, which was successfully used
for cluster identiﬁcation.28,29 Fortunately, the native asq cluster
from A. nidulans is usually silent,8 so that expression of the vdo
cluster can be achieved without the inﬂuence of the
endogenous viridicatin biosynthetic genes. For heterologous
expression, a DNA segment of approximate 18 kbp containing
vdoABCD, 500 bp upstream, and 200 bp downstream regions
was cloned via homologous recombination in Saccharomyces
cerevisiae30 into pJN017,31 resulting in the expression construct
pZZ01 (Tables S3 and S4 and Figure S8). PEG-mediated
protoplast transformation into A. nidulans LO8030 and
selection with riboﬂavin autotrophy resulted in the trans-
formant A. nidulans ZL01 (Table S2 and Figure S9). The
correct integration of the cluster into the genome was veriﬁed
by PCR ampliﬁcation with genomic DNA (Figure S9).
The strain ZL01 was subsequently cultivated in mCDH
medium (pH 5.5) for 10 days, and the secondary metabolite
production was monitored by LC-MS (Figure 3). On
comparison with the negative control strain A. nidulans BK06
with the empty vector pJN017,32 all of the six products 1−6
isolated from NRRL 2033 were detected in ZL01 by UV and
extracted [M + H]+ ions of the respective compounds. Their
integrity was veriﬁed by a comparison of their UV, MS, and
MS2 data with those of the authentic compounds isolated in
this study. In comparison to the dominant products 1 and 6 in
the original producer, 1, 3, and 4 were accumulated as major
metabolites in ZL01, indicating a diﬀerent gene expression or
regulation in both strains. Nevertheless, this result provides
direct evidence that the enzymes coded by the four genes
vdoABCD are indeed responsible for the biosynthesis of

As speculated above, the cytochrome P450 enzyme VdoD is
likely the assumed hydroxylase. We therefore constructed an
expression plasmid pZZ02 containing only three genes vdoABC
without vdoD (Tables S3 and S4 and Figure S8) and
transferred it into A. nidulans LO8030 to create the expression
strain A. nidulans ZL02 (Table S2 and Figure S9). Afterward,
ZL02 was cultivated under the same conditions as for ZL01. As
shown in Figure 3, 2−5 without a m-hydroxyl group were
detected. 1 and 6 with a m-hydroxyl group could not be
detected, even with extracted ions. These results proved
unequivocally VdoD to be a m-hydroxylase in the biosynthesis
of 1 and 6 and also suggested hydroxylation at the late stage of
the biosynthesis, very likely after the formation of 2.
To prove the hydroxylation step catalyzed by VdoD, we
constructed the expressed plasmid pZZ03 by cloning vdoD and
its 458 bp upstream and 156 bp downstream sequence into the
vector pYH-gpdA-pyrG (Tables S3 and S4 and Figure S8),
created the strain A. nidulans ZL03 (Table S2, Figure S9), and
carried out the feeding experiment with 2 (Figure 4 and Figure
S10). 2 was converted with a yield of 30% to a compound with
the same retention time, UV spectrum, and MS data as those
of 1 after cultivation for 7 days, while no consumption of 2 was
observed in the control strain pYH-WA-pyrG with the empty
vector pYH-gpdA-pyrG.33 To conﬁrm the structure of the
biotransformation product, 2 (5 mg) was fed into ZL03. A 1.3
mg portion of the product was isolated on semipreparative
HPLC and subjected to 1H NMR and HREISMS analyses,
which conﬁrmed it to be viridicatol 1. This result proved that
VdoD is responsible for the conversion of 2 to 1 and catalyzes
the key meta hydroxylation step in the viridicatol biosynthetic
pathway. Our ﬁnding is also in agreement with the reported
cyclopenin meta hydroxylation activity of a crude protein
extract from Penicillum cyclopium.15,16
Afterward, we tried to increase the conversion yield by
VdoD and prepared the new expression construct pZL19
(Tables S3 and S4 and Figure S8) by cloning vdoD directly
after the gpdA promoter in pJN017 without the additional 458
bp upstream sequence. Feeding 2 into the obtained trans-
formant A. nidulans ZL19 (Table S2 and Figure S9) with the
expression plasmid pZL19 resulted in almost complete
conversion to 1 after cultivation for 5 days (Figure 4 and
Figure S10). In comparison, no consumption of 2 was detected
in the control strain BK06. The increased VdoD activity
allowed us to test its acceptance for other precursors such as
3−5. As shown in Figure 4, no diﬀerence between the
transformant ZL19 and the control strain BK06 was detected
after feeding 3−5 and cultivation for 5 days. These results
proved the high substrate speciﬁcity of VdoD for its substrate
2. In the cultures with 4, both 4 and one additional peak 4*
with same UV and MS data were detected. The conversion was
also observed after incubation of 4 in mCDH medium (Figure
S11). We speculated that 4* could be the E isomer of 4.
However, due to the low quantity of 4, its structure could not
be elucidated in this study.
In conclusion, we isolated cyclopenol (1) and viridicatol (6)
with a m-hydroxyl group at a benzene ring and related
compounds from a culture of P. palitans NRRL 2033. Genome
mining led to the identiﬁcation of a gene cluster with high

vdoABCD are responsible for the formation of viridicatol. The

cyclodipeptide cyclopenin (3). The 2-oxoglutarate-dependent

mediate 4 to the epoxide cyclopenin (2). As demonstrated in
this study, the cytochrome P450 enzyme VdoD catalyzes the
conversion of 2 to cyclopenol 1, which undergoes rearrange-
ment to 6 catalyzed by VdoA. VdoA also uses 2 as a substrate
for the formation of viridicatin (5) (Scheme 1). It was reported
that 4′-methoxycyclopenin can be quickly transformed into 4′-
methoxyviridicatin spontaneously, but the spontaneous con-
version of cyclopenin (2) to viridcatin (5) was very slow, as we
observed (Figure S11).12 A nonenzymatic rearrangement of 1
to 6 was also observed in this study by incubation in mCDH
(pH 5.5) medium at 25 °C for 10 days (Figure S10).
