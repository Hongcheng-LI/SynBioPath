ABSTRACT
Two new compounds, 5-[2-hydroxypropane-1-yl]-2,6-dimethlben-
zene-1,3-diol (1) and coniochaetone L (2), together with 19
known compounds (3–21), were isolated from a deep-sea fungus,
Penicillium sp. SCSIO 06720. Their structures and absolute configu-
rations were elucidated by detailed NMR, MS spectroscopic analy-
ses, chiral-phase HPLC analysis, and electronic circular dichroism
spectra. All the isolated compounds (1–21) were tested for their
antibacterial and HIV latency-reversal activities. Among these com-
pounds, compound 16 showed moderate antibacterial activities
against
Staphylococcus
aureus
ATCC
29213
and
Methicillin-
Resistant
Staphylococcus
Aureus-shh-1
with
MIC
values
of
10.4 ± 3.7 lg/mL and 46.9 ± 29.7 lg/mL, respectively, which were
comparable to that of the positive control ampicillin with MIC val-
ues of 0.5 ± 0.4 lg/mL and 2.7 ± 0.9 lg/mL, respectively.

1. Introduction
Over the last two decades, deep-sea-derived fungi are considered to be a new source
of pharmacologically active secondary metabolites of drug discovery and up to now
over
200
new
metabolites
have
been
identified
from
deep-sea-derived
fungi
(Fredimoses et al. 2014; Guo et al. 2015; Chen et al. 2016; Daletos et al. 2018). The
genus Penicillium is one of the largest fungal genera with ubiquitous occurrence,
including marine habitats (Youssef et al. 2013; Perrone and Susca 2017). Chemical
investigations on Penicillium sp. manifested the existence of structural diversity of
metabolites consisting of polyketides (Samanthi et al. 2015), peptides (Luz et al. 2017),
alkaloids (Yang et al. 2018), etc and these isolations have attracted a great deal of
attentions because of their wide range of biological activities, including antimicrobial
(Wang et al. 2016), antiviral (Shope 1953), antitumour (Jouda et al. 2016) and cytotoxic
activities (Youssef and Alahdal 2018). In our current research for novel bioactive
compounds from deep-sea-derived fungi, the fungal strain Penicillium sp. SCSIO 06720
isolated from the deep-sea sediment collected from the Indian Ocean drew our
attention. Chemical research on the fermentation products of the strain resulted in
obtaining two new compounds (1 and 2) (Figure 1). Herein we reported the isolation,
structure elucidation and biological activities of these compounds.
2. Results and discussion
Compound 1 was obtained as yellow powder. Its molecular formula C11H16O3 (four
unsaturations) was determined via HRESIMS at m/z 197.1174 [M þ H]þ and 219.0995
[M þ Na]þ. The 1H NMR data (Table S1) displayed three methyl protons (dH 0.99, 1.94
and 2.00); two methylene protons (dH 2.36, 2.64); two methine protons (dH 3.69, 6.15);
three hydroxyl protons (dH 8.67, 7.79 and 4.49). The 13C NMR data (Table S1) exhibited
11 resonances for three methyl carbons (dC 23.0, 11.9 and 9.2); one methylene carbon
(dC 43.5); two methine carbons (dC 66.8, 108.7), including one aromatic methine and
one oxygenated; and four quaternary carbons (dC 153.5, 152.9, 113.6 and 108.7), two
of which was oxygenated carbons (dC 153.5, 152.9) adjacent to hydroxyl. The chemical
shifts of 1 suggested the present of a benzene ring. Based on the above findings, the
structure of 1 was found to be similar to the reported 3-(3-hydroxy-5-methoxy-2-meth-
ylphenyl) propan-2-ol (He et al. 2004), and the difference between them was the sub-
stituents on the benzene ring at C-2 and C-3, which the hydrogen and methoxy
groups were replaced by methyl and hydroxyl groups in 1, respectively. Additionally,
the methyl group at dH 1.94 showed HMBC correlations (Figure S2) to C-1/2/3 and the
hydroxyl group at dH 8.67 displayed HMBC correlations to C-2/3/4, implying that 11-
CH3 was attached to C-2; 3-OH was located on C-3. Hence, the planer structure of
compound 1 was determined as 5-[2-hydroxypropane-1-yl]-2,6-dimethlbenzene-1,3-
diol. The small specific optical rotation of 1 suggested it was a racemic mixture.

3. Experimental
3.1 General experimental procedures
NMR spectra were recorded on a Bruker AC 500 and 700 NMR (Broker, Fallanden,
Switzerland) spectrometer with TMS as internal standard. High resolution mass spectra
(HR-ESI-MS) were measured on a Bruker micro TOF-QII mass spectrometer (Bruker).
Optical rotations were tested using Anton Par MCP-500 polarimeter (Hertford, UK).
ECD spectra were recorded with a Chirascan circular dichroism spectrometer (Applied
Photophysics, Surrey, UK). UV spectra were recorded on a Shimadzu UV-2600 PC spec-
trometer. TLC and column chromatography (CC) were performed on plates precoated
with silica gel GF254 (10  40 lm) and over silica gel (200  300 mesh) (Qingdao
Marine Chemical Factory) and Sephadex LH-20 (Amersham Biosciences), respectively.
Spots were detected on TLC (Qingdao Marine Chemical Factory) under 254 nm UV
light. All solvents employed were of analytical grade (Tianjin Fuyu Chemical and
Industry Factory). Semi-preparative HPLC was carried out using an ODS column (YMC-

3.3. Fermentation, extraction and isolation
Penicillium sp. SCSIO 06720 stored on MB ager slants at 4 C was cultured on MB ager
plants and incubated at 25 C for 7 days. Seed medium (malt extract 15 g, sea salt
10 g, distilled water 1000 mL) inoculated with Penicillium sp. SCSIO 06720 and incu-
bated at 25 C for 48 h on a rotating shaker (180 rpm, 25 C). Large-scale fermentation
in a solid rice medium of 1000 mL flasks (rice 200 g, sea salt 2.5 g, distilled water
200 mL) (n ¼ 48) was inoculated with 10 mL of seed solution. Flasks were incubated at
25 C under static condition and fermented for 30 days.
Strain cultures were harvested after 30 days. Then fungal mycelium were cut into
small pieces and immersed into acetone for 2 days, sonicated for 10 min and filtered
using gauze, yielding the rice solid medium and water phases, which were extracted
with EtOAc (61 L and 610 L, respectively). Both organic extracts were combined to
gain 164 g of crude extract. The crude EtOAc extract was subjected to silica gel col-
umn chromatography and eluted with petroleum ether/CH2Cl2 in gradient eluent
(100:0-0:100), and followed by CH2Cl2/MeOH in gradient eluent (99:1-0:100), obtaining
eight fractions (fractions 1-8). Fraction 3 was applied to C-18 reversed-phase column
(H2O/MeOH, 95:5-0:100), and gained seven subfractions (F3-1-F3-7). F3-1 was further puri-
fied by semi-preparative reversed-phase HPLC (28% MeCN/H2O, 2 mL/min) to give 15
(4.4 mg, tR 13 min). F3-2 was applied to Sephadex LH-20 (MeOH) to give two fractions
(F3-2-1 and F3-2-2). F3-2-1 was purified by semi-preparative reversed-phase HPLC (42%
MeCN/H2O, 2 mL/min) to give 9 (1.6 mg, tR 12 min), 13 (7.1 mg, tR 37 min), 14 (4.0 mg,
tR 34 min), 19 (1.9 mg, tR 24 min) and F3-2-2 was purified by semi-preparative reversed-
phase HPLC(30% MeCN/H2O, 2 mL/min) to give 2 (1.1 mg, tR 10 min), 3 (6.4 mg, tR

4. Conclusion
Two new aromatic polyketides, 5-[2-hydroxypropane-1-yl]-2,6-dimethlbenzene-1,3-diol
(1) and coniochaetone L (2), together with 19 known compounds (3–21), were
isolated from a deep-sea fungus, Penicillium sp. SCSIO 06720. Their structures
and absolute configurations were elucidated by detailed spectroscopic analyses,
chiral-phase HPLC analysis, and electronic circular dichroism spectra. All the isolated
