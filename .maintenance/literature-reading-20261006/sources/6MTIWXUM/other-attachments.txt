Experimental Procedures 
General materials and methods 
Emodin, chrysophanol, and physcion were purchased from Shanghai Yuanye Bio-Technology Co., Ltd (Shanghai, China), 
catenarin from Carbosynth Co., Ltd. (Berkshire, UK), emodin anthrone from Yuanshu Pharmaceutical  Technology Co., Ltd 
(Nanjing, China), citreoresein from ChemFaces Biochemical Co.,Ltd. (Wuhan, China), aloe emodin from Sangon Biotech 
Co., Ltd (Shanghai, China), and alaternin from BioBioPha Co., Ltd. (Kunming, China). Skyrin (1), rugulosin A (2), and 
endocrocin were isolated in our laboratory. Primer synthesis and DNA sequencing were performed at TsingKe Biotech Co., 
Ltd. (Nanjing, China). All the primers used in this study are listed in Table S3. Optical rotation was determined in MeOH on 
a Rudolph Research Analytical Autopol IV automatic polarimeter. High resolution LC-MS data were measured on an 
Agilent 6546 TOF LC-MS spectrometer with a ZORBAX RRHD SB-C18 column (2.1×100 mm, 1.8 μm, Agilent 
Techonologies). With TMS and solvent signals used as internal standards, all 1D and 2D NMR experiments were run on a 
Bruker AV-500 spectrometer (500 MHz for 1H and 125 MHz for 13C nuclei). Semipreparative RP-HPLC (reverse phase-high 
performance liquid chromatography) were accomplished on Agilent Technologies 1260 Infinity LC System by an Agilent 
Eclipse XDB-C18 column (5 μm, 9.4  250 mm). HPLC analysis was carried out on an Agilent 1260 HPLC system with a 
DAD detector equipped with a  Proshell 120 EC-C18 (150 × 4.6 mm, 4µm, Agilent Techonologies). 
 
Strains and culture conditions 
Talaromyces sp. YE3016 and its mutants were maintained on PDA (potato dextrose broth containing 2% agar) at 28 °C 
and stored as 15% glycerol stocks at 80 °C. For the isolation of polyketides like anthraquinones, the fresh mycelium of 
Talaromyces sp. YE3016 was inoculated into 400 mL potato dextrose broth medium in 1000 mL flasks. After a 3-day 
incubation at 28 °C at 180 rpm, a 20 ml culture liquid was transferred as seed into 200 g sterilized solid fermented rice 
culture in 1000 mL flasks for the ensuing 7-day fermentation at room temperature. Aspergillus oryzae NSAR1 (niaD−, sC−, 
∆argB, and adeA−) was used as the host for heterologous expressions. The A. oryzae transformants were cultivated in 400 
mL DPY medium (20 g/L dextrin, 10 g/L polypeptone, 5 g/L yeast extract, 5 g/L KH2PO4 and 0.5 g/L MgSO4•7H2O) in 
1000 mL baffled flasks for 2 days at 28 °C and 140 rpm. The fresh cells were inoculated (1:10) into 400 mL CDM medium 
(3 g/L NaNO3, 1 g/L K2HPO4•3H2O, 1 g/L yeast extract, 0.5 g/L MgSO4•7H2O, 0.01 g/L FeSO4•7H2O, 0.55 g/L CaCl2, 2 
g/L starch, 1 g/L polypeptone, and 20 g/L maltose) in 1000 mL flasks, and shaken at 180 rpm for the ensuing 4 days at 28 °C. 
Saccharomyces cerevisiae strain BJ5464-NpgA (MATα ura3-52 his3-∆200 leu2-∆1 pep4::HIS3prb1 ∆1.6R can1 GAL) was 
used for biotransformation test. Saccharomyces cerevisiae RC01, which contains a chromosome-integrated gene encoding a 
cytochrome P450 reductase (CPR) from Aspergillus terreus, was used for P450 gene overexpression.1 Yeast strains were 
cultured in synthetic drop-out media for selection or YPD (1% yeast extract, 2% peptone, 2% dextrose) media for 
biotransformations at 30°C. E. coli DH5α was used as a host for molecular cloning, and the E. coli cells carrying certain 
plasmid were grown at 37 °C and 200 rpm in Luria-Bertani (LB) medium with appropriate antibiotics added. 
 
General molecular biology methods. 
Talaromyces sp. YE3016 was cultivated in PDB medium at 28 °C for 3 days to extract genomic DNA. The mycelia were 
collected by centrifugation and dried out with filter paper. Approximately 0.2 g of mycelia was flash-frozen in liquid 
nitrogen and grinded using a tissue grinder. The subsequent operations were carried out according to the manufacturer’s 
instructions of Rapid Fungi Genomic DNA Isolation Kit (Sangon Biotech). The concentration of genomic DNA was 
determined by Thermo Fisher Scientific U-3000 NanaDrop 2000C. Talaromyces sp. YE3016 was cultivated in solid rice 
medium at 28°C for 5 days to extract total RNA. The mycelia was scraped off from the surface of the solid culture and 
crushed using a mortar and pestle. The subsequent operations were carried out in accordance with the manufacturer’s 
instructions of TRLZOL® Reagent (Vazyme Biotech). The concentration of total RNA was determined by Thermo Fisher 
Scientific U-3000 NanaDrop 2000C. Residual genomic DNA in the extracts was digested by RNase-free DNase I (Thermo 
Fisher Scientific) at 37°C for 4 h. RevertAid First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific) was used for 
cDNA synthesis with Oligo-dT primers following the instruction from the user manual. 
 
Whole-genome sequencing and bioinformatic analysis. 
The whole-genome sequencing of Talaromyces sp. YE3016 was performed by Benagen Tech Solutions Company 
Limited (Wuhan, China) with both Illumina NovaSeq and Oxford Nanopore PromethION sequencing platform. The NECAT 
(https://github.com/xiaochuanle/NECAT) sequence assembly system was used to produce 9 contigs, covering about 37.8 Mb. 
The function of coding sequence was predicted by AUGUSTUS (http://bioinf.uni-greifswald.de/webaugustus/). The natural 
product biosynthetic gene clusters in the Talaromyces sp. YE3016 genome was analyzed by antiSMASH fungal version 
(https://fungismash.secondarymetabolites.org).2 The function of proteins in the rug gene cluster was further analyzed by 
2ndFind (http://biosyn.nih.go.jp/2ndfind/) and BlastP (https://blast.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Blast.cgi). The multiple sequence 
alignments were performed by DNAMAN.  
 
Phylogenetic analysis of RugH. 
Fifty-two members of the aldo-keto reductase superfamily, with at least one member in each subfamily, were selected 
from the Aldo-Keto Reductase Superfamily database (https://hosting.med.upenn.edu/akr/). Their sequences were obtained 
from the NCBI database. Sequence alignments were carried out using ClustalX, and the evolutionary tree was obtained by 
the maximum likelihood method based on the JTT matrix-based model using MEGA 6.0 software.3

Gene deletion in Talaromyces sp. YE3016. 
Split-marker strategy based on homologous recombination was used to knock out the target gene.4 Briefly (Figure S3), the 
whole gene-deletion cassette contained the 5’ flanking sequence of target gene, hygromycin-resistance marker hph, and the 3’ 
flanking sequences of target gene. It was divided into two fragments, named A (5’ flanking sequence of target gene and 
forward half of hph) and B (backward half of hph and 3’ flanking sequences of target gene). Recombinant plasmid 
containing fragment A or B was generated by homologous recombination using a ClonExpress MultiS One Step Cloning Kit 
(Vazyme Biotech). About 1.8 kb of 5’ and 3’ flanking sequences of target gene were PCR amplified from the genomic DNA 
of Talaromyces sp. YE3016. For splitting a resistance marker, a plasmid pSH75 containing the PtrpC promoter, hph gene, and 
TtrpC terminator was used as a template for amplification two pieces of DNA named hph1 and hph2. The KpnI-linearized 
vector pTAex3 was used to carry the fragment A or B. The constructed recombinant plasmid containing fragment A or B 
was used as template to amplify A or B in large quantities. The mycelia from Talaromyces sp. YE3016 cultured in PDA for 
36 h were collected and inoculated to YPED (20 g/L sucrose, 10 g/L yeast extract, 10 g/L polypeptone and 10 g/L casein 
hydrolyzate) for 18 h at 28 °C and 180 rpm for germination. The harvested hyphae were digested with 20 mg/mL lysing 
enzyme (Sigma-Aldrich, L1412), 5 mg/mL yatalase (Takara, T017), and 2 mg/mL lysoenzyme from chicken egg (Sigma-
Aldrich, L6876) for 15 min at 28 °C to produce the protoplasts. The protoplasts were collected by centrifugation at 4,000 
rpm for 15 min, washed with MN buffer (0.3M MgSO4•7H2O and 0.3M NaCl), and then treated with STC buffer (1.2M 
sorbitol, 50mM CaCl2, and 10mM Tris-HCl, pH 7.5). The treated protoplasts were transformed with the gene-deletion 
cassette fragments A and B using polyethylene glycol (PEG)-mediated transformation strategy. After transformation, the 
protoplasts were poured onto the PDA plate supplemented with 100 μg/mL of hygromycin B as a selective agent. The 
mutant strains were identified by diagnostic PCR using primer pairs diagnostic F/R (Diag F/R) listed in Table S3. 
 
Construction of fungal expression system in A. oryzae NSAR1. 
For the construction of plasmids for heterologous expression in A. oryzae, each gene used in this study was amplified 
from the genomic DNA of Talaromyces sp. YE3016, using the primers listed in Table S3. Each gene was initially ligated 
into the KpnI-digested pTAex3 vector using ClonExpress Ultra One Step Cloning Kit (Vazyme Biotech) according to the 
manufacturer’s protocol. To construct the coexpression plasmid of rugA and rugB, DNA fragment containing rugA-TamyB 
(amyB terminator) and PamyB (amyB promoter)-rugB were amplified from the pTAex3-based plasmids and ligated into the 
KpnI-digested pUSA vector using ClonExpress MultiS One Step Cloning Kit, to produce pUSA-rugAB (Table S4). To 
construct the coexpression plasmid of rugE and rugF, DNA fragment containing rugF-TamyB and PamyB-rugE were amplified 
from the pTAex3-based plasmids and ligated into the KpnI-digested pTAex3 vector using ClonExpress MultiS One Step 
Cloning Kit, to produce pTAex3-rugEF (Table S4). For the introduction of rugG or rugH into pAdeA, a fragment containing 
the PamyB and TamyB was amplified from the pTAex3-based plasmids and ligated into XbaI-digested pAdeA vector, to produce 
pAdeA-rugG and pAdeA-rugH (Table S4), respectively. To construct the coexpression plasmid of rugG and rugH, DNA 
fragments containing PamyB-rugG-TamyB and PamyB-rugH-TamyB were amplified from the pTAex3-based plasmids and ligated 
into the XbaI-digested pAdeA vector using ClonExpress MultiS One Step Cloning Kit, to produce pAdeA-rugGH (Table S4), 
or DNA fragments containing rugH-TamyB and PamyB-rugG were amplified from the pTAex3-based plasmids and ligated into 
the KpnI-digested pUSA vector, to produce pUSA-rugGH (Table S4). The expression plasmids were transformed into A. 
oryzae NSAR1 (AO) by the PEG-mediated transformation, and the positive transformants were verified by PCR and 
fermented in CDM for 3 days. 
 
Site-directed mutagenesis of RugH. 
For constructing RugH mutants with single point mutation (D52A, Y57A, K81A, and H117A), pUSA-rugGH was used as 
the template, and site-directed mutagenesis PCR was performed to generate the coexpression plasmids of mutant rugH and 
wild-type rugG. The mutant plasmids were transformed into A. oryzae NSAR1 by the PEG-mediated transformation. The 
activity analysis of mutants were performed by feeding of emodin into the cultures of the A. oryzae transformants and 
cultured for the ensuing 4 days at 28 °C and 180 rpm, followed by extraction with ethyl acetate for LC-MS analysis. 
 
Feeding experiments using the AO-rugG, AO-rugH, and AO-rugGH strains 
For feeding experiments in A. oryzae, emodin, skyrin or rugulosin A (1 mg each) were separately added to 100 mL of 
CDM medium, 2 days after the cultivation of the AO-rugG, AO-rugH, and AO-rugGH transformants. After further 
incubation at 28 °C for 2 days, the fermentation broth was extracted with ethyl acetate twice and concentrated in vacuo. The 
dried extracts were dissolved in methanol for the HPLC analysis. 
 
Heterologus biotransformation in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. 
For the expression in S. cerevisiae, the intron-free rugG and rugH were amplified from the cDNA which was synthesized 
by RT-PCR, and then combined with the NdeI/PmeI-linearized vector pYET (containing TRP1 marker) flanking with PADH2 
promoter and TADH2 terminator (alcohol dehydrogenase, ADH) to result in the expression plasmids pYET-rugG and pYET-
rugH. Considering that the PADH2 promoter and TADH2 terminator are not present in vector pRS426 (containing URA3 
marker), the ADH2p-rugH-ADH2t cassette was amplified from the pYET-rugH using the primers IF-pRS426-rugH F/R 
listed in Table S3, and then ligated into KpnI/SacI-digested vector pRS426 to generate pRS426-rugH. The plasmid pYET-
rugG and pYET- rugH were electrically transformed into S. cerevisiae RC01 and BJ5464-NpgA, respectively, and screened 
on synthetic dropout complete medium (SDCM) without tryptophan to generate S. cerevisiae RC01/pYET-rugG and 
BJ5464-NpgA/pYET-rugH strain. Similarly, the plasmid pRS426-rugH was electrically transformed into S. cerevisiae

RC01/pYET-rugG and screened on SDCM without tryptophan and uracil to generate the strain S. cerevisiae RC01/ pYET-

Evaluation for the RugG substrate specificity. 
The S. cerevisiae RC01 containing RugG was streaked on solid uracil drop-out media for 2-3 days, and single colonies 
were inoculated into 5 mL tryptophan drop-out media, grown for 24 h at 30 °C, and then transferred into 20 mL of YPD for a 
24-hour culture before each of emodin analogs was added as the substrate candidate at 33 μM. To test for heterodimer 
generations, the substrate candidates were pairwisely added with each at 33 μM. After addition of the substrate candidates, 
the culture was grown for 24 h, followed by extracting the supernatant with ethyl acetate and the mycelium with methanol 
under sonication for 30 min. The extracts were dried under reduced pressure and dissolved in methanol for LC-UV-MS 
analysis. The reaction yield of 1 was estimated through LC-MS analysis according to the EIC (extracted ion chromatogram) 
peak integration. For the homo- or heterodimers without authentic samples in hand, such quantifications were accomplished 
by correlating to the standard curve of 1 while keeping it in mind that the maximal (100%) yield corresponded to 16.5 μM of 
the dimers generated from the 33 μM of input substrates. 
 
Protein expression and purification of RugH. 
The RugH encoding gene (rugH) was amplified from the cDNA of Talaromyces sp. YE3016 by PCR with  the  primers 
IF-pET22b-rugH-F/IF-pET22b-rugH-R. The gene was ligated with pET22b(+) linearized by NdeI and XhoI through 
homologous recombination by ClonExpress Ultra One Step Cloning Kit. After sequenced to confirm its identity, the 
resulting plasmid was transformed into E. coli BL21 (DE3). The obtained transformant was cultured for 12 h at 37 °C and 
220 rpm in 5 mL of the LB medium supplemented with 100 μg/mL ampicillin. This culture was inoculated to 400 mL of the 
LB medium followed by incubation at 37 °C until the optical density at 600 nm increased to 0.6~0.8. To the culture, 400 L 
of 0.6 M isopropylthio-β-D-galactoside (IPTG) were added. After further incubation for 18 h at 16 °C and 220 rmp, the cells 
were collected by centrifugation at 8000 rpm for 10 min, resuspended in 20 mL of lysing buffer (200 mM NaCl, 5 mM 
imidazole, 10% glycerol, 100 mM Tris-HCl, pH 8.0), and disrupted by sonication on ice for 30 min. The lysate was 
centrifuged at 15000 rpm for 1 h. The supernatant was filtered by 0.45 μm filtrator (Merck millipore), and the enzyme was 
purified by the Ni2+-chelating affinity chromatography to >95% purity (SDS-PAGE analysis). The protein concentration was 
determined by Thermo Fisher Scientific U-3000 NanaDrop 2000C. Finally, the RugH protein was flash frozen in liquid 
nitrogen and stored at ‒80 °C until being used for in vitro assays. 
 
In vitro enzymatic assay of RugH. 
The enzymatic reaction of RugH with each of emodin (5), skyrin (1), and rugulosin A (2) was performed in reaction 
mixtures containing 50 mM phosphate buffer (pH 7.0), 2 mM NADPH or NADP, 0.2 mM of substrate, and 10 μM RugH, in 
a final volume of 100 μL. After incubation at 30 °C for 3 h, the reaction was terminated by adding 50 μL of methanol and 
mixed by vortex. After centrifugation, the supernatant was analyzed by HPLC for the product that might form. 
 
Isolation of skyrin (1) and rugulosin A (2) from Talaromyces sp. YE3016. 
The extract obtained from the fungal rice culture (2 kg) was fractionated using microporous resin eluted with the EtOH-
H2O mixtures (v/v, 1:100→ 100:1). Four fractions (F1-F4) were collected based on the ratio of eluent (MeOH/H2O, 30, 50, 
80, 100%). Fraction F-4 (3.2 g) was separated by silica gel column eluted by CH2Cl2-MeOH mixtures (v/v, 100:1→ 1:1) to 
give four subfractions (F-4a~F-4d). F-4a was separated by RP-HPLC purification to afford 1 (3.8 mg). F-4b was separated 
by RP-HPLC to give 2 (1.8 mg). 
 
Fractionation of metabolites from the A. oryzae transformant cultures. 
For small scale A. oryzae transformant product analysis, the transformants were grown in liquid CDM (20 mL × 3) for 4 
days under continuous shaking at 250 rpm and extracted with ethyl acetate. The organic phase was dried under reduced 
pressure and dissolved in methanol for HPLC or LC-MS analysis. For large scale isolation of compounds, A. oryzae 
transformants were grown for 3-4 days in 4 L liquid CDM and then filtered to separate the culture liquid. The culture was 
extracted with ethyl acetate three times and the extracts were dried under reduced pressure by Buchi Rotavapor. The extracts 
were then separated by column chromatography over silica gel eluted by CH2Cl2/MeOH mixtures (v/v, 100:1→ 1:1). After 
this, the fractions containing the target compounds were further fractionated by an ODS-A gel column with MeOH 
(20%→100%). Fractions containing target compounds were then combined and used for further purification by RP-HPLC 
purification. For elucidation of chemical structures, 1D and 2D NMR spectra were obtained on a Bruker AV-500 NMR 
spectrometer.
