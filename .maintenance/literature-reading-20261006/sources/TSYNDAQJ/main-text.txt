ABSTRACT: Crosstalk-oriented chemical evolution of natural
products (NPs) is an efficacious strategy for generating novel
skeletons through coupling reactions between NP fragments. In
this study, two NOD-like receptor protein 3 (NLRP3)
inflammasome inhibitors, sorbremnoids A and B (1 and 2), with
unprecedented chemical architectures were identified from a
fungus Penicillium citrinum. Compounds 1 and 2 exemplify rare
instances of hybrid NPs formed via a major facilitator superfamily
(MFS)-like enzyme by coupling reactive intermediates from two
separate biosynthetic gene clusters (BGCs), pcisor and pci56. Both
sorbremnoids A and B are NLRP3 inflammasome inhibitors.
Sorbremnoid A demonstrated strong inhibition of IL-1β by
directly binding to the NLRP3 protein, inhibiting the assembly
and activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome in vitro, with potential application in diabetic refractory wound healing through the
suppression of excessive inflammatory responses. This research will inspire the development of anti-NLRP3 inflammasome agents as
lead treatments and enhance knowledge pertaining to NPs derived from biosynthetic crosstalk.
■INTRODUCTION
Chronic inflammation has posed a formidable public health
challenge.1 The NOD-like receptor protein 3 (NLRP3)
inflammasome has emerged as a pivotal mediator of
pathological inflammation across various diseases, rendering
it a promising target for pharmacological intervention.2 NLRP3
triggers an inflammatory response and subsequently promotes
fibrosis through pathogen- or danger-associated molecular
patterns in neurodegenerative disorders, cancer, diabetes,
cardiovascular diseases, etc.3−5 Inhibition of the NLRP3
inflammasome promises broad therapeutic potential for
conditions including Parkinson’s disease, Alzheimer’s disease,
diabetic wound healing, etc.4,6 Despite advances,4 the develop-
ment of effective inhibitor MCC950 has been hindered by its
liver toxicity, underscoring the need for safer alternatives.7
Fungi are an important source of bioactive metabolites,8,9
such as lovastatin, mycophenolic acid, and pyripyropene
A.10−13 Research on fungal biosynthesis has significantly
facilitated the discovery of novel natural products (NPs).
Unlike the conventional synthesis by a single biosynthetic gene
cluster (BGC),9 hybrid NPs (hbNPs) are constructed via a
heterocoupling of structurally diverse precursors from separate
BGCs,14 enhancing chemical diversity and expanding their
potential as drug leads (Figure S1A). Such functional crosstalk
between BGCs involves both enzymatic and spontaneous
nonenzymatic reactions, such as those leading to azasperpyr-
anone A and penilactone B, which has proven crucial for
generating compounds with unique biological activities
(Figures 1A and S1B for other examples).14−16 Here, we
activated the silent BGCs in a halophyte Suaeda salsa-derived
Penicillium citrinum HDN11−186, which led to the discovery
of three novel hbNPs with unique skeletons formed through
crosstalk of highly reactive blocks from separate BGCs:
sorbremnoid A (1), which directly binds to the NLRP3
protein, sorbremnoid B (2) and sorbtalone (3) (Figures 1B
and 2A). The pseudonatural products’ (PNPs’) strategy, a
human-driven evolution of NPs, has provided chemically
diverse classes enabling the exploration of biologically relevant
chemical space through the fusion of NP fragments in various
combinations and arrangements.17,18 Biology-oriented syn-
thesis and chemoinformatic tools have guided the establish-
ment of PNP libraries that cannot be accessed in nature.13,19 In
this study, the discovery of compounds 1, 2, and 3 proved that

does not occur naturally. This human intervention-derived

Compounds 1−3 are all generated via Diels−Alder (DA)
[4π + 2π] cycloaddition, a prominent mechanism in both
synthetic and biosynthetic reactions.20,21 Although extensive
research has identified several intramolecular Diels−Alderases
(DAases), like PyrE3 (Figure S1C),22,23 those catalyzing
intermolecular cycloadditions remain limited. In neosetopho-
mone B biosynthesis, EupfF catalyzes the coupling of two
distinct skeletons produced by a single BGC (Figure S1D).24
However, DAases effectively catalyzing cycloadditions between
compounds originating from separate BGCs are rare. Notably,
the enzymatic synthesis of compound 1 exemplifies this
paradigm which was via a major facilitator superfamily (MFS)-
like enzyme. Further bioactivity evaluation showed that the
noncytotoxic compounds 1 and 2 significantly inhibited
NLRP3 inflammasome activation in vitro by reducing the
level of inflammatory cytokine IL-1β with IC50 values of 2.11
and 2.40 μM. Compound 1 also significantly improved the
healing process of refractory wounds in an in vivo diabetic
mouse model.

To activate SMs systematically, we modified the network

Structure Elucidation of Novel Structures 1−3.
Compound 1 (Table S4), with the molecular formula
C29H28O10 with m/z 535.1597 [M −H]−in negative-ion
mode HRESIMS and requiring 16 degrees of unsaturation
(Figure S8), was isolated from strain B1. The 1H and 13C
NMR spectra of 1 (Table S4) revealed five methyls, six sp2
hybridized methines, three sp3 hybridized methines, and 15
quaternary carbons (Figures S9 and S10). The degrees of
unsaturation required the presence of five rings. Detailed 2D-
NMR analysis confirmed the entire planar structure of 1,44
including the connection of highly substituted cyclopentene,
[2,2,2]-octanone, and meta-disubstituted benzo-γ-pyrone
moieties (Figures S11−S13 and Table S4).
The relative configuration of the chiral centers was defined
by NOE correlation analysis and molecular modeling (Tables
S14−16). The correlations of H-10/H-12 and H-11/H-13
identified the E-configuration for both the Δ10 and Δ12 double
bonds. The NOESY correlation of H-4/H-10 indicated the Z-
configuration of Δ3(9) and that H-4 is cooriented with the
sorbyl chain. Additionally, the correlation of H3-27/H-10 in
the NOE difference spectra indicated that CH3-27 points over
C-3. Thus, the relative configurations of C-4 and C-5 were
determined as 4S*,5S*, with H-4 and CH3-36 possessing
equatorial orientations on the [2,2,2]-octanone core.43
Molecular modeling further revealed that the relative
configuration of C-1 is 1R* and the cyclopentane can only
exist if H-7 and H-8 are in a cis arrangement. With this
information, there are four possible diastereomers 1−1, 1−2,
1−3, and 1−4 (Figures 2B). The NOESY correlation between
singlet H3-29 and H-13, along with a weak NOE correlation of
H-8/H3-26, suggested the relative configurations of C-7, C-8,
and C-15 as 7S*, 8S*, and 15R*, respectively. Consequently,
the total relative configuration of compound 1 was assigned as
(1R*, 4S*, 5S*, 7S*, 8S*, and 15R*). The absolute
configuration of 1 was determined by electronic circular
dichroism (ECD) calculations (Figure S91). The calculated
ECD spectra of (1R, 4S, 5S, 7S, 8S, 15R)-1 closely matched the
experimental spectrum, confirming the absolute configuration
of 1.
Compound 2 displayed a deprotonated molecular ion peak
at m/z 519.1647 [M −H]−suitable for the molecular formula
C29H28O9, and compound 3 (425.1601 [M + H]+ for
C24H24O7) was also isolated (Figures S17−S33, Tables S5
and S6). The NMR spectra of 2 revealed moieties similar to
those in 1, including an enol-sorbyl chain, a [2,2,2]-octanone
moiety, a meta-disubstituted benzo-γ-pyrone fragment, and a
methyl methanoate substituted cyclopentene. However,
compound 2 originated from a DA reaction involving a C-5
dehydroxylated monomer (9a′) in a different orientation from
9a (remisporine A) that leads to 1, resulting in another skeletal
architecture. The HMBC from H-7 to C-28 suggested that C-
15 was linked to C-7. The relative configuration of 2 was
determined from NOE correlations, molecular modeling, and
J-based configurational analysis in a similar manner to 1. The
absolute configuration of 2 was established as (1R, 4S, 5S, 7R,
8R, 15S) by comparing the experimental and calculated ECD
spectra (Figure S92). NMR data indicated that compound 3
shared the same [2,2,2]-octanone and enol-sorbyl fragments as
1 but differed in the structure attached to the [2,2,2]-octanone
core. The formation of compound 3 required a benzo-
cyclohexanone core as the dienophile for the DA reaction
(Figures 1B and 2A), as evidenced by
1H−1H-COSY
correlations of H-8/H-7/H2-15 and HMBCs (H2-15 to C-1/
7/8/16/17, H-21 to C-8/17/19/20). The locations of two
hydroxy groups at C-18 and C-20 were confirmed by the
chemical shifts of C-18/20 (δC 164.8, 166.9) and HMBCs
from H-21 to C-20 and from H-19 to C-18 and C-20. The
configuration of 3 was determined by a combination of
molecular modeling, NMR spectra, DP4+ methodology, and
ECD calculations as (1R, 4S, 5S, 7S, 8R) (Figure S93). The
structure elucidations of pyranonaphtholone-type compound 4
and xanthone-derived compound 5 are detailed in Figures S34
and S41.
The Biogenic Crosstalk Origin of Sorbremnoid A.
Figure S1 displays the NPs formed from the crosstalk of
separate BGCs. Echinocandin B and chrodrimanin I are
synthesized via assembly line insertion of core structural
elements from other BGCs, with identified specific en-
zymes.14,15 The remaining structures illustrate the direct
coupling between blocks derived from the separate BGCs.
Amodesmycin A and azasperpyranone A are generated through
specific enzymes, and penilactone B results from nonenzymatic
spontaneous coupling, whereas enzymatic involvement in
others remains uncertain.14 Retrosynthetic analysis of 1−3
revealed sorbicillinol as the biosynthetic precursor for an
intermolecular DA cycloaddition (Figures 1B and 2C). The
formation of compounds 1−3, particularly through potential
enzymatic heterodimerization, introduced an interesting aspect
to the hbNP generation. BGC pcisor in HDN11−186 shares
conserved genes with the reported BGC pcsor, both involved in
sorbicillin-type structural construction (Figures 1B and S59).
In such a pathway, polyketide synthetases SorA and SorB form
the core of sorbicillin,34 with SorC oxidizing it to sorbicillinol
for further dimerization (Figure S7).45,46

As shown in Figure 2D(iii and iv), knocking out pcisorA and
pcisorC eliminated all peaks of sorbicillin-derived dimers,
including compound 1. The highly reactive precursor of 1,
building block 9a (Figure 2C), presented isolation challenges,
complicating the verification of its existence. Based on the
aromatic ring with ketone modifications, the benzo-γ-pyrone-
cyclopentadienoic acid skeleton of 9a is likely derived from a
nonreducing polyketide synthetase (NR-PKS) pathway.
However, none of the potential initiation and extension units
align with the assembly logic of existing fungal NR-PKS.47
Therefore, a ring contraction rearrangement leading to
compound 9a from the presumed precursor 10 (atrochrysone
carboxylic acid, ACA) is proposed due to their similar
xanthone-like skeletons (Figure 2C(i)). To validate this
hypothesis, a genome-wide search for NR-PKS-like enzymes
yielded three candidate core PKS genes: pci9A, pci33A, and
pci56A. Further in silico analysis uncovered that the production
of 1 was solely abolished upon inactivation of pci56A, situated
in a xanthone-like BGC pci56 (Figure 2D(v)).
We also validated the products of Pci9A, Pci33A, and
Pci56A by heterologous expression in Aspergillus nidulans
A1145 (Figure S60). The precursor YWA1, produced by Pci9A
and involved in the fungal dihydroxynaphthalene (DHN)-
melanin pathway, could undergo biotransformation to scyt-
alone (Figure 2C(ii)). Subsequently, the C3−C4 dehydrated
product, scytalone-DH, would react with sorbicillinol to form
compound 3,48−50 while pci56 encodes required enzymes
necessary for generating xanthone-type structures, the
precursors of 1 and 2.15 These results strongly indicated that
compounds 1−3 are biosynthesized by the crosstalk of totally
independent BGCs, pcisor, pci9, and pci56.
Functional Elucidation of PciSorD. Homologs of
PciSorD, including reported PcSorD and TrSorD, can facilitate
symmetrical DA and MA cyclizations (Figure S7), both of
which can occur spontaneously in solution among homotypic
molecules.38−41,51,52 Nevertheless, employing nonsorbicillin-
type SMs as dienophile monomers remains relatively
uncommon.40 Given the low concentrations of 2 and 3 in
the crude extracts of B1 (Figure S61(i and ii)), our focus was
directed toward the main products 1, 6, 7, and 8 to validate the
function of PciSorD. Subsequently, we knocked out pcisorD in
mutant B1 and analyzed its crude extracts. Contrary to
reported spontaneous reactions, despite our conventional
extraction procedure, the knockout mutant B1ΔPciSorD1
exhibited significant metabolic alterations, resulting in the
complete absence of compounds 1, 6, 7, and 8 in both HPLC
and LC-MS analyses (Figures 3A(ii) and S61(iii to vii)).
However, reintroducing PciSorD failed to restore the
production of such dimers (Figure 3A(iii)). We hypothesized
that transcription factor (TF) PciSorE, located adjacent to
PciSorD, might have been concurrently affected by the
homologous fragments present in the PciSorD-knockout
cassette (Figure S62A). To test this hypothesis, we overex-
pressed PciSorE in B1ΔPciSorD1 (to get mutant B1ΔPci-
SorD2). HPLC and LC-MS analyses showed that compound 6
was completely absent compared to mutant B1, whereas
compounds 1, 7, and 8 persisted (Figures 3A(iv) and
S62B(i)). These experiments indicated that PciSorE functions
to upregulate the expression of pcisor BGC. We also attempted
to substantiate the function of PciSorD in vitro, but all attempts
failed. Fortunately, a feeding assay using the fungus Penicillium
brocae as the heterologous strain successfully confirmed the
function of PciSorD in the formation of 6 (Figure S62B(ii to
iii)).
To delve deeper into the catalytic capabilities of PciSorD, we
submitted it to Alphafold2 modeling with a high Precision
Angle Estimate (PAE) and per-residue Global Distance Test
(pLDDT) value (Figure S63A).53 Subsequently, FAD-added
PciSorD was employed for AutoDock Vina tasks using 6 as the
substrate.54,55 As depicted in Figures 3B and S63B, PciSorD-6
achieved a highly favorable binding energy (−9.6 kcal/mol),
with Asn361 and Glu136 critically stabilizing C1O and C4a−
OH by hydrogen bonding, at interatomic distances of 2.1 and
1.5 Å. These interactions, while maintaining the substrate’s
conformation, direct the MA reaction toward the formation of
6, instead of leading to enol tautomerization to yield alternative
adducts (Figure 3C). To validate these findings, we
constructed and reintroduced plasmids containing PciSorD,

The Metabolic Channel for the Benzo-γ-Pyrone−
Cyclopentadienoic Acid Skeleton. The biosynthesis of
compound 1 reveals the necessity of monomer 9a, but its
presumed instability complicates its isolation from crude
extracts. In analyzing HPLC profiles of the mutants, two
conspicuous peaks associated with the disappearance of
sorbicillinol-derived dimer peaks in B1ΔPciSorA and B1Δ:Pci-
SorD1 were identified as xanthone derivative 8-hydroxy-6-
methyl-9-oxo-9H-xanthene-1-carboxylic acid methyl ester (11)
and remisporine B (12) (Figure 2D(iii) and 3A(iii)). The
oxolane-[5,5,6] skeleton of 12 is believed to be formed via
spontaneous [4π + 2π] cycloaddition and nucleophilic
addition of the chemically activated intermediate 9b, the
isomer of 9a (Figure 4A).57 To verify whether compound 11 is
the direct precursor of 9 with enzymatic ring contraction, we
manipulated BGC pci56. Enzymatic ring contraction in
conjugated benzene systems is rare, exemplified by the
ferredoxin-like protein AlpJ in kinamycin biosynthesis
(Streptomyces ambofaciens) (Figure S65A)58 and the collabo-
ration between a flavin-dependent monooxygenase (FMO)
with a repurposed O-methyltransferase (OMT) in AS2077715
biosynthesis (Talaromyces coalescens) (Figure S65B).59 The
annotation of BGC pci56 revealed five unique genes compared
to its highly homologous agnPKS.60 These five genes encode
two FMOs (Pci56F and Pci56J), a MT (Pc56D), a TF
(Pc56H), and an MFS (Pc56G) (Figure S66).60 Based on the
subsequent phylogenetic analysis, we hypothesized that Pc56F
and Pc56J may play roles in the hydroxylation of the aromatic-
containing 11, inducing rearrangement to form 9 (Figure S67).
After deleting pci56F, the mutant B1ΔPci56F completely
abolished the production of 1 and 12, concurrently leading to
the accumulation of 8 and 11 (Figure 4B(ii)). Additionally,
reintroducing Pci56F restored the production of 1 and 12
(Figure 4B(iii)). Deleting Pci56J halted the production of 12,
significantly reducing the amount of 1 (Figure 4B(iv)). These
findings suggest that Pci56F is essential for the production of
9, while Pci56J might contribute to an alternative pathway with
synergistic effects. However, in vitro experiments involving 11
with Pci56F (purified from BL21) or coexpression of Pci56F
and Pci56J, adding crude extracts from mutants B1ΔPci56F
and B1ΔPci56J, all failed to yield any new compounds (Figure
S68A).
Considering genetic preferences, we readopted P. brocae as
the host for further verification of Pci56F. P. brocae harbors
highly conserved BGC pb36, similar to pci56 but lacking
pci56F and J (Figure S69). P. brocae does not naturally produce
12. After expressing Pci56F in P. brocae and adding excessive
11, a discernible conversion of 11 to 12 occurred (Figure
S68B). Additionally, several compounds (marked with *),
sharing the same molecular weight as 12, were detected in
trace amounts, presumed to be stereoisomers of 12 (see
Figures S70 and S71 for further analysis of the formation of 12
through nonenzymatic self-dimerization using 9b as the
dienophile). This conversion confirmed the in-line pathway
of 11, 9, and 12, establishing that Pci56F is involved in the ring
contraction process of 11 (Figure 4A). Furthermore, the
potential transformation between 9a and 9b may explain the
increased yield of 12 in B1Δ::PciSorD1 (see Figure S72 for
detailed discussion). Molecular docking of Pci56F with 11 and
its demethylated product, 11de (Figure S73A),53−55 suggested
that the methyl ester in 9 acts as a key switch, determining
whether to form 12 directly or to release the carboxyl group of
9de as CO2 during the isoconiochaetone C formation (Figure
S73B).57 Keeping all this in mind, it is surprising that in the
presence of sorbicillinol compound 9 tends to engage in
heterologous intermolecular DA cycloaddition as 9a, rather
than spontaneously dimerizing as 9b. This implies that once
generated, compound 9a needs specific enzymes for stabiliza-
tion, ensuring a preferential reaction with sorbicillinol.

like clade, lacking unique evolutionary characteristics (Figure
S76). Additionally, the significant amino acid sequence and 3D

they may share similar functions (Figure S77). To investigate

Therefore, GFP labeling and confocal microscopy sub-
cellular localization studies were employed to unveil the
original functional sites of PciSorD, Pci56F, and PciSorT
(Figure 6A). PciSorD is a protein covering the outer surface of
the cell membrane, Pci56F is an intracellular dissociative
protein, and PciSorT matures within the intracellular trans-
membrane (Figure 6A). The intracellular SMs of B1 (B1in)
and B1ΔPciSorT (B1ΔTin) displayed dimers 1, 7, and 12
could be formed inside the cell, while 6 and 8 were generated
directly outside (Figures 5B(iii and iv) and S78B). Considering
the low concentrations of 1, 7, and 12 in B1ΔPciSorT, they are
likely partially generated spontaneously within the cell and
then exported along with some other sorbicillin-type by-
products. These distributions suggested that such diverse
dimers were produced at distinct spatial locations.
Most DAases accelerate energetically favorable stereo-
selectivity, as indicated by DFT calculations, where monomers
usually undergo spontaneous DA cycloadditions, resulting in
corresponding diastereomers or racemic mixtures.23 Docking
simulations with PciSorT revealed that the two dominant
conformations of sorbicillinol and 9a exhibited significant
overlap within the pocket of PciSorT, aligning with the
position of TS-1c (binding energy −7.2 kcal/mol) (Figure
S79). In contrast, TS-1a showed unreliable binding in PciSorT
with a binding energy of only −4.5 kcal/mol, supporting TS-1c
as the primary product. Furthermore, the inability to
subculture B1ΔPciSorT and the elevated expression of
PciSorT in mutant B1, relative to other secondary metabolic
genes, highlight its potential physiological importance in the
strain (Figure S80).
Building upon these results, we propose a biosynthetic
pathway involving the crosstalk of two BGCs (Figure 6B). In
this pathway, once generated, a small portion of sorbicillinol
and 9 undergo spontaneous nonenzymatic dimerization, or
using other shunts, resulting in the formation of 1, 7, and 12.
Then, they are exported out of the cell by unknown
transporters to reduce the level of SM accumulation. The
bulk of sorbicillinol and 9 will be rapidly enriched by PciSorT
to generate 1, 7, and 12. This process follows specific spatial
orientations within the cavity of PciSorT, directing the
spontaneous dimerization reactions. Subsequently, either
modified PciSorT or unidentified cell membrane MFS proteins
facilitate the transportation of such monomers and dimers. The
exported sorbicillinol monomer spontaneously dimerizes to
form 8 or is oxidized to oxosorbicillinol by PciSorD,
subsequently undergoing a MA reaction to produce 6. Notably,
the varying functions of SorD and SorT across different species
highlight diverse strategies for producing the same compounds,
shedding light on the convergent and decentralized evolution
of sor-like BGCs.
The Anti-inflammatory Activity of 1−3. Compounds
1−3 exhibited no cytotoxicity against four human cancer cell
lines (K562, MDA-MB-231, ASPC-1, and H69AR) with IC50
values exceeding 30 μM.63 To further explore their bioactivity,
we conducted a multireceptor virtual screening of them,
revealing a strong binding affinity with the NLRP3
inflammasome. Compounds 1−3 were evaluated for their
inhibitory potential on LPS/ATP-induced IL-1β release in
murine macrophage J774A.1 cells following NLRP3 inflam-
masome activation and showed dose-dependent inhibition
with the IC50 values of 2.11, 2.40, and 18.95 μM, respectively
(Figures 7A−C and S81A). With sufficient sorbremnoid A (1)
in hand, 1 was selected for further investigation. Western blot
assays demonstrated that like MCC950 (positive control), 1
effectively suppresses the production of caspase-1 P20 in cells
stimulated with LPS/ATP in a dose-dependent manner
(Figure 7J). Apart from ATP, the NLRP3 inflammasome can
be activated by multiple stimuli like nigericin and MSU.64
Compound 1 demonstrated a dose-dependent inhibitory effect
on nigericin-induced caspase-1 activation and IL-1β secretion
within the range of 6.25−25 μM in LPS-primed J774A.1 cells
(Figure S82), providing additional evidence for its function as
an inhibitor of the NLRP3 inflammasome. These findings
suggest that 1 impedes the assembly and activation of the
NLRP3 inflammasome. Compound 1 exhibited no effect on
TNF-α or IL-6 levels in the range 0.1−25 μM, and it did not
suppress the RNA expression of NLRP3, Pro-IL-1β, IL-6, and
TNF-α in LPS-induced J774A.1 cells compared to the control
group (Figures 7D,E and S81D−G). These results suggested
that compound 1 does not significantly affect the NF-κB
pathway. Furthermore, we assessed the specificity of
compound 1 toward inflammasomes and found that it did
not impact poly(dA:dT)-triggered AIM2 inflammasome
activation or flagellin-induced NLRC4 inflammasome activa-
tion (Figure7 7F−G). A surface plasmon resonance (SPR)
assay revealed that compound 1 can directly bind to the
human full-length NLRP3 protein, with a dissociation constant
KD of 15.57 μM (Figure 7H). We also utilized the human
NLRP3 protein with a key amino acid (Lys232) of the ATP
binding site mutated to identify the binding domain of 1 within
the NLRP3 protein. This result demonstrated that 1 can bind
to the Lys232-mutated NLRP3 protein directly with a KD of
9.75 μM, which is comparable to its affinity for the full-length
NLRP3 protein (Figure 7I). This suggests that the binding site
of compound 1 does not overlap with the ATP binding site on
the NLRP3 protein. Recent studies have revealed the cryo-EM
structure of the NLRP3 protein’s NACHT domain (PDB:
7PZC) bound to the small molecule inhibitor MCC950.65
Consequently, our attention was directed toward this particular
domain. Subsequently, compound 1 was docked into the
NLRP3 NACHT domain using the recently revealed cocrystal
structure with MCC950 (Figure S83). The results showed that
the triene segment of 1 was inserted into a hydrophobic
pocket, leading to hydrophobic interactions. Additionally, the
hydroxyl groups of 1 form stable hydrogen bonds with Glu629
and Asp662 in the NLRP3 protein, resulting in a stable binding
configuration. Collectively, these findings indicated that
compound 1 exhibits potent and selective inhibition of
NLRP3. Consequently, 1 was examined for its ability to effect
the repair of NIH3T3 fibroblast damage induced by HG,
H2O2, and LPS/RAW264.7-CM, which indicated compound 1
had no significant effect on NIH3T3 proliferation under
multicondition-induced injury (Figure S84). Taken together,
these results suggest that compound 1 could inhibit the
production of inflammatory factors and thus reduce the
damage to fibroblasts by inflammatory factors in the coculture
system.

To further evaluate the in vivo potency of 1 in obstructing
NLRP3 inflammasome activation, we established an STZ-
induced diabetic mouse model with full skin wounds. Diabetic
foot ulcers (DFUs) are one of the most common and
challenging chronic complications of diabetes mellitus, and
elevated levels of NLRP3 in diabetic patients lead to chronic
inflammation and oxidative stress, which can impair wound
healing.66,67 As shown in Figures 7K and S85, the hyper-
glycemic state significantly delayed the healing of diabetic
wounds. After topical application of a solution of compound 1
to the wounds of mice, the wound healing rate was significantly
increased and the time required for healing was significantly
shortened, indicating that compound 1 has a superior healing-
promoting activity on diabetic wounds, which may be related
to the inhibitory effect of compound 1 on NLRP3
inflammasome.
■CONCLUSION
In summary, three novel hybrid natural products (hbNPs) with
unprecedented structures, sorbremnoids A and B (1 and 2)
and sorbtalone (3), as well as two additional new structures 4
and 5, were identified from a fungus Penicillium citrinum. The
biosynthetic investigation confirmed compounds 1−3 as cross-
pathway adducts of separate biosynthetic gene clusters
(BGCs), in which 1 was formed through an MFS based on
spatially restricted and regioselective intermolecular Diels−
Alder (DA) dimerization. Some other dimers, such as 6−8,
were generated by spontaneous and enzymatic processes at
different localities within the strain, demonstrating the diverse
metabolic strategies of sorbcillin derivatives. Furthermore,
mechanistic studies indicated that compound 1 directly binds
to the NLRP3 protein, inhibiting the assembly and activation
of the NLRP3 inflammasome, and promotes the healing of
diabetic refractory wounds. Additionally, compound 1
significantly promoted the healing of refractory wounds in a
diabetic mouse model.
In general, the crosstalk-oriented chemical evolution strategy
provides an important concept for efficiently discovering new
drug molecules, where biological sources and enzyme catalysis
serve as vital resources for rational selection and combinatorial
design. The discovery of fungal hbNPs, assembled from
building blocks derived from separate BGCs, will not only
reveal new regulatory and catalytic mechanisms but also hasten
the creation of novel NP molecules with greater complexity,
diversity, and clinical relevance.
