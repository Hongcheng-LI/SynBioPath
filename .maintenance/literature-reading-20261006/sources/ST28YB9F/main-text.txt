Supporting Information
ABSTRACT: Eight new decahydrofluorene-class alkaloids, microascones A and B (1 and 2), 2,3-epoxyphomapyrrolidone C (3),
14,16-epiascomylactam B (4), 24-hydroxyphomapyrrolidone A (5), and microascones C−E (6−8), along with five known analogs
(9−13) were isolated from the marine-derived fungus Microascus sp. SCSIO 41821. Compounds 1 and 2 have an unprecedented
complex macrocyclic alkaloid skeleton with a 6/5/6/5/6/5/13 polycyclic system. Their structures and absolute configurations were
determined by spectroscopic analysis, quantum chemical calculations of ECD spectra, and 13C NMR chemical shifts. Compounds
10−13 showed selective enzyme inhibitory activity against PTPSig, PTP1B, and CDC25B, and 4, 9, and 10 exhibited strong
antibacterial activity against seven tested pathogens. Their structure−bioactivity relationship was discussed, and a plausible
biosynthetic pathway for 1−8 was also proposed.
D
ecahydrofluorene-class alkaloids are a class of complex
12- or 13-membered-ring macrocyclic alkaloids. In total,
about 50 such metabolites have been isolated from fungi. They
possess a tricyclic or tetracyclic decahydrofluorene skeleton,
such as hirsutellones,1 GKK1032s,2 pyrrocidines,3 embelli-
cines,4 phomapyrrolidones,5 and ascomylactams.6 And, some
of them showed significant cytotoxic,4,6 antimicrobial,7
antiproliferative,7 and antitubercular activities.8,9
In our previous study, we obtained seven new decahydro-
fluorene-class alkaloids pyrrospirones K−Q from a marine-
derived fungus Penicillium sp. SCSIO 41512.10 Recently, we
continued to investigate the secondary metabolites of the
fungus Microascus sp. SCSIO 41821 isolated from a gorgonian
collected from the South China Sea, which led to the isolation
of eight new decahydrofluorene-class alkaloids, microascones A
and B (1 and 2), 2,3-epoxyphomapyrrolidone C (3), 14,16-
epiascomylactam B (4), 24-hydroxyphomapyrrolidone A (5),
and microascones C−E (6−8), together with five known
analogs ascomylactams A and B (9 and 10)6 and
phomapyrrolidones A−C (11−13).5 For compounds 1−13,
their antibacterial and enzyme inhibitory activities against
protein tyrosine phosphatase (PTP) were evaluated.
■RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Microascone A (1) was found to have the molecular formula
C34H41NO4 with 15 degrees of unsaturation based on
HRESIMS (m/z 528.3109 [M + H]+) and 13C NMR data.
The
1H NMR spectrum (Table 1) showed four aromatic
hydrogens [δH 7.01 (dd, J = 8.8, 2.0 Hz), 6.94 (dd, J = 8.7, 2.6
Hz), 6.86 (dd, J = 8.1, 2.0 Hz), 6.72 (dd, J = 8.1, 2.6 Hz)], two
olefinic hydrogens [δH 5.38 (s), 5.00 (s)], and five methyls [δH
1.68 (s), 1.65 (s), 1.32 (s), 1.06 (d, J = 6.2 Hz), 0.90 (d, J = 6.6
Hz)]. The 13C NMR and DEPT spectra (Table 2) displayed
the presence of 34 carbons including five methyls, four
methylenes, 16 methines, three aliphatic quaternary carbons
(one oxygenated), four olefinic nonprotonated carbons, one
carboxyl group, and one ketone group. Comparison of these
NMR data with those of 9−135,6 suggested that 1 was also a
decahydrofluorene-type alkaloid.
Detailed analysis of the HSQC, HMBC, and COSY spectral
data (Figure 1) proved that 1 contained the same A/B/C ring
and G/H ring system as that of ascomylactam B (10).6 In

The relative configurations of 12 of the 13 stereocenters in
compound 1 were determined by analysis of the NOESY

Meanwhile, the NOE correlations of H-12β with H-9/H-
10β, H-10β with H3-23, H-13β with H-16, H-16 with H-8′,
and H-9′ with H-3′β revealed these groups to be β oriented.
Thus, the relative configuration of 1 was determined to be
4S*,5R*,8S*,9S*,11R*,13S*,14R*,15R*,16S*,17R*,1′S*,2′R*.
The absolute configuration of 1 was assigned using quantum
chemical calculations of ECD spectra and 13C NMR chemical
shifts. Since the relative configuration of C-19 was still
unknown, the ECD spectra of three different isomers of 1
including (19R,1′S)-1a, (19S,1′R)-1b, and (19S,1′S)-1c were
calculated (Tables S2−S6), while (19R,1′R)-1d was an
unreasonable model in the ECD calculation progress. Three
different functionals including B3LYP, CAM-B3LYP, and
PBE0 were used to calculate the ECD spectra of model-1b
and model-1c (Figure S82). Finally, the calculated ECD
spectrum of model-1c by using the PBE0 functional perfectly
matched the experimental ECD spectrum of 1 (Figure 3),
which

suggestion was further proved by quantum chemical
calculations of the 13C NMR chemical shifts of model-1a,
model-1b, and model-1c (Figures S80 and S81, Table S20). A
plausible biosynthetic pathway for 1−8 was proposed (Scheme
S1).11,12
The molecular formula C34H41NO5 of microascone B (2)
was determined by HRESIMS and NMR data. The 1H and 13C
NMR data of 2 (Tables 1 and 2) exhibited great similarity to
those of 1 with the additional presence of one oxymethylene
(δC 66.4; δH 3.24, d, J = 4.7 Hz) and one hydroxy group (δH
4.40, s) and the absence of one methyl in 2. The COSY
correlations of δH 3.24 with H-11 and δH 4.40, and the HMBC
correlations of δH 3.24 with C-9/C-10/C-11 suggested the
assignment of C-24 (δC 66.4; δH 3.24). Furthermore, the
NOESY spectrum of 2 (Figure S17) showed consistent NOE
correlations with those of 1, which suggested that the relative
configurations of 1 and 2 were the same. Finally, comparison
of the experimental ECD spectra of 1 and 2 (Figure 3)
confirmed that the absolute configuration of 2 was also the
same as that of 1.

■CONCLUSIONS
In summary, HPLC-UV-targeted isolation of the extracts of
solid rice cultures of the marine-derived fungus Microascus sp.
SCSIO 41821 yielded 13 decahydrofluorene-class alkaloids,
including 8 new ones. Compounds 1 and 2 have a novel 13-
membered-ring macrocyclic alkaloid skeleton with an un-
precedented 6/5/6/5/6/5/13 polycyclic system. Previously
reported alkaloids 10−13 displayed selective inhibitory activity
against PTPSig, PTP1B, and/or CDC25B, and 4, 9, and 10
had strong antibacterial activity with a broad spectrum. These
findings greatly expand the chemical variety of decahydro-
fluorene-class alkaloids and offer candidate compounds for
developing antibacterial agents.
■EXPERIMENTAL SECTION
General Experimental Procedures. Procedures are the same as
those described in our previous studies.10 Optical rotations were
measured with a MCP 500 polarimeter (Anton Paar). ECD and UV
spectra were measured with a Chirascan circular dichroism
spectrometer (Applied Photophysics Ltd.). The crystallographic
data were collected on a Rigaku MicroMax 007 diffractometer
equipped with Cu Kα radiation and a graphite monochromator. 1H
NMR, 13C NMR, and 2D NMR spectra were acquired with a Bruker
AVANCE III HD 700 MHz NMR spectrometer (Bruker) with TMS
as a reference. ESIMS and HRESIMS spectroscopic data were
acquired with an amaZon SL ion trap mass spectrometer and a MaXis
quadrupole-time-of-flight mass spectrometer (Bruker), respectively.
Semipreparative reversed-phase (SP-RP) HPLC was performed on a
Shimadzu LC-20A preparative liquid chromatography system with an
YMC-Pack ODS column, 250 × 10 mm, S-5 μm, 12 nm. RP-MPLC
(reversed-phase-medium-pressure preparative liquid chromatography)
was carried out using the CHEETAH MP200 system (Agela
Technologies) and Claricep Flash columns filled with ODS (40−63
μm, YMC). Sephadex LH-20 (GE Healthcare) was used for column
chromatography (CC). Silica gel (200−300 mesh) for CC and GF254
for TLC were obtained from Yantai Jiangyou Silica Gel Development
Co., Ltd.
Fungal Material. The fungus Microascus sp. SCSIO 41821 was
isolated from a gorgonian Melitodes squamata collected from the
South China Sea, Sanya (18°11′N, 109°25′E), Hainan, China, in
August 2010.14 The strain showed 100% similarity to Microascus sp.
SC230909 through comparing the 18S RNA sequence, registering a
Genbank accession number of PP188619, and deposited in the South
China Sea Institute of Oceanology, Chinese Academy of Sciences.
Fermentation and Extraction. The spores of the fungus
Microascus sp. SCSIO 41821 were added to 500 mL Erlenmeyer
flasks each containing 200 mL of sterilized potato dextrose (PD)
medium and fermented for 3 days at 28 °C. Then, 5 mL of the spore
suspension was transferred into each of the 66 1000 mL Erlenmeyer
flasks containing rice medium (80 g of rice, 0.4 g f glucose, 0.4 g of
yeast extract, 3% sea salt, 120 mL of water). Static fermentation was
performed for 28 days at 26 °C. The mycelia was crushed and
extracted with acetone three times and extracted with 1:1
CH2Cl2:MeOH three times; then, two extracts were evaporated
under reduced pressure to afford an aqueous solution. Each aqueous
solution was extracted with EtOAc to obtain two crude extracts (total
117.7 g) and combined for further isolation.
Isolation and Purification. The crude extract (117.7 g) was
chromatographed on a silica gel column by eluting with a stepwise
gradient of CHCl2:MeOH (v/v 100:0, 98:2, 90:10, 80:20, 75:25,
70:30, 50:50, 0:100, using 5 L of each eluent) to obtain seven
fractions (Fr.1−Fr.7) based on TLC and HPLC analysis. Fr.2 was
subjected to a silica gel column, gradually eluting with petroleum:-
EtOAc (v/v 100:0, 100:1, 50:1, 20:1, 10:1, 5:1, 4:1, 7:3, 6:4, 1:1) to
give five subfractions (Fr.2.1−Fr.2.5). Fr.2.3 (330.5 mg) was isolated
by Sephadex LH-20, eluting with MeOH, and further purified by
semipreparative reversed-phase (SP-RP) HPLC, eluting with
CH3CN:H2O:TFA (v/v/v 94:6:0.03), to afford compounds 6 (1.2
mg, tR = 27.8 min), 7 (1.4 mg, tR = 31.0 min), and 11 (37.0 mg, tR =
25.5 min). Fr.2.5 was separated with MPLC using an ODS column,
eluting with CH3CN:H2O (v/v 30:70−100:0), to offer subfractions
(Fr.2.5.1−Fr.2.5.8). Fr.2.5.5 was purified by HPLC, eluting with
CH3CN:H2O (v/v 81:19, 3 mL/min), to give 1 (2.7 mg, tR = 28.0
min). Fr.2.6 (8.32 g) was separated with MPLC using an ODS
column, eluting with MeOH:H2O (v/v 50:50−100:0) to offer 10
subfractions. Fr.2.6.2 was purified by HPLC eluting with
CH3OH:H2O:TFA (v/v/v 76:24:0.01) to give compound 2 (3.7
mg, tR = 24.2 min). Fr.2.6.3 was isolated by Sephadex LH-20, eluting
with MeOH, and further purified by HPLC, eluting with
CH3OH:H2O (v/v 80:20), to give compound 5 (1.5 mg, tR = 25.7
min). Fr.2.6.6 was further purified by HPLC, eluting with
CH3CN:H2O (v/v 67:33, 3 mL/min), to give 3 (4.3 mg, tR = 34.0
min). Fr.2.5.7 was purified by HPLC, eluting with CH3CN:H2O (v/v
81:19, 3 mL/min), to give 4 (10.0 mg, tR = 64.50 min), 8 (2.3 mg, tR
= 30.60 min), 9 (5.0 mg, tR = 21.30 min), 12 (3.5 mg, tR = 35.00
min), and 13 (5.5 mg, tR = 26.10 min).

Computational Methods. Procedures used are the same as those
described in our previous studies.15 Details are listed in the
Supporting Information. The theoretical calculations of the ECD
spectra were conducted in MeOH using the time-dependent DFT
(TDDFT) method at the B3LYP/6-311G(d,p) level for 3, 6, and 8
and at the PBE0/6-311G(d,p) level for 1. The number of excited
states was 80 for 1−3, 6, and 8 The ECD spectra were generated by
the software SpecDis using a Gaussian band shape with a 0.20−0.35
eV exponential half-width from dipole-length dipolar and rotational
strengths. The 13C NMR calculations of 1a, 1b, and 1c were done
with the GIAO method at the PBE0//6-311+G(d,p) level in DMSO
with PCM.
Antibacterial Activity Assay. The antibacterial activities of
compounds 1−13 were tested using the broth microdilution method
in 96-well plates. The indicator bacteria included 11 common
pathogens B. amyloliquefaciens GDMCC 60233, B. subtilis BS01, E. coli
ATCC 25922, S. aureus ATCC 6538, methicillin-resistant S. aureus
SC41993, S. agalactiae ATCC 13813, S. iniae SC41992, P. aeruginosa
ATCC 9027, M. luteus SC41991, and two plant pathogenic bacteria
(Acidovorax citrulli SC42000 and Dickeya chrysanthemi SC41999).
Gentamicin sulfate and vancomycin were used as positive controls.
Briefly, wells containing 100 μL of bacteria dilutions (OD600 = 0.01)
in LB medium were supplemented with different concentrations of
1−13 (1.6, 3.1, 6.3, 12.5, 25, 50, and 100 μg/mL) for primary
screening, 4 and 9−10 (1.6, 0.80, 0.40, 0.20, 0.10, 0.050, and 0.025
μg/mL) for secondary screening, and positive controls gentamicin
sulfate and vancomycin (0.039, 0.080, 0.16, 0.31, 0.63, 1.3, 2.5, 5.0,
10, 20, and 40 μg/mL). The MICs were determined after 12 h
incubation at 37 °C with the tested compounds.
Cytotoxicity Assay. Cytotoxic activity was evaluated using the
human lung adenocarcinoma cell line H1975 and HepG2 by the
CCK-8 method as described previously.16 Details are listed in the
Supporting Information.
Phosphatase Enzyme Inhibition Assays. The same methods as
those described in the previous study15 were applied to test the
inhibition activities of 2, 3, 5, and 10−13 against five kinds of human
protein tyrosine phosphatases (CDC25B, PTP1B, PTPSig, VHR, and
LAR).
