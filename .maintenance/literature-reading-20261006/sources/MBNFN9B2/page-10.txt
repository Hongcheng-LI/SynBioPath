have distinct effects on fungal growth (Fig. 4). Besides, the
polyketides in fungi often function as pigments that protect
fungi against environmental stresses such as irradiation and
oxidation (Durán et al., 2002). Melanins contribute to the
integrity of the fungal cell wall structure and play a role in
protecting fungal cells from environmental damages caused
by UV radiation, temperature and oxidizing agents (Zhong
et al., 2008; Eisenman and Casadevall, 2012). As a type of
pigments, ustilaginoidins and their intermediates likely con-
tribute to U. virens survival in unfavourable environments.
Due to the cytotoxicity of ustilaginoidins (Lu et al., 2014; Lu
et al., 2015), the metabolites may also function as antago-
nists to inhibit the growth of other microorganisms in rice
panicles and facilitate the colonization of U. virens. The pro-
posed physiological functions may eventually contribute to
U. virens virulence.
In conclusion, uncovering the ugs gene cluster and path-
way for ustilaginoidin biosynthesis generally deepens under-
standing secondary metabolism in fungi. The ﬁndings in this
study will facilitate understanding the exact physiological
and virulence functions of ustilaginoidins in U. virens.
Experimental procedures
Fungal strains and culture conditions
The U. virens isolate P1 was used as the wild-type strain in this
study. The U. virens strains were cultured on potato-sucrose
agar (PSA) plates (200 g/l potato, 20 g/l sucrose and 15 g/l
agar). The Agrobacterium tumefaciens strain AGL1 was
grown on LB agar plates at 28C. Unless speciﬁcally noted,
the concentrations of antibiotics used in this study were, rifam-
picin,
25 μg/ml;
kanamycin,
50 μg/ml;
hygromycin
B,
100 μg/ml; Timentin, 400 μg/ml. All primers used in this study
are listed in Supporting Information Table S1.
Plasmid construction
To generate pGKO2-hyg, a approximately 1.4 kb fragment
containing the A. nidulans trpC promoter and hygromycin
B resistance (hph) gene was released from pKOV21 (Zhou
et al., 2018) by Hind III and Pst I digestion and was then
ligated into pGKO2 (Khang et al., 2005). For constructing
the plasmids for gene replacement, 50- and 30-ﬂanking
sequences (~1 kb) of the open reading frames of UvPKS1,
ugsO, ugsJ, ugsR2 and ugsL were separately ampliﬁed from
U. virens genomic DNA. PCR products were digested with
restriction enzymes (Supporting Information Table S1) and
were then inserted into 50- and 30-regions of the hph gene
in the binary vector pGKO2-hyg, respectively. For gene
replacement via the CRISPR/Cas9 system, the target site
and speciﬁc sgRNA for ugsT knockout were designed follow-
ing the procedure on the website http://www.rgenome.net/.
The synthetic sgRNA oligos were annealed and inserted into
the BsmB I-digested pCAS9-tRp-gRNA (Liang et al., 2018).
The upstream and downstream ﬂanking sequences (~1 kb)
of ugsT were ampliﬁed using the primers listed in Supporting
Information Table S1. The resultant PCR products were
fused to the trpC::hph fragment ampliﬁed from pGKO2-hyg
with the primer pair fusion-hph-F/R by fusion PCR. The plas-
mid constructs were all conﬁrmed by sequencing.
U. virens transformation
The pGKO2-hyg constructs were transformed into the
Agrobacterium strain AGL1 using the freeze–thaw method
(Weigel and Glazebrook, 2006) and were then introduced
into U. virens via Agrobacterium-mediated transformation
as described previously (Yu et al., 2015). Brieﬂy, the coni-
dia were harvested from 5 day-old U. virens culture by ﬁl-
tration through three layers of lens wiping paper. The
transformed A. tumefaciens AGL1 was cultured at 28C
for 12 h in LB medium with shaking at 180 r.p.m.
A. tumefaciens cells were washed and diluted with induc-
tion medium (IM) to the cell density of OD600 = 0.15. The
cells were further cultured with shaking to OD600 = 0.25,
and were then mixed with equal volume of P1 conidial sus-
pension (106 spores/ml). The mixture was spread onto co-
cultivation medium (CM) plates with a layer of nitrocellu-
lose ﬁlter and incubated at 28C for 3 days (Mullins et al.,
2001). The transformants on nitrocellulose membranes
were then screened through two rounds of selection, one
on the TB3 plates with hygromycin B and Timentin while
the other on the TB3 plates containing hygromycin B and
50 μg/ml F2dU.
PEG-mediated U. virens transformation was performed
as described previously (Zheng et al., 2016). Brieﬂy, the
collected conidia were inoculated into 50 ml of PS medium
and cultured with shaking at 28C for 18–20 h. The
hyphae were harvested by ﬁltration with three layers of
lens wiping paper, and were then rinsed with 1.2 M KCl.
The hyphae were incubated in Lysis solution (25 g/l Drise-
lase, 10 g/l Lysing enzyme, 0.05 g/l lyticase in 1.2 M KCl)
at 30C for 3 h with shaking at 90 r.p.m. The released pro-
toplasts were collected through ﬁltration followed by centri-
fugation. After washing with STC buffer (200 g/l sucrose,
0.05 M Tris-Cl, pH 8.0, 0.05 M CaCl22H2O), the proto-
plasts were re-suspended in STC to a concentration of
108/ml. The pCAS9-tRp-gRNA construct (5 μg) and fusion
PCR products (5 μg) were incubated with 150 μl of proto-
plasts for 20 min before mixing with 1 ml PTC solution
(40% PEG 8000 in STC). After further incubation for
20 min, 5 ml of TB3 liquid medium (3 g/l yeast extract, 3 g/l
casamino acids, 20% sucrose) was added into the trans-
formation mixture and was then incubated at 28C for 36 h
with shaking at 90 r.p.m. The culture was then mixed with
