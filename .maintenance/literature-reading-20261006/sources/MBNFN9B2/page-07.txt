(Supporting Information Fig. S7). The 13C NMR spectrum
exhibited a total of 15 carbon signals including two methy-
lene carbons (δC 78.1, 46.0), one carbonyl carbon (δC
200.4), 10 sp2-hybridized carbons and two methyl carbons
(δC 18.6, 9.0; Fig. 3C). The 1H NMR spectrum showed the
presence of two methylene protons (δH 4.15 and 2.67),
three aromatic protons (δH 6.19, 6.35 and 6.37) and two
methyl protons (δH 1.23 and 1.48; Fig. 3C). The chemical
shifts are similar to the 1H and 13C chemical shifts of ustilagi-
noidin D (7) (Koyama and Natori, 1988), except that an aro-
matic proton (δH 6.35) was present at C-9 in compound 11.
This was corroborated by analysis of the heteronuclear mul-
tiple bond coherence (HMBC) spectrum, as correlations
were seen between H-7 and C-6/C-8/C-9/C-5a, H-10 and
C-5a, H-11 and C-2/C-3 and also H-12 and C-2/C-3/C-4
(Fig. 3C and D, Supporting Information Fig. S7). The large
coupling constant (J = 13.8 Hz) between the two methine
protons (H-2 and H-3) suggests a trans orientation between
two methyl groups at C2 and C3 in the naphtho-γ-pyrone
ring. Collectively, the compound 11 shares the structure with
the monomer of ustilaginoidin D and was therefore estab-
lished as 3-methyl-dihydro-nor-rubrofusarin. However, we
failed to collect NMR data for the metabolites 9 and 10 due
to low quantity of these compounds. The molecular formula
of the compound 9 is predicted to be C14H12O5, which has
two more hydrogen atoms than nor-rubrofusarin (C14H10O5)
and might be dihydro-nor-rubrofusarin. Since the com-
pounds 10 and 11 had the same molecular mass, they may
be chiral molecules with different absolute conﬁguration. Fur-
thermore, HPLC analysis showed that the complementary
strain of ΔugsL partially rescued the ability to produce the
dimerized products but with little amount of the ustilaginoi-
dins N (5), O (6) and M (8) (Fig. 3A). Collectively, the data
indicate that UgsL functions as a laccase that is responsible
for monomer dimerization during ustilaginoidin biosynthesis
and that 3-methyl-dihydro-nor-rubrofusarin (11) is one of the
monomer precursors of ustilaginoidins (Fig. 5).
Deletion of ugsR2 has no dramatic effect on
ustilaginoidin biosynthesis
The predicted ugs gene cluster encodes a putative tran-
scription initiation factor, UgsR1 and a MADS-box MEF2
type transcription factor, UgsR2. UgsR2 having 668 amino-
acid residues contains a putative MEF2-like/type II MADS-
box domain in its N-terminal region. We demonstrated that
targeted deletion of ugsR2 had no dramatic effect on the
amount and chemical proﬁles of ustilaginoidins (Supporting
Information Fig. S8A). To determine whether the genes
in the ugs cluster are regulated by UgsR2, the expression
patterns of the target genes in different mutants were deter-
mined by RT-PCR. The expression of UvPKS1 and ugsJ
was slightly up-regulated in the ΔugsR2 mutant, while
other tested genes exhibited a similar expression pattern in
the wild-type and ΔugsR2 strains (Supporting Information
Fig. S8B). Collectively, the data indicate that UgsR2 may
not be the major transcription regulator of the ugs gene
cluster.
The phenotypes in mycelial growth of different ugs
mutants
To investigate whether ustilaginoidin biosynthesis has any
effect on U. virens mycelial growth, the wild-type, mutant
and complementary strains were cultured on the PSA
medium plates for 2 weeks, and were then observed for
growth phenotypes. The ΔugsR2, ΔUvPKS1, ΔugsT,
ΔugsJ and ΔugsL showed no signiﬁcant difference in col-
ony size from the wild type. By contrast, deletion of ugsO
caused an accelerated mycelial growth phenotype com-
pared with the wild-type. The ΔugsO/ugsO complemen-
tary strain restored the mycelial growth rate to the wild-
type level (Fig. 4). The data indicate that different ratios
of ustilaginoidin derivatives have a regulatory role in
U. virens mycelial growth.
