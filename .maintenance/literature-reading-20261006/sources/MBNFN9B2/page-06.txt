UgsL is responsible for monomer dimerization in
ustilaginoidin biosynthesis
The 2639 bp-long ugsL gene consists of seven exons,
encoding a 710 amino-acid protein. The protein contains
three copper oxidase-like domains and is predicted to be
a laccase responsible for the dimerization of two mono-
mer molecules. In HPLC analysis, eight compounds in the
wild-type culture were all missing from the ugsL-deletion
mutant, while three novel peaks (the metabolites 9, 10 and
11) were detected in the mutant with shorter retention
times of 10.4, 12.7 and 14.2 min, respectively (Fig. 3A).
These data indicate that these compounds are more
hydrophilic than the ustilaginoidin derivatives identiﬁed
above. These compounds had a maximal UV absorption
at 280 nm while the UV absorption of ustilaginoidins
peaked at 290 nm (Supporting Information Fig. S6). The
UV–visible spectra of these compounds displayed high
similarity to those of rubrofusarin and nor-rubrofusarin, but
were different from those of ustilaginoidins, suggesting that
these compounds were related with rubrofusarin and nor-
rubrofusarin with respect to their conjugated ring systems
(Frandsen et al., 2011). HR-ESI-MS analysis showed that
the three compounds had an m/z of 259.0581, 273.0738
and 273.0742 (= [M-H]−), equivalent to molecular formula of
C14H12O5, C15H14O5 and C15H14O5, respectively (Fig. 3B).
