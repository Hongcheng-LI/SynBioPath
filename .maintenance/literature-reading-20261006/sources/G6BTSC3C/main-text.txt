ABSTRACT: Pleosmaranes A−R (1−18), 18 new isopimarane-type
diterpenoids, together with four known analogs (19−22), were
isolated from the mangrove endophytic fungus Pleosporales sp.
HNQQJ-1. Their structures and absolute configurations were
established by analysis of their spectroscopic data and electronic
circular dichroism (ECD) calculations. Compounds 1−9 possess an
unusual aromatic B ring and a 20-nor-isopimarane skeleton.
Compounds 15−17 contain a unique 2-oxabicyclo[2.2.2]octane
moiety. Compound 18 features an unexpected 2-oxabicyclo[3.2.1]-
octane moiety. Compounds 8 and 12 exhibited a moderate inhibitory
effect against LPS-induced NO production, with IC50 values of 19 and
25 μM, respectively.
T
he mangrove plants are a rich source of fungal
endophytes. Owing to their unique ecological environ-
ment (high temperature, high salt, soil hypoxia, and periodic
seawater impregnation), diverse secondary metabolites with
complex structures and remarkable pharmacology activities
have been produced from mangrove endophytic fungi.1−4 To
date, over 1500 compounds have been isolated from
mangrove-derived fungi.5 Isopimarane diterpenoids are
common secondary metabolites of fungi with various
pharmacological activities, including anti-inflammatory, cyto-
toxic, and antibacterial effects.6−8 Cytotoxic libertellenone D,9
antibacterial aspewentins D−H,10 anti-inflammatory tedanol,11
immunosuppressive xylarinorditerpene N,12 and phytotoxic
taichunin G13 serve as examples illustrating the potential of
isopimarane diterpenoids as lead compounds for new drugs.
In our ongoing research to discover bioactive compounds
from mangrove endophytic fungi, the strain Sporormiaceae sp.
HNQQJ-1 from Kandelia candel attracted attention by virtue of
its in vitro anti-inflammatory activity. The extract was active at
6.25−50 μg/mL. Therefore, the secondary metabolites of
Pleosporales sp. HNQQJ-1 were investigated in this experiment.
As a result, 18 new isopimarane diterpenes, pleosmaranes A−R
(1−18), were isolated from the mangrove endophytic fungus
Pleosporales sp. HNQQJ-1. The anti-inflammatory effects of all
compounds were evaluated against lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-
activated RAW264.7 macrophages. Compounds 8 and 12
exhibited moderate anti-inflammatory activity with IC50 values
of 19 and 25 μM, respectively. Herein, the isolation, structure
elucidation, biological assays, and structure−activity relation-
ship (SAR) of these isolated compounds are described.
■RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Compound 1, isolated as a yellow oil, had a molecular formula
of C20H28O2 with seven degrees of unsaturation as determined
by HRESIMS. The 1H NMR spectrum (Table 1) revealed four
methyl groups at δH 1.16 (s, H3-17), 1.26 (s, H3-18), 1.24 (s,
H3-19), and 3.57 (s, H3-21), one aromatic proton signal at δH
6.73 (s, H-6), and three olefinic proton signals at 6.0 (dd, J =
17.6, 10.9 Hz, H-15), 5.21 (dd, J = 17.6, 1.0 Hz, H-16a), and
5.03 (dd, J = 10.8, 1.0 Hz, H-16b). The 13C NMR (Table 3)
and HSQC spectral (Figure S5) data of 1 exhibited 20 carbon
signals, including four methyls (one oxygenated), six
methylenes (one olefinic), three methines (two olefinic and
one oxygenated), and seven non-hydrogenated carbons (five
olefinic). Analysis of the 1H−1H COSY correlations revealed
three spin coupling systems (Figure 1). The HMBC cross-
peaks from H2-1 to C-5, C-9, and C-10; from H3-18 and H3-19
to C-3, C-4, and C-5; and from H-6 to C-4, C-7, C-8, and C-10
(Figure 1) permitted construction of the 6/6 fused-A/B ring

In conclusion, 18 new isopimarane diterpenoids, pleosmar-

endophytic fungus Pleosporales sp. HNQQJ-1. It is noteworthy
that pleosmaranes A−I (1−9) contain an unusual aromatic B
ring in the isopimarane skeleton. Pleosmaranes O−Q (15−17)
and R (18) contain a 2-oxabicyclo[2.2.2]octane and 2-

known compounds 21 and 22 were first named pleosmarane S
and pleosmarane T, respectively. The results of anti-
inflammatory assays of 1−22 suggest that isopimarane and
20-nor-isopimarane diterpenes are promising NO inhibitors.
Moreover, our work enriches the structural diversity of
isopimarane diterpenoids.
■EXPERIMENTAL SECTION
General Experimental Procedures. The optical rotations were
measured at 25 °C in MeOH using an MCP 300 polarimeter (Anton
Paar). The UV spectra in MeOH were recorded using an Evolution
200 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher). A model 420SF CD
spectrometer (Aviv Biomedical Inc.) was used to obtain ECD data.
IR spectra were obtained using KBr discs on a Nicolet Nexus 670
spectrophotometer. All NMR data were acquired at room temperature
on a Bruker Avance 500 spectrometer using the signals of residual
solvent protons (CDCl3: δH 7.26; CD3OD: δH 3.31; DMSO: δH 2.50)
and carbons (CDCl3: δC 77.1; CD3OD: δC 49.2; DMSO: δC 39.5) as
reference. HRESIMS spectra were obtained using a Q-TOF4600 mass
spectrometer (AB SCIEX). Column chromatography (CC) was
conducted using silica gel (200−300 mesh) and Sephadex LH-20.
Thin-layer chromatography (TLC) was conducted on silica gel plates
using HSGF254 from Yantai HuangHai Silica company. The
RAW264.7 cells were provided by the Typical Culture Preservation
Committee Cell Bank. Gibco supplied the fetal bovine serum (FBS),
and ProCell provided Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM).
LPS and L-NMMA were obtained from Sigma, and the NO kit was
supplied by Shanghai Beyotime Biotechnology.
Fungal Material. The strain Pleosporales sp. HNQQJ-1 was
obtained from the healthy leaves of Kandelia candel, which was
collected in July 2020 from Dongzhai Harbor Mangrove Nature
Reserve in Hainan Province, China. Molecular biological techniques
were employed to identify fungal species by amplifying DNA and
analyzing ITS sequences.17 GenBank accession number OR616722
was assigned to the sequence data of the strain, which exhibited
99.13% homology to the sequence of Pleosporales sp. (HM116753.1)
as determined by BLAST analysis. The strain is preserved in the
School of Pharmacy, Anhui Medical University.
Fermentation, Extraction, and Isolation. The fungal culture
was transferred to a solid rice medium in 60 1 L Erlenmeyer flasks
(each containing 100 g of raw rice and 70 mL of 0.3% seawater) and
incubated at room temperature for 30 days. The mycelia and solid rice
media were extracted with methanol three times, and the solution was
concentrated under reduced pressure to yield 35.8 g of extract. The
extract was fractionated by silica gel CC (200−300 mesh) with a
gradient of petroleum ether/ethyl acetate (1:0, 90:10, 80:20, 70:30,
60:40, 50:50, 40:60, 0:1) to yield seven fractions (Fr.1−Fr.7).
Compounds 1 (5.0 mg) and 2 (4.1 mg) as well as subfractions
(Fr.3.1−3.3) were separated by CC on silica gel eluting with CH2Cl2
from Fr.3 (3.1 g). Fr.3.1 was subjected to further purification using
CC on silica gel eluting with PE/EtOAc (4:1) to obtain compounds 3
(2.8 mg) and 17 (3.2 mg). Compounds 14 (4.2 mg, tR = 9.1 min) and
15 (4.0 mg, tR = 12.1 min) were obtained by semipreparative HPLC
(Shim-pack GIST column, 5 μm; 10.0 × 250 mm; MeOH/H2O,
85:15, 1.5 mL/min) from Fr.3.2. Compound 7 (3.2 mg, tR = 13.5
min) was obtained by semipreparative HPLC (MeOH/H2O, 83:17)
from Fr.3.3. Fr.4 (3.8 g) was purified by CC on silica gel eluting with
CH2Cl2/MeOH (125:1) to obtain compound 9 (3.3 mg) and
subfractions (Fr.4.1−4.5). Compounds 10 (2.8 mg) and 11 (2.6 mg)
were obtained by purification of Fr.4.2 using silica gel CC eluting with
CH2Cl2/MeOH (95:1). Fr.4.3 was purified using CC on silica gel
eluting with PE/EtOAc (3.5:1) to yield 4 (6.8 mg) and 6 (5.4 mg).
Compound 5 (3.8 mg) was obtained from Fr.4.4 by CC on silica gel
eluting with PE/EtOAc (3:1). Fr.4.5 was purified by CC on silica gel

Anti-inflammatory Assay. The RAW264.7 cells were cultured in
DMEM (Gibco, USA) containing 10% FBS (Gibco, USA), 100 U/
mL penicillin, and 100 μg/mL streptomycin 1% double antibodies at
37 °C with a 5% CO2 humidified incubator. The cell viability was
measured by MTT assay in 96-well plates. Approximately 3 × 106
cells/mL were inoculated overnight at 37 °C with 5% CO2. Then, the
cells were pretreated with different concentrations of L-NMMA or
compound (5, 10, 20, 30, 40, and 50 μM) for 24 h. Thereafter,
approximately 10 μL of MTT (0.5 mg/mL) was added to each well
for 4 h. The absorbance was checked at 540 nm. Cells were inoculated
overnight in 24-well plates with a density of 3 × 106 cells/mL (500
μL/well). Various concentrations of compounds were pretreated with
LPS (1 μg/mL) for 24 h. The content of NO was measured according
to the instructions of the Griess assay. The absorbance of the final
product was measured at 540 nm. L-NMMA was used as the positive
control.
MeOH Incubation Experiments. A solution of 5 (2.0 mg) in
MeOH (2 mL) was stirred for 24 h at room temperature, and no
change was observed. Then, a catalytic amount of silica gel and 1.0 M
aqueous HCl were added to the solution, which was stirred for 24 h at
50 °C. However, no additional products were detected. Similarly,
compounds 7, 17, 21, and 22 were incubated with MeOH,
respectively, and no other products were detected.
