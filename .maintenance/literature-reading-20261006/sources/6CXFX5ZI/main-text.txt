Biosynthesis of Ester-Bond Containing Quinolone Alkaloids with
(3R,4S) Stereoconfiguration

ABSTRACT: Asperalins represent a novel class of viridicatin
natural products with potent inhibitory activities against fish
pathogens. In this study, we elucidated the biosynthesis of asperalins
in the Aspergillus oryzae NSAR1 heterologous host and identified
the FAD-dependent monooxygenase AplB stereoselectively hydrox-
ylates viridicatin to yield a unique 3R,4S configuration. The
monomodular NRPS AplJ catalyzes a rare intramolecular ester bond formation reaction using dihydroquinoline as a nucleophile.
Subsequent modifications by cytochrome P450 AplF, chlorinase AplN, and prenyltransferase AplE tailor the anthranilic acid portion,
leading to the formation of asperalins. Additionally, we explored the potential of AplB for the hydroxylation of viridicatin analogs,
demonstrating its relaxed substrate specificity. This finding suggests that AplB could be developed as a biocatalyst for the synthesis of
viridicatin derivatives.
Q
uinoline and quinolone alkaloids represent essential
classes of secondary metabolites renowned for their
diverse and drug-like properties.1,2 Among these alkaloids, 4-
aryl-3-hydroxyquinolin-2(1H)-one, such as viridicatin,1,3,4 is a
prominent member found in numerous natural products from
Penicillium and Aspergillus species.1 These natural products
exhibit a wide range of beneficial biological activities, including
insecticidal,5 antibacterial,6 antifungal,7 antitumor8,9 and
antiviral10 properties.1,2 Recently, our group isolated several
novel viridicatin-type alkaloids, named asperalins (Figure 1),
from seagrass-derived fungi Aspergillus alabamensis SYSU-
6778.6 Asperalins display moderate or potent inhibitory
activities against fish pathogenic bacteria, including Edward-
siella ictalurid, Streptococcus iniae, and Streptococcus parauberis.6
Asperalins possess several unique structural features that
draw our attention to their biosynthesis. First, in contrast to
commonly isolated quinolone natural products, which are
predominantly modified through prenylation and subsequent
cyclization,11−15 such as aspoquinolones,11,16 penigequino-
lones,11 aniduquinolones,17 and aflaquinolones10,14,15 (Figure
1), asperalins are formed through an intramolecular ester
linkage between dihydroquinoline and anthranilic acid.
Although intermolecular ester bond formation is common in
cyclic NRPS, the formation of an intramolecular ester linker is
rare. Second, the stereochemistry of the C3 and C4 positions
are opposite, and the only natural product with such a
configuration is aflaquinolone G,1,15,18 whose biosynthesis is
still unresolved. Finally, the anthranilic acid portion of the
asperalins is chlorinated and prenylated to generate diversity.
Understanding asperalins’ biosynthesis could facilitate the
generation of analogs with potentially improved biological
activities.

has been investigated by the Watanabe and Tang groups,
respectively.11−13,19 The biosynthesis starts with the con-

(2), and the subsequent epoxidation forms cyclopenin

AsqI/PenL catalyzes the ring-contraction of cyclopenin to
form viridicatin by eliminating methyl isocyanate.4,11 Recently,
the Li group reported the biosynthesis of meta-hydroxylated
viridicatin from Penicillium palitans.4 Similar to aspoquino-
lones/penigequinolones biosynthesis, VdoB (NRPS), VdoC
(dioxygenase), and VdoA (hemocyanin-like protein) catalyze
the formation of viridicatin. Subsequently, a cytochrome P450
(VdoD) forms viridicatol by metahydroxylation of viridicatin.
Given the structural similarity between asperalins with
aspoquinolones,16 penigequinolones,11 and viridicatol,4 we
envision they share similar biosynthetic steps. After genome
sequencing of Aspergillus alabamensis SYSU-6778 and sub-
sequent antiSMASH analysis,23 we identified one biosynthetic
gene cluster (BGC), namely the apl cluster, resembling that of
asq, pen, and vdo BGCs (Figure 2). The apl cluster harbors
three key enzymes presumably responsible for the formation of
viridicatin: an NRPS (AplL), a nonheme α-KG-dependent
dioxygenase (AplC), and a hemocyanin-like protein (AplK).
The NRPS AplL exhibits 56% protein sequence identities with
AsqK, 62% with PenN, and 58% with VdoB. AplC shares 53%
with AsqJ, 80% with PenM, and 76% with VdoC. AplK shares
45%, 59%, and 54% with AsqI, PenL, and VdoA, respectively.
As illustrated in Figure 3A, these enzymes are responsible for
the generation of the viridicatin scaffold.
To determine if those three genes are responsible for
viridicatin biosynthesis, we heterologously expressed AplLCK
in Aspergillus oryzae (AO) NSAR1, a robust platform for
elucidating fungi natural product biosynthesis.24,25 Although
we detected the formation of viridicatin, the yield is very low
(Figure 3B). The major products we obtained are
intermediates toward viridicatin, including cyclopeptin (1),
dehydrocyclopeptin (2), and cyclopenin (3). This suggests
that the activities of AplC and AplK are relatively low, making
it difficult to elucidate the remaining biosynthetic pathway
relying on the production of viridicatin from AplLCK (Scheme
1). Fortunately, viridicatin can be chemically synthesized in a
simple scheme (Scheme 2),26 therefore, the functions of the
remaining genes in the apl cluster could potentially be
investigated by feeding chemically synthesized viridicatin.
In addition to AplLCK, apl BGC contains 12 more genes.
AplB is predicted as a FAD-dependent monooxygenase,
presumably responsible for the hydroxylation of viridicatin,
sharing 66% homology with PenA of the pen BGC and 37%
homology with AsqM of the asq BGC, respectively. The
remaining 11 enzymes share low similarity with asq, pen, or vdo
BGCs, mainly including two P450 (AplD and AplF), one
halogenase (AplN), one aromatic prenyltransferase (AplE),
one methyltransferase (AplH), one oxidoreductase (AplO),
one additional monomodular NRPS (AplJ), and one NRPS-
like protein (AplG).
When chemically synthesized viridicatin was fed to AO-
AplB, viridicatin was completely abolished after 5 days, and a
new peak at 11 min appeared in the HPLC trace (Figure 4).
Isolation and structure elucidation confirmed the peak to be
indeed hydroxylated viridicatin (5) (Scheme 1). Compound 5
shares identical NMR spectra and optical rotation with the
known natural product aflaquinolone G,15,18,27 strongly
suggesting that it indeed has a 3R,4S configuration. These
results suggest that despite sharing relatively high homology
with PenA and AsqM,11 AplB could produce differently
stereoconfigured dihydroquinoline.
Having confirmed that chemically synthesized viridicatin can
be taken up by the AO strain, we next embarked on elucidating
ester bond formation. It has been reported that monomodular
NRPS can form intramolecular ester bonds. Li et al. reported
that NRPS ApmB from P. brevicompactum could form an ester
bond between two dipeptides;28 Yasutomo et al. reported
NRPS AstA catalyzed the ester bond formation between a
sesquiterpene and benzoic acid or 4-hydroxybenzoic acid;29
Rose et al. recently identified NRPS-like enzymes AceN and
NomN that utilize hydroxylated indole diterpenes as
nucleophiles to esterify with leucine and valine, respectively.30
Besides, NRPSs in the biosynthesis of CJ-12662, paeciloxazine,
flavunoidine, and aculenes are all reported capable of forming
intramolecular ester bonds.31−33

5 days, we observed the formation of the expected ester
asperalin G (6) by LC-MS; however, the conversion rate was
very low (Figures 4, 5). After extensive efforts, we managed to
obtain only a minimal amount of 6 from AO-AplBJ (less than

Given that we could synthesize viridicatin and its analogs in
a single step, we sought to determine whether we could
generate dihydroquinoline analogs. To streamline the synthesis
process, we attempted to express AplB in E. coli BL21(DE3)
and feed it viridicatin analogs.
Despite AplB being predominantly expressed in inclusion
bodies, AplB-expressing whole cells could still fully convert
viridicatin to 5 within 12 h (Figure S15). AplB also displayed a
relatively relaxed substrate specificity, effectively converting
most of the tested analogs (Scheme 2, Figures S16−S31). We
found the para-modified viridicatin analogs were well tolerated,
with the exception of the analogue containing a nitro group.
Modifications at meta- or ortho-positions resulted in a lower
yield. We envision that improving the expression of AplB with
solubilization tags, in combination with direct evolution, could
render AplB a valuable biocatalyst for generating viridicatin
family natural product analogs.
In conclusion, we identified the apl BGC that is responsible
for the biosynthesis of asperalins from the marine fungus
Aspergillus alabamensis SYSU-6778 and demonstrated the
complete biosynthesis of asperalins in the A. oryzae NSAR1
heterologous host. The viridicatin backbone of asperalins was
assembled by AplLCK, and the oxygenase AplB then
stereoselectively hydroxylates viridicatin to give a unique
3R,4S stereoconfiguration. Subsequently, the monomodular
NRPS AplJ forms a rare intramolecule ester bond between
dihydroxyquinoline and anthranilic acid. Finally, the P450
AplF, chlorinase AplN, and prenyltransferase AplE further
tailor the anthranilic acid portion to give asperalins. In
addition, we found that AplB displays a relatively relaxed
substrate specificity, which suggests that it could potentially be
developed into a valuable biocatalyst.
