Methods 
Bacterial strains and growth media 
Vibrio ruber DSM 16370 was acquired from DSMZ Germany. E. coli strains β3914 and π3813 
were gifts from the Didier Mazel laboratory.6 E. coli strain WO153 (recJ asmB1 ΔtolC::Kanr) was 
a gift from the Kim Lewis laboratory. Unless otherwise specified, E. coli was grown in LB Miller 
(Fischer), and V. ruber was grown either in LB + sodium chloride (40 g/L) + sodium acetate 
trihydrate (2.72 g/L) or Difco Marine Broth 2216 (VWR International). The media was adjusted 
to pH 7.6 using sodium hydroxide and sterilized by autoclaving. 
In silico analysis 
Representative self-resistance genes were adapted from Vandova et al.7  Genes were searched 
against a curated database of polyketide synthase clusters8 using NCBI BLASTp. Gene clusters 
were considered potential hits if they had at least one potential self-resistance gene with a greater 
than 30% sequence identity (greater than 60% required for FabB/FabF homologs) and a E value 
less than 10-8. Potential hits were manually curated based on the following criteria. First, gene 
clusters where the potential self-resistance gene was more than 10 kB from a core PKS or NRPS 
gene were removed. Then, available genomes were manually analyzed for the presence of a second 
enzyme with the same function as the potential-self resistance gene. Finally, gene clusters were 
manually curated to remove ones that did not possess traditional PKS architecture, requiring most 
modules to possess both a KS and an ACP domain, as well as either an AT domain within the 
module or a trans-AT domain. 
Plasmid construction 
V. ruber genes were amplified out of pre-prepared genomic DNA from V. ruber DSMZ 16370, 
while E. coli genes were amplified directly out of liquid stocks of E. coli DH5α. pSW7848 was a 
gift from the Dider Mazel laboratory.9  Cloning was performed in E. coli Stellar Cells (Takara Bio) 
and completed plasmids were retransformed into E. coli DH5α for storage, except for plasmids 
derived from the pSW7848 backbone, where cloning and storage were instead performed in E. coli 
π3813. E. coli π3813 was grown at all steps in media supplemented with 300 μM deoxythymidine. 
Vibrio ruber genome editing 
Each plasmid was transformed into E. coli strain β3914 and grown on LB agar supplemented with 
25 ug/ml chloramphenicol and 300 μM 2,6-diaminopimelic acid (DAP). Simultaneously, the 
appropriate V. ruber strain was streaked from a frozen glycerol stock onto a MB2216 agar plate. 
Single colonies from each plate were used to inoculate 5 ml cultures of Marine Broth (V. ruber) or 
LB with DAP and chloramphenicol (E. coli β3914) which were grown for 16 h at 37°C. The V. 
ruber was diluted 1:50 into 10 ml LB Miller supplemented with 40 g/L NaCl and grown at 37°C 
to an OD600 of 0.2. One hour after the V. ruber dilution, E. coli β3914 cultures were centrifuged 


[Attachment CS8QXXT8; PDF page 125]
 
 
125 
at 4000xg for 5 min, then resuspended in 5 ml fresh LB with DAP. The culture was then diluted 
1:50 into 5 ml fresh LB with DAP and grown to an OD600 of 0.2. 2.5 ml of E. coli β3914 was 
combined with 5 ml of V. ruber and the combined culture was centrifuged at 2000xg for 5 min. 
The supernatant was decanted, and the culture was resuspended in the residual volume and 
incubated at room temperature for 1 h. The culture was then spread on a MB2216 agar plate 
containing 10 g/L glucose and 5 ug/ml chloramphenicol and grown overnight at 37°C. 
At least 2 isolated, well-formed colonies from the conjugation plate were selected and each colony 
was used to inoculate both an 8 ml MB2216 and an 8 ml MB2216 5 ug/ml chloramphenicol culture, 
both of which were grown at 37°C for 8 h. Colonies which showed growth in the chloramphenicol 
culture moved to the next step, where the culture of that colony containing no chloramphenicol 
was diluted 1:10,000 in MB2216 and 200 μl of the dilution was streaked on an MB2216 plate 
containing 2 g/L of arabinose, which was grown overnight at 37°C. 
For the ΔP450 knockout, SacB was utilized as a counter-selection method for genome editing of 
V. ruber (pSK22). Following previously outlined procedures, exconjugants from the original 
conjugation plate were individually re-streaked to MB2216 agar plates containing 50 ug/ml 
apramycin and 10% sucrose to screen for double-crossover mutants. Colonies that grew in 
apramycin and sucrose were subsequently re-streaked to MB2216 agar plates containing 50 ug/ml 
apramycin and 10% sucrose for a second time.  
6-12 colonies from the arabinose plate (sucrose plate for ΔP450 knockout) were selected and used 
to inoculate 20 μl MB2216 stocks in sterile tubes, and then the remainder of each colony was 
resuspended in 20 μl water in a PCR tube. The water tubes were boiled at 95°C for 30 min, then 1 
μl of each tube was used as template for a PCR reaction using appropriate primers to PCR across 
the insert or deletion region of the chromosome. Colony PCRs were performed using CloneAmp

Threonyl-tRNA synthetase expression and puriﬁcation 
The appropriate plasmid was transformed into E. coli BL21(DE3) and single colonies were used 
to inoculate four 10 ml seed cultures of LB broth containing 50 ug/ml kanamycin monosulfate. 
Seed cultures were grown for 16 hours at 37°C, then diluted 1:100 into 1L LB containing 50 ug/ml 
kanamycin monosulfate in a 2.5 L Tunair shake flask. These expression cultures were grown at 
37°C until the OD600 had reached 0.2-0.3, at which point the incubator temperature was set to 
18°C and growth was continued at this temperature until the OD600 had reached 0.6-0.8. Cultures 
were then induced by addition of 100 μM IPTG and grown for 16 h at 18°C. Cells were harvested 
by centrifugation at 5000xg for 20 min at 4°C. Cell pellets were resuspended in 35 ml of ThrS 
Lysis Buffer (50 mM sodium phosphate, 300 mM sodium chloride, 10 mM imidazole, pH 8.0) and 
sonicated. Lysates were clarified by centrifugation at 25,000xg for 1 h at 4°C. Clarified lysates 
were mixed with Ni-NTA resin (2 ml of 50% resin per 1L of initial culture) pre-equilibrated in 
ThrS Lysis Buffer and incubated with end-over-end rotation at 4°C for 1 h. The lysates were 
applied to a Kimble Konte column and allowed to flow through. The resin was washed with 20 ml 
of ThrS Lysis Buffer per L of initial culture, followed by 15 ml of ThrS Wash Buffer (50 mM 
sodium phosphate, 300 mM sodium chloride, 50 mM imidazole, pH 8.0) per 1L of initial culture, 
and then eluted with 8 ml of ThrS Elution Buffer (50 mM sodium phosphate, 300 mM sodium 
chloride, 500 mM imidazole, pH 8.0) per L of initial culture. Eluent was diluted to 50 ml with ThrS 
IEX Buffer A (50 mM sodium phosphate, 10% glycerol, pH 8.0) and further purified on an ÄKTA 
pure chromatography system (GE Healthcare Life Sciences) equipped with a HiTrap Q HP anion 
exchange chromatography column (5 mL).  The column was first washed with 4 column volumes 
of ThrS IEX Buffer A at 4 ml/min, then the protein was purified using a gradient of 100% ThrS 
IEX Buffer A to 100% ThrS IEX Buffer B (50 mM sodium phosphate, 500 mM sodium chloride, 
10% glycerol, pH 8.0) over 20 column volumes at 4 mL/min, using a 4-column volume wash with 
FPLC A prior to the gradient. Fractions were screened by SDS-PAGE and those containing the 
protein of interest were pooled, concentrated using Amicon Ultra Centrifugal Filters, frozen in 
liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80°C. Protein concentrations were determined from the A280 as 
measured on a Thermo Scientific NanoDrop 2000c spectrophotometer, using extinction 
coefficients estimated from the primary sequence of the protein by the Expasy ProtParam tool. 
Threonyl-tRNA Synthetase Continuous Plate Reader Assay

components, 30 mM KCl, 1 mM DTT, 10 mM MgCl2, 500 μg/mL E. coli total tRNA and 5 µM 
threonyl-tRNA synthetase were used in each assay. Absorbance was read at 360 nm for 2 h. The

Minimal Inhibitory Concentration Assay 
To test the minimal inhibitory concentration against B. subtilis, E. coli WO153 and E. coli 
WO153+pSK23, we picked colonies from an agar plate and grew the colonies in liquid culture 
overnight (with appropriate antibiotics as mentioned in the following sentence). The overnight 
culture was back diluted 1:100 and grown in LB medium with 50 µg/mL kanamycin (E. coli

in the assay. Vibriomycin was dissolved in DMSO at the appropriate concentration and added to 
each well such that concentration of DMSO was <1%. The assay was done in a 96 well plate; each 
well containing 100 µL liquid. The cultures were incubated at 37 °C for 18 hours and the MIC 
inferred by eye where there was no growth of bacteria. 
Disk DiFusion Assay 
We grew an overnight culture of B. subtilis in LB Broth for 16 hours. The culture was then diluted 
to obtain B. subtilis at 0.5 OD600 nm. An LB agar plate was warmed to room temperature and 150 
μL of B. subtilis at 0.5 OD600 nm was spread on to the plate. A disk was placed on the plate and the 
desired compound applied onto the disk and allowed to dry. The plate was then incubated overnight 
at 37 °C.
