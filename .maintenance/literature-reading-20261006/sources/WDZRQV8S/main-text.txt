ABSTRACT: Aspongopyrimidine A (1), a hexa-1,3-diene-histidine-hexanoic acid adduct featuring a 4,5-dihydro-2H-10λ4-
imidazo[5,1-f]pyrrolo[2,1-b]pyrimidine motif, was isolated from the insect Aspongopus chinensis. The structure was clarified by
spectroscopic and computational methods and X-ray diffraction. Peralkylation of N-atoms in histidine by two C6 units makes 1 an
inner salt with a 5/6/5 tricyclic system. Biological evaluation found that 1 exerts activity against Alzheimer’s disease targeting
MAPRE3 through a chemical proteomics approach. This study revealed unusual modifications of amino acids as the fundamental
units of protein.

nimal medicine is an essential part of traditional Chinese
medicine, accounting for 18.4% of the total 365 species in
“Shennong Bencao Jing”, an ancient medicinal classic in China.
Among all of the animal medicine, insects occupy a
considerable proportion. Actually, several countries around
the world also have a long history of using insects as food or
medicine in their traditional culture.1 Secondary metabolites in
plants and microorganisms have been shown to be
pharmacologically active substances, and macromolecules
such as proteins or peptides in insects are generally considered
to be of medicinal value. Evidence shows that insects make use
of small molecules for chemical defense or chemical
communication.2−4 As a result, volatile compounds in insects
have been given much attention in the past decades from a
view of chemical ecology.2−4 A recent example is that 4-
vinylanisole was found to be a pheromone in locusts
responsible for aggregation, unveiling the disaster caused by
locust aggregation.5 In spite of this, both the structure types of
small molecules in insects and their medicinal significance
remain largely unknown.6 In recent years, we have undertaken
a chemical investigation on medicinal insects commonly used
in Chinese clinical practice or in folk aimed to disclose
substances responsible for the efficacy of medicinal insects,
leading to the isolation of several active substances such as
(±)-cyclopeltains A, (±)-pipajiamide A, and ZL170.7−9 More
recently, structurally intriguing nitrogenous compounds with a
ten-membered macrolactam or a morpholine functional group
were characterized by us from Periplaneta americana (American
cockroach).10 Aspongopus chinensis is a traditional Chinese
insect medicine mainly distributed in southern China. It has
been long used for the treatment of pain and kidney diseases.
Chemical studies revealed the presence of N-acetyldopamine
derivatives and nucleoside derivatives.11,12 Pharmacological
studies disclosed that A. chinensis extracts exhibit antitumor
and antibacterial properties.13 Our previous efforts on A.
chinensis afforded (±)-aspongamide A, aspongdopamine A, and
aspongopusamide A,14−16 demonstrating the structure diver-
sity thereof. As an in-depth study on this insect, aspongopyr-
imidine A (1) (Figure 1), a histidine-C6 unit adduct
possessing a 5/6/5 tricyclic ring, was characterized from 6 kg
of material. This paper will describe its isolation, structural
characterization, and biological property toward Alzheimer’s
disease (AD).

Aspongopyrimidine A (1), obtained as a yellowish gum, was
found to have a molecular formula of C18H23N3O4 by analysis
of its HRESIMS, 13C NMR, and DEPT spectra, having 9
degrees of unsaturation. The 1H NMR spectrum of 1 (Table
1) displays two methyl groups (δH 0.85, t, J = 7.0 Hz, H-6″; δH
1.15, t, J = 7.2 Hz, H-7′), two olefinic protons (δH 5.91, d, J =
3.8 Hz, H-4′; δH 6.39, d, J = 3.8 Hz, H-3′), and two downfield
protons at δH 9.90 (s, H-2) and δH 7.90 (s, H-4). The 13C
NMR and DEPT spectra (Table 1) give 18 carbons classified
into two CH3, five sp3 CH2, six CH (four sp2 and two sp3), and
five nonprotonated carbons (two carbonyls, three sp2). The
structure of 1 was mainly determined by 2D NMR experi-
ments. The
1H−1H COSY spectrum (Figure 2) gives the
interaction of H2-6/H-7, in combination with the HMBC
correlations (Figure 2) of H2-6, H-7/C-8 (δC 170.1), C-5 (δC
125.5), H2-6/C-4 (δC 116.7), H-4/C-5, C-2 (δC 129.6), and
H-2/C-5 and diagnostic chemical shifts for C-2, C-4, C-5, and
C-8, indicating the presence of a histidine moiety. The 1H−1H
COSY correlations of H-7′/H-6′ and H-3′/H-4′ and the
HMBC correlations of H-7′, H-6′/C-5′ (δC 120.9), H-6′/C-4′
(δC 103.7), H-3′, H-4′/C-2′ (δC 133.8), and H-3′/C-5′
indicate the fragment of C-2′−C-7′, which is a C6 chain. The
HMBC correlations of H-7/C-2′ and C-5′, in consideration of
the chemical shifts of C-2′ and C-5′, suggest the presence of a
pyrrole formed via C-5′−N-1′ and C-2′−N-1′. Additionally,
the HMBC correlation of H-2/C-2′ is observed, indicating that
the C-2′−N-1 bond is present to form a pyrimidin-1-ium. This
conclusion is also supported by the observed ROESY
correlation of H-2/H-3′ (Figure 2). Analysis of the residual
signals found the 1H−1H COSY correlations of H-2″/H-3′′/
H-4′′/H-5′′/H-6′′ and HMBC correlations of H-2′′ and H-
3′′/C-1′′ (δC 171.2), suggesting a hexanoic acid residue. This
fragment is connected to histidine via C-3″−N-3 evidenced by
HMBC correlations of H-3″/C-2 and C-4 and the typical
chemical shift of C-3′′ (δC 57.7). Futhermore, a
1H-15N
HMBC spectrum (Figure 2) in DMSO-d6 clearly shows
correlations of H-3′, H-4′, and H-6/N-1′ (δN 154.2), H-2, H-4,
and H-6/N-1 (δN 180.8), and H-2, H-4, and H-2″/N-3 (δN
188.3). In this way, the planar structure of 1 was deduced as a
zwitterion. As for the potential for ionization of both carboxyl
groups, it was speculated that the planar structure of 1 may be
1A or 1B (Figure S2). Further confirmation was carried out
using the below computational methods.
There are two stereogenic centers in 1. It is a great challenge
to assign the stereochemistry at each stereogenic carbon.
Quantum calculations have been proven to be a powerful tool
for clarifying the stereochemistry of diverse natural products.17
In the study presented here, 1H and 13C NMR calculations
were first employed to assign the planar structure and relative
configuration of 1. As for the details, the DP4+ probability
analyses were used to evaluate the results. It was found that the
calculated results for (7S*,3″S*)-1A instead of (7S*,3″R*)-1A,
(7S*,3″S*)-1B, and (7S*,3″R*)-1B (Figure S3) exhibit the
highest R2 and a significantly higher DP4+ probability score
(Table S3 and Figures S3 and S8).18 With these results, the
calculated ECD calculations were accomplished on the basis of
(7S*,3″S*)-1. It was found that the spectrum of (7S,3″S)-1
instead of that of (7R,3″R)-1 (Figure 3) agrees well with the
experimental one, suggesting the 7S,3″S configuration of 1,

Modification of DNA/RNA or proteins, such as methylation
of DNA and methylation, acetylation, phosphorylation, or
ubiquitylation of histone, is the main research topic in
epigenetics. We have found that different bases could be
modified by a C6 or C8 unit.12,19 Previous studies also revealed
that histidine is involved in adduct formation with dopamine
derivatives during insect cuticle sclerotization.20−22 The
present finding of histidine hybridization with two C6 units
via N-alkylation represents a novel pattern of amino-acid-based
modification. It is well-known that bases and amino acids are
fundamental building blocks of DNA/RNA proteins/peptides
in organisms. Whether such C6 alkylation in insects is also
present in DNA or proteins of human beings remains
unknown. We believe that further studies toward this direction
either confirming or modifying our conclusions will contribute
to an in-depth understanding of the significance of C6 N-
alkylation of histidine or the other amino acids in organisms.
Considering that compound 1 has a novel heterozygous
mode, plausible biogenetic pathways for 1 are postulated in
Scheme 1. As can be seen from the foregoing analysis,
compound 1 is heterozygous with histidine and two C6
fragments. We think 2-hexenal is an important initiator because
it is one of the main volatile compounds in A. chinensis and is
used for chemical defense. First, 2-hexenal was oxidized and
reacted with L-histidine in two successive steps of aldimine
condensation to form intermediate A. The key intermediate A
by dehydration produces iminium B. B was subsequently
transformed into D by cyclization, dehydration, and proto-
nation reactions. Finally, compound 1 was generated by a
Michael addition reaction between intermediate D and 2-
hexenoic acid obtained by oxidation of 2-hexenal.

proteomics approach was first used in HEK-293T cells. In
detail, the basic strategy of drug affinity responsive target
stability (DARTS)23 was adopted in this study (Figure 5A).

Differentially expressed proteins (DEPs) were subjected to GO

The GO analysis showed that differentially expressed genes
(DEGs) were highly enriched in microtubule cytoskeleton,
among which protein MAPRE3 (EB3) is essential for
neuritogenesis.24 Given that A. chinensis has been used for
kidney disease and it has been considered for thousands of
years that there exists a kidney−brain axis in the traditional
Chinese medicine system, we therefore investigated the
regulatory effects of 1 from an angle of mediating neuro-
degenerative diseases such as AD by MAPRE3. β-Amyloid
plaques are the histopathological hallmarks of AD, and in order
to explore whether compound 1 affected the activation of AD,
we examined major pathological hallmarks of AD in N2aSW
cells. As expected, compared with the control, treatment with 1
could inhibit Aβ deposition.25 Meanwhile, GPX4-mediated
cellular antioxidant activity was enhanced after being treated
with the compound.26 To gain additional information about
the function of MAPRE3, we first examined the effect of
siRNA-mediated knockdown of MAPRE3 in N2aSW cells. As
shown in Figure 6B, silencing the MAPRE3 gene resulted in
downregulation of β-amyloid plaques to different extents.
Moreover, the activity of 1 was suppressed in MAPRE3
knockdown cells compared with that in the control cells
(Figure 6B and Figure S12). Interestingly, compound 1
effectively regulated and suspended AD activity without
affecting MAPRE3 expression (Figure 6B). Thus, it is likely
that the activity of 1 is associated with targeting MAPRE3.
This finding shows that compound 1 could be a novel
structural type with the potential for producing anti-AD drugs,
which warrants further investigations.
In conclusion, this study afforded a structurally intriguing N-
peralkylation product of histidine of two C6 units from the
insect source. Proteomics analysis revealed its role in anti-AD
by targeting MAPRE3. The collective findings bring more
insights into the insect-derived natural products.
