ABSTRACT: One novel rearranged pimarane diterpenoid,
pestanoid A (1), and two reported molecules, nodulisporenones
A (2) and B (3), were discovered from Pestalotiopsis sp. NBUF145
fungus associated with a 62 m deep mesophotic (“twilight”) zone
Chalinidae sponge. The structures of 1−3 were identified by
spectrometry, spectroscopy, quantum-chemical calculations, and X-
ray crystallography. Compounds 1 and 2 inhibited bone marrow
monocyte osteoclastogenesis in vitro with the IC50 values 4.2 ± 0.2
μM and 3.0 ± 0.4 μM, respectively, without observed cytotoxicity.
Both 1 and 2 suppressed the receptor activator of NF-kB ligand-
induced MAPK and NF-κB signaling by inhibiting the phosphor-
ylation of ERK1/2-JNK1/2-p38 MAPKs and NF-κB nuclear
translocation.
O
steoporosis (OP) is a skeletal metabolic disease that
presents with chronic trabecular bone loss leading to
increased bone fragility and fractures.1,2 OP affects over 200
million people worldwide, making it a significant public health
problem.3 Despite its low fatality rate, OP reduces quality of
life and causes long-term complications in children, young
adults, and elderly people.4,5 However, current OP treatments
are limited due to side effects and complications that result
from long-term administration.2,6 There is thus an urgent need
to explore more effective prevention measures and treatments
for osteoporosis, and natural product drug discovery is one
effective avenue.
Mesophotic coral ecosystems (MCEs) are viable sources for
discovering biologically active compounds. MCEs are dis-
tinguished by light-dependent corals and associated species,
which are commonly found in both tropical and subtropical
zones at depths of 30 to 150 m in the ocean.7 Exploration by
remotely operated vehicles (ROVs) has revealed that MCEs
are highly rich and diverse ecosystems, holding complex 3D
animal forests above benthic substrates in both tropical and
temperate regions.8−11 Despite this, a 2008 review showed that
less than 10% of publications about coral provide ecology and
health/mortality data regarding populations deeper than 30
m.12 However, detailed reports from MCEs have increased
over the past decade as oceanographic technologies modernize,
like scuba diving equipment, and access to ROVs and
submersible vehicles improves.13,14 Natural products discov-
ered from biological resources in MCEs have been relatively
rarely documented to date.
In our ongoing effort to discover intriguing natural products
from sponge-associated fungi in MCEs,15−17 Pestalotiopsis sp.
NBUF145, derived from a 62 m deep Chalinidae sponge, was
prioritized using the “One Strain, MAny Compounds”
(OSMAC) approach18 and chemical investigation. As a result,
one rearranged pimarane diterpenoid, named pestanoid A (1)
after the scientific name of the producing strain, and two
known analogs, nodulisporenones A (2) and B (3), were
characterized. The fermentation of the producing strain,
isolation, structure elucidation, and biological activities of 1
and 2 are presented herein.

Pestanoid A (1) was obtained as colorless prisms with the
molecular formula C20H26O6 based on a sodium adduct peak at
m/z 385.1629 [M + Na]+ (calcd for C20H26O6Na, 385.1622),
corresponding to eight double-bond equivalents (DBEs). The
1H, 13C, and DEPT-135 NMR data of 1 (Table 1) suggested
the presence of three methyl groups (CH3-17; CH3-18; CH3-
20), four methylenes (CH2-2; CH2-3; CH2-12; CH2-14), one
hydroxymethyl at δH/C 3.88/61.4, four methines [including
two oxygenated (δH 4.85, δC 78.2, CH-1; δH 4.27, δC 86.4, CH-
15), one aliphatic (δH 2.86, δC 45.1, CH-5), and one olefinic
(δH 6.46, δC 129.6, CH-7)], one vinylidene owing to
equivalent proton signals at δH/C 4.87/112.0, two sp3
quaternary carbons (δC 58.4, C-10; δC 42.6, C-13), two sp2
quaternary carbons (δC 147.2, C-4; δC 146.2, C-8), one
conjugated carbonyl (δC 206.8, C-9), and two ester groups (δC
165.2, C-6; δC 175.3, C-11) (Table 1). The functional groups
mentioned above in total accounted for five DBEs, suggesting
the existence of three ring systems in the skeleton of 1.
The planar structure of 1 was constructed with support from
2D NMR data (1H−1H COSY, HSQC, HMBC). The HMBC
cross peaks from both H-1 and H2-19 to C-3 and C-5, H3-18 to
C-4 and C-10, and H3-20 to C-1 and C-5, together with the
coupling system H-1/H2-2/H2-3 revealed by the
1H−1H
COSY spectrum, were used to construct the six-membered ring
A (Figure 1). HMBC correlations from H2-14 to C-7, C-8, and
C-9 and the conjugated carbonyl at δC 206.8 revealed the
location of one α,β-unsaturated ketone (C-7−C-9), which was
determined to be attached to C-10 by the HMBC correlation
from H3-20 to C-9. The HMBC correlations from both H-1
and H-7 to C-6 supported the ester linkage (C-6) between C-1
and C-7, forming ring B (Figure 1). The coupling system H-
15/H2-16, with C-15 decorated by one hydroxymethyl,
together with HMBC cross peaks from H3-17 to C-12, and
C-15, from H2-12 and H-15 to C-11, and from H2-16 to C-13,
established a 5-(hydroxymethyl)-4-methyldihydrofuran-
2(3H)-one (ring C). The key HMBC correlations from H2-
14 to C-12 and C-15 linked C-14 and C-13 to afford the planar
structure of 1, a rearranged pimarane diterpenoid.
To determine the relative configuration of 1, the NOESY
experiment was employed. As shown in Figure 1, the NOESY
correlations of H-1/H-5/H3-20 indicated their β-orientation.
Conversely, H3-18 were suggested to be α-oriented. H-15 and
H3-17 were designated as antifacial based on evidence from the
NOESY interactions of H-15/H-12α and H3-17/H-12β. Due
to the flexibility of methylene units (CH2-14), there were still
two relative configurations (1S*,5R*,10R*,13R*,15R*-1, or
1a; and 1S*,5R*,10R*,13S*,15S*-1, or 1b) possible for 1. To
further clarify the relative configuration of 1, NMR chemical
shift calculations with DP4+ were performed at the
mPW1PW91-SCRF/6-31+G(d,p) level. The results showed
that 1a had a DP4+ probability of 100% (all data) and lower
mean absolute error (MAE) and corrected MAE values
compared to 1b. Therefore, the relative configuration of 1
was assigned as 1S*,5R*,10R*,13R*,15R*. Later, single crystals
suitable for X-ray diffraction were obtained in MeOH/H2O

Compounds 2 and 3 were assigned as the known natural
products nodulisporenones A and B after comparison of MS,
NMR, and specific rotation data with reported values.20 The
planar structure and configuration of compound 2 were further
supported by X-ray diffraction (Figure 2) with a Flack
parameter of −0.03(7).19
The in vitro anti-osteoclastogenesis activity of compounds 1
and 2 was evaluated in bone marrow monocytes (BMMs). The
bioassay results showed that compounds 1 and 2 inhibited
osteoclastogenesis with the IC50 values of 4.2 ± 0.2 μM and 3.0
± 0.4 μM, respectively (Table 2). Meanwhile, 1 and 2 showed
lower cytotoxicity toward BMMs with the concentration of
cytotoxicity 50% (CC50) values greater than 30 μM, indicating
some selectivity (Table 2). Furthermore, 1 and 2 inhibited
phosphorylation of ERK1/2-JNK1/2-p38 mitogen-activated
protein kinases (MAPKs) at 5.0 and 10.0 μM in a dose-
dependent fashion (Figure 3). In addition, 1 and 2 displayed
inhibitions of NF-κB nuclear translocation, which was
especially apparent at 10.0 μM. Since phosphorylation of
MAPK signaling and NF-κB nuclear translocation are essential
for osteoclast differentiation, 1 and 2 may act as anti-
osteoclastogenic agents through this mechanism of action.
Compound 3 was obtained in too scarce of a quantity to be
bioassayed in this study.
Compound 1 is structurally unique among the natural
products that have been reported to date, with no other
molecules in the SciFinder database having a structural
Tanimoto similarity score ≥85. The structure of 1 is
consistent with the rearrangement of a pimarane diterpenoid,
offering some shared plausible biosynthetic relationship to the
rearranged pimaranes 2 and 3. In a similarity search of the very
recently reported compounds 2 and 3 in the SciFinder
database, only scuterepenin H had a structural Tanimoto
similarity score ≥85.20,21 When 2 and 3 were discovered from
an endophytic Nodulisporium fungus derived from the
medicinal plant “Cyclosorus parasiticus” (accepted name
Thelypteris parasitica) [Aspleniaceae], each was reported with
no known biological activity despite being tested in vitro for
NO production in lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-stimulated
RAW264.7 macrophages, cytotoxicity against A549, HeLa,
HepG2, and MCF-7 cancer cell lines, and antibacterial activity
against methicillin-resistant and -sensitive strains of Staph-
ylococcus aureus.20 Scuterepenin H was discovered from the
leaves of an unrelated medicinal plant, Scutellaria repens
[Lamiaceae],21 and no known function or bioactivity has ever
been reported for this compound.
In summary, one novel rearranged pimarane diterpenoid,
pestanoid A (1), and two likely biogenetically related analogs,
2 and 3, were elucidated from the MCE Chalinidae sponge
associated fungus Pestalotiopsis sp. NBUF145. At the cellular
level, 1 and 2 triggered significant suppression of RANKL-
induced MAPK signaling by inhibiting ERK1/2-JNK1/2-p38
MAPK phosphorylation and also suppressed RANKL-induced
NF-κB signaling via inhibiting NF-κB nuclear translocation.
This work is the first example showing rearranged pimarane
diterpenoids as potential molecules for the development of
anti-osteoclastogenic therapeutics, which could inspire further
research exploring marine organism resources from MCEs for
natural product drug discovery.
■EXPERIMENTAL SECTION
General Experimental Procedures. The melting point was
measured with a WRS-1A digital melting-point apparatus. Optical
rotations were obtained on a JASCO P-2000 automatic polarimeter in
MeOH at 20 °C; specific rotations were calculated from the optical
rotations. The UV spectra were measured with a Thermo Scientific
Evolution 201 UV−visible spectrophotometer. A JASCO J-1500
spectropolarimeter was used to collect ECD spectra (wavelength,
200−400 nm; data pitch, 0.1 nm; D.I.T., 1 s; bandwidth, 0.5 nm; in
MeOH). IR spectra were collected with a Thermo Scientific Nicolet
iS20 FTIR spectrometer. NMR spectra were determined on a Bruker
AVANCE NEO 600 spectrometer equipped with 5 mm inverse
detection triple resonance (H-C/N/D) cryoprobe using standard
pulse programs. Chemical shifts were recorded in ppm relative to the
residual solvent signals (δH 7.26 and δC 77.00 for CDCl3). HRESIMS
data were measured with a Xevo G2 QTof mass spectrometer. Silica
gel (200−300 mesh) from Qingdao Marine Chemical Co. was used
for normal phase column chromatography. Sephadex LH-20
(Pharmacia Biotech) was utilized for gel filtration. Reversed phase
column chromatography was performed on an ODS-A GEL
(AA12S50; YMC Co., Ltd.). Thin-layer chromatography (TLC;
GF254, 10−20 μm; Qingdao Marine Chemical Company) was
conducted to monitor the separation of extracts and subfractions.
Semipreparative HPLC purification was performed with an Agilent
1260 system (Agilent Technologies) with a YMC C18 column (250 ×
10 mm, 5 μm).

from fresh inner tissues of a Chalinidae sp. sponge that was collected
from MCEs near the Paracel Islands, Hainan, China, by scientific deep
diving in October 2018. The protocols for isolation and purification of
fungus NBUF145 were previously reported.22 The fungal strain was
identified as belonging to the genus Pestalotiopsis by observation of
morphological traits and sequence alignment of the ITS region of the
rDNA in a BLAST search.23 The gene sequence has been submitted
to GenBank with accession number ON040634. The NBUF145 strain
was inoculated on 90 mm Petri dishes containing 15 mL of PDA
(glucose 20.0 g, potato 200 g, sea salt 35.0 g, agar powder 20.0 g, and

Cultivation, Extraction, and Isolation. As described previ-
ously,22 the OSMAC approach18 was employed to prioritize fungi.
The fungus Pestalotiopsis sp. NBUF145 was prioritized and was shown
to produce more plentiful metabolites in Czapek-Dox medium than in
others. Subsequently, the Pestalotiopsis strain was recultivated in larger
scale for chemical investigation. The mycelia were cut into squares
(0.125 cm3) and incubated in 265 × 1 L Erlenmeyer flasks with 400
mL of Czapek-Dox medium (sucrose 30.0 g, NaNO3 3.0 g, MgSO4·
7H2O 0.5 g, FeSO4·7H2O 0.001 g, KH2PO4 1.0 g, KCl 0.5 g, yeast
extract 1.0 g, sea salt 35.0 g, and H2O up to a total volume of 1 L).
The cultures grew at 28 °C for 16 days with agitation (120 ppm)
followed by EtOAc (v/v, 1:1) extraction three times.
The condensed EtOAc extract (120 g, light yellow gum) was
subjected to column chromatography (CC) over silica gel (PE/
EtOAc, v/v, 100:0 →0:100 then EtOAc/MeOH, v/v, 100:0 →
0:100) to afford 13 fractions (Fr.1−Fr.13). Of these, Fr.7 (eluted with
100% EtOAc) was further separated with Sephadex LH-20 (MeOH/
DCM, v/v, 4:1) to give 10 subfractions (Fr.7.1−Fr.7.10). Fr.7.4 was
purified by RP-HPLC (CH3CN/H2O, 23:77, 2 mL/min) to yield
compound 1 (7.2 mg, tR = 45 min). Fr.5, which was eluted from the
column with PE/EtOAc (v/v, 1:1), was applied to Sephadex LH-20
(MeOH/DCM, v/v, 6:1) to give 9 subfractions (Fr.5.1−Fr.5.9).
Fr.5.3 was purified by CC on silica gel eluted with gradient PE/EtOAc
(v/v, 100:0 →0:100) to yield 9 subfractions (Fr.5.3.1−Fr.5.3.9).
Subfraction Fr.5.3.7 was separated on Sephadex LH-20 (MeOH/
DCM, v/v, 4:1) and RP-HPLC (CH3CN/H2O, 24:76, 2 mL/min) to
obtain compound 2 (1.9 mg, tR = 105 min). Fr.5.3.9 was purified via
RP-HPLC (CH3CN/H2O, 30:70, 2 mL/min) to afford compound 3
(0.3 mg, tR = 95 min).

of 1 and 2. The data refinement of 1 and 2 was performed with full-

3; SHELXL-2017/1), respectively.

NMR Calculations. The conformer rotamer ensemble sampling
tool (crest) was employed to generate potential conformers. All
theoretical calculations were carried out using density functional
theory (DFT) in Gaussian 16.24−27 Additional details regarding the
computational method, the optimized conformation geometries,
thermodynamic parameters, population of all conformations, and
the DP4+ probability analysis are elaborated in the SI.
Cell Culture. According to guidelines for the ethical treatment of
animals, animal experiment protocols have received approval from the
Animal Care and Use Committee of Peking University. As described,
BMMs were collected from the femurs and tibias of 4- to 6-week-old
male C57BL/6 mice and cultivated in alpha modification of Eagle’s
minimal essential medium (α-MEM, 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS)
supplemented with 30 ng/mL macrophage colony stimulating
factor).28 Three days later, the adherent cells were applied as
osteoclast precursor cells.
Cell Viability Assay. The MTT assay was used to detect the cell
viability. Obtained BMMs were incubated on 96-well plates (1 × 104
cells/well) for 24 h and treated with gradient-diluted testing
compounds for another 48 h. MTT reagents were added in the
wells, and the cells were cultivated for 4 h. Finally, absorbance at 570
nm was observed using a microplate spectrophotometer (Thermo
Scientific). The experiments were carried out in triplicate, and data
were reported as mean ± standard deviation.
Osteoclastogenesis and TRAP Activity Assay. BMMs (1 × 105
cells/well) were then treated with RANKL (100 ng/mL) and
macrophage colony stimulating factor (30 ng/mL) with or without
test compounds for 5 days. If containing three or more nuclei, the
treated cells were classified as osteoclasts.
