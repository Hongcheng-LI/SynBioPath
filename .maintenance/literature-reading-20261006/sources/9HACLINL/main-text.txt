Tagpyrrollins A and B and Tagpyrrollidone A: Three Pyrrole Steroid
Analogues with AKR1B1-Targeting Inhibitory Activity from the
Sponges Stylissa massa and Pseudospongosorites suberitoides
Di Peng,⊥Xiangchao Luo,⊥Runwei Zhu,⊥Wenli Tong, Yanan Yang, Guoqiang Li, and Qi Wang*

ABSTRACT: Pyrrole alkaloids (PAs) are a diverse class of natural
products with complex carbon frameworks and broad bioactivities
that are usually derived from marine sponges. Stylissa massa and
Pseudospongosorites suberitoides are two independent sponges
collected from the South China Sea in 2013 and 2018, respectively.
We discovered PAs are common constituents in both two sponges;
more specifically, S. massa produces pyrrole-imidazole alkaloids,
and P. suberitoides contains pyrrolidone alkaloids. In this study,
three pyrrole steroid metabolites were obtained. Compounds 1 and
2 are a pair of epimers sharing a new 5/7/5/6/6 pentacyclic
structural configuration, and compound 3 has a new rigid 5/6/6
tricyclic structure. Interestingly, their scaffolds all possess a 6/6
bicyclic system on the featured classic pyrrole/pyrrolidone skeletons, so-dubbed tagpyrrollins A and B (1 and 2, respectively) and
tagpyrrollidone A (3). From a biosynthetic viewpoint, 4,5-dihydroxypent-2-enal probably plays a crucial role in constructing these
pyrrole steroid analogues. Based on our previous study on the inhibitory activity of spongiacidin targeting AKR1B1, a drug target for
the treatment of chronic diabetic complications, in this study we found that tagpyrrolin A (1) also exhibits an inhibitory effect
against AKR1B1.
M
arine alkaloids containing pyrrole/imidazole ring
systems are a group of naturally occurring compounds
marked by their structural variability and biological activity.1,2
Sponges are important sources for producing pyrrole/
imidazole alkaloids,3,4 as well as some soft corals.5,6 Our
group has had a long-standing interest in discovering alkaloids
from invertebrates, especially those collected from Xisha
islands of the South China Sea.7−9 During the past decade,
some interesting pyrrole/imidazole alkaloids were reported by
our group; for example, (±)-spiroreticulatine, a pair of unusual
spiro quinoline-imidazole alkaloids, was acquired from the
sponge Fascaplysinopsis reticulata in 2015 (Figure 1A).7 Then,
a series of highly rearranged nardosinane sesquiterpenoids
were obtained from the soft corals Clavularia koellikeri, which
represented a first case in which pyrrole units exist in this
sesquiterpene family.8 In 2022, dozens of pyrrole-imidazole
alkaloids (PIAs) were isolated from the sponge Stylissa massa.9
Some of these documented alkaloids are characterized by N-
substituted by aldehyde, ethanol, and methyl groups. This
indicates the reactive nature of amino groups in pyrrole/
imidazole alkaloids (Figure 1A). The lessons learned during
those initial discoveries of marine sponge-derived alkaloids
inspired a further investigation by our group that ultimately led
to the identification of tagpyrrollins A and B and
tagpyrrollidone A (1−3, Figure 1B).
Two projects of “alkaloid-targeted” isolation from sponges S.
massa and Pseudospongosorites suberitoides were performed
simultaneously by our group. We were surprised to discover
two similar structural scaffolds from different independent
sponges. Tagpyrrollins A and B (1 and 2, respectively) share
the same 5/7/5/6/6 pentacyclic scaffold, exhibiting a more
complicated structure by introducing an extra 6/6 bicyclic
system under the common 5/7/5 tricyclic PIA, spongiacidin,
which is the main constitute of S. massa. Tagpyrrollidone A
(3), possessing a 5/6/6 rigid scaffold, was obtained from the
sponge P. suberitoides collected off the South China Sea in
2018 (Figure S1).

basis of HRMS (ESI-quadrupole) (m/z [M + H]+ calcd. for

The absolute configuration of 1 was determined as
(17R,19R,20S,Z)-1 by X-ray results; the assignment could
also be determined using NMR data. First, the Z-configuration
of double bond Δ10,11 could be confirmed by the chemical shift
values of C-8 (δC‑8 39.0) and C-9 (δC‑9 30.9) (1 in DMSO-d6).
For 5/7/5 PIA alkaloids, the values of chemical shifts at C-8
and C-9 are influenced by the configuration of double bond
Δ10,11, that is, their chemical shift values are approximately at
δC‑8 39 and δC‑9 31 when Δ10,11 is in the Z-configuration, while
the values are around δC‑8 38 and δC‑9 36 when Δ10,11 is in the
E-configuration. The above description could consider an
empirical rule for identifying Z/E configurations of double
bond Δ10,11 in 5/7/5 spongiacidin PIA analogues. In the
NOESY spectrum of 1, there are no correlations between H-20
and H2-18, indicates the relative equatorial configuration of H-
20. Further, the calculated ECD spectrum of 17R,19R,20S-1
matched well with the experimental one (Figure S4), which is
consistent with the result of the X-ray diffraction experiment.

Tagpyrrollin B (2) (1.5 mg, 0.0008%)12 was obtained as a
yellow oil. HRMS (ESI- quadrupole) (m/z [M + H]+ calcd. for
C16H18N5O4 344.1353, found 344.1353) determined its
molecular formula to be C16H17N5O4, the same as compound
1. Detailed analysis of the 1D and 2D NMR spectra (Tables S1
and S2, Figure 3) of 2, compared with 1, indicated that they
share the same planar 5/7/5/6/6 scaffold structure with the
different absolute configurations. First, values of chemical shifts
at C-8 (δC 39.3) and C-9 (δC 31.2) determine the Z-
configuration of double bond Δ10,11 of 2. The rigid 5/7/5/6/6
scaffold results in the configurations of C-17 and C-19, limited
by the chair conformer of the six-membered ring system, being
assigned to equatorial configurations. According to the γ-
gauche effect,13 the deshielded shifts of C-18 (δC 27.3) and C-
20 (δC 65.5) in 2, compared to compound 1, indicated that the
20-OH in compound 2 was equatorial rather than axial as in
compound 1. Also, clear correlation signals between H-20 (δH
3.85) and H-18b (δH 2.15) in the NOESY spectrum disclosed
the axial orientation of H-20, which is different from
compound 1. The absolute configuration of 2 was confirmed
as (17R,19R,20R, Z)-2 by ECD calculation (Figure S4).
Tagpyrrollidone A (3) (2.6 mg, 0.0012%)14 was obtained as
a colorless oil from sponge P. suberitoides collected off the
Xisha islands in 2018. Its molecular formula was deduced as
C12H15NO5 based on HRMS (ESI-quadrupole) (m/z [M +
H]+ calcd. for C12H16NO5 254.1023, found 254.1022).
Comprehensive analysis of 1H, 13C NMR, and HSQC spectra
of 3 in DMSO-d6 (Tables S1 and S2) disclosed three methines
(δH‑8 5.46, m, δC‑8 71.7; δH‑10 2.93, m, δC‑10 31.7; δH‑11 3.40, m,
δC‑11 65.5), four methylene groups (δH‑3a 2.52, m, δH‑3b 2.61,
ddd, J = 18.5, 9.8, 4.7, δC‑3 28.2; δH‑4a 3.08, ddd, J = 18.5, 9.8,
4.7, δH‑4b 3.15, ddd, J = 18.5, 9.8, 4.7, δC‑4 24.7; δH‑9a 1.12, dt, J
= 12.5, 3.0, δH‑9b 2.46, dt, J = 12.5, 3.0, δC‑9 22.5; δH‑12a 3.35, m,
δH‑12b 3.39, m, δC‑12 64.1), one hydroxyl moiety (δ11‑OH 5.13,
brs), and one methyl group (δ7‑OMe 3.66, s, δ7‑OMe 50.9). The
HMBC correlations (Figure 3) from H2-3 to C-2, C-4, and C-5
and from H2-4 to C-2, C-3, C-5, and C-6, together with the
1H−1H COSY correlations of H2-3 and H2-4, allowed the first
outline of a pyrrolidone ring system. Then, serial COSY
correlations from H-8, along the path of H-8/H2-9/H-10/H-
11, and finally to H2-12 allowed the formation of one
consecutive spin system. Together with the HMBC correla-
tions from H-8 to C-2, C-5, C-10, and C-12, from H2-9 to C-6,
C-8, C-10, and C-11, from H-10 to C-5, C-6, C-8, C-9, C-11,
and C-12, from H-11 to C-9, C10, and C-12, and from H2-12
to C-8, C-9, C-10, and C-11, this determined the extra 6/6
bicyclic system by building one N−C bond of N-1/C-8 and
one C−C bond of C-6/C-10. Additionally, the HMBC
correlation from H-10 to C-7, as well as the HMBC correlation
from 7-OMe to C-7, finally determined the planar structure of
3, a rigid 5/6/6 tricyclic ring scaffold.
In the NOESY spectrum of 3, there are no correlations of H-
11 and H-9, which determined the equatorial configuration of
H-11. Also, comparison between experimental and calculated
NMR data with high DP4+ probability (100% for 8R,10S,11S
vs 0% for 8R,10S,11R) was performed using the GIAO method
at the b3lyp/6-311+g(d,p) level. The above confirmed the
relative configuration of 3. Finally, the absolute configuration
of 3 was deduced to be 8R,10S,11S by ECD calculations
(Figure S4).
Importantly, the same 6/6 bicyclic unit appears in
compounds 1−3 (Figure 1B). This coincidence encouraged
us to figure out the possible manner of construction of
compounds 1−3. A closely related reference concerning the
total synthesis of structures containing the key 6/6 bicyclic
system led us to propose a biosynthesis of compounds 1−3.10
2-Deoxy D-ribose and 3-deoxy D-xylose are two monosacchar-
ide in nature. They undergo ring opening and the dehydration
of one molecular water to produce two key precursors, (R)-
and (S)-4,5-dihydroxypent-2-enal (highlighted in yellow,
Scheme 1). The initial 5/7/5 tricyclic PIA underwent Michael
addition with 4,5-dihydroxypent-2-enal to deliver intermediate
A and then furnished the first six-membered ring system
through intramolecular cyclization from 16-NH2 to C-1.
Further dehydration steps constructed the final 6/6 bicyclic
system, which finally built the scaffolds of tagpyrollins A and B
(1 and 2) with the observed S/R stereochemistry at C-20. A
same mechanism involving Michael addition, intramolecular
cyclization, and dehydration steps between pyrrolidone and
(S)-4,5-dihydroxypent-2-enal for the biosynthesis of tagpyr-
ollidone A (3) was also proposed (Scheme 1).
In our earlier study, spongiacidin-PIAs shown AKR1B1
inhibitory activities.9 This inspired us to test the AKR1B1
inhibitory activities of compounds 1−3. Results showed that
compound 1 inhibited AKR1B1 with an IC50 value of 9.6 μM
(Figure 4A), while compounds 2 and 3 have no inhibitory
effect on the AKR1B1 enzyme in vitro assay under the initial
concentration of 20 μM. Compounds 1 and 2 have the same
planar structure with the only difference being the absolute
configuration of C-20, but their different AKR1B1 inhibitory
activities are significant. For further verifying this result, we
performed surface plasmon resonance (SPR)-based binding
analysis. The results show a considerable binding affinity
between 1 and AKR1B1 (Kd = 3.40 μM, Figures 4B and S6), as
well as a low affinity between 2 and AKR1B1 (Kd = 43.16 μM,
Figures S7).

was incubated with 1 (50 μM), the thermal stabilization of

In HepG2 live cells, we conducted immunofluorescence
experiments.15 Confocal imaging of the fluorescence samples
under an excitation luminescence of 405 nm revealed that
tagpyrrollin A (1) comes with inherent blue fluorescence at
405 nm (Figure 4H and Figure S11). Immunofluorescence
analysis of 1 (50 μM, blue) in HepG2 cells shown that
compound 1 was distributed in the cytoplasm (Figure 4I).
After HeLa cells (also a cell line that highly expresses
AKR1B1) were incubated with 50 μM 1 for 10 min, the
cells were labeled with anti-AKR1B1 primary antibody and
AlexaFluor-488 secondary antibody, step by step. Merged
images showed significant colocalization with AKR1B1 and 1,
exhibiting green and blue fluorescence, respectively (Figure
S12).
In summary, we describe the discovery of three marine
sponge-derived PAs, which all possess the same 6/6 bicyclic
system. New scaffolds 1 (5/7/5/6/6, compounds 1 and 2) and
2 (5/6/6, compound 3) display extraordinary sets of
structures. A key precursor, 4,5-dihydroxypent-2-enal, led us
to propose the biosynthetic pathways of these pyrrole steroid
analogues. Tagpyrollin A (1) with a 5/7/5/6/6 pentacyclic
structure, considered a spongiacidin analogue, is also
determined to be an inhibitor of aldose reductase AKR1B1,
which corresponds to our previous work.9 A detailed study of
the interaction of compound 1 with AKR1B1 is important for
treating diabetic complications. Additionally, it provides a bit
of structure−activity relationship information, since tagpyrollin
B (2), a C-20 epimer of 1, has little activity against AKR1B1.
