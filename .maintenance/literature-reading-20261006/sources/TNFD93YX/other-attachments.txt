ABSTRACT

Taxus, commonly known as yew, is a well-known gymnosperm with great ornamental and medicinal value.
In this study, by assembling a chromosome-level genome of the Himalayan yew (Taxus wallichiana) with
10.9 Gb in 12 chromosomes, we revealed that tandem duplication acts as the driving force of gene family
evolution in the yew genome, resulting in the main genes for paclitaxel biosynthesis, i.e. those encoding
the taxadiene synthase, P450s, and transferases, being clustered on the same chromosome. The tandem
duplication may also provide genetic resources for the nature to sculpt the core structure of taxoids at
different positions and subsequently establish the complex pathway of paclitaxel by neofunctionalization.
Furthermore, we conﬁrmed that there are two genes in the cluster encoding isoenzymes of a known
enzyme in the paclitaxel biosynthetic pathway. The reference genome of the Himalayan yew will serve as
a platform for decoding the complete biosynthetic pathway of paclitaxel and understanding the chemodi-
versity of taxoids in gymnosperms.

Key words: Taxus, genome sequencing, tandem duplication, paclitaxel biosynthetic pathway, gene cluster
