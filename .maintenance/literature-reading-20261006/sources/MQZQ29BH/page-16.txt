TARGETING CoA METABOLISM TO MODULATE SUSCEPTIBILITY TO EXISTING
ANTIFUNGALS

Building on efforts to directly inhibit CoA biosynthesis, broader investigations into
fungal CoA metabolism have highlighted its potential as a modulator of drug sensitivity.
There is growing evidence that perturbation of CoA metabolism, either through genetic
or pharmacological means, can significantly enhance yeast susceptibility to clinically
approved antifungals that target ergosterol biosynthesis (27). By modulating fungal
susceptibility, drugs targeting CoA metabolism represent promising tools for enhanc­
ing the potency of existing antifungal agents and combating the emerging threat of
multidrug resistance.

Pantothenate utilization as a modulator of ergosterol biosynthesis

Evidence of a link between CoA metabolism and drug susceptibility  first emerged
from early genetic screens that discovered the fungal FEN2 gene (encoding a pan­
tothenate transporter) in the context of antifungal resistance (89). FEN2 was so named
because loss-of-function mutations in this gene conferred resistance to fenpropimorph,
a morpholine-derived antifungal that inhibits sterol-14-reductase and sterol-8,7-isomer­
ase, key enzymes in the ergosterol synthesis pathway (115). Further characterization of
fen2 mutant yeast revealed decreased intracellular pantothenate and reduced ergo­
sterol levels (89, 115). The mechanistic link between CoA biosynthesis and ergosterol
biosynthesis was hypothesized to be mediated by CoA derivatives, specifically acetyl-
CoA (the initial substrate of the ergosterol synthesis pathway) (89). In this context,
sensitivity to fenpropimorph was hypothesized to depend on the intracellular availability
of pantothenate and CoA to form the relevant CoA thioesters involved in fungal lipid
metabolism. This suggests that interfering with pantothenate uptake or CoA biosynthe­
sis can indirectly modulate the efficacy of antifungal drugs targeting sterol metabolism.
   Such early studies laid the groundwork for later investigations more directly exploring
the role of CoA metabolism in modulating susceptibility to ergosterol-targeting drugs.
