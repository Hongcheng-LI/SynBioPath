ABSTRACT: Hypseudohenones A−C (1−3), the first rearranged
homoadamantane-type polycyclic polyprenylated acylphloroglucinols,
were isolated from Hypericum pseudohenryi. Their structures with an
unprecedented tricyclo[4.3.1.13,8]undecane-2,4,10-trione core were
determined by spectroscopic analysis, quantum-chemical calculations,
and X-ray crystallography. A method for determining the relative
configuration at C-3 was established by the peak shape of H-28 or J-
value of H-3/H-28. Moreover, 2−3 exhibited significant AChE
inhibitory activity, and the interactions of 2−3 with AChE were
evaluated by molecular docking.

tructures with an adamantyl group exhibited attractive
potential in drug design, since the adamantyl group can be
used as a lipophilic group to adjust the logP value of a highly
water-soluble compound to a more clinically useful region.1
Also, some adamantyl-based molecules, such as amantadine,
memantine, and rimantadine, etc., have been used in the
clinic.2−4 As the most important natural source of adamantane
derivatives, caged polycyclic polyprenylated acylphlorogluci-
nols (PPAPs) from the Guttiferae family, mainly including
adamantane-type and homoadamantane-type PPAPs, represent
a class of structurally appealing and synthetically challenging
secondary metabolites.5 However, these two subclasses derived
other skeletons that were rarely reported, and only 10 1,2- and
1,9-seco structures have been reported to date,6−9 which could
be attributed to the stable caged “diamond-like” core. Even as
an important mechanism for creating the diversity of natural
products,10−12 rearrangement reactions have not been found in
adamantane-type and homoadamantane-type PPAPs.
In this study, hypseudohenones A−C (1−3), the first
rearranged homoadamantane-type PPAPs, were discovered
from Hypericum pseudohenryi (Figure 1). Their structures with
an unprecedented tricyclo[4.3.1.13,8]undecane-2,4,10-trione
core were elucidated by spectroscopic analysis, quantum-
chemical calculations, DP4+ probability analysis, and X-ray
diffraction experiment. The relative configurations of 2 and 3
at C-3 were confidently determined by calculated NMR and
homonuclear decoupling experiments. We also proposed a
reliable method to determine the orientation of H-3 by the
peak shape of H-28 or coupling constant of H-3/H-28.
Biogenetically, 1−3 could be derived from rearrangement of
the skeleton of homoadamantane-type PPAP. Further bio-
logical evaluation revealed that compounds 2 and 3 possessed
significant acetylcholinesterase (AChE) inhibitory activity with
IC50 values of 2.0 and 10.8 μM, respectively. Possible
interactions between 2−3 and AChE were investigated by
molecular docking studies. Herein, we describe the isolation,
structural elucidation, possible biosynthetic pathway, AChE
inhibitory activity, and molecular docking of these compounds.

Finally, according to the downfield chemical shift of C-1 (δC =
87.8) and the degrees of unsaturation, C-9 and C-10 were all
both attached at C-1. In the ROESY spectrum, the cross-peak
of Me-25/H2-22 indicated a trans double bond. Consequently,
the gross structure of compound 1 was elucidated as a caged
PPAP with an unprecedented tricyclo[4.3.1.13,8]undecane-
2,4,10-trione core, as shown in Figure 1.
Due to the rigid caged architecture of 1, the relative
configurations at C-1, C-5, C-7, and C-28 were fixed. Then,
only the stereocenter at C-3 needs to be determined. In the
ROESY spectrum (Figure 2), the cross-peaks of Me-37/H-27a,
Me-38/H-6a, and H-17b/H-27b determined the orientation of
H-3 as α. Fortunately, high-quality crystals suitable for X-ray
diffraction analysis were obtained in a mixed solvent system
(methanol/H2O) using a slow evaporation method (Figure 3),
which not only allowed the identification of the relative
configuration of C-3 but also unambiguously assigned the
absolute configuration of 1 as 1S,3S,5S,7S,28R [Flack
parameter = 0.03(10)].
Comparative analysis of 1D NMR data showed that the
planar structure of 2 was similar to that of 1 (Table S1), except
that the signals for the geranyl group at C-5 were replaced by a
prenyl group, which could be confirmed by HMBC
correlations from H2-22 to C-4/C-5/C-6/C-9 (Figure S1).
Similar to 1, the relative configurations of all chiral centers
except for C-3 have been proposed using a rigid caged
framework. The chemical shifts of C-3, C-17, C-27, and C-28
were shifted from δC values of 58.1, 31.1, 35.4, and 50.0 in 2 to
54.6, 28.4, 29.4, and 47.9 in 1, respectively. From these data,
we speculated that the relative configuration of 2 at C-3 was
different from that of 1. However, partially overlapped H-27b
and H-6b in the ROESY spectrum make the orientation of H-3
uncertain.
Considering the ambiguity of relative configuration at C-3,
several unsuccessful crystallizations were made after trying
various solvent systems. Therefore, the 1H and
13C NMR
chemical shifts of two possible structures, 2a and 2b, with
different configurations at C-3, were calculated via a quantum-
chemical calculation method to ensure the correctness of the
structure (Figure 4). The related parameters of R2, MAE,
CMAE, MaxErr, and DP4+ probability13 were further
analyzed. Although R2, 1H-MAE, CMAE, and DP4+ predicated
2a with higher probability, other parameters were inconsistent
(Table 1).
Taking into account the imperfection of calculated results,
we attempted to assign the relative configuration of 2 at C-3 by
coupling constants analysis, using the confident structure of 1
as reference. In the 1H NMR data, the H-28 signals of 1 (δH

obviously different (Table S1), which indicated the orientation
of H-3 may affect the coupling constant between H-3 and H-
28. The coupling constants of 2a and 2b were calculated to
assist in analyzing the signal of H-28 (Figure 4).14 The results
showed that the coupling constants of H-3 and H-28 were
distinct between 2a and 2b (3.2 vs 0.8 Hz). Also, small
coupling constants of H-28 and H-27b in 2a (0.9 Hz) and 2b

conclusions according to the calculated data.
1. The peak shape of H-28 in 2a and 2b could not be
influenced by H-27b. This implied that we could obtain
the experimental coupling constant of H-3 and H-28 by
selective irradiation of H-27a using a homonuclear

2. The peak shape of H-28 in 2a could be influenced by H-
3 and H-27a, exhibiting a doublet of doublets, while 2b
could be only influenced by H-27a, exhibiting a doublet.
This implied that the configuration at C-3 may be
determined by the peak shape of H-28.
In a homonuclear decoupling experiment (Figure 5),15
irradiation of H-27b in 2 did not induced any change in the
coupling pattern of H-28, which confirmed conclusion 1.
Moreover, a doublet of doublets of H-28 turned to a doublet
when H-27a or H-3 was irradiated, with coupling constants of
4.0 Hz (JH‑28,H‑3) and 7.0 Hz (JH‑28,H‑27a). The results showed
that the calculated coupling constants of H-28/H-3, H-28/H-
27a, and H-28/H-27b for 2a were closer to the experimental
values of 2, suggesting the β-orientation of H-3 (Figure 4).
In order to propose a fast and accurate method for
identifying the relative configuration at C-3, homonuclear
decoupling experiments were also used in compound 1 due to
the same configuration at C-3 with 2b (Figure 5). When H-3
was irradiated, the signal of H-28 did not change, indicating a
small coupling constant of H-28/H-3. Based on the above-
mentioned analyses, it could be inferred that the α- and β-
orientation of H-3 correspond to the small (JH‑3,H‑28 ≈0 Hz)
and large (JH‑3,H‑28 ≈4.0 Hz) coupling constant, respectively.
More easily, the orientation of H-3 can be also determined
according to the peak shape of H-28 at 600 MHz. The doublet
of H-28 suggests an α-orientation of H-3, while a doublet of
doublets of H-28 suggests β-orientation of H-3 (Table 2).
Based on this rule, H-3 of hypseudohenone C (3) was
determined to be β-orientation by the coupling constant of H-
28/H-3 (J = 4.0 Hz) and the doublet of doublets of H-28.
Finally, the absolute configurations of 2 and 3 were assigned to
be 1S,3R,5S,7S,28R by comparing calculated ECD spectra of 2
and ent-2 and experimental ECD spectra of 2 and 3 (Figure 6)
(detailed structural elucidation of 3 was shown in Supporting
Information).

Alzheimer’s disease (AD) is a neurodegenerative disorder
that is associated with reduced levels of acetylcholine.17
Therefore, developing efficient AChE inhibitors is an
important strategy for treating AD. Considering several
PPAPs being reported as inhibitors of AChE,18 the AChE
inhibitory activity of hypseudohenones A−C (1−3) was
evaluated. Compounds 2 and 3 were found to be potent
AChE inhibitors with IC50 values of 2.0 ± 0.1 and 10.8 ± 0.8
μM, respectively. In addition, the interactions of the bioactive
compounds with AChE (PDB ID: 4M0F)19 were examined by
molecular docking using the automated program AutoDock
Vina. The results showed that 2 and 3 could occupy the pocket
of AChE with binding affinities of −11.8 and −10.5 kcal/mol
(Figure S3). The major ligand−protein interaction forms are
π−σ stacking and van der Waals.
In conclusion, hypseudohenones A−C (1−3), three caged
PPAPs with an unprecedented tricyclo[4.3.1.13,8]undecane-
2,4,10-trione core, were isolated from H. pseudohenryi. Their
structures were characterized as the first rearranged homo-
adamantane-type PPAPs. Moreover, we proposed a reliable
method to determine the relative configuration of C-3 by the
peak shape of H-28 or coupling constant of H-28/H-3, which
provides valuable references for structural determination of
complex natural products. Further biological evaluation
showed that 2 and 3 could be as potent AChE inhibitors.
Molecular docking revealed possible interactions between
bioactive compounds and AChE.
