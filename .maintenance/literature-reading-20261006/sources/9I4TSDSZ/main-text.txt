Vinigrol Tricyclic Scaffold Biosynthesis Employs an Atypical Terpene
Cyclase and a Multipotent Cyclization Cascade

ABSTRACT: Vinigrol (1) is a fungal diterpenoid consisting of a
decahydro-1,5-butanonaphthalene ring system with no analogs in
nature. Despite immense efforts in synthetic studies, the vinigrol
biosynthesis pathway remains largely unknown. Herein, we
identified a biosynthetic gene cluster for 1 and fully elucidated
the biosynthetic pathway. By employing an AlphaFold-generated
model structure, we identified the possible catalytic residues of the
noncanonical terpene cyclase and analyzed their function by site-
directed mutagenesis. We found that the G340A mutation opened
a cryptic pathway for an unprecedented tetracyclic diterpene,
defined here as virgarene. Retro-biosynthetic theoretical analysis
provided a solid foundation for the complex cyclization pathway
for the vinigrol scaffold, its chemical transformation to a
structurally distinct bonnadiene, and redirection of the enzymatic cyclization cascade to virgarene. Close inspection of the terpene
cyclization pathway via integrated experimental and theoretical approaches would allow efficient exploration of novel terpenoid
chemistries.
■INTRODUCTION
Vinigrol (1, Figure 1A) is a diterpenoid isolated from the
fungus Virgaria nigra F-5408 that exhibits antihypertensive
activity and inhibits platelet aggregation.1,2 Recent studies also
revealed that 1 induced apoptotic cell death by activating the
PERK/eIF2a arm of the unfolded protein response3 and
exhibited anti-inflammatory activity via blockage of the TNF-
α-dependent pathway.4 In addition to these promising
pharmaceutical effects, its distinctive decahydro-1,5-butano-
naphthalene ring system makes 1 an attractive target for
synthetic studies.5,6 Following Baran’s first total synthesis in
2009 involving simple construction of the core structure of 1
via Diels−Alder reactions and Grob fragmentation,7,8 several
efficient strategies have been established.9−13 Gram-scale
asymmetric synthesis by Luo and co-workers paved the way
for chemical derivatization to prepare photoaffinity probes, but
undesired side products were still recovered during the
synthesis.4,13 Despite these advances in synthetic studies, the
way nature synthesizes this complex scaffold had not been
investigated until recently.
Although the bicyclo[5.3.1]undecane core of 1 can be found
in paclitaxel, there are no known molecules harboring the 6/6/
8 tricyclic ring system of 1, making 1 quite unique among
known diterpenoids (Figure 1A).14 With respect to the
biosynthesis of fungal diterpenoids, labdane-related diterpe-
noids and those derived from dolabellane skeletons have been
analyzed extensively (Figure 1B). The core structures of the
former are synthesized by the successive action of class II and I
terpene cyclase (TC) domains that are usually fused as one
polypeptide, whereas the latter are generated by class I
TCs.15,16 In addition to these well-known examples, non-
canonical TCs have also been identified. EriG,17 a UbiA-type
TC involved in erinacine biosynthesis, and PhmA,18 a class I
TC with noncanonical catalytic motifs involved in phomactin
biosynthesis, are examples of noncanonical TCs. Despite the
accumulation of knowledge on fungal diterpenoid biosynthesis,
the TC responsible for constructing the characteristic skeleton
of 1 had remained unidentified until recently.19 Given the
distinctive carbon framework of 1, the corresponding TC
might have a unique sequence and/or structure and may have

been missed in previous analyses, even with technical advances,

with hidden Markov models, which have enabled the sensitive
identification of new TCs from genomic information.20−22
Therefore, the biosynthetic pathway of 1 and the biosynthetic
enzymes involved therein are of particular interest. The
biosynthetic pathways of structurally unique and/or complex
terpenoids serve as a rich source of new chemistries.23,24
Furthermore, considering that the initial conformation of the
substrate is a key determinant of the direction of TC-mediated
catalysis,25,26 the cyclization pathway affording the unique
terpenoid skeleton should fold the linear substrate in a manner
distinct from known pathways. We expect that such unique
folding of the substrate and the resulting cyclization
intermediates would express unique chemical reactivity,
providing access to the underexplored chemical space
associated with these chemical species.
We herein identified the biosynthetic gene cluster (BGC)
for 1 encoding VigB, a noncanonical TC with a distinct amino
acid sequence and protein size, in the producing strain V. nigra
F-5408. Through biochemical and ColabFold-based structural
analyses of VigB, we identified possible catalytic residues that
are different from those of known TCs. Retro-biosynthetic
analysis based on density functional theory (DFT) calculations
revealed the cyclization pathway of VigB (Figure 1C). DFT
calculations revealed that the highly condensed scaffold of 1
and its cyclization intermediate could be transformed into
distinct scaffolds both enzymatically and nonenzymatically.
While these side reactions were prohibited for native VigB, a
subtle change in its reaction cavity or acid treatment of the
isolated compound enabled us to access a previously unknown
pathway for unprecedented diterpene nuclei, showing that the
cyclization pathway of VigB can be redirected via protein
engineering and chemical transformations.
■RESULTS
Identification of the BGC for 1. First, we conducted
genome sequencing of the production strain V. nigra F-5408.
Our initial approach of searching for homologues of known
TCs did not retrieve any candidates. We then chose
geranylgeranyl diphosphate synthase (GGS) as a probe for
genome mining because diterpene BGCs often contain GGSs.

We readily identified one BGC containing genes encoding the
GGS VigA, the hypothetical protein VigB and the cytochrome
P450 VigC (Figure 2A, Table S1). The amino acid sequence of
VigB was not similar to known TCs, and the protein (589
amino acids in length) was much larger than known TCs.
However, close inspection of the protein sequence suggested
that its central 125-aa region exhibited slight homology to
guaia-6,10(14)-diene synthase (30% identity in the aligned
region (37/125)).27 As no candidate TCs were identified in
the vicinity of other GGS homologues, we hypothesized that
this protein was a TC that constructed the characteristic
skeleton of 1.
To verify the functions of the identified genes, we
introduced the three genes in different combinations into
Aspergillus oryzae to construct the transformants AO-vigA, AO-
vigAB, and AO-vigABC (Tables S2−S4). The transformant
AO-vigABC produced 1, whereas AO-vigAB and AO-vigA did
not produce 1, demonstrating that these three genes are
responsible for the biosynthesis of 1 (Figure 2B, S1, S2, Table
S7). Given these results, the hypothetical protein VigB was
indicated to be a TC that constructs the tricyclic structure of 1.
Recently, Zou et al. reported that the orthologous BGC in V.
nigra NBRC 9238 was responsible for the biosynthesis of 1.19
Characterization of the Noncanonical Terpene
Cyclase VigB. VigB and its homologues fell into a distinct
clade in the phylogenetic tree (Figure S3A, Table S6). As
amino acid sequence alignment with known TCs did not yield
typical catalytic motifs, we constructed a structural model of
VigB by using ColabFold28,29 to obtain insights into its active
site (Figure S4). A DALI server search revealed CLM1, a
longiborneol synthase derived from Fusarium fungi,30 as a
structural homologue. The central region of VigB could be
superposed onto CLM1 (the RMSD value is 4.51 in the
aligned region) (Figure 3A). Although 11 α helices, αN1, A−I,
and αC5, of VigB were located at similar positions to those
constituting the typical class I terpene synthase fold,31 their
connectivity was different from that typically observed,
suggesting that VigB has a new protein fold as a TC (Figure
3BC).
Next, we identified several compounds, including 2−7, as
candidate products from the mycelial extract of AO-vigAB
(Figure 4A). The two homologues in Daldinia decipiens and
Poronia punctata, VigBDd and VigBPp, produced product
profiles similar to those of VigB. NMR analysis revealed that
2 has the same tricyclic structure as 1 (Figures 5A, S1, S2;
Table S8). The crystalline sponge method33,34 analysis
supported the structure of 2 solved by NMR analysis and
suggested the absolute stereochemistry of 2, as shown in
Figure 5B (Figure S5A). We also confirmed that 2 was
converted to 1 by VigC, indicating that 2 is a biosynthetic
intermediate of 1 (Figure S6). Compounds 4 and 3 are
(−)-bonnadiene and its new isomer, possessing a 6/7/5
spirotricyclic skeleton (Figures 5A, S1, S2; Tables S9, S10).
The absolute configuration of 3 was determined via the VCD
method using DFT calculations35 (Figure S7A). The
spectroscopic data, including the
13C NMR and specific
rotation data, of 4 were identical to those reported in the
literature (Figure S8), confirming its absolute configuration as
shown in Figure 5A.36 Compound 5, which we defined as
vinigradiene, possesses the same 6/6/8 tricyclic structure as 1
and 2 (Figures 5A, S1, S2; Table S11). Compounds 6 and 7
are monocyclic isocembrene and cembrene A, respectively

We then conducted a biochemical analysis of recombinant
VigB fused to an N-terminal hexahistidine tag and maltose-
binding protein (MBP) (Figure S9). Upon incubation in the

clearly observed the formation of 2 with several minor
products, such as 3 and 6 (Figure 4B). In the absence of

dependent on divalent cations, as is known for other TCs
(Figure S10). A comparison of the active site residues of VigB

N369DIQDYERD sequences of VigB were located at positions
similar to those of the aspartate-rich and NSE motifs of CLM1
(D114DAE and N243DVLSFYKE, respectively). We also
identified the residues corresponding to the conserved RY
pair (Figure 4CD). The side chain of R339 was positioned
similarly to the known pyrophosphate sensor, while its
carbonyl oxygen sat similarly to the effector residue that
stabilizes the carbocation generated at C3 of the substrate.32
The orientation of these residues suggested that this model
structure reflects the closed conformations of TCs. When the
residues proposed to bind pyrophosphate and Mg2+ ions were
replaced with alanine, the enzymatic activity of most of the

same residues with the ones with similar structures or similar
sizes. Most of these mutants also abolished enzymatic activity
(Figure S11). Among the mutants, Y261F retained the
production of 6, suggesting that this residue was not involved
in the lysis of pyrophosphate moiety. These analyses indicated
that VigB initiates cyclization by lysis of the pyrophosphate

residues are located within the central region of VigB,

We also introduced the mutation at G340, which is
positioned adjacent to R339 and at the bottom of the
hydrophobic cavity. G340 is located at the kink of the F1 and
F2 helices (corresponding to the G1 and G2 helices of CLM1)
similar to the effector residue G207 of CLM1,37 the carbonyl
oxygen of which facilitates the cleavage and release of the
pyrophosphate moiety of the substrate to initiate cyclization.
However, G340 is unlikely to perform the same catalytic
function as an effector because the carbonyl group of R339 is
located at the effector-like position. To test the above
hypothesis, we prepared the G340A mutant and checked its
effect on cyclization activity. Surprisingly, the G340A mutant
afforded 8, which was scarcely produced by the native enzyme,
as a major product (Figure 4B). Introduction of bulkier or
polar residues at this position merely abolished the activity,
suggesting that smaller residues are preferable at this position
of VigB (Figure S9, S13). The planar structure of 8 was
elucidated by NMR and HR−MS analysis, and its absolute
configuration was determined via the crystalline sponge
method, as shown in Figure 5AC (Figure S1, S2, S5B; Table
S14).33,34 The VCD method using DFT calculations35 also
indicated the same absolute configuration (Figure S7B). 13C
NMR spectral calculations and DP4+ analysis supported the
same relative configuration (Figure S14). Overall, the structure
of 8 was unequivocally determined. Compound 8 possesses a
fused 5/6/6/6 tetracyclic skeleton with six contiguous chiral
centers, which is distinct from the structures of 2−7.
Biosynthetic and Nonenzymatic Conversion of 2.
During our analysis, the contents of diterpene hydrocarbons,
such as 3 and 4, dramatically decreased in the mycelial extract
of AO-vigABC, whereas the extract of AO-vigAB contained
multiple products, including 2−7 (Figure 4A). This observa-
tion was rather confusing because 1 was observed solely as an
oxidized diterpene in the culture broth of AO-vigABC (Figure
2B). Oxidation of 3 and 4 by VigC or endogenous enzymes of
A. oryzae was unlikely because no related metabolites other
than 1 were detected by LC−MS analysis (Figurse 2B).
Therefore, we hypothesized that 3 and 4 were nonenzymati-
cally produced from 2 under acidic conditions on the basis of
the following observations. 1) 2 clearly accumulated when the
pH of the fungal fermentation broth was maintained at
approximately neutral by the addition of NaOAc (Figure 4A).
Without NaOAc, the pH became acidic during fermentation.
2) Compound 2 was converted to 3 during silica gel column
chromatography (Figure S15). Similar protonation-initiated
conversions of diterpene hydrocarbons were reported for
eunicellane diterpenes.39−41
To elucidate the VigB-catalyzed cyclization cascade leading
to 2 and to examine the nonenzymatic transformations to 3
and 4, DFT calculations were subsequently carried out. We
recently reported that “retro-biosynthetic theoretical (RBT)
analysis”42,43 can eliminate vast possibilities in biosynthetic
analysis because the conformation of the final product is
relatively fixed in contrast to the high conformational flexibility
of the starting materials. This strategy should also be effective
for biosynthetic analysis with little information on enzymes,

hydrogen transfer. To analyze this enigmatic pathway, we
chose the dehydrated cation of 2 (PD) as the target molecule
and employed RBT analysis to evaluate putative alkyl- or H-
shifts of adjacent bonds to the cation center at the C6 position
(Figure 6). On the basis of the activation barriers (ΔG‡),
energy gap (ΔG), and skeletal variation (RMSD), we ranked
these intermediates from A_1 to A_5. Then, we performed
RBT analysis again for the most plausible intermediate A_1
and obtained intermediate B_1, whose cation center was
shifted to the C11 position by 1,2-H transfer. By repeating this
process (tracing the biosynthetic pathway in the reverse
direction), we finally located an energetically favorable
pathway from GLPP to PD with a sufficiently low activation
energy (up to 14.9 kcal mol−1) and strong stabilization (−26.9
kcal mol−1) (Figure 7A).
The result of isotope labeling of 1 by feeding [1-13C] or
[1,2-13C2] NaOAc into the transformant AO-vigABC was fully
consistent with our computational predictions (Figure S16,
S17). We also conducted the enzymatic reactions of MBP-
VigB employing 10-d-GGPP, 1-d2-GGPP, and 5-d2-GGPP as
substrates. The reactions with each substrate afforded 11-d-2,
1,15-d2-2, and 5,10-d2-2, respectively (Figure 7B, Figures S18−
S20). These results further supported the proposed pathway.
This pathway also provided a rational basis for the formation of
minor products such as 3-7 (Figure S21A): it appears that
bonnadiene-type compounds 3 and 4 are synthesized by the
deprotonation of B_1 and A_1, respectively, and 5 is derived
from A_2, generated by a 1,2-H shift from PD. We assume that
the cembrene-type compounds 6 and 7 were synthesized from
GGPP without the initial isomerization to GLPP. Notably, in
the late stage of the biosynthesis of 2, a skeletal rearrangement
from A_1 (bonnadiene-type) to PD (vinigrol-type) was
slightly endothermic (+5.7 kcal mol−1). Thus, we scrutinized
the potential energy surface (PES) around A_1 and identified
an alternative pathway via B_1′ (a conformational isomer of
B_1), leading to L_1, which undergoes deprotonation
(olefination) to yield 8 (Figures 8, S21B). Given this
kinetically and thermodynamically favorable pathway, we
assumed that VigB suppresses the conformational change
from B_1 to B_1′. VigBG340A switched the biosynthetic
pathway by changing its cavity and allowing for a distal
cation-π interaction to form B_1′, thus opening a novel route
to 8. The results of the 13C-labeling analysis of 8 were fully
consistent with the above theoretical calculations (Figures S17,
S22). These results also support the idea that the isomerization
of 3 and 4 (via A-1 and B-1, respectively) can occur
spontaneously from 2 under acidic conditions (Figure
S21A). To check the feasibility of spontaneous nonenzymatic

■DISCUSSION
We herein characterized the role of VigB in the biosynthesis of
1, adding a new member to the noncanonical TC in the natural
product biosynthesis.44 Although a previous study identified a
VigB ortholog,19 the details of its function and enigmatic
cyclization pathway remained unknown. In the present study,
we proposed the cyclization pathway based on the DFT-based
RBT analysis, which was supported by isotope labeling
experiments. Notably, the previous proposal by Zou et al.
does not involve the hydrogen shifts proposed by our
analysis.19 Our theoretical analysis were fully consistent with
the experimental observations, including isotope labeling
patterns of 1, 2, and 8, and chemical transformations of 2,
thus providing good starting point for further analysis and
manipulation of this cyclization pathway. As the experimentally
determined structure is currently unavailable, we constructed a
model structure of VigB by using ColabFold to identify the
catalytic residues. Although the central region of VigB
exhibited some structural similarity to known class I TCs,
their topologies were different, suggesting that VigB has a
unique evolutionary origin. In its putative active site, we
identified possible catalytic residues that were different from
typical residues. Strict conservation of the identified residues
indicated that these residues can be used as beacons in genome
mining to distinguish whether the targeted homologue is active
(Figure 4C, Figure S3B).
DFT-based RBT analysis not only revealed the cyclization
cascade of VigB but also unveiled the novel chemical route
between bonnadiene-type and vinigrol-type diterpenes, which
has not been discussed previously. Synthetic studies on 4
revealed that its 6/7/5 spirotricyclic core could be accessed by
semipinacol-type rearrangement of the precursor having a
strained bridged 7/8/5 ring system.45 Our study demonstrated
a new chemistry in which 4 and 3 could be generated from 2
by acid-catalyzed transformation, which probably occurred
during fermentation. As the transformation from A_1 to PD

We also found that the G340A mutation of VigBG340A led to
the distinctive product 8, which we defined as virgarene.
Although the same tetracyclic ring system was found as a
substructure of periconiastone A,48 the tetracyclo-
[8.3.1.01,10.04,9]tetradecene core of 8 is a new scaffold as a
diterpene directly formed from GGPP. TCs change their
reaction profiles in response to subtle mutations in their active
sites49,50 and hence are attractive targets for protein engineer-
ing. As shown for VigB catalysis, the cyclization pathway,
which involves a small energy barrier(s), might be a good
target for pathway engineering because subtle changes in the
active site environment can easily induce conformational
changes in the intermediate and reroute the pathway to a more
energetically favored pathway that is cryptic under native
conditions. The model structure suggested that the G340A
mutation narrowed the size of the pocket (Figure S25B). This
modification might be the reason for the expected conforma-
tional change from B_1 to B_1′, which could direct the
pathway to L_1. Interestingly, VigB homologues with alanine
or bulkier residues at this position are present in the public
database, suggesting the possibility that diterpene scaffolds
other than 2 or 8 are present in nature but remain unknown
(Figure 4C and S3B).
DFT calculations combined with RBT analysis not only
provide detailed insights into unknown biosynthetic mecha-
nisms but also elucidated the multipotency of terpene
cyclization intermediates and provide clues for the generation
of complex functional molecules by enzymatic editing or
nonenzymatic (in vitro) reactions. Indeed, RBT analysis
suggested the presence of possible intermediates, such as
A_2 and A_3. These theoretical intermediates could hint at
further mutations and open new routes to unprecedented
natural products, enabling the further exploration of chemical
spaces related to vinigrol/bonnadiene/virgarene. Chemical
transformations based on theoretical analysis can alternatively
provide facile access to complex (di)terpene scaffolds without
laborious screening of mutant TCs and thus be an efficient tool
for the creation of pseudonatural products.51
■CONCLUSION
In summary, we identified the noncanonical TC involved in
the biosynthesis of 1 and elucidated its cyclization pathway
through the efficient integration of theoretical and exper-
imental analyses. We revealed all the steps of the cyclization
cascade, branching into the new scaffold, and chemical
conversions to different ring systems. Given the reasonable
model structure obtained using ColabFold and AlphaFold3, we
also identified catalytic residues and, notably, identified G340
as a key residue that determined the direction of the reaction
pathway. Although functional switching caused by the G340A
mutation was serendipitously identified during our mutational
analyses, our in-depth analysis employing both experimental
and theoretical approaches provided solid bases for the
inherent reactivity of cyclization intermediates and products,
paving the way for further exploration of the previously
unknown chemistries associated with this untapped class of
diterpenes.
