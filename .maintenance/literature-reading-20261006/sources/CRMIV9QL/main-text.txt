ABSTRACT: Cyclopentachromone, distinguished by its 6/6/5 hetero-
tricyclic ring structure, is a key building block in many bioactive natural
products, yet its enzymatic origin remains unclear. We identified a new
class of cyclopentachromone-containing compounds, termed isochro-
mosulfines, characterized by unique C−S bonds. A distinct FAD-
dependent monooxygenase, IscL, was identified to catalyze the
formation of the 6/6/5 cyclopentadiene intermediate, 2S-remisporine
A, from a 6/6/6 xanthone precursor via benzene ring contraction. The
high reactivity of 2S-remisporine A further promotes a spontaneous
thiol-Michael addition reaction with thiol-containing compounds,
forming the C−S bond in isochromosulfines. Additionally, we
demonstrate that IscL homologues mediate a bifurcated pathway of
benzene ring modification in the xanthone intermediate, leading to
either ring contraction or cleavage, which is determined by a critical residue at position 230 to be phenylalanine or tyrosine. Our
findings highlight the pivotal role of IscL in forming the 6/6/5 cyclopentachromone scaffold and offer deep insights into its catalytic
mechanism. Our work lays the foundation for genome mining of cyclopentachromone-containing compounds and shows the
potential application of IscL in biocatalysis.
■INTRODUCTION
Chromone derivatives are prevalent and hold significant
importance as privileged structures in medicinal chemistry.1
Among these, cyclopentachromones, a unique subset of the
chromone family, are distinguished by their 6/6/5 scaffold and
are commonly produced by various fungal species. These
cyclopentachromone-containing natural products exhibit a
diverse bioactivity and structural variety (Figure 1a), as
exemplified by the antituberculosis diaportheone B, antifungal
coniochaetones A and B, anticancer chromosulfine, and anti-
inflammatory remisporine B and sorbremnoid A.2−7
The incorporation of the five-membered ring in cyclo-
pentachromones was accomplished by [3 + 2]-cycloadditions,
where substituted chromones act as dipolarophiles and react
with various dipoles.8−10 Additionally, intramolecular cycliza-
tion via the Reformatsky reaction or persistent radical effect-
mediated radical reactions has also been employed.11,12
Despite these advances in synthesizing chromone derivatives,
achieving high regioselectivity and stereoselectivity in con-
structing the 6/6/5 scaffold remains challenging. Biosyntheti-
cally, most cyclopentachromones are polyketides, thought to
be derived from a key cyclopentadiene-bearing intermediate,
remisporine A (Figure 1a).6 This highly reactive intermediate,
serving as a dienophile, can undergo Diels−Alder reactions
with itself or different dienes to produce remisporine B,
sorbremnoids, and aspergichromones, respectively (Figure
1a).6,7,13 Although remisporine A was proposed to originate
from a xanthone precursor, the precise mechanism of benzene
ring contraction within the 6/6/6 xanthone scaffold to yield
the 6/6/5 cyclopentachromone structure remains unclear
(Figure 1a).3,7,14
Ring contraction reactions, which typically involve intricate
carbon skeleton rearrangement, are crucial in the synthesis of
natural products and pharmaceuticals. Aromatic ring con-
traction is particularly desirable due to the inherent stability of
the aromatic structures. This type of ring contraction often
occurs during the biosynthesis of various fungal natural
products, such as xenovulene A, terrein, aflatoxin B1, and
funiculosin; however, the mechanisms underlying many of
these transformations remain poorly understood.15−18 There-
fore, elucidating the enzymatic basis driving benzene ring
contraction could provide deeper insights into nature’s
chemical strategies and broaden the potential for biocatalytic
applications.
Here, we discovered a pair of new cyclopentachromones,
3S/3R-isochromosulfines (ISCs), featuring distinct C−S

pathway, we identified a FAD-dependent monooxygenase
(FMO), IscL, that catalyzes the dearomatization and
contraction of the benzene ring in the 6/6/6 xanthone
precursor into 6/6/5 2S-remisporine A. The subsequent
formation of C−S bonds in cyclopentachromones occurs
when 2S-remisporine A reacts with various thiol-containing
compounds via a thiol-Michael addition reaction, resulting in a
diverse array of sulfur-containing cyclopentachromones, there-

found that IscL homologues can modify the benzene ring of
the xanthone intermediate through divergent pathways, leading
to either ring contraction or ring cleavage, mediated by the key
residues F230 or Y230, respectively. Our work reveals the
fundamental mechanisms underlying ring contraction in the
formation of the 6/6/5 cyclopentachromone scaffold, which
paved the way for genome mining of novel natural products
within this family and highlighted the potential of IscL-like
enzymes as biocatalysts.
■RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Discovery of Isochromosulfines. We conducted a
detailed analysis of the metabolic profile of Penicillium citrinum
ATCC9849 cultured using YES medium and observed that the
UV spectrum of compound 1 is similar to that of cyclo-
pentachromone derivatives, including chromosulfine (Figure
S1). Furthermore, LC-MS analysis revealed that 1 has a
molecular weight (MW) of 410 Da, distinct from any known
compounds in this family, suggesting it may be a novel
cyclopentachromone derivative. However, the low yield of this
compound in the wild-type (WT) strain and its complex
metabolic profiles, made its characterization challenging.

Notably, the yield of the targeted compound 1 in QCCS was
highly increased, and a new compound 2, sharing the same
MW and similar UV spectrum with 1, was also detected

Compound 1 was determined to have a molecular formula
of C18H18O9S according to HRESIMS data (Table S5). The
1H and 13C NMR data analysis revealed that 1 shares a similar
scaffold with remisporine A, but its Δ2,3-double bond in the
cyclopentadiene ring was reduced, as indicated by the observed
HMBCs (Figure 1d). 1H−1H COSY correlations of H-16/H-
17 and HMBC correlation from H-16 to C-3 confirmed a 3-
mercaptolactic acid side chain located at C-3. Compound 2
was found to have the same molecular formula as 1 based on
HRESIMS data, and its 1H and 13C NMR spectra showed high
similarities to those of 1, however, slight differences at C-2, C-
3, and C-17 suggested that compound 2 is likely a C-3 epimer
of 1 (Table S6). The absolute configuration of C-17 in both
compounds was deduced to be S using Mosher’s method
(Figure S3). Based on the NMR calculations, DP4 analysis,
and the comparison of the ECD spectra with corresponding
calculated spectra, the absolute configurations of 1 and 2 were
assigned as 2S, 3S, 17S, and 2S, 3R, 17S, respectively (Figures
S4−S6). These results confirm that ISCs possess a 6/6/5
cyclopentachromone core structure with a sulfur-containing
side chain at C-3, highlighting the structure diversity within the
cyclopentachromone family.
Additionally, two other compounds, 3 and 4, which
accumulated in this QCCS mutant, were identified as the
previously reported emodin derivatives emodic acid (3) and
endocrocin (4), as confirmed by NMR analysis (Figure 1d).
The production of 3 and 4 has been associated with the
emodin pathway.25 Furthermore, unidentified oxidases in the
host strain could be responsible for the oxidation of methyl
group in emodin, leading to the formation of 3.26 Since
emodin serves as a key precursor for xanthone compounds, the
production of compounds 1-4 was suggested to be regulated
by the same gene cluster.

IscL-Facilitated Formation of the Cyclopentachro-
mone Core and C−S Bond. To verify the function of IscL,
we first expressed iscL in the heterologous host Aspergillus
nidulans LO8030. Feeding 10 to A. nidulans expressing IscL led
to the production of 1 and 2, as verified by LC-MS analysis,
indicating the crucial role of IscL in the formation of the 6/6/5
cyclopentachromone core (Figure 3a). Next, we expressed IscL
in E. coli BL21(DE3) and obtained the soluble protein to
reconstitute IscL activity in vitro (Figure S8). The incubation
of IscL with 10, sulfur donor 12, FAD, and NADPH led to the
generation of compounds 1 and 2, whereas the control group
with the denatured enzyme did not generate any new
compounds (Figure 3b traces iii and iv). These results
unambiguously confirm that IscL can transform the 6/6/6
xanthone scaffold to the 6/6/5 cyclopentachromone core.
The absence of the sulfur donor 12 in the reaction mixture
led to the production of a new compound 16 with m/z 289 [M
+ H]+ (Figure 3b trace v). To elucidate the chemical structure
of 16, we performed large-scale enzymatic assays and
successfully purified this compound. NMR analysis revealed
that the planar structure of 16 is identical to that of
remisporine A, which features a cyclopentadiene structure
with R-configuration in C-2. However, further analysis through
comparison of experimental ECD spectra with calculated
spectra indicated that the C-2 position in 16 is S-configuration,
aligning with the stereochemistry of the final ISCs (Figures 3d
and S9). Remisporine A has been previously reported to be
unstable due to its tendency to undergo spontaneous
dimerization, which has hindered its complete characterization
in previous studies.6 The enzymatic reaction employed in our
study enabled efficient purification of 16, allowing for a
comprehensive structural characterization. Moreover, the

the formation of the benzo[b]fluorene skeleton from the

Based on these findings, we proposed that the enzymatic
mechanism of IscL involves hydroxylation at C-2 of ring C in
the substrate 10, followed by B−V oxidation, ring opening via
the hemiacetal cleavage reaction to produce 15 (Figure 3d). 15
subsequently undergoes decarboxylation, leading to the
formation of compound 16 (Figure S10). These results
demonstrate the unique catalytic capability of IscL to achieve
regioselective and stereoselective ring condensation in
xanthone precursor, culminating in the formation of a
distinctive cyclopentachromone structure, which represents a
novel enzymatic pathway responsible for benzene ring
contraction.
Interestingly, when 16 was incubated with the sulfur donor
12 in the absence of enzymes, ISCs could be produced,
indicating that the C−S bond in ISCs is formed through a
nonenzymatic reaction (Figure 3b trace vi). When the reaction
was carried out in D2O, we observed +1 Da mass increment in
ISCs, indicating the incorporation of one deuterium atom in 1
(Figure 3c). Therefore, these results suggest that the thiol
group in sulfur donor 12 can attack the C-3 position of 16 via a
thiol-Michael addition reaction (Figure 3d).30 Moreover, the
steric bulk of the methyl-carboxyl group, as compared to a
hydroxyl group, may contribute to the stereoselectivity of this
reaction, resulting in a diastereomeric ratio of ∼9:1 for
compounds 1 and 2. Notably, sulfur incorporations in fungal
natural products have been reported in only a few cases, such
as ergothioneine, gliotoxin, and ustiloxin B. In these cases,
sulfur is introduced by the nonheme iron-dependent enzyme
EgtB, glutathione S-transferase GliG, and the P450 enzyme
UstC, respectively.31−33 This study uncovers a novel pathway
for sulfur incorporation into fungal natural products. Given the
efficient reaction between the cyclopentadiene intermediate
16, produced by IscL, and thiol-containing compound 12, we
explored the potential of IscL to convert the xanthone
substrate into a variety of sulfur-containing cyclopentachro-
mones.
To this end, we expanded our panel of sulfur donors to
include 2-mercaptoethanol, thiophenol, and various cysteine
derivatives such as
L-cysteine, N-acetyl-L-cysteine,
L-homo-
cysteine, and N-carbamoyl-L-cysteine, as well as cysteine-
containing peptides, including γ-Glu-Cys and glutathione

IscL, and other cofactors. Remarkably, all tested thiol
compounds successfully attached to the cyclopentadiene
chromone, yielding the corresponding products with high
efficiency, as confirmed by LC-MS (Figure S11). The
significant reactivity observed between the cyclopentadiene
intermediate and cysteine-containing peptides highlights the
potential of this system for selectively labeling cysteine residues
within proteins. This finding lays a promising foundation for
leveraging IscL as a catalyst in the development of probes for
studying cysteine modifications in proteins or for facilitating
proximity-based chemical reactions.
Divergent Pathways of Ring C Modification Mediated
by IscL Homologues. Interestingly, we identified another
biosynthetic gene cluster, ccx, from Bipolaris sorokiniana strain
11134, which shows high homology to the isc and is
responsible for producing a series of ring C-cleaved chromones
including 21 (Figure 4e).34 Notably, CcxS, encoded by ccx and
showing 77% similarity to IscL, was initially annotated as a
decarboxylase involved in the decarboxylation of 3 to produce
emodin.34 However, based on our results, we hypothesized
that CcxS more likely catalyzes the cleavage of the ring C, in
contrast with IscL, which facilitates ring contraction. This
difference in catalytic functions between the two homologous
enzymes promoted our further investigation.
To explore this, we purified CcxS from E. coli BL21(DE3)
and incubated it with 10 and the necessary cofactors, resulting
in the transformation of 10 into 19, while IscL converted 10 to
16 (Figure 4a traces ii and iii). To identify the structure of 19,
we conducted a large-scale enzymatic reaction. However, 19
was proved to be unstable and spontaneously converted into
compound 20, which shares the same molecular weight, during
purification. 20 possesses a similar scaffold of 16 with a C-ring
cleaved structure but has an additional (E)-Δ9,10-double bond,
revealed by NMR analysis. Based on the chemical structure of
20, we propose that 19 may be a C9−C10 isomer of 20 and
that either 19 or 20 could be further reduced to 21 through the
ccx pathway. Additionally, we observed that IscL was unable to
transform either 19 or 20 back into 16, indicating these two
homologous enzymes mediate a bifurcated pathway for
tailoring the ring C of 10 (Figure S12).
The distinct catalytic roles of IscL and CcxS prompted us to
explore the molecular mechanisms underlying these divergent
pathways. Despite numerous attempts, we were unable to
crystallize these proteins. As an alternative, we employed
AlphaFold3 to predict their structures, which revealed a high
degree of structural similarity between IscL and CcxS (Figure
S13). The Dali search35 of IscL and CcxS revealed that they
are structurally similar to AfoD (PDB ID: 7LO1) and TropB
(PDB ID: 6NES), FAD-dependent monooxygenases involved
in the hydroxylation of phenolic rings involved in the
biosynthetic pathways of stipitatic acid and azanigerone A,
respectively (Table S8).36,37 To identify the essential amino
acids responsible for their catalytic functions, we divided the
proteins into six segments and conducted protein grafting
experiments, where each corresponding segment of CcxS was
systematically replaced with IscL (Figures 4c and S13). When
we incubated the resulting six chimeric proteins with
compound 10 and the essential cofactors, we found that
replacing segment IV almost completely converted the catalytic
function of IscL to that of CcxS (Figure 4a traces iv-ix),
highlighting the critical role of this segment in determining the
enzyme’s catalytic activity.
To pinpoint the specific residues responsible for the
divergent pathways of IscL and CcxS, we conducted molecular
docking using substrate 10 and FAD with AutoDock Vina,
aiming to identify distinct amino acids within the binding
pocket of IscL. Combining with the protein grafting results, we
identified phenylalanine at position 230 (Phe230) in segment
IV of IscL as a key residue, with the corresponding site in CcxS
being a tyrosine. Remarkably, the single mutagenesis of Phe230
to Tyr in IscL enabled the enzyme to catalyze the production

Mutations at R200, I221, F219, F232, and Y241 to alanine
completely abolished or significantly reduced IscL activity
(Figures 4d and S15), indicating their crucial roles in catalysis.
Previous studies have highlighted the importance of basic
amino acids in the hydroxylation and decarboxylation reactions
in FAD-dependent enzymes.38 Notably, R200, located only 3.4
Å from C3-OH, may serve as this crucial residue. The
mutation of Phe230 to Tyr could introduce an additional 3.1 Å
hydrogen bond between Tyr and C3-OH (Figure S13). This
interaction may interfere with the initial hydroxylation step,
thereby shifting the reaction pathway to produce 19 instead of
16. Additionally, other amino acids within the active site likely
contribute to substrate stabilization through π−π stacking or
steric interactions. These results provide deeper insights into
the molecular mechanism underlying the distinct catalytic
functions of IscL and CcxS. We revealed that this unique
enzyme family can catalyze two divergent pathways for the
dearomatization of ring C in the xanthone substrate 10. This
versatility highlights the adaptive evolution of IscL-like
enzymes in modifying xanthone compounds and demonstrates
nature’s remarkable ability to develop diverse enzymatic
functions. Moreover, these insights provide new opportunities
for discovering novel xanthone derivatives through genome
mining.
■CONCLUSION
Cyclopentachromone is an important scaffold found in many
bioactive natural products, yet the biosynthetic origin of its
core structure has remained elusive. This study uncovers the
molecular basis for the biosynthesis of this important family of
natural products. A unique FAD-dependent monooxygenase,
IscL, was characterized to catalyze benzene ring contraction of
xanthone intermediate 10 to produce the key intermediate 16.
The highly reactive cyclopentadiene derivative 16 undergoes
spontaneous regioselective and stereoselective C−S bond
formation via thiol-Michael addition, representing a novel
pathway for sulfur incorporation with significant potential for
biochemical applications. These findings highlight the
remarkable strategies employed by nature in the biosynthesis
of cyclopentachromone derivatives, offering valuable insights
for future application in drug development and synthetic
biology. Furthermore, we identified two paradigms for the
dearomatization of the ring C in 10, leading to the formation
of either the 6/6/5 cyclopentachromone scaffold or the ring-
cleaved chromone scaffold, dictated by the key amino acids
Phe230 or Tyr230, respectively. This discovery positions IscL-
like enzymes as versatile catalytic tools for modifying the
xanthone scaffold and opens up new opportunities for genome
mining of related natural products.
