           Abstract
                 The last few steps in the biosynthesis of the anticancer drug Taxol in yew (Taxus) species are thought
                       to involve the attachment of β-phenylalanine to the C13-O-position of the advanced taxane
                     diterpenoid intermediate baccatin III to yield N-debenzoyl-2′-deoxytaxol, followed by hydroxylation
                  on the side chain at the C2′-position to afford N-debenzoyltaxol, and finally N-benzoylation to
                   complete the pathway. A cDNA encoding the N-benzoyl transferase that catalyzes the terminal step
                     of the reaction sequence was previously isolated from a family of transferase clones (derived fromNIH-PA                   an induced Taxus cell cDNA library) by functional characterization of the corresponding
                   recombinant enzyme using the available surrogate substrate N-debenzoyl-2′-deoxytaxol [Walker et
                             al., Proc. Natl. Acad. Sci. USA 99(2002) 9166–9171]. Semi-synthetic N-debenzoyltaxol wasAuthor                 prepared by coupling of 7-triethylsilybaccatin III and (2R,3S)-β-phenylisoserine protected as the
                N-Boc N,O-isopropylidene derivative by means of carbodiimide activation and formic acid
                      deprotections. The selectivity of the recombinant N-transferase for N-debenzoyltaxol was evaluated,
                  and the enzyme was shown to prefer, by a catalytic efficiency factor of two, N-debenzoyltaxol over
                    N-debenzoyl-2′-deoxytaxol as the taxoid co-substrate in the benzoyl transfer reaction, consistent withManuscript                  the assembly sequence involving 2′-hydroxylation prior to N-benzoylation. Selectivity for the acyl/
                  aroyl-CoA co-substrate was also examined, and the enzyme was shown to prefer benzoyl CoA.
                     Transfer from tigloyl-CoA to N-debenzoyltaxol to afford cephalomannine (taxol B) was not
                    observed, nor was transfer observed from hexanoyl-CoA or butanoyl-CoA to yield taxol C or taxol
                  D, respectively. These results support the proposed sequence of reactions for C13-O-side chain
                   assembly in Taxol biosynthesis, and suggest that other N-transferases are responsible for the
                    formation of related, late pathway, N-acylated taxoids.

                            Taxol [1] (generic name paclitaxel, 7,Fig. 1) is a taxane diterpenoid (taxoid) derived from yew
                             (Taxus) species [2] that is now well established as a potent antineoplastic drug of wide
                                application in cancer intervention [3,4]. Elegant total syntheses of Taxol have been devised
                                 [5] but none are commercially viable [6], and the destructive isolation of the drug from the
                                   original, low-yielding source (the bark of the Pacific yew, Taxus brevifolia) could not be
                               sustained [7]. The increased demand for Taxol has thus led to semisynthetic methods ofNIH-PA
                              production [8] involving the attachment of the functionally important N-benzoyl
                               phenylisoserine side chain to the C13-O-position of more readily available derivatives of
                               baccatin III (4,Fig. 1) that can be isolated in higher yield from the renewable needles of variousAuthor
                           Taxus species. It is clear that, for the foreseeable future, the supply of Taxol and its precursors
                                 for semisyntheses must continue to rely on yew species [9] or derived cell cultures [10], and
                                   that drug sourcing and cost will remain significant issues [11]. It is important, therefore, to
                              understand the biosynthesis of Taxol and its underlying molecular genetics, with the aim of
                            improving drug yields by transgenic means, particularly by Taxus cell culture productionManuscript                           methods [12].

                         The biosynthesis of Taxol requires nearly 20 steps beginning with the prenyl transferase-
                            mediated construction of the universal diterpenoid precursor geranylgeranyl diphosphate
                           which is cyclized, in the committed step, to taxa-4(5),11(12)-diene [13]. This parental olefin
                                       is then functionalized by a series of oxygenations, acylations, and other reactions to yield the
                                    late stage intermediate baccatin III (4,Fig. 1) [14], upon which the functionally significant N-
                             benzoyl phenylisoserine side chain [15] is then assembled at the C13-O-position to complete
                                the pathway to Taxol (7).NIH-PA
                            Feeding studies with Taxus tissues, using both amino acid precursors and advanced taxoid
                                intermediates, metabolite profiling of Taxus cells in culture, and demonstration of relevant
                                    activities in cell free enzyme systems (see [14,16,17] for reviews of this work) have indicatedAuthor                                   that side chain construction requires five steps (Fig. 1) involving aminomutase-catalyzed
                              conversion of (S)-α-phenylalanine (1) to (R)-β-phenylalanine (2), ligation to the corresponding
                     CoA ester (3), transfer of the aminoacyl group to the C13-hydroxyl of baccatin III (4) to yield
                             N-debenzoyl-2′-deoxytaxol (i.e., 13-O-β-phenylalanoyl baccatin III, 5), hydroxylation at the
                                 2′-position in the side chain to afford N-debenzoyltaxol (i.e., 13-O-phenylisoserinoyl baccatinManuscript                                     III, 6), and N-benzoylation as the final side chain modification.

                          Genes encoding the phenylalanine aminomutase [18] and the two responsible side chain
                                 transferases [19,20] have been isolated. In the case of the transfer reactions, functional
                              screening of a family of previously acquired Taxus acyl/aroyl transferase cDNAs [13] yielded
                                the C13 phenylpropanoid side chain-CoA acyl transferase that mediates the 13-O-esterification
                               of baccatin III [20], and the benzoyl CoA-dependent transferase responsible for the final
                             benzamidation step of the Taxol pathway [19]. The lack of absolute substrate specificity by
                                the recombinant transferases led to some ambiguities with regard to the precise timing of
                              hydroxylation and acyl transfer steps in side chain assembly [14] that were only recentlyNIH-PA
                               resolved by demonstration that the cytochrome P450 2′-hydroxylase operates only on N-
                              debenzoyl-2′-deoxytaxol (5), thus implicating the construction sequence of O-esterification,
                             followed by 2′-hydroxylation, followed by N-benzamidation [17]. In this context, it isAuthor                              important to note that the recombinant terminal N-transferase was characterized using the more
                                 readily available N-debenzoyl-2′-deoxytaxol (5) as a surrogate taxoid co-substrate [19]. With
                                the recent preparation of N-debenzoyltaxol (6) described herein, the selectivity of this
                                 transferase for both acyl/aroyl-CoA and taxoid co-substrates was revisited. The implications
                               of these findings for the production of Taxol (7), and for the origin of the closely related taxoidsManuscript                            cephalomannine (taxol B, 8), taxol C (9) and taxcultine (taxol D, 10) (Fig. 1), are discussed.

           Results

               Selectivity for the taxoid co-substrate
                             Considerable evidence now indicates that the final stage of Taxol biosynthesis involvesNIH-PA
                            assembly of the N-benzoyl phenylisoserinoyl side chain at the C13-O-position of the advanced
                               intermediate baccatin III (4, Fig. 1), with the terminal step of the reaction sequence being the
                             N-benzoylation of 3′-N-debenzoyltaxol (6) to complete the pathway to Taxol (7) [14,16,17].Author                  A cDNA encoding the presumptive N-benzoyl transferase was previously isolated by
                                functional screening of a family of Taxus acyl/aroyl transferase clones [13], and the
                             recombinant enzyme was characterized using the surrogate substrate 3′-N-debenzoyl-2′-
                             deoxytaxol (5), not 3′-Ndebenzoyltaxol (6), because the former was more readily available
                                  [19]. With the recent synthetic preparation of N-debenzoyltaxol, described herein, it becameManuscript                            possible to compare the presumed natural substrate of the reaction with the previously
                          employed surrogate substrate, and to determine whether selectivity of this enzyme for the
                               taxoid co-substrate is consistent with the proposed side chain assembly sequence (Fig. 1) in
                           which 2′-hydroxylation precedes N-benzoylation, and not the reverse in which N-benzoylation
                                   (to 2′-deoxytaxol) precedes 2′-hydroxylation.

                         The recombinant N-benzoyl transferase (the TAX10 clone, previously designated DBTNBT
                                 for 3′-N-debenzoyl-2′-deoxytaxol N-benzoyl transferase) was prepared using protocols similar
                                 to those described previously [19] which involve expression of the corresponding cDNA in E.
                                   coli and purification of the resulting soluble enzyme (to 75% purity by SDS-PAGE) byNIH-PA
                             chromatographic means. Assay conditions were optimized, and reaction linearity with respect

           Discussion

                           Because the N-benzoyl transferase catalyzes the final step in side chain construction, it was
                                 anticipated that this enzyme would show a kinetic preference for the last intermediate of theManuscript                         Taxol biosynthetic pathway N-debenzoyltaxol (6) [14]. As demonstrated in the present work,
                            however, the selectivity of the N-benzoyl transferase for N-debenzoyltaxol over N-
                              debenzoyl-2′-deoxytaxol (5) as substrate is quite modest (i.e., a two-fold difference in kinetic
                                efficiency based on V/Km); nevertheless, it appears to be sufficient, in combination with the
                                  specificity of the 2′-hydroxylase for N-debenzoyl-2′-deoxytaxol (5) as substrate [17], to direct
                                the pathway sequence as proposed by 2′-hydroxylation in the side chain followed by N-
                               benzoylation. The reverse sequence, involving N-benzoylation of N-debenzoyl-2′-deoxytaxol
                                 prior to 2′-hydroxylation would yield 2′-deoxytaxol, and this product would be expected to
                            accumulate because it is not an effective substrate for the 2′-hydroxylase to afford TaxolNIH-PA                              [17]. 2′-Deoxytaxol, however, is not observed in metabolite profiling of Taxus cell cultures
                              [30–32], although both N-debenzoyltaxol and N-debenzoyl-2′-deoxytaxol have been noted at
                          low levels.Author                         The related N-acylated derivatives taxol B, C and D (8 – 10) are, like Taxol (7), prominent
                               metabolites produced in Taxus cell cultures [32]. The second, significant observation to derive
                           from the present work is that the N-benzoyl transferase responsible for Taxol formation does
                              not effectively catalyze N-acylation of N-debenzoyltaxol (6) using tigloyl-, hexanoyl-, or
                           butanoyl-CoA as co-substrate, and so cannot be involved in the biosynthesis of these relatedManuscript                             metabolites. Another transferase, or several transferases, must be responsible for converting
                             N-debenzoyltaxol to these N-acylated taxoids, and the corresponding cDNA(s) likely reside
                              within the available family of transferase clones [13] now being expressed and functionally
                              screened for these alternative transferase activities.

                         The observed selectivity of the N-benzoyl transferase has biotechnological implications for
                                the production of Taxol in Taxus cell cultures, for which a stable transformation system has
                                recently been developed [36]. Overexpression of the TAX10 N-benzoyl transferase in culture
                            might be expected to increase the biosynthesis of Taxol at the expense of taxols B, C and D,
                             with the potential for improving production yield nearly three-fold, if the N-benzoyl transferaseNIH-PA
                                  fully outcompetes N-acyl transferases in the utilization of N-debenzoyltaxol as co-substrate.
                             Transgenic experiments to test this possibility, and to determine the influence of gene specific
                                  silencing, are underway.Author
