                                    Summary


          The extract of Eupenicillium javanicum IFM 58214 showed the antifungal activity against Asper-
        gillus fumigatus. The active principle from E. javanicum IFM 58214 was determined to be
        2-(2-carboxyethyl)-3-decylmaleic anhydride (1), by chemical and spectroscopic method. The methyl
         ester (2) of 1 was identiﬁed with the semi-synthetic one derived from spiculisporic acid. Semi-synthet-
         ically prepared 1 inhibited the growth of A. fumigatus, while other related compounds showed only
        marginal or no activity. To our knowledge, compound (1) is the ﬁ rst example of identiﬁ cation from
        natural sources.

        Keywords：Eupenicillium javanicum, 2- (2-carboxyethyl)-3-decylmaleic anhydride, spiculisporic
                                                  acid, antifungal activity


                                          (Received June 6, 2011; Accepted July 5, 2011)


                                           Introduction


     Eupenicillium javanicum contained in the series Javanica is one of the commonest species in the genus
Eupenicillium and widely distributed in the world. Previously, we found that the organic extract of E.
javanicum IFM 52670 showed characteristic and strong antifungal activity against Aspergillus fumigatus and
identiﬁed the acyclic form of the lactone in compactin as the active principle1). We also reported isolation of a
new cyclic depsipeptide, eujavanicin A, having potent anti-A. fumigatus activity, from the organic extract of
another E. javanicum strain IFM 54704 2). From the above facts, we believed that isolates of E. javanicum are
one of the good sources to discover new metabolites having antifungal activity.
     In the screening for antifungal substances from fungal sources, the organic extracts of 22 isolates of E.
javanicum were subjected to the antifungal test against A. fumigatus. As a result, the extracts from three
isolates showed anti-A. fumigatus activity. In this paper, we report on the identiﬁ cation of the active principle,
2-(2-carboxyethyl)-3-decylmaleic anhydride (1), from E. javanicum IFM 58214, one of the three strains that
showed anti-A. fumigatus activity.

     In the antifungal assay of 1 to 8 by the paper disc method, 2-(2-carboxyethyl)-3-decylmaleic anhydride
(1) showed the strong inhibition against a human pathogenic filamentous fungus A. fumigatus (Table 1 ).
Methyl ester (2) of 1 also showed the weak antifungal activity (Table 1), whereas 3 to 8 showed no antifungal
activity at 100 μg / disc against A. fumigatus. Hosoe et al. reported that nonadride derivatives having the part
of maleic anhydride showed antifungal activity4). We therefore conclude that the part of maleic anhydride is
necessary in order to show the anti-A. fumigatus activity.
     This fungus strain IFM 58214 produced a large yield of SPA (3), which was well established as a natural
surfactant5,6). However, Fujimoto et al. reported that the lethal principle existed in ethyl acetate extract of the
fermented rice of Talaromyces panasenkoi was identified as 3 in a pharmacological study with mice 6 ). It is
therefore noteworthy for mycotoxin study that one isolate of widely distributed species indicated a large yield
of 3, which could be potentially hazardous to animals.


                                       Experimental


    General experimental procedures  The UV and IR spectra were recorded on a JASCO V- 560 and a
JASCO FT/IR- 4100 spectrophotometer, respectively. 1 H and 13 C NMR spectra were recorded on a JNM
ECA-500 ( 1H, 500.16 MHz; 13C, 125.77 MHz) spectrometer, using CDCl3 or CD3OD solutions containing
tetramethylsilane as an internal standard. FABMS was taken with a JMS-MS700V spectrometer.
     Culture condition and extraction  E. javanicum IFM 58214 was cultured at 25 °C for 21 days in 10
500mL Roux ﬂasks containing 250 g soaked rice in each ﬂ ask. The 400 mL CHCl3-MeOH (1:1) solution was
poured into each Roux ﬂ ask containing the cultivated rice and kept for one day. Two layers were made in
each Roux flask and anti-A. fumigatus activity existed in the upper layer. After concentration, the upper
extract (10 g) was suspended in H2O and extracted with EtOAc, and then the organic layer was evaporated in
vacuo.
     Isolation of compounds  The resultant extract (8.2 g) was separated by a column chromatography over
HP 21 resin (DIAION) (1 L in 80 % MeOH) and eluted with stepwise solutions (2 volumes) as follows: 80 %
MeOH, 90 % MeOH, MeOH, and acetone. The 90 % MeOH fraction showed antifungal activity, though the
activity decreased obviously. The 90 % MeOH fraction was a solid like microcrystal (4.0 g) and showed
antifungal activity. It was crystallized from ether to obtain SPA (3). Only mother liquor showed anti-A.
fumigatus activity, though 3 showed no activity. 3 in the mother liquor was removed using repeated recrystal-
lization from n-hexane-AcOEt to give an active fraction showing 14 mm inhibition zone at 66 μg / disc. Total
yield of SPA (3) was 3.34 g.
     Methylation of active substance with diazomethane  An excess ethereal solution of diazomethane was
added to a solution of active fraction (220 mg) in ether, and the solution was allowed to stand for 5 min at
room temperature, followed by evaporation to give colorless solid. The residue was separated by HPLC on
silica gel with toluene-acetone (18:1) to give 2-(2-carboxyethyl)-3-decylmaleic anhydride monomethyl ester
(2) (10.1 mg), SPA dimethyl ester (4) (154 mg), and 2-(2-carboxyethyl)-3-decyl-2-hydroxysuccinic acid
trimethyl ester (6) (55 mg) in turn. Compounds 27), 4 6), and 68) were identiﬁ ed by comparision with published
data.
