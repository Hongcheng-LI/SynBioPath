Stereochemical Assignment of the 36-Membered Macrolide Ring
Portion of Poecillastrin C

ABSTRACT: Absolute configuration at 12 stereocenters in the 36-membered
macrocyclic ring portion of poecillastrin C (1) was disclosed by chemical
degradation and NMR analyses of 1, chemical synthesis, and molecular modeling
techniques.
T
he chondropsin class of compounds, including chon-
dropsins,1−3 poecillastrins,4−8 and mirabalin,9 derived
from marine sponges, is one of the largest (ca. 1400−1600 Da)
PKS-NRPS hybrid natural products.10,11 Chondropsins and
poecillastrins were found to strongly inhibit tumor cell growth
in the National Cancer Institute’s 60-cell screen. Subsequently,
they were identified as a novel class of inhibitors targeting
vacuolar H+-ATPases (V-ATPases).12 Most compounds in this
class possess a 35-membered macrocycle,1,3,5,6,9 while chon-
dropsin D2 and poecillastrin A4 have 37-membered and 33-
membered macrocyclic structures, respectively. These macro-
cycles contain both amide and ester linkages formed with a β-
hydroxyaspartic acid (OHAsp) residue and are also charac-
terized by having a polyketide side chain via another amide
linkage. Due to the complex functionality, instability, and
scarcity of these compounds, only the relative configuration
around the tetrahydropyran (THP) ring in chondropsin A and
mirabalin, comprising 24−26 stereocenters, was initially
determined by NMR analysis.1,9 Our study has focused on
elucidating the complete stereochemical configuration of these
compounds to understand their mode of action on V-ATPases
in detail. In 2017, we determined the configuration of the
OHAsp residue in poecillastrin C (1, Figure 1) to be D-threo by
Marfey’s analysis.13,14 We also revised the mode of formation
of the ester linkage with the OHAsp residue by comparing the
degradation product of natural 1 with synthetic specimens.14
This methodology has subsequently facilitated structural
revision across several related compounds.7,8
As part of our continuing efforts to complete configurational
analyses, herein, we report determination of the absolute
configuration at 12 stereocenters in the macrocyclic portion of
1. Initially, we employed NMR analysis to independently
determine the relative configurations within three subunits
(Figure 1); subunit a containing three stereogenic centers
(C33−C35), subunit b with two stereogenic centers (C28−
C29), and subunit c with seven stereogenic centers (C16−
C25). Subsequently, the absolute configurations at C28 and
C29 in subunit b were determined by a combination of the
chemical degradation of natural compound 1 and chemical
synthesis. Finally, the configurational relationships between
subunits a and c, relative to subunit b, were elucidated by both
NMR analysis and molecular modeling techniques.

In 1H NMR and DQF−COSY spectra of natural 1 measured
at 25 °C, neither broad nor duplicated signal was observed,
indicating the presence of only one conformational isomer,
although there may be some fluctuation.15 Relative config-
urations of chiral centers in each subunit a−c in 1 were then
determined by analyzing vicinal coupling constants (3JH,H)
obtained from 1H NMR and DQF−COSY spectra, along with
relevant NOE correlations (Figure 2). In this study, the J-based
configuration analysis was not feasible, as JC,H values were
unavailable due to the limited amount of the sample.
To analyze the relative configurations for C33−C35 subunit
a (Figure 2a), the configuration at C35 was tentatively set as
R*. A large
3JH34,H35 value of 10.4 Hz indicated an anti
orientation between H35 and H34. NOE correlations between
H35 and 34-Me, H35 and H33, and that between H36 and 34-
Me suggested the 34R* configuration (a-1). A small 3JH33,H34
value of 1.3 Hz indicated a gauche relationship between H34
and H33 and, furthermore, when an NOE correlation between
H35 and H33 was taken into account, implied the 33S*
configuration (a-2). Relevant NOE correlations (H34/H32b,
H33/H32b, and H33/H31)16 further supported the config-
uration (a-2 and a-3). Thus, the relative configuration for
C33−C35 subunit a was unambiguously assigned as
(33S*,34R*,35R*).
A large
3JH29,H28 value of 9.2 Hz indicated an anti
relationship between H29 and H28, and a NOE correlation
between 28-Me and H31 revealed that 28-Me and C30 are in a
gauche relationship (b-1, Figure 2b). To determine the
absolute configuration at C28 and C29 as either (28S,29S)
or (28R,29R), we prepared the C27−C30 fragment 2 by
chemical degradation of the natural product (Scheme 1); (1)
methylation of carboxylic acid at C4 and concomitant
methanolysis of the macrolactone at C1,17 (2) global
acetylation of four hydroxy groups including 29-OH, (3)
ozone treatment to cleave olefins including those at C26−C27
and C30−C31 which present at both ends of the subunit b,
(4) oxidative workup to give a C27−C30 γ-ketocarboxylic
acid, and (5) its p-bromophenacylation. We then compared the
chromatographic behavior of fragment 2 with those of
(28R,29S)−S1 and (28S,29R)−ent-S1 (Supporting Informa-
tion), independently prepared by enantioselective synthesis, on
LC−MS using a chiral phase column. Note that Cahn−
Ingold−Prelog priorities at C28 in S1/ent-S1 are reversed from
that of 1; i.e., if the configuration in the degradation fragment
S1 is found to be (28R), then the absolute configuration at
C28 in natural 1 is (28S). It was found that the retention time
of (28R,29S)−S1 completely matched with that of natural
product-derived 2 (Supporting Information). The absolute
configurations at C28 and C29 in natural 1 were thus
determined to be (28S,29S).
The severely overlapping signals in the 1H NMR and DQF−
COSY spectra of 1 initially hampered determination of the
vicinal 1H−1H coupling constants in the THP ring moiety.
However, three NOE correlations (H16/H19, H16/H21, and
H19/H21) indicated that C16, H19, and H21 are located on
the same side of the six-membered ring. Consequently, the
THP ring portion (C17−C21) was determined to be in chair
conformation, as shown in Figure 2c, with C16 in the axial
position, and both 19-Me and C22 are in equatorial positions.
An anti relationship between H16 and H17, suggested by a
large 3JH16,H17 value of 9.5 Hz, along with transannular NOE
correlations (H14/H21 and H14/H23), indicated an anti
relationship between C18 and C15 (c-1). This conformation
of C14 and C17 was also supported by NOE correlations
(H14/H16 and H15/H17), which allowed the relative
configuration at C16 to be determined. With C17 tentatively

Finally, we determined the relative configurations in the
C21−C25 region. A small 3JH21,H22a value of 2.6 Hz and a large
3JH21,H22b value of 10.5 Hz indicated the orientation of H21/
H22a as gauche and H21/H22b as anti (c-2). The severely
overlapping NOE correlation between CH2−20 and CH2−22
did not allow for the determination of the orientations of C20
and C23. However, a transannular NOE correlation observed
between H14 and H23 suggested an anti relationship between
C20 and C23. This assignment was supported by a large
3JH22a,H23 value of 11.1 Hz and a small 3JH22b,H23 value of 3.9 Hz
along with an NOE correlation between H22b and H23,
suggesting that the relative configuration at C23 is R* (c-3).
Additionally, a small 3JH23,H24 value of 1.7 Hz, three NOE
correlations (H23/H24, H23/H25, and H22a/24-Me), and
two transannular NOE correlations (H10a/H25 and H10a/
H27) unambiguously established the relative configuration at
C24 as S* (c-4). The relative configuration at C25 was
established to be S* by a large 3JH24,H25 value of 10.6 Hz and
three NOE correlations (24-Me/H25, 24-Me/H27, and H24/
26-Me; c-5). Furthermore, given the transannular NOE
correlations (H10a/H25, H10a/H27, and H25/H27), H27 is
likely oriented inside the macrocycle, suggesting that the C26−
C27 double bond aligns conformationally with the extension
from the C19−C26 region, i.e., the region from C19 to C28
adopts a fully extended conformation.
The relative configurations for subunit a (33S*,34R*,35R*),
subunit c (16R*,17S*,19S*,21S*,23R*,24S*,25S*), and the
absolute configurations for subunit b (28S,29S) and those in
the OHAsp moiety (2R,3R)14 are now established. We then
conducted a molecular modeling study to determine the
relationship between these partial structures with reference to
the reported methods.19,20
Four model structures X1−X4 were generated by a
combination of enantiomers for a and c (Table 1). Note
that C36 is substituted by an isopropyl group in these models.
DP4+ statistical analysis based on DFT calculations could not
be applied to X1−X4 here due to their large molecular size and
their flexibility. Conformational search was, therefore, con-
ducted (MMFF94)21 for X1−X4 each, and the geometry of
the stable conformers was furthermore optimized (HF/3-21G
level).22 Important twenty-two 3JH,H values (see Supporting
Information) were calculated at the same level, and the average
values were calculated based on the Boltzmann weighting.
RMS values of the averaged 3JH,H values with reference to
the experimental values were next calculated for the four
candidate structures X1−X4. As shown in Table 1, RMS values
based on top 50 stable conformers (89.3−100.0% populations)
indicate that the experimental values for poecillastrin C (1)
match best with 3JH,H values calculated for isomer candidate X1
(RMS 1.37) with (16R,33S) configuration, among four
candidates X1−X4.

In this work, the absolute structure of the 36-membered
macrocyclic portion of poecillastrin C (1) was disclosed by a
combination of NMR analysis, chemical degradation, and
molecular modeling. This is the first stereochemical assign-
ment of the entire macrocyclic moiety among chondropsin-
type macrolides. Many macrocyclic natural products with
complex three-dimensional structures offer diverse pharmaco-
logical properties that have led to their use as drugs.25 Since
the macrocyclic portion is thought to be essential for the
bioactivity of poecillastrins,9,12 the present work will be an
important milestone for structure−activity relationships study
of poecillastrins, which is in progress in our group. Studies are
also ongoing with regard to stereochemical analysis of the side
chain of 1. It should also be noted here that, as demonstrated
in this study, theoretical data easily obtained from a
combination of CONFLEX conformational searches and JH,H
calculations may be commonly used to determine the
stereochemistry of molecules with conformational flexibility.
