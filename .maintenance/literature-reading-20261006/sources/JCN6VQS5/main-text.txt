Introduction


   Polyketides are a representative class of natural products
with enormous  structural  diversity and broad  biological
activities, and thus have been the components of many
pharmaceutical drugs throughout human history. Polyketide
synthases (PKSs) are the central enzymes for polyketide
biosynthesis, and therefore, the underlying mechanisms by
which PKSs synthesize diverse products have been intensively
studied over the past few decades.[1] In fungi, most polyketides
are synthesized by iterative type I PKSs, in which, unlike
bacterial modular PKSs, each catalytic domain is repeatedly
used to furnish a polyketide chain.[2] Although the general
