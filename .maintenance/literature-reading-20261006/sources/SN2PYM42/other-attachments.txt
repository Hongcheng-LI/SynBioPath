Experimental Procedures  
Materials  
All chemicals were purchased from Aladdin Reagent (Shanghai, China) and Sigma-Aldrich (Schnelldorf, Germany). 
Primers and genes were synthesized by GenScript Biotech (Nanjing, China). Phanta® Max Master Mix and the ClonExpress 
II One Step Cloning Kit were purchased from Vazyme Biotech Co., Ltd (Nanjing, China). For cloning and plasmid 
propagation, E. coli DH5α was used as the host strain, whereas heterologous protein and peptide expression were 
performed in E. coli BL21(DE3). Bacterial culture media components were sourced from Thermo Fisher (Waltham, MA, 
USA). Streptomyces corchorusii was obtained from the China General Microbiological Culture Collection Center 
(CGMCC).  
General methods  
Matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionization time-of-flight mass spectrometry (MALDI-TOF MS) was carried out on a 
Bruker UltraFlextreme instrument. Liquid chromatography electrospray ionization tandem mass spectrometry (LC/ESI-
MS/MS) analysis was performed using a Triple TOF 4600 System (AB SCIEX) equipped with a Prominence Ultra-Fast 
Liquid Chromatography (UFLC) system (Shimadzu). High-resolution mass spectrometry (HRMS) was conducted on an 
Agilent mass spectrometer using ESI-TOF (electrospray ionization-time of flight) in positive ion mode (ESI+). The ESI-
MS and MS/MS conditions were as follows: Gas settings: Nebulizer gas (55 psi), heater gas (55 psi), curtain gas (35 psi); 
Temperature/voltage: Drying gas (550 °C), ion spray voltage (5500 V); Energy parameters: Declustering potential (100 V), 
collision energy (35 V, positive mode), collision energy spread (10 V); Mass ranges: ESI-MS: m/z 200–2000 (accumulation 
time: 250 ms), MS/MS: m/z 100–2000 (accumulation time: 100 ms). Collision-induced dissociation (CID) was used for 
peptide ion fragmentation. Instrument calibration was performed using standard solutions from AB SCIEX. 
NMR experiments were performed at 298 K on Bruker AVANCE III 600 MHz and AVANCE NEO 800 MHz 
spectrometers each equipped with a 5 mm z-gradient ¹H/¹³C/¹⁵N TCI cryogenic probe. Two-dimensional spectra including 
¹H-¹H TOCSY for through-bond correlations, ¹H-¹³C/¹⁵N HSQC for direct heteronuclear couplings, and ¹H-¹³C HMBC for 
long-range heteronuclear correlations were acquired to determine chemical shifts. Spatial constraints were obtained from 
¹H-¹H ROESY experiments with a 500 ms mixing time. All NMR data were processed using TopSpin 4.1.1 and analyzed 
with NMRFAM-SPARKY, with ¹H chemical shifts referenced to DSS (sodium 2,2-dimethyl-2-silapentane-5-sulfonate) and 
¹³C/¹⁵N chemical shifts referenced indirectly.  
DNA sequencing was performed by GenScript Biotech (Nanjing, China). SDS-PAGE analysis was conducted using 
the One-Step PAGE Gel Fast Preparation Kit (Vazyme Biotech, Nanjing, China). PCR amplifications were performed 


[Attachment F6X5PAJV; PDF page 3]
3 
 
using a C1000 Touch™ thermal cycler (Bio-Rad, USA). For bioinformatics analyses, sequence similarity networks (SSNs) 
and genome neighborhood networks (GNNs) were constructed using the EFI-EST webtool, while RODEO analysis and 
MMseqs2 clustering were performed using locally installed software obtained from GitHub. Protein structure predictions 
were generated using AlphaFold2 for monomeric proteins and AlphaFold3 for protein-protein multimers, with all molecular 
visualizations created using PyMOL (version 2.5.0, Schrödinger). 
Bioinformatic analysis of JmjC domain-containing proteins (JMJDs) 
Sequence similarity network (SSN) analysis was performed using the EFI-EST platform to examine JmjC domain-
containing proteins (JMJDs, IPR003347) from bacterial taxa in the UniProt database (n = 20,743 entries as of December 
2024) with the following parameters: alignment score = 75, protein length = default, E value = 1e-5.1 The resulting SSN 
was visualized with cluster-specific coloring and highlighted the reported JMJD representatives (Figure 2A). Subsequent 
genome neighborhood network (GNN) analysis was conducted for co-occurrence analysis with several class-defining RiPP 
post-translational enzymes with the following parameters: neighborhood size = 10, minimal co-occurrence percentage 
lower limit = 0. These included class I lanthipeptide dehydratase domains (PF04738, PF14028), class II lanthipeptide 
dehydratase domain (PF13575), class V lanthipeptide lyase domain (PF17914), class I/II/III/IV lanthipeptide cyclase 
domain (PF05147), lasso peptide cyclase domain (PF00733), cyanobactin/thiopeptide cyclodehydratase domain (PF02624), 
graspetide cyclase domain (PF21068) and methanobactin/pearlin MNIO domain (PF05114) (Table S1). A total of 102 
biosynthetic gene clusters (BGCs) containing the class II lanthipeptide dehydratase domain (PF13575) were identified 
across clusters 2, 7, 20, 39, 144, and 163. Among them, cluster 20 contained the majority (66/102) of these BGCs and was 
therefore selected for further investigation. 
To expand our genome mining coverage, homology searches were initiated using class II lanthipeptide synthetases 
(LanMs) from cluster 20 as seed sequences. Specifically, 88 LanM sequences were queried against the NCBI RefSeq 
database using BLASTP (E-value = 1e-50), identifying 335 LanM homologs. These LanM homologs were subsequently 
analyzed using RODEO with default parameters to examine neighboring gene compositions and to retrieve putative 
precursor peptides (open reading frames (ORFs) containing 20 to 120 amino acids).2 BGCs lacking either JMJD genes or 
precursor peptides were excluded, resulting in a final collection of 292 putative BGCs and 1,328 putative precursor peptides. 
These precursors were clustered using MMseqs2 (parameters: --min-seq-id 0.3 -c 0.8 --cov-mode 1) and subsequently 
visualized as sequence logos (Figure S2).3 
Construction of the pCOR cosmids 
Two 3.5 kb PCR products with 19 bp homologous sequences were amplified from the genome of S. corchorusii using 
the primer pairs Cor-L-F/Cor-L-R and Cor-R-F/Cor-R-R, respectively (Table S3). The recombinant cosmids pCOR were 


[Attachment F6X5PAJV; PDF page 4]
4 
 
then generated by homologous recombination between these PCR products and the linearized plasmid pSET-152-kasO. 
For transformation, E. coli DH5α cells were heat-shocked with 10 μL of the ligation product and plated on LB-agar plates 
containing the appropriate antibiotics. After 16 h of incubation at 37 °C, a single colony was selected for each plasmid 
transformant and inoculated into 5 mL of LB medium supplemented with antibiotics. The cultures were grown at 37 °C for 
12 h, and plasmids were extracted using an Omega Biotech Plasmid Mini Kit. Finally, the sequences of the recombinant 
plasmids were verified by DNA sequencing. 
Heterologous expression and fermentation of the cor BGC in S. lividans TK24 
The cor BGC was heterologously expressed in Streptomyces lividans TK24 through E. coli ET12567-mediated 
conjugation of the pCOR plasmid, generating apramycin-resistant transformants. For initial culture, transformants were 
plated on MS agar medium (20 g/L mannitol, 20 g/L soybean meal, 20 g/L agar, pH 7.0) and incubated at 28 °C until 
sporulation. Approximately 1 cm² of sporulated agar culture was then inoculated into 150 mL of PTM liquid medium (50 
g/L soluble starch, 12.5 g/L soybean flour, 5 g/L MOPS sodium salt, 5 g/L CaCO₃, 15 mg/L MnCl₂·4H₂O, 30 mg/L 
(NH₄)₆Mo₇O₂₄·4H₂O, pH 7.0) and cultured at 28 °C with shaking (220 rpm) for 7 days. 
Purification of corsin  
The fermentation broth of S. lividans TK24/pCOR was centrifuged to separate the mycelia from the aqueous 
medium. The mycelial pellet was subjected to triple methanol extraction, and the combined extracts were filtered and 
concentrated under reduced pressure. Meanwhile, the aqueous supernatant was treated with XAD-16 resin (3 times), which 
was subsequently eluted with methanol. The concentrated extracts from both mycelia and resin were combined and initially 
fractionated on a reverse-phase silica gel column using a H₂O-MeCN gradient. Corsin-containing fractions were pooled, 
concentrated, and further purified by Sephadex LH-20 size-exclusion chromatography. Final purification was achieved by 
reversed-phase HPLC using an Ultimate Polar RP column (250 × 10 mm, 5 μm; Welch Technology) with a gradient of 0.1% 
formic acid in H₂O (A) and 0.1% formic acid in MeCN (B) at 4 mL/min: isocratic 10% B for 0-5 min, linear gradient to 
50% B over 5-25 min, then to 98% B over 25-27 min, isocratic 98% B for 27-33 min, and finally returning to 10% B over 
33-35 min. The purified corsin fractions were lyophilized, yielding approximately 2 mg of compound from 10 L of culture 
broth. 
Molecular cloning of corA, corM, corB and corD genes 
The target genes were commercially synthesized by GenScript Biotech and amplified by PCR using Phanta Max 
Super-Fidelity DNA Polymerase. PCR conditions consisted of 30 cycles of denaturation at 95 °C for 30 s, annealing at 
65 °C for 30 s, and extension at 72 °C (1 kb/min). The amplified products were purified by agarose gel electrophoresis and 
extracted using an Omega Biotech Gel Extraction Kit. Through homologous recombination of the respective PCR products

with linearized vectors, we constructed the following expression plasmids: pRSFDuet-His₆-CorA, pRSFDuet-His₆-CorA-
CorM, pACYCDuet-CorB-CorD, pET28a-His₆-CorB, and pET28a-His₆-CorD.  
Mutagenesis of corA gene 
All CorA mutants were generated using a four-primer PCR approach, consisting of two universal primers (RSF-F and 
RSF-R) and two mutation-specific primers (CorA-mutant-F and CorA-mutant-R). PCR amplification was performed for 
30 cycles under the following conditions: denaturation at 95 °C for 30 s, annealing at 65 °C for 30 s, and extension at 72 °C 
(1 min/kb) using Phanta Max Super-Fidelity DNA Polymerase. The resulting ~2.5 kb PCR products were purified by 
agarose gel electrophoresis and extracted using an Omega Biotech Gel Extraction Kit. Mutant expression plasmids were 
subsequently constructed by homologous recombination of the purified PCR fragments with linearized vectors.  
Co-expression and purification of the His6-CorA peptides with CorM, CorB and CorD enzymes 
E. coli BL21 (DE3) cells were co-transformed with the corA-containing plasmid and various combinations of corM, 
corB, and corD plasmids. Transformants were selected on LB agar plates supplemented with 50 mg/L kanamycin, with 
additional 35 mg/L chloramphenicol when required. A single colony was used to inoculate a 5 mL LB starter culture (50 
μg/mL kanamycin) and grown at 37 °C for 12 h. This culture was then used to inoculate four 1 L LB production cultures 
(in 2 L flasks) containing 50 mg/L kanamycin. When cultures reached OD600 of 0.6-0.8 at 37 °C, they were chilled on ice 
for 20 min before induction with 0.2 mM IPTG. Protein expression proceeded at 18 °C for 20 h. Cells were harvested by 
centrifugation (12,000 × g, 15 min, 4 °C) and resuspended in lysis buffer (6 M guanidine HCl, 20 mM NaH2PO4, 500 mM 
NaCl, 50 mM imidazole, pH 7.5). Cell lysis was performed using an Avestin high-pressure homogenizer, followed by 
centrifugation (23,700 × g, 20 min, 4 °C) to remove debris. The clarified lysate was loaded onto a 5 mL HisTrap HP IMAC 
column pre-charged with Ni2+ and equilibrated with lysis buffer. After washing with 100 mL lysis buffer, bound proteins 
were eluted with 50 mL elution buffer (6 M guanidine HCl, 20 mM NaH2PO4, 500 mM NaCl, 500 mM imidazole, pH 7.5). 
Eluted fractions were desalted using C18 Sep-Pak columns, digested with GluC protease, and analyzed by MALDI-TOF 
MS. 
Overexpression and purification of the His6-CorA peptide, His6-CorM, His6-CorB and His6-
CorD enzymes 
E. coli BL21 (DE3) cells were transformed with either pRSFDuet-1 carrying the CorA precursor peptide gene or pET-
28a plasmids containing genes encoding CorM, CorB, or CorD enzymes. Transformants were grown in 5 mL LB medium 
supplemented with 50 mg/L kanamycin at 37 °C for 12 h, then used to inoculate four 1 L LB cultures (in 2 L flasks) 
containing 50 mg/L kanamycin. When cultures reached OD600 0.6-0.8, they were chilled on ice for 20 min before induction 
with 0.2 mM IPTG, followed by incubation at 18 °C for 20 h. Cells were harvested by centrifugation (12,000 × g, 15 min,

4 °C) and resuspended in lysis buffer (20 mM Tris, 500 mM NaCl, 1.0 mM TCEP, 10% glycerol, pH 8.0). After lysis using 
a high-pressure homogenizer (Avestin) and clarification by centrifugation (23,700 × g, 20 min, 4 °C), the supernatant was 
loaded onto a 5 mL HisTrap HP IMAC column pre-charged with Ni²⁺ and equilibrated with lysis buffer. The column was 
washed with 30 mL lysis buffer, followed by stepwise elution with buffer B (20 mM Tris, 300 mM NaCl, 500 mM imidazole, 
pH 7.5) using gradients of 5% (30 mL), 10% (30 mL), and 20% (30 mL), before final elution with 100% buffer B (20 mL) 
at 3 mL/min. For CorA peptide purification, eluted fractions were desalted using C18 Sep-Pak columns and analyzed by 
MALDI-TOF MS. For enzyme purification (CorM/CorB/CorD), fractions were analyzed by SDS-PAGE, with target 
protein fractions pooled and concentrated using Amicon Ultra-50 centrifugal filters, then further purified by gel filtration 
(Superdex 75, XK16 16/60 column) and concentrated using Amicon Ultra-15 centrifugal filters. Protein concentrations 
were determined by Bradford assay, and all samples were stored at -80 °C. 
In vitro modification of the CorA or mutant peptide by CorM, CorB and CorD enzymes 
Reaction assays of CorA modified by CorM: The reaction mixture contained 100 µM His6-CorA or mutants, 10 µM 
His6-CorM, 10 mM ATP, 10 mM MgCl2, 50mM HEPES, pH 8.0. The reaction proceeded at room temperature (25 °C) for 
2 hours. Upon completion, the reaction was quenched by adding formic acid to a final concentration of 0.1% (v/v). 
Precipitated proteins were removed by centrifugation. The supernatant was desalted, lyophilized, re-dissolved in H2O and 
digested by GluC before analysis by MALDI-TOF-MS. 
Reaction assays of CorA or CorAMeLan modified by CorB and CorD: The reaction mixture contained 100 µM His6-
CorA or His6-CorAMeLan, 10 µM His6-CorB, 10 µM His6-CorD, 100 μM Fe2+, 200 μM α-ketoglutarate, 100 μM ascorbate, 
50 mM HEPES, pH 8.0. The reaction proceeded at room temperature (25 °C) for 2 hours. Upon completion, the reaction 
was quenched by adding formic acid to a final concentration of 0.1% (v/v). Precipitated proteins were removed by 
centrifugatio. The supernatant was desalted, lyophilized, re-dissolved in H2O and digested by GluC before analysis by 
MALDI-TOF-MS. 
Reaction assays to determine the cofactors of CorB and CorD: The standard reaction mixture contained 100 µM His6-
CorAMeLan, 10 µM His6-CorB, 10 µM His6-CorD, 100 μM Fe2+, 200 μM α-ketoglutarate, 100 μM ascorbate, 50 mM HEPES, 
pH 8.0. All factors except the variable were the same as the standard conditions. The reaction proceeded at room 
temperature (25 °C) for 2 hours. Upon completion, the reaction was quenched by adding formic acid to a final concentration 
of 0.1% (v/v). Precipitated proteins were removed by centrifugation. The supernatant was desalted, lyophilized, re-
dissolved in H2O and digested by GluC before analysis by MALDI-TOF-MS. Specifically, to remove the pre-loaded metal 
ions in CorAMeLan-CorB-CorD reaction mixture, EDTA treatment was carried out at a final concentration of 20 µM before

Reaction assays to determine the leader-dependence of CorB-CorD: The reaction mixture contained 100 µM His6-

Reaction assays to determine the leader-dependence and in trans activity of CorM: The reaction mixture contained

proceeded at room temperature (25 °C) for 2 hours. Upon completion, the reaction was quenched by adding formic acid to 
a final concentration of 0.1% (v/v). Precipitated proteins were removed by centrifugation. The supernatant was desalted, 
lyophilized and re-dissolved in H2O before analysis by MALDI-TOF-MS. 
Reaction assays of CorAMeLan modified by CorB and CorD in D2O: The reaction mixture contained 100 µM His6-
CorAMeLan, 10 µM His6-CorB, 10 µM His6-CorD, 100 μM Fe2+, 200 μM α-KG, 100 μM ascorbate in 50 mM HEPES, pH 
8.0. All components except His6-CorB and His6-CorD were prepared in D2O. The reaction proceeded at room temperature 
(25 °C) for 2 hours. Upon completion, the reaction was quenched by adding formic acid to a final concentration of 0.1% 
(v/v). Precipitated proteins were removed by centrifugation. The supernatant was desalted, lyophilized, re-dissolved in H2O 
and digested by protease K before analysis by MALDI-TOF-MS. 
Biochemical derivatization of peptides  
Modification of peptides by NEM: Target peptides (100 µM) were incubated with 1.0 mM NEM and 1.0 mM TCEP 
in 50 mM HEPES (pH 8.0) at room temperature (25 °C) in the dark for 0.5 h. The reaction was quenched by the addition 
of 10 mM DTT and analyzed by MALDI-TOF-MS. 
βME derivatization for α,β-dehydroamino acids: Target peptides (100 µM) were treated with 1.0 mM βME in 50 mM 
HEPES (pH 8.0) at 37 °C for 1 h. The resulting mixture was further analyzed by MALDI-TOF-MS. 
Marfey’s assay for chiral analysis of amino acid residues in corsin  
Corsin (1.0 mg) was hydrolyzed in 1 mL of 6 M hydrochloric acid at 150 °C for 3 hours in a sealed glass vial. After 
removing the acid by vacuum evaporation, the hydrolysate was neutralized with 120 μL of 1 M sodium bicarbonate 
(NaHCO₃, pH ~8.0) and transferred to a 1.5 mL microcentrifuge tube. For stereochemical determination, the amino acids 


[Attachment F6X5PAJV; PDF page 8]
8 
 
were derivatized with 80 μL of FDAA reagent (N-(5-fluoro-2,4-dinitrophenyl)-L-alaninamide; 10 mg/mL in acetonitrile) 
at 67 °C for 3 hours with intermittent mixing. The reaction mixture was then cooled to 25 °C, quenched with 20 μL of 6 M 
HCl, and diluted with 100 μL methanol for subsequent analysis. Parallel derivatization reactions were performed using 
authentic L-amino acid standards. The absolute configuration of the MeLan ring was established by comparative analysis 
with DL-MeLan-containing nisin as the chiral reference standard. 
 Chromatographic separation was performed on an Agilent 1260 HPLC system coupled to a G6530 Q-TOF mass 
spectrometer, using a Zorbax SB-C18 column (2.1 × 150 mm, 3.5 μm particle size). The mobile phase consisted of (A) 
0.1% formic acid in water and (B) 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile, delivered at 0.2 mL/min with the following gradient 
program: 1% B (0-2 min), 1-30% B (2-4 min), 30-80% B (4-14 min), 80-99% B (14-15 min), isocratic 99% B (15-17 min), 
returning to 1% B (17-17.5 min), and re-equilibration at 1% B (17.5-20 min). Mass spectrometric detection was conducted 
in positive ionization mode (50-2000 m/z range), with specific monitoring of FDAA-derivatized amino acid adducts by 
extracted ion chromatogram (EIC) for stereochemical determination. 
Desulfurization and linearization of cyclized peptides  
The modified peptide (1.0 mg) was suspended in a 1:1 mixture of deuterated methanol (CD₃OD, 500 μL) and 
deuterium oxide (D₂O, 500 μL) in a Reacti-Vial™ containing nickel(II) chloride (10 mg). Sodium borodeuteride (NaBD₄, 
10 mg) was added to the suspension, and the vial was immediately sealed under inert atmosphere. The reduction reaction 
proceeded at 50 °C for 4 hours with constant agitation. The resulting mixture was concentrated by rotary evaporation and 
lyophilized to complete dryness. The desulfurized product was subsequently desalted, subjected to GluC enzymatic 
digestion, and purified by reversed-phase HPLC (C18 column, 0.1% formic acid/acetonitrile gradient). Final structural 
characterization was achieved by liquid chromatography-high resolution tandem mass spectrometry (LC-HRMS/MS) using 
positive ion mode electrospray ionization. 
Titration of CAS/EBT-metal complexes by corsin4, 5  
Stock solutions for metal-binding assays were prepared as follows: (1) For the CAS-Cu(II) system, a solution 
containing 400 μM chrome azurol S (CAS), 800 μM hexadecyltrimethylammonium bromide (HDTMA), and 22 μM CuSO₄ 
was prepared in 50 mM HEPES buffer (pH 7.5); (2) For the CAS-Fe(III) system, a solution containing 400 μM CAS, 800 
μM HDTMA, and 80 μM FeCl3 was prepared in 100 mM acetate/acetic acid (pH 5.5); (3) For the EBT-Zn(II) system, a 
solution containing 400 μM Eriochrome Black T (EBT), and 80 μM ZnSO4 was prepared in 50 mM HEPES buffer (pH 
7.5); (4) For the EBT-Mg(II) system, a solution containing 400 μM EBT and 200 μM MgCl2 was prepared in 50 mM 
HEPES buffer (pH 7.5). For all assays, serial dilutions of corsin (800, 400, 200, 100, 50, 25, 12.5, 6.25, 3.13, 1.56, 0 μM) 
in ultrapure water were mixed in equal volumes with the respective metal-dye stock solutions to achieve final assays. After

9 
 
incubation at 25 °C for 10 min under light-protected conditions, UV-Vis absorption spectra (400–800 nm) were recorded 
using a NanoDrop 2000c spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific). As the control, parallel experiments were 
conducted under identical conditions using 100 μM EDTA (final concentration) in place of corsin. 
 
Cu(II) affinity determination5, 6 
Cu(II) affinities were typically determined by competition experiments with the chromophoric Cu(II) chelator chrome 
azurol S (CAS) and hexadecyltrimethylammonium bromide (HDTMA) in 25 mM HEPES at pH 7.5 and 25 °C. The Cu(II)-
CAS-HDTMA complex exhibits an absorption maximum at 610 nm. A molar extinction coefficient of 10,400 L·mol⁻¹·cm⁻¹ 
was determined from Cu(II) titrations into an excess of CAS and HDTMA in 25 mM HEPES at pH 7.5 and 25 °C. The 
Cu(II)-CAS complexation ratio of 1: 3 and the stability constant β3 of 10¹³·⁷ M⁻³ was calculated through mole-ratio method.6 
Titrations of corsin into 22 μM [Cu(CAS)3]7- required an excess of CAS (200 μM) to be added to promote effective 
competition. The Cu(II) affinity (Kb) of corsin can be expressed as follows: 
Kb =
([Cu]t−[Cu(CAS)3])([CAS]−3[Cu(CAS)3])3β3
[Cu(CAS)3]([Corsin]t+[Cu(CAS)3]−[Cu]t)  (1) 
where [Cu]t is the total concentration of Cu(II), [Corsin]t is the total concentration of corsin, [CAS]t is the total 
concentration of CAS, and [Cu(CAS)3] is the concentration of the [Cu(CAS)3]7- complex which can be calculated through 
Lambert-Beer law. 
 
Enzymatic digestion by GluC and proteinase K

at 37 °C for 2 h. The reaction was quenched by adding formic acid to a final concentration of 0.1% (v/v) and analyzed by 
MALDI-TOF-MS. 
Digestion of peptides by proteinase K: Target peptides (100 µM) were incubated with 5 µM proteinase K in 50 mM 
HEPES (pH 8.0) at 37 °C for 2 h. The reaction was quenched by adding formic acid to a final concentration of 0.1% (v/v) 
and analyzed by MALDI-TOF-MS.
