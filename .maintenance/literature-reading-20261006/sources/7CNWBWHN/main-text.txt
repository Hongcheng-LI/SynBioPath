ABSTRACT: A chemical investigation of a cold-seep-sediment-
derived fungus, Pseudallescheria boydii CS-793, resulted in
characterization of 10 novel bergamotene-derived sesquiterpe-
noids, pseuboyenes A−J (1−10). Their structures were elucidated
by spectroscopic and X-ray crystallographic analyses as well as
using the modified Mosher’s method. Compound 1 represents the
first example of a β-bergamotene containing a 6-oxobicyclo[3.2.1]-
octane nucleus adducted with a methyl lactate unit, while 8−10
involve a skeletal rearrangement from bergamotene. Compounds
2−5 showed significant antifungal activities against Colletotrichum
gloeosporioides Penz. and Fusarium oxysporum with MICs ranging
from 0.5 to 8 μg/mL. Compound 4 exhibited an in vitro anti-F.
proliferatum effect with an EC50 value of 1.0 μg/mL.
F
ungal pathogens infect staple and economically important
crops, causing food security and significant losses.1−3 It is
estimated that a ∼20% reduction in crops is caused by
phytopathogenic fungi in both the field pre- and postharvest,
despite the continued release of new resistant cultivars and
antifungal agrochemicals.4,5 Among them, Colletotrichum spp.
and Fusarium spp. are two seriously damaging phytopathogenic
fungi, listed in the top 10 fungal pathogens in economic
importance worldwide, which cause a variety of diseases in
different crops.6 The marine ecosystem is a rich reservoir for
structurally unique and biologically active natural products,7
with enormous potential for producing antifungal agents for
use in agriculture.8,9 As a part of marine natural products,
secondary metabolites derived from marine fungi play an
important role in providing new lead compounds, and many
compounds isolated from marine fungi have been found to
have the potential for the control of fungal pathogens, such as
thiocladospolides B and D, which were isolated from
Cladosporium cladosporioides, exhibiting inhibitory activity
comparable to amphotericin B against Fusarium oxysporum f.
sp. cucumerinum.10 During our search for biocontrol agents for
use in agriculture,11−16 a cold-seep-sediment-derived fungus of
Pseudallescheria boydii CS-793 attracted our attention because
of its inhibitory effect on Fusarium proliferatum (Figure S110).
The search for its antagonistic metabolites resulted in the
discovery of 10 novel bergamotene-derived sesquiterpenoids
(1−10) (Figure 1). Details of the isolation, structural
elucidation, and antagonistic evaluation of the isolated
metabolites are discussed herein.
■RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Pseuboyene A (1) was obtained as a colorless gum, and the
molecular formula was determined to be C19H30O5 on the
basis of HRESIMS data, indicating five degrees of unsaturation.
The 1H and 13C NMR data (Tables 1 and 2) of 1 showed four
singlet methyls (including one oxygenated), five sp3-hybridized
methylenes, one olefinic methylene, three sp3-hybridized
methines (with two oxygenated), one olefinic methine, two
nonprotonated sp3-hybridized carbons (with two oxygenated),
two nonprotonated olefinic carbons, and one carboxyl carbon
(δC 174.3).
Interpretation of the COSY spectrum of 1 revealed the
presence of four discrete proton−proton spin systems
corresponding to a −CH2−CH2−unit (I, C-3 to C-4), a
−CH−CH2−unit (II, C-1 to C-7), a −CH(OH)−CH2−
CH residue (III, C-8 to C-10), and a −CH2−CH(OH)−
moiety (IV, C-1′ to C-2′) (Figure 1). HMBC correlations from
H-4, H-1, and H-7 to C-5 and from H-15 to C-1, C-2, and C-3
connected units I and II from C-1 to C-3 via the
nonprotonated olefinic carbon C-2, as well as from C-4 to
C-7 via the nonprotonated oxygenated carbon C-5, which led
to the construction of cyclohexane with an exocyclic olefin at
C-2 for 1. Further HMBC correlations from H-12/H-13 to C-

Pseuboyene B (2) was assigned a molecular formula of
C15H24O3 with four indices of hydrogen deficiency according
to the HRESIMS data. Inspection of 1H and 13C NMR data
(Tables 1 and 2) of 2 revealed the presence of three methyls,
three aliphatic methylenes, one olefinic methylene, five
methines (three of which were oxygenated and one was
olefinic), and three nonprotonated carbons (with two olefinic
and one bonded to oxygen). The NMR data of 2 revealed
some structural similarity to compound 1. However, signals for
one carboxyl resonating at δC 174.3 (C-3′), one oxymethine at
δC/H 67.7/4.15 (CH-2′), one methoxy at δC/H 51.3/3.61
(CH3-4′), and one methylene at δC/H 44.4/1.98, 1.76 (CH-1′),
accounting for the methyl lactate moiety, as well as the
oxygenated nonprotonated carbon signal resonating at δC 81.1
(C-5) and the methylene signals at δC/H 25.5/2.4, 2.24 (CH2-
3), in 1 were absent in the NMR spectra of 2. Instead, two
oxymethine groups resonating at δC/H 73.0/4.2 (CH-5) and
δC/H 63.3/4.13 (CH-3) were observed in 2 (Tables 1 and 2),
indicating that one hydrogen at CH2-3 in 1 was replaced by a
hydroxy group at C-3 in 2. These observations were supported
by the relevant COSY and HMBC correlations (Figure 1).
The NOE correlation from H-14 to H-4a indicated the
cofacial orientation of these groups, while correlations from H-
8 to H-1 and H-7a, from H-1 to H-3 and H-5, and from H-7b
to H2-4b placed them on the other side of the molecule
(Figure 2). The absolute configurations for the chiral centers
C-3 and C-8 were determined as 3R and 8R by a modified
Mosher’s method (Figure 3). Then, the absolute configuration
of compound 2 was deduced to be 1S, 3R, 5R, 6R, and 8R,
which was characterized as a β-tran-bergamotene-type
sesquiterpenoid with a 6-oxobicyclo[3.2.1]octane nucleus.
Pseuboyene C (3) was assigned the molecular formula
C15H22O3 by HRESIMS data, implying five degrees of
unsaturation. Its NMR spectroscopic data were very similar
to those of 2, except for the disappearance of one oxymethine
group at δH/δC 4.13/63.3 (CH-3) and the appearance of an
additional keto group at δC 198.7 (C-3) in 3 (Tables 1 and 2).
These data suggested that the oxymethine at C-3 in 2 was
oxidized to a ketone group in 3. These observations were
supported by the relevant COSY and HMBC correlations
(Figure 1). The NOE correlation from H-14 to H-4a suggested
the same orientation of these protons, while NOE correlations
from H-8 to H-1 and H-7 and from H-7 to H2-4b indicated
that these protons were orientated on the opposite side (Figure
2). From a biosynthetic point of view, the absolute
configuration of 3 was assumed to be 1S, 5R, 6R, and 8R,
the same as that of 2.

To unambiguously clarify the structure and configuration of
4, suitable crystals were obtained by dissolving the samples in
MeOH−MeCN (1:1) followed by slow evaporation of the
solvents over 1 week, allowing for the execution of an X-ray
crystallographic experiment to confirm the planar structure and
relative configuration of 4 (Figure 4). As the Flack parameter
of −0.2(4) with a large standard uncertainty could not be used
to unambiguously assign the absolute configuration, optical
rotation calculation was then performed. The Boltzmann-
averaged optical rotation value of (1S,5R,6R,8R)-4 was found
to be negative (Table S2), comparable to the experimental
value for 4 ([α]D
25 −12.5 (c 0.16, MeOH)). Therefore, the
absolute configuration of 4 was determined to be 1S, 5R, 6R,
8R.
Pseuboyene E (5) was confirmed to have the molecular
formula C17H26O3 by its HRESIMS data, indicating five
degrees of unsaturation. The NMR data (Tables 1 and 2) of 5
were very similar to those of 4, except for the absence of one
olefinic methine resonating at δC/H 142.3/5.52 (CH-9) and the
oxygenated nonprotonated carbon at δC 68.9 (C-11). Instead,
signals for a methylene group at δC/H 29.4/2.19 (CH2-9), as
well as for an olefinic nonprotonated carbon at δC 132.2 (C-
11), were observed in the NMR spectra of 5. Furthermore, the
proton signals of two methyl groups resonating at δH 1.64
(CH3-12) and δH 1.58 (CH3-13) shifted downfield in the 1H
NMR spectrum of 5, due to the deshielding effect of the
double bond at C-11. This deduction was further supported by
the COSY and HMBC correlations (Figure 1). The relative
configuration was deduced to be the same as compound 4
from the observed NOESY correlations from H-14 to H-4a,
from H-8 to H-1 and H-7, and from H-7 to H2-4b (Figure 2).
Similarly, the chemical shift of the upfield-shifted methyl
proton H-14 (δH 0.81) indicated the β-trans-bergamotene
configuration of 5. The absolute configuration of 5 was also
studied by computed optical rotations. The Boltzmann-
averaged optical rotation value of (1S,5R,6R,8R)-5 was found
to be positive (Table S3) compared to the experimental value
{[α]D
25 +4.2 (c 0.72, MeOH)}, showing the absolute
configurations of 5 as 1S, 5R, 6R, 8R.
Pseuboyene F (6) was found to have the molecular formula
C17H24O4 with six degrees of unsaturation, as determined by
HRESIMS analysis. The 1H and 13C NMR data of 6 (Tables 1
and 2) showed close similarity to those of 5, except for the
absence of one sp3-hybridized methylene resonating at δH/δC
2.19/29.4 (CH2-9). Instead, a signal for a ketone group at δC
197.1 (C-9) was observed in the NMR spectra of 6. These
differences suggested that the methylene CH2-9 in 5 was
oxidized to a ketone group in 6, and this observation was
supported by the COSY and HMBC correlations (Figure 1).
The relative configuration of compound 6 was determined to
be the same as 5 based on the same NOE correlations (Figure
2). From the biosynthetic view, the absolute configuration of
compound 6 was tentatively assigned as 1S, 5R, 6R, 8S.

■EXPERIMENTAL SECTION
General Experimental Procedures. Melting points were
determined by an SGW X-4 micro-melting-point apparatus. Optical
rotations were measured in MeOH using an Optical Activity AA-55
polarimeter. UV spectra were obtained with a PuXi TU-1810 UV−
visible spectrophotometer. IR data were recorded using a Nicolet IS10
spectrophotometer. LC-MS were obtained with a Bruker maXis Plus
Q-TOF. ECD spectra were acquired through a JASCO J-715
spectropolarimeter. NMR spectra data were recorded on a Bruker
Avance 500 or 600 MHz spectrometer. HRESIMS data were
measured using an API QSTAR Pulsar 1 mass spectrometer.
Semipreparative HPLC was performed by an Alltech HPLC system
using an ODS column (Elite ODSBP column, 5 μm; 10 × 250 mm; 3
mL/min). Column chromatography was carried out using commer-
cially available Si gel (200−300 mesh, Qingdao Haiyang Chemical
Co.), Lobar LiChroprep RP-18 (40−63 μm, Merck), and Sephadex
LH-20 (Pharmacia). Thin-layer chromatography (TLC) was
performed with precoated Si gel GF254 plates (Merck, Darmstadt,
Germany). Solvents used for extraction and purification were distilled
prior to use. Peptone from yeast extract was purchased from Sigma-
Aldrich. Rice, monosodium glutamate, and corn steep liquor were
purchased from China Oil & Foodstuffs Corporation.
Fungal Material. The fungus strain of Pseudallescheria boydii CS-
793 was isolated from the cold seep sediment at the northeast of the
South China Sea, collected in September 2020, and identified
according to the ITS (internal transcribed spacer) molecular analyses.
These sequence data were most similar (98.98%) to those of P. boydii
(accession no. OW986361) and deposited in GenBank with the
accession no. OQ390095. The strain was preserved at the Key
Laboratory of Experimental Marine Biology, Institute of Oceanology,
Chinese Academy of Sciences (IOCAS).
Fermentation and Extraction. P. boydii CS-793 was statically
fermented at room temperature for 30 days on modified rice medium,
which was conducted in 80 × 1 L conical flasks containing rice (100
g/flask), peptone (0.3 g/flask), yeast extract (0.5 g/flask), corn steep
liquor (0.2 g/flask), monosodium glutamate (0.1 g/flask), and
naturally sourced and filtered seawater (acquired from the Huiquan
Gulf of the Yellow Sea near the campus of IOCAS, 100 mL/flask).
The fermented rice substrate was fragmented mechanically and
extracted thoroughly with ethyl acetate (EtOAc) to obtain a crude
extract (45.8 g).

X-ray Crystallographic Analysis of Compound 4.24 Com-
pound 4 was crystallized in MeOH−MeCN mixtures. All crystallo-

were corrected for absorption by using the program SADABS.25 The
structures were solved by direct methods using the SHELXTL
software package.26,27 All non-hydrogen atoms were refined
anisotropically. The H atoms were located by geometrical
calculations, and their positions and thermal parameters were fixed
during the structure refinement. The absolute structures were
determined by refinement of the Flack parameters,28 based on
anomalous scattering. The structures were optimized by full matrix
least-squares techniques.

In Vitro Antifungal Effect of Compound 4 against F.
proliferatum. The in vitro antifungal effect of compound 4 against
F. proliferatum was evaluated using a mycelium growth rate method
that is briefly described as follows: a tested compound dissolved in
200 μL of DMSO was added into 20 mL of potato dextrose agar
(PDA) (with the final concentration of 1, 5, 10, 100 μg/mL). After
shaking well, the obtained mixture was divided and poured into five
nine-centimeter Petri plates. Equal DMSO was used to that of the
blank control.31 F. proliferatum was cultivated in LB broth medium at
28 °C for 72 h. Then the liquid culture mycelia was picked and
aseptically inoculated in the center of the PDA medium. The
morphology of the pathogenic fungus was observed after 4 days of
incubation at 28 °C.
Calculation Part. All theoretical calculations were performed via
the Gaussian 09 program package.32 Conformational searches were
conducted via molecular mechanics using the MMFF method in
Macromodel software (Schrödinger, LLC, New York City, NY, USA).
Then, the B3LYP/6-31G(d) level was used to optimize the
conformers. After that, the optimized conformers were subjected to
optical rotation (OR) calculation using TDDFT at CAM.33
