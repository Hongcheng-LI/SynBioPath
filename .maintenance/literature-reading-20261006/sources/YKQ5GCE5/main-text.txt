Discovery of Cytotoxic Barbiturate-Bridged Dimeric
Chrolactomycins from Catenulispora acidiphila TX15

ABSTRACT: Six new spirotetronate polyketides, chrolactomycins A−
F (2−7), and the known chrolactomycin (1) were isolated and
identified from Catenulispora acidiphila TX15. Chrolactomycins D−F
(5−7) feature an unprecedented dimeric skeleton bridged by a rare
barbiturate unit. Sequencing and characterization of a type I polyketide
synthase biosynthetic gene cluster led to the proposal of a biosynthetic
pathway for 1−7. Compounds 2−4 exhibited antibacterial activities,
while 3−7 displayed cytotoxicity to multiple cancer cell lines, with
dimer 5 demonstrating the most potent effects (IC50 = 2.2−7.7 μM).
D
imeric natural products (DNPs), arising from the
covalent linkage of two monomeric units (often identical
or structurally analogous), represent a privileged class of
secondary metabolites distinguished by remarkable structural
diversity and frequently enhanced biological activities.1−3
These complex architectures feature varied connectivity
patterns, ranging from direct C−C bonds between monomeric
subunits to bridged linkages via heteroatoms (sulfur, oxygen,
nitrogen) or alkyl spacers (e.g., methylene or ethylene
groups).1−3 The biogenesis of DNPs typically involves
intriguing oxidative coupling processes, which may be
enzymatically mediated by cytochrome P450s, laccases, and
radical-generating enzymes or occur spontaneously through
reactive intermediates.4,5 This inherent biosynthetic complex-
ity, coupled with their significant pharmacological potential as
drug leads, drives intense research into novel structural
discovery, biological evaluation, chemical synthesis, and
biosynthetic pathway dissection.
In our continuing exploration of novel bioactive natural
products from nonmodel strains,6−9 a rare actinomycete,
Catenulispora acidiphila TX15, isolated from a moss sample
collected from Huilong Mountain (Hunan Province, China),
was prioritized for investigation due to its distinctive HPLC-
UV profiles and antibacterial and cytotoxic activities (Figure
S1). Fermentation and bioassay-guided fractionation of the
crude extract yielded three novel barbiturate-bridged dimers,
chrolactomycins D−F (5−7), along with three new mono-
meric derivatives, chrolactomycins A−C (2−4), and the
known chrolactomycin (1) (Figure 1). Genome sequencing
and bioinformatic analysis identified its corresponding
biosynthetic gene cluster (BGC, named clm), enabling a
proposed biosynthetic pathway for 1−7. Compounds 1−7
exhibited differential antibacterial and cytotoxic activities.
Herein, we report the details of their isolation, structure
elucidation, biosynthetic pathway, and bioactivity evaluation.
The rare actinomycete Catenulispora acidiphila TX15 was
initially fermented in three media (Table S1) under shaking
conditions at 30 °C for 9 days. The methanol extract of the
YEME fermentation showed the most abundant products
(Figure S1) and was then fractionated by silica gel vacuum
liquid chromatography (VLC), ODS MPLC, and finally HPLC
to yield 1−7. Structural elucidation was performed via
spectroscopic analysis (HRESIMS, NMR, ECD) comple-
mented with a literature comparison.10

Structurally, chrolactomycins feature a characteristic tetro-
nate scaffold (ring A), classifying them within the spirotetr-
onate family of polyketides.10,12 However, chrolactomycins
possess a unique γ-pyranone moiety (ring D) that distinguishes
them from other spirotetronate congener examples such as
well-known abyssomicins,13 lobophorins,14 and quartromi-
cins.15 To date, only seven chrolactomycin derivatives have
been reported, including chrolactomycin,10 6-hydroxychrolac-
tomycin,16 and okilactomycins.17,18 Although isotopic labeling
experiments have elucidated the biosynthetic origin of the
carbon atoms of okilactomycin using 13C-labeled precursors,19
the biosynthetic gene cluster and biosynthetic pathway of
chrolactomycins remain uncharacterized. Then, we performed
whole-genome sequencing of Catenulispora acidiphila TX15,
and bioinformatic analysis enabled the proposal of a
biosynthetic pathway for 1−7 (Figure 3).
antiSMASH analysis indicated that a type I polyketide
synthase (PKS) gene cluster (clm, GenBase accession number
C_AA111525.1) from the genome of Catenulispora acidiphila
TX15 showed similarity (25−46%) to the BGCs for
abyssomicin C,20 neoabyssomicin,21 tetronomycin,22 quartro-
micin A1,23 and chlorothricin,24 all of which are spirotetronate
family polyketides (Figure S8). Based on comparison with
these characterized BGCs, the clm gene cluster was proposed
to span 80 kb containing 36 open reading frames (Figure 3a
and Table S7). The core clm BGC comprises 5 PKS genes
(clmA1−clmA5) that include 8 PKS modules, which were
involved in the biosynthesis of the polyketide chain (Figure
3b). Five conserved genes, clmB1−B5 (are of 52−61% identity
to AbmA1-A521), were hypothesized to generate a C3 unit and
catalyzed its condensation with the polyketide chain, ultimately
forming the characteristic tetronate ring scaffold (ring A)
(Figure 3). Then, the intermediate I might undergo an
intramolecular Diels−Alder (IMDA) reaction catalyzed by
ClmO5 (homologue of AbyU25), followed by multistep
oxidation reactions to form intermediate II. ClmM (predicted
methyltransferase) catalyzed the 11-OH methylation, leading
to the formation of chrolactomycin (1) (Figure 3).
Subsequently, compound 1 undergoes Michael addition with
H2O, MeOH, and anthranilic acid, yielding compounds 2−4,
respectively. Two molecules of 1 linked by one molecule of
barbituric acid which possibly originate from the oxidative
pyrimidine degradation pathway26 (Figure S9) could form
symmetric dimer 5 and its epimer 6 through two successive
Michael addition reactions. Similarly, compound 1 and
intermediate II reacted with a barbituric acid to create
unsymmetrical dimer 7 (Figure 3). However, we found that
2−4 were unstable during the separation process and
spontaneously transformed into 1 (Figure S10). Therefore,
we hypothesized that compounds 1 and 2−4 may be
interconvertible, which was also experimentally confirmed by
its reaction with H2O, MeOH, and anthranilic acid,
respectively (Figure S10). To assess whether dimers 5 and 6
form spontaneously, we incubated 1 with barbituric acid at 30
°C for 36 h. HPLC analysis revealed only a single peak
corresponding to m/z 561.2431 ([M + H]+), consistent with a
1:1 adduct between 1 and barbituric acid (Figure S10). No
dimeric 5 and 6 were detected. These results suggest that while
the formation of monomeric derivatives 2−4 may be
spontaneous, the generation of dimers 5−7 likely requires
enzymatic catalysis.
In the bioassay, chrolactomycin (1) has been well
documented to display antibacterial and cytotoxic activities.10
Accordingly, all isolated compounds 1−7 were evaluated for
antibacterial activity against Staphylococcus aureus and Bacillus
subtilis and for cytotoxicity against seven human cancer cell
lines (786-O, TE-1, 5637, CAL-62, MCF7, HOS, and A-375).
Compounds 1−4 exhibited antibacterial activity, while dimers
5−7 were inactive. Cytotoxicity was observed for compounds 1
and 3−7 against several human cancer cell lines, whereas 2 was
inactive (Table 2). Considering these data, the primary
structure−activity relationships (SARs) are summarized as
follows: (i) The new monomeric derivatives (2−4) retained
antibacterial activity comparable to that of 1 and were superior
to dimers 5−7. (ii) In contrast, dimers 5−7 displayed broader
and enhanced antitumor cytotoxicity. Notably, the symmetric
dimer 5 exhibited the most potent antitumor cytotoxicity. (iii)
Comparison of 5 with 6−7 revealed that the C-9/9′
stereochemical configuration and the 11/11′-OCH3 group
critically influence cytotoxic bioactivity.
In summary, we identified six new chrolactomycin
derivatives (2−7) from the moss-derived rare actinomycete
Catenulispora acidiphila TX15. Particularly, the dimeric
chrolactomycins D−F (5−7) with a new type of barbiturate-
bridged linkage are unprecedented in natural products. The
biosynthetic pathway for 1−7 was deduced from sequencing
and functional characterization of its corresponding BGC. The
spontaneous interconversion between 1 and 2−4, coupled with
their potent antibacterial activity, indicates that 1 represents an
excellent reactive Michael acceptor for further chemical
semisynthetic and pharmacological investigations. The remark-
able cytotoxicity activity of 5, combined with its unique
dimeric architecture, provides valuable inspiration for relevant
drug discovery. These findings enrich the chemical library of
spirotetronate polyketides and expand the dimeric bridged-
linkage type, laying the foundation for biosynthetic studies of
chrolactomycins.
