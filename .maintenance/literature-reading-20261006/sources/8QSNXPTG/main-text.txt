ABSTRACT: Nine new (1−3, 5−8, 11, and 12; named ﬁlipins VI−XIV) and three known (4, 9, and 10) ﬁlipin-type polyene
macrolides were isolated from the deep-sea-derived Streptomyces antibioticus OUCT16-23 using a genome-guided strategy coupled
with bioassay. Their structures were elucidated based on the extensive MS and NMR spectroscopic analyses together with ECD
calculations. In an antifungal assay, compounds 4, 5, and 7−10 showed diﬀerent degrees of growth inhibition against Candida
albicans with minimum inhibitory concentrations (MICs) of 1.56−12.5 μg/mL, by which the alkyl side-chain substitution aﬀecting
the activity was preliminarily studied. A biosynthetic pathway to 1−12 in S. antibioticus OUCT16-23 is also proposed.
I
n the past 30 years, the number of patients with fungal
infections has dramatically been increasing. Among the
pathogenic fungi, Candida albicans is the most common human
fungal pathogen worldwide,1 which accounts for more than
90% of serious fungal infection cases.2 However, with a
worldwide increase of drug resistance in C. albicans, the drugs
available to eﬀectively treat C. albicans-caused infection are
very limited,2−4 which poses a serious threat to public health.
Thus, there is an urgent demand to discover novel antibiotics
to address this concern.
Polyene macrolide antibiotics (PMAs), composed of 20−44-
membered lactone rings with 3−8 conjugated double bonds,5
are recognized as some of the most eﬀective antifungal agents.6
PMAs such as amphotericin B, levorin, nystatin, pimaricin, and
candicidin are widely used in human therapy against
disseminated (systemic) fungal infections caused by Candida
species.6,7 Although the mechanisms of action of PMAs have
not been fully elucidated, they are considered to be
membranotropic agents that interact with sterols localized in
the cellular membranes to disrupt sterol-dependent intra-
cellular biochemical processes.7 Due to the relatively low
emergence frequency of resistant fungal pathogens,6 PMAs
have great potential for the development of new-generation
antifungal agents with improved eﬃcacy.
The extreme environment of the deep sea delivers microbes
with high biodiversity and novel metabolic pathways, providing
a huge opportunity to discover new natural products, thus
accelerating drug discovery.2,8−11 PMAs are biosynthesized via
polyketide synthetase (PKS) machinery, during which acyl
transferase (AT) domains are typically in charge of the start
units and extender units. In this study, genome mining of the
deep-sea-derived Streptomyces antibioticus OUCT16-23 was
carried out, revealing the presence of a ﬁlipin-like PMA
biosynthetic gene cluster (BGC), named Safil. Detailed
analysis of the SaFil PKS revealed a unique AT domain.
Inspired by this, we targeted these compounds and obtained
nine new (1−3, 5−8, 11, and 12) and three known (4, 9, and
10) ﬁlipin-type PMAs from OUCT16-23. Anti-C. albicans
activity of these compounds was also evaluated.
■RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
The OUCT16-23 strain was isolated from a deep-sea sediment
sample (4495 m depth, Indian Ocean). Primary bioassays of
the crude fermentation products indicated that OUCT16-23
has a latent capacity to produce antimicrobial substances.
Thereby, the genome of OUCT16-23 was sequenced. A
neighbor-joining phylogenetic tree based on 16S rRNA gene
sequences (949 bp) was constructed (Figure S1) and showed

To mine the production of PMAs encoded by Safil in S.
antibioticus OUCT16-23, an antifungal assay-coupled meta-
bolic analysis was performed. Small-scale (50 mL) fermenta-
tions using 11 media were carried out (Figure S3), and then,
each culture broth was extracted with an equal volume of
EtOAc. The EtOAc extracts were subjected to HPLC-
HRESIMS followed by radial diﬀusion assay (100 μg/well)
against the human pathogenic fungus C. albicans CMCC(F)
98001 (Figure S3). As shown in Figure S3A, the metabolite
production of S. antibioticus OUCT16-23 in diﬀerent culture
media was quite diﬀerent. Interestingly, the growth inhibition
against C. albicans was notably increased in Medium F2 with
the production of a class of compounds (m/z 494−654)
displaying typical UV spectra for ﬁlipin-type pentaene
macrolides (Figure S3D). Thus, these compounds were
proposed to be the major anti-C. albicans components in S.
antibioticus OUCT16-23. Large-scale fermentation (20 L) of S.
antibioticus OUCT16-23 in Medium F2 was then conducted,
and with UV-guided HPLC puriﬁcation, compounds 1−12
were obtained.

Compound 3 was isolated as a yellowish amorphous solid.
The molecular formula of 3 was established as C29H46O10 on
the basis of the (+)-HRESIMS data. According to the NMR
assignment of 3 (Figures S15 and S16), it showed a

29 (δH 1.68) to C-16 (δC 140.8) revealed that a methyl group
was substituted on C-16 (Figure S15). The deshielded carbon
C-26 (δC 72.5) combined with the COSY correlation of H-26
(δH 3.88)/26-OH (δH 5.29) revealed that a hydroxy group was
substituted on C-26 (Figure S15). The NOESY correlations of

4.66) suggested an anti-conformation between 26-OH and 27-

Compound 11 was isolated as a yellowish amorphous solid.
Based on (+)-HRESIMS analysis, the molecular formula of 11
was established as C27H42O8. Despite a 44 Da smaller
molecular weight of 11 compared to 2, these two compounds
displayed very similar COSY and HMBC correlations (Figures
2 and S15), indicative of the same substitutions on the
macrolide ring of each compound. Thus, it was proposed that
the diﬀerence between compounds 11 and 2 occurs in the
overlapped 1,3-polyol or pentaene regions. Considering the
maximum UV absorptions of 11 (Figure S4) not showing blue
shifts compared to those of 1−10, we concluded that 11 lacks
one CH2CHOH unit, which comprises a new 26-membered
pentaene macrolide. Both the UV spectrum (Figure S4) and
the NOESY data assignment (Figure 2) of 11 supported an all-
trans pentaene chromophore. According to the predicted
stereoselectivity of the KR domains within SaFilA1−A5
(Figure 5 and Table S2), the absolute conﬁgurations of 11
were proposed to be 3S, 5S, 7S, 9R, 11R, 13S, and 25R. Unlike
the ECD spectra of other ﬁlipins displaying negative Cotton
eﬀects (Figure S5), compound 11 showed positive Cotton
eﬀects with the maxima at 355, 336, and 320 nm (Figure 3). In
the ECD calculations at the CAM-B3LYP/6-31G(d) level of
theory using Gaussian 09, the calculated ECD spectrum of a
selected conformer with biosynthetically proposed absolute
conﬁgurations was in good agreement with the experimental
ECD spectrum of 11 (Figure 3). Thus, compound 11 was
identiﬁed as a new 26-membered pentaene macrolide, named
ﬁlipin XIII. The 1H and 13C chemical shifts of 11 are listed in
Table 3.

General Experimental Procedures. Optical rotations were
recorded with a JASCO P-1020 digital polarimeter. UV spectra
were recorded on a Hitachi UH5300 spectrophotometer. The ECD
spectra in MeOH were recorded on a JASCO J-715 spectropolarim-
eter. The IR spectra were measured on a Nicolet NEXUE470 FT-IR.
1D and 2D NMR spectra were obtained on Bruker Avance III 600
spectrometers. Chemical shifts were reported with reference to the
residual solvent peaks (δH 2.50 and δC 39.5 for DMSO-d6; δH 3.31
and δC 49.0 for CD3OD). LC-MS data were obtained on an Agilent
1260 HPLC system coupled with a Q-TOF Ultima Global GAA076
MS spectrometer. High-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC)
was performed on an Agilent 1260 Inﬁnity equipment with a diode
array detector. The optical density (OD) measurements were
recorded on a Biotech Epoch2 microplate reader.

yeast extract, 30 g tryptic soya broth, 1 g tryptone, 1 L water). The
media used in fermentation were Medium F2 (10 g soluble starch, 20

Isolation and Identiﬁcation of the OUCT16-23 Strain. The
deep-sea sediment was collected from 4495 m depth of the Indian
Ocean and was stored at −80 °C before being processed. After
thawing, the sediment sample was suspended in distilled H2O, which
was then diluted into three gradients: 10−1, 10−2, and 10−3 g/mL. The
suspension was inoculated on R2A, MS, and MM media to incubate at
28 °C for 1−2 weeks. Then, the OUCT16-23 strain was isolated as
ﬁlamentous colonies and stored in 50% glycerin at −80 °C. The
OUCT16-23 strain was grown in 5 mL of TSBY medium for 3 days at
28 °C. The cell mass was separated by centrifugation, and genomic
DNA was isolated by the standard phenol-chloroform extraction
procedure. The 16S rDNA fragment was ampliﬁed using universal
primers 27F and 1492R21 using the following thermal cycling
conditions: predenaturation of the target DNA at 95 °C for 5 min,
denaturation for 30 s, primer annealing at 56 °C for 30 s, and primer
extension at 72 °C for 90 s, followed by 25 cycles. At the end of the
cycling, the reaction mixture was held at 72 °C for 5 min for ﬁnal
extension and then cooled to 4 °C. The ampliﬁed product was
subjected to gel extraction puriﬁcation, ligation, and transformation
and sequenced. The resulting 16S rDNA sequence was deposited in
the NCBI database under accession number OK324134.
Bioinformatics Analysis of the Safil Gene Cluster from the
OUCT16-23 Strain. The sequence was analyzed for putative genes
with the FramePlot 2.3.2 program.22 The proposed function of the
genes was accomplished by using BLAST.23 The module and domain
organizations of SaFilA1-A5 were deduced by SBSPKS analysis.14

220, 260, 280, 300, 340, and 380 nm.

ECD Calculations. Conformational searches were run by the
“Random search” procedure implemented in the Sybyl 2.0 program
using the Molecular Merck force ﬁeld (MMFF94). The selected low-
energy conformer for (3S,5S,7S,9R,11R,13S,25R)-11, which was in
accordance with the biosynthetically proposed absolute conﬁgu-
rations, was subjected to the geometry optimization with DFT
calculations at the TZVP/6-31G(d) level using the Gaussian 09
program. The TD calculations were performed on the optimized
conformer using the long-range-corrected hybrid CAM-B3LYP.
Solvent (MeOH) eﬀects were considered by using the polarizable
continuum model. The ECD spectra were generated by the program
GaussView 5.0.
Radial Diﬀusion Assay. The C. albicans CMCC(F) 98001 strain,
being purchased from National Center for Medical Culture
Collections in China, was grown overnight at 30 °C in PDB medium
and diluted to 10−1. Then, 10 μL of the diluted fungal culture was
added to 50 mL of PDA medium at 40−50 °C. Once the fungus was
adequately dispersed, the gel was poured into a plate (90 × 15 mm).
After solidiﬁcation, wells were made using a 2 mm punch. Each
sample (10 mg/mL, 10 μL) was added to the well, and the plates were
incubated at 30 °C for 18 h. Amphotericin B (≥98%, 30 μg/well) was
used as a positive control. MeOH and DMSO were used as negative
controls. The diameters of the inhibition zones surrounding the wells
were measured in millimeters.
Minimum Inhibitory Concentration (MIC) Assay. Minimum
inhibitory concentrations of the compounds against Candida albicans
CMCC(F) 98001 were determined using the broth microdilution
method. The strain was grown in PDB at 30 °C overnight, and the
inoculum was standardized to approximately 1 × 105 CFU/mL. The
2-fold serial dilution of the compounds was obtained in the 12.5 to
0.39 μg/mL range in MeOH. MeOH and DMSO were used as the
negative controls, while amphotericin B was used as a positive control.
PDB broth was used as a blank. Then the plate was incubated at 30
°C for 24 h, and the MIC values were recorded as the lowest
concentration of the compounds in which no visible microbial growth
was observed. Each experiment was performed three times.
