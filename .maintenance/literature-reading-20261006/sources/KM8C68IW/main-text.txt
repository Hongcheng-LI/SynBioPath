Keywords:                                           Plants represent a natural source of diverse secondary metabolites with anticancer activities. Among these
Paclitaxel                                          metabolites, paclitaxel (Taxol®), a well-known chemotherapeutic agent isolated from Taxus spp., is widely used
Biosynthsis                                           in the treatment of multiple types of cancers. However, the low endogenous content of paclitaxel and the highTaxadiene                                               complexity of its biosynthetic process render both plant extraction and total chemical synthesis inadequate toBaccatin III                                          meet the growing market demand. To address this challenge, researchers have been actively exploring heter­Synthetic biology

                                                    crobial fermentation technology, and bioinformatics have significantly enhanced paclitaxel yields. This review
                                                    highlights recent progress in paclitaxel production, with particular emphasis on research advancements related
                                                    to enzymes that play critical catalytic roles in its biosynthetic pathway, as well as emerging trends in synthetic
                                                 biology strategies for the biosynthesis of key intermediates. Collectively, these studies provide theoretical sup­
                                                  port for the sustainable production of paclitaxel and pave the way for future drug development targeting cancer.

start with curating enzyme sequence, structure, and functional data to train machine learning models. These models then predict promising mutations and guide the
generation of a focused mutant library. Finally, laboratory screening of this library validates mutant activity and feeds data back to refine model predictions,
accelerating optimal enzyme identification. Computational metabolic design utilizes genome-scale metabolic network models for computational analysis, enabling
the automated synchronized evolution of multiple key genes in metabolic pathways. The resulting optimal experimental scheme is then validated in the laboratory to
observe whether there is a significant increase in the yield of the target product.
