(CYPs) play a crucial role by introducing oxygen groups and in some
cases triggering backbone rearrangements, thereby greatly increasing
the diversity of diterpenoids (Banerjee and Hamberger, 2018; Bathe and
Tissier, 2019). Many of the CYPs involved in diterpenoid biosynthesis
are promiscuous, accepting not only their natural substrate but also
structurally related compounds (Bathe and Tissier, 2019). Modularity,
multi-product formation by diTPS, and promiscuous CYPs are features
that make diterpenoid biosynthesis highly amenable to combinatorial
biosynthesis, which is a powerful approach to generate libraries of
natural products, either existing or new-to-Nature. The synthesized
products can then be screened for biological activity. In the natural
product field, there are successful demonstrations of combinatorial
biosynthesis of polyketides, which include compounds with antitumor
or antimicrobial activities (Wong and Khosla, 2012; Zhang et al., 2018).
For diterpenoids, the promiscuity of class I diTPS was harnessed by
generating  class  II/class  I combinations to produce new-to-Nature
backbones (Andersen-Ranberg et al., 2016; Jia et al., 2019). Further­
more, specific diterpene backbones were combined with CYPs, either
from the same or closely related organisms or that have a highly
conserved function resulting in significant but limited extension of the
chemical space (Bathe et al., 2019; Mafu et al., 2016; Wang et al.,
2012a).
   Our objective for this work was to extend the approach to CYPs from
unrelated plant species that act on related diterpene backbones but
oxidize at different positions. Combining these CYPs with non-native
substrates (either diterpene backbones or oxidized diterpenes) should
allow the production of new-to-Nature diterpenoids. Mining the litera­
ture for enzymes involved in diterpenoid metabolism showed that more
than 50 diTPS and 80 CYPs that oxidize diterpenes have been published
in the last two decades. As a proof of concept to demonstrate the
feasibility of the approach we decided to focus on three types of back­
bones derived from (+)-copalyl diphosphate ((+)-CPP) and selected
CYPs that are known to oxidize one or more of these backbones. These
backbones contain the tricyclic pimarane and abietane, as well as the
bicyclic labdane backbone, that are at the heart of a number of diter­
penes with intriguing bioactivities, such as antiviral (Wardana et al.,
2021), anticancer (Islam, 2017) neuroprotective - as activator of po­
tassium channels (Ottosson et al., 2017) or anti-atherosclerotic (Suh
et al., 2012).
