ABSTRACT: Thiazole scaffold-based small molecules exhibit a
range of biological activities and play important roles in drug
discovery. Based on bioinformatics analysis, a putative biosynthetic
gene cluster (BGC) for thiazole-containing compounds was
identified from Streptomyces sp. SCSIO 40020. Heterologous
expression of this BGC led to the production of eight new
thiazole-containing compounds, grisechelins E, F, and I−N (1, 2,
5−10), and two quinoline derivatives, grisechelins G and H (3 and
4). The structures of 1−10, including their absolute configurations,
were elucidated by HRESIMS, NMR spectroscopic data, ECD
calculations, and single-crystal X-ray diffraction analysis. Grisechelin
F (2) is a unique derivative, distinguished by the presence of a
salicylic acid moiety. The biosynthetic pathway for 2 was proposed based on bioinformatics analysis and in vivo gene knockout
experiments. Grisechelin E (1) displayed moderate antimycobacterial activity against Mycobacterium tuberculosis H37Ra (MIC of 8
μg mL−1).

hiazole, encompassing thiazolidine and thiazoline, is a
distinct five-membered heterocyclic structure that
features both sulfur and nitrogen.1−3 The thiazole motif
represents a privileged molecular scaffold with broad-ranging
pharmacological activities.4,5 Such heterocycles are incorpo-
rated into numerous therapeutically active agents, including
the antitumor drug bleomycin,6 the antimicrobial drug
sulfathiazole,7 the schistosomicide drug niridazole,8 and the
anti-HIV drug ritonavir.9 Biogenetically, these heterocycles are
derived from the heterocyclization between the cysteine side
chain and the adjacent carbonyl group of the peptide substrate,
leading to the formation of thiazoline.10,11 Thiazoline can then
be transformed into either thiazolidine or thiazole by a
reduction or oxidation step.12 Importantly, the biosynthesis of
thiazole occurs not only in the nonribosomal peptide
synthetase (NRPS) system but also in the ribosomally
synthesized and post-translationally modified peptides
(RiPPs).13,14 Cysteine is specifically recognized by the
adenylation (A) domain, while the cyclization is catalyzed by
the cyclization (Cy) domain of the NRPS.10,11,13 Heterologous
expression of natural product biosynthetic gene clusters
(BGCs) has been developed as a powerful strategy for natural
products discovery.15−18 Our research, along with others’, has
demonstrated that the heterologous expression of marine-
derived microbial BGCs in terrestrial hosts could also be
successful in producing previously unknown natural products,
such as fluostatins,19,20 PoTeMs,21 taromycin A,22 ansaseomy-
cin,23 bosamycins,24 and berninamycins J and K.25
Secondary metabolites originating from marine micro-
organisms represent a valuable source for drug development
due to their unique chemical structures and diverse
bioactivities.26−28 A considerable subset of these bioactive
compounds features the thiazole motif.3,26 Therefore, it is
worth focusing on the BGCs predicted to produce compounds
containing the thiazole motif, to further explore natural
products that incorporate the thiazole unit. Previously, we
have identified two new phenylhydrazone derivatives,
penzonemycins A and B, along with bafilomycins and
mycemycin from Streptomyces sp. SCSIO 40020, isolated
from the Pearl River estuary sediment.29,30 Genomic
sequencing and further BGC analysis of this strain led to the
identification of a unique NRPS BGC (grsc) harboring four Cy
+ Acys domain-embedded modules. Eight new thiazole-
containing compounds, grisechelins E, F, and I−N (1, 2, 5−
10), and two quinoline derivatives, grisechelins G and H (3
and 4), were obtained via expression of the grsc BGC in three
different hosts, Streptomyces lividans SBT18,31 Streptomyces
coelicolor M1154,32 and Streptomyces albus J1074.33 Here, we

■RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
To fully explore the chemical potential of the marine-derived
Streptomyces sp. SCSIO 40020, its genome was sequenced.
Subsequently all the putative BGCs within it were annotated
by antiSMASH. One BGC, designated as grsc (GenBank
accession number OR789736), comprising four modular
NRPS genes, attracted our attention due to its potential to
encode a thiazoline-containing peptide. This BGC spans 112
kb in the genome and includes 67 open reading frames (ORFs)
(Figure 1A, Figure S1, and Table S1). Bioinformatics analysis
showed that the four modular NRPSs (Grsc18, Grsc19,
Grsc33, and Grsc39) could be responsible for Cys incorpo-
ration and heterocyclization, given their Cy + Acys domain-
embedded modules (Figure S2). The upstream genes, grsc5−8
and grsc13−17, are probably involved in the formation of 3-
hydroxy-quinaldic acid due to their high sequence identities
(46−65%) to their homologues in thiocoraline biosynthesis
(Table S1). Based on these analyses, we infer that the grsc
BGC could potentially encode novel thiazoline−quinaldic acid
hybrid compounds.
To obtain the intact grsc BGC for subsequent heterologous
expression studies, the Bacterial Artificial Chromosome (BAC)
library of Streptomyces sp. SCSIO 40020 was first screened with
two primer pairs, Gris-sk-1F/R and Gris-sk-2F/R (Table S2).
Fortunately, one positive clone, pCSGzsl001 (Table S3),
bearing the entire grsc BGC was obtained and further
confirmed by end-sequencing (Figure S3). Afterward,
pCSGzsl001 was introduced into three different hosts, S.
lividans SBT18, S. albus J1074, and S. coelicolor M1154.
Compared with the negative control, a number of new peaks
were detected in the fermentation culture of all transformants
harboring pCSGzsl001 (Figure 1B). Following a 20 L
fermentation, eight new thiazole-containing compounds,
grisechelins E, F, and I−N (1, 2, 5−10), and two new
quinoline derivatives, grisechelins G and H (3 and 4), were

Compound 1 was isolated as yellow needles. Its molecular
formula was deduced to be C13H8Cl2N2O2S based on the
HRESIMS (Figure S4), indicating 10 degrees of unsaturation.
More specifically, compound 1 presented the isotope pattern at
m/z 326/328/330 (9:6:1 ratio), consistent with the presence
of two chlorine atoms in the molecule. The 1H, 13C, and
HSQC NMR data of compound 1 (Table 1, Table 2) showed
four sp2 methines, eight sp2 nonprotonated carbons, and one
oxygenated methylene group (δH 4.92, δC 61.0). The COSY
analysis of 1 revealed one aromatic ABX spin system with
signals [δH 7.53 (1H, dd, J = 8.9, 2.0 Hz), δH 7.97 (1H, d, J =
8.9 Hz), and δH 8.12 (1H, d, J = 2.0 Hz)], which suggested the
presence of 1,2,4-trisubstituted benzene in 1. The NMR data
of 1 were similar to those of grisechelin C with the difference
being substitution of chlorine atoms.34 The substitution of Cl
atoms at C-4 and C-6 in 1 was supported by the COSY
correlation of H-7/H-8 and HMBC correlations between H-5/
H-7 and C-8a, as well as H-5 and C-4. Finally, a high-quality
crystal of 1 was obtained from methanol, which allowed the
confirmation of the structure of 1 (Figure 2, CCDC 2285442).
1 was designated as grisechelin E.
Compound 2 was obtained as a yellow solid. The molecular
formula of 2 was assigned as C20H12Cl2N2O4S according to the
HRESIMS (Figure S5), indicating 15 degrees of unsaturation.
Detailed analysis of NMR data of 2 (Table 1, Table 2, Figure
S5) revealed it was similar to 1. Compound 2 was different
from 1 by the presence of an additional salicylic acid fragment,
which was supported by COSY correlations of H-11′/H-12′/
H-13′/H-14′, as well as HMBC correlations from 10′-OH to
C-9′/C-10′/C-11′ and from H-14′ to C-8′ (δC 169.8)/C-9′/
C-10′. The HMBC correlation from H-6′ (δH 5.58) to C-8′
indicated that the salicylic acid fragment was linked to C-6′ via
an ester bond. Further, the structure of 2 was confirmed by the
detailed 2D NMR assignment. 2 was designated as grisechelin
F.
Compound 3 was obtained as a yellow solid. The molecular
formula of 3 was established as C10H6Cl2N2O2 by HRMS
analysis, indicating 8 degrees of unsaturation. Comparison of
NMR data of 3 and 1 (Table 1, Table 2, Figure S6) revealed 3
was similar to 1, both of them possessing a dichlorinated
quinoline moiety. Compound 3 differed from 1 by having an
amide group, instead of a thiazol-4-ylmethanol unit in 1. This
difference was supported by the presence of an amide carbonyl
(δC 170.6) and the molecular formula of 3. Detailed 2D NMR
data confirmed the structure of 3. 3 was designated as
grisechelin G.

Compound 4 was obtained as a yellowish solid. The
molecular formula of 4 was established as C10H6ClNO3 by
HRMS analysis, indicating 8 degrees of unsaturation. Careful
analysis of NMR data of 4 (Table 1, Table 2, Figure S7)
showed 4 was similar to 3. Compound 4 featured a hydrogen
atom at C-4 instead of a chlorine atom in 3, which was
supported by HMBC correlations from H-4 to C-4a/C-3/C-2.
In addition, based on the molecular formula of 4, the carboxyl
group in 4 was suggested, instead of an amide group as in 3. 4
was designated as grisechelin H.
Compound 5 was obtained as a yellow solid. The molecular
formula of 5 was established as C16H12N4O2S2 by HRMS
analysis, indicating 13 degrees of unsaturation. Detailed
analysis of the NMR data (Table 1, Table 2, Figure S8)
revealed 5 was similar to 1. Compound 5 was different from 1
by the presence of a dihydrothiazole-4-carboxamide unit,
instead of a hydroxymethyl group as in 1, which was supported
by COSY correlations of H-8′/H-9′, as well as HMBC
correlations from H-8′/NH-12′ to C-11′ and from H-8′/H-9′
to C-6′. Furthermore, the unit was connected to C-4′ by the
HMBC correlation from H-3′ to C-6′. Moreover, compound 5
featured hydrogen atoms at C-4 and C-6 instead of chlorine
atoms as found in 1, which was supported by corresponding
COSY and HMBC correlations and the molecular formula of 5
(Figure 2). Thus, the planar structure of 5 was assigned and
designated as grisechelin I. The absolute configuration of 5 was
assigned to be 9′R by comparison of the experimental ECD
spectrum of 5 with the calculated ECD spectra for (9′R)-5 and
(9′S)-5 (Figure 2C).
Compound 6 was obtained as a yellow solid. The molecular
formula of 6 was established as C16H11ClN4O2S2 by HRMS
analysis, indicating 13 degrees of unsaturation. Detailed
analysis of NMR data (Table 1, Table 2, Figure S9) revealed
6 was similar to 5. Compound 6 was different from 5 by the
presence of a chlorine atom at C-6, which was supported by
the characteristic ABX spin in 6, as well as HMBC correlations
from H-8 to C-6 and H-5 to C-4. The detailed 2D NMR
assignment confirmed the planar structure of 6. 6 was
designated as grisechelin J. To determine the absolute
configurations of 6, its ECD spectra were compared with
that of 5. The almost identical Cotton effects of 6 and 5
(Figure S10) implied that 6 should possess the 9′R
configuration, the same as that of 5.
Compound 7 was obtained as a yellow solid. The molecular
formula of 7 was established as C13H9ClN2O2S by HRMS
analysis, indicating 10 degrees of unsaturation. The 1H and 13C

those of 1. Compound 7 was different from 1 by the absence of
the chlorine atom at C-6, which was supported by COSY
correlations of H-5/H-6/H-7/H-8. The detailed 2D NMR
assignment confirmed the planar structure of 7. 7 was
designated as grisechelin K.

Compound 10 was obtained as a yellow powder. The
molecular formula of 10 was established as C16H10Cl2N2O3S
by HRMS analysis, indicating 12 degrees of unsaturation.
Detailed analysis of NMR data of 10 (Table 1, Table 2, Figure
S14) revealed it was similar to 1. Compound 10 was different
from 1 by the presence of a but-3-en-2-one unit, instead of a
hydroxymethyl group, which was supported by COSY
correlations, as well as HMBC correlations (Figure 2). Further,
the structure of 10 was confirmed by the detailed 2D NMR
assignment. 10 was designated as grisechelin N.
Compounds 2−10 were inactive against Mycobacterium
tuberculosis H37Ra, Acinetobacter baumannii ATCC 19606, and
methicillin-resistant S. aureus (MRSA) ATCC 43300, except
compound 1 was active against M. tuberculosis H37Ra with an
MIC value of 8 μg mL−1 (Table S4).
Compound 2 represents a novel grisechelin analogue that
contains a quinaldic acid core, a thiazole unit, and a salicylic
acid moiety. The gene cluster responsible for the synthesis of
grisechelins (grsc BGC) encompasses a range from grsc1 to
grsc67 (Figure S1), and it is postulated that this gene cluster
encodes the enzymatic machinery for the biosynthesis of 2.
The quinaldic acid core is hypothesized to be derived from L-
tryptophan, mirroring the formation of 3-hydroxy-quinaldic
acid in thiocoraline biosynthesis.35−37 Therefore, 10 enzymes
(Grsc3, 4, 14, 17, 13, 7, 16, 63, 5, and 6) are likely involved in
the conversion of L-Trp into 3-hydroxy-6-chlor-quinaldic acid
(4) (Figure 3A, Figure S1B). Subsequently, 4 undergoes
halogenation by the halogenase Grsc12 and activation by the
AMP-binding ligase Grsc8 and then loads onto the carrier
protein to form the protein-compound complex M (Figure 3A,
Figure S1B).38 L-Cysteine is then incorporated and assembled
with M by the NRPS assembly line to give the thiazole-
containing compound 1 (Figure 3A). Within the grsc BGC,
there are four NRPS genes (grsc18, grsc19, grsc33, and grsc39);
one of them should be related to the incorporation of L-
cysteine for the biosynthesis of 1 (Figure 3A, Figure S2).
Indeed, inactivation of grsc19 in S. lividans SBT18/
pCSGzsl001 abolished the production of 1 and 2 (Figure
3B, Figure S15), supporting the role of Grsc19 in thiazole
moiety formation (Figure 3A). Based on bioinformatics
analysis, the formation of the salicylic acid unit could be
derived from chorismic acid catalyzed by Grsc59, which shows
52% identity with the salicylate synthase MycA (Table S1)39 or
SLIV_00845 from Streptomyces lividans (GenBank No.
AIJ11198.1). Furthermore, the salicylic acid unit is loaded

Heterologous Expression of the Grisechelins’ Biosynthetic
Gene Cluster (grsc BGC). Two pairs of specific primers, Gris-sk-1F/
R and Gris-sk-2F/R (Table S2), targeting the grsc BGC were used to
screen the BAC library of Streptomyces sp. SCSIO 40020. The positive
BAC clone (E. coli DH10B/pCSGzsl001) was confirmed by end-
sequencing (Figure S3). The plasmid pCSGzsl001 was respectively
transformed into heterologous hosts S. lividans SBT18, S. albus J1074,
and S. coelicolor M1154 by conjugation with the help of E. coli
ET12567/pUB307.43 Briefly, the donor strain E. coli DH10B/
pCSGzsl001 and the helper strain E. coli ET12567/pUB307 were
respectively prepared by growth to an OD600 value of 0.6−0.8. Both
donor and helper strains were harvested, washed twice, and
resuspended in LB medium to a concentration of 5 × 109 cells
mL−1. The spores of S. lividans SBT18, S. albus J1074, and S. coelicolor
M1154 were respectively harvested, then resuspended in TSB
medium at a concentration of 3 × 106 CFU mL−1. After heating at
50 °C for 10 min, the spore suspensions were incubated at 28 °C for
1−2 h to serve as recipient strain. Next, an equal volume (100 μL) of
the recipient strain, the donor strain, and the helper strain were mixed
and spread onto an ISP4 agar plate containing 20 mM MgCl2. After
incubation at 28 °C for 18−20 h, the plate was covered with
apramycin (50 μg mL−1) and trimethoprim (100 μg mL−1) and
incubated at 28 °C for 4−5 days. The exconjugants were randomly
selected and transferred to the MS agar plate containing 50 μg mL−1
apramycin. The clones were identified by diagnostic PCR using the
pairs of primers Gris-sk-1F/R and Gris-sk-2F/R (Table S2). Three
positive clones were randomly selected for small-scale fermentation
and subjected to metabolite analyses by HPLC.
General Method for Inactivation of grsc19 and grsc60
Genes. The grsc19 gene was inactivated following the lambda-RED-
mediated gene replacements.44 The gene cassette comprising aadA
and oriT was amplified from plasmid pIJ778 (Table S3) with
appropriate primers (Table S2) and followed by gel purification. The
resulting cassette was introduced into E. coli BW25113/pIJ790
containing BAC plasmid pCSGzsl001. The positive clones where the
targeted gene was replaced by the aadA and oriT cassette were
selected with spectinomycin resistance. The corresponding BAC
carrying the mutated alleles (Table S3) was transformed into DH5α
as the donor strains for conjugation. The conjugation was performed
as described above, with the spores of S. lividans SBT18 as the
recipient strains and the E. coli ET12567/pUB307 as the helper strain,
except that the plate was covered with spectinomycin (100 μg mL−1)
and trimethoprim (100 μg mL−1). The exconjugants were randomly
selected and transferred to the MS agar plate containing 100 μg mL−1
of spectinomycin. The clones were identified by diagnostic PCR with
corresponding primers (Table S2). Three positive clones of each
mutant were randomly selected for small-scale fermentation and
subjected to metabolite analyses by HPLC. The inactivation of grsc60
genes was performed similarly.

Method for ECD. The conformational analysis of (9′R)-5 was
performed using Sybyl 8.1 software with the MMFF94s force field,
which afforded the conformers for (9′R)-5 with an energy cutoff of
3.0 kcal mol−1 to the global minima. All of the obtained conformers
were optimized using the B3LYP/6-31+G(d) level in gas phrase with
Gaussian09 software.48 TDDFT ECD calculations for the optimized
conformers were performed at the B3LYP/6-31+G(d) level in
acetonitrile using the polarizable conductor calculation model
(CPCM). The overall ECD curves of all the compounds were
weighted by a Boltzmann distribution after a UV correction of +15
nm. The ECD curves were produced by SpecDis 1.6 software49 with
sigma = 0.25 eV. Calculated ECD of (9′S)-5 was produced on the
basis of the mirror-image ECD curves of (9′R)-5.
Cytotoxic Activity Assay. The in vitro cytotoxic activities of
compounds 1−10 were evaluated in SF-268 (human glioma cell line),
HepG2 (human liver carcinoma cell line), MCF-7 (human breast
adenocarcinoma cell line), and A549 (human lung adenocarcinoma
cell) by the SRB method, according to a previously described
protocol.50 The cells were cultivated in RPMI 1640 medium. Cells
(180 μL) with a density of 3 × 104 cells/mL were seeded onto 96-well
plates and incubated for 24 h at 37 °C and 5% CO2. Subsequently, 20
μL of different concentrations of grisechelins, ranging from 0 to 100
μM in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), was added to each plate well. An
equal volume of DMSO was used as a negative control. After further
incubation for 72 h, the cell monolayers were fixed with 50% (w/v)
trichloroacetic acid (50 μL) and then stained for 30 min with 0.4%
(w/v) SRB dissolved in 1% acetic acid. The unbound dye was
removed by repeatedly washing with 1% acetic acid. The protein-
bound dye was dissolved in 10 mM tris-base solution (200 μL) for the
determination of the optical density (OD) at 570 nm using a
microplate reader. The cytotoxic compound cisplatin was used as a
positive control. All data were obtained in triplicate and are presented
as means ± SD. IC50 values were calculated with the SigmaPlot 14.0
software using the nonlinear curve-fitting method.
Antibacterial Activity Assay. The antibacterial activities of
compounds 1−10 were assayed against Acinetobacter baumannii
ATCC 19606 and methicillin-resistant S. aureus (MRSA) ATCC
43300 by the broth microdilution method.51 Compounds were
dissolved in DMSO at the final concentration of 2.56 mg mL−1. The
indicator strain was grown for 24 h on a rotary shaker at 37 °C. The
cultures were diluted with sterilized medium to achieve a
predetermined optical absorbance at 600 nm, then diluted 1000-
fold before being placed into 96-well microtiter plates. Three
replicates of each compound were tested in dilution series, ranging
from 128 to 0.25 μg mL−1. The MIC values were recorded after 24 h
of cultivation from two independent experiments. Ciprofloxacin,
vancomycin, and rifampicin were used as positive controls. For
detection of anti-Mycobacterium tuberculosis activities, we used
autoluminescent Mycobacterium tuberculosis H37Ra (AlRa) with the
method described in our previously study.52 Compounds were
dissolved in DMSO and then serially diluted to final concentrations
(μg/mL) of 128−0.031 for testing with 7H9 broth (with 0.2%
glycerol and 10% v/v oleic acid albumin dextrose catalase added).
The MIC using the light method was named as MIClux. MIClux was
defined as the lowest concentration of a compound that can inhibit
90% light strength compared with that of the sample containing only
the solvent. MIClux values were measured on day 7.
