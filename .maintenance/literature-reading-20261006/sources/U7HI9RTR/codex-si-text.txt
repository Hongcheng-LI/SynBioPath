Materials and Methods



1.1 Chemicals, strains and general experimental procedures

The Aspergillus terreus AT01 (A. terreus AT01) was collected in our laboratory. Questin

(1), questinol (5), and ω-hydroxyemodin (13) standards were prepared from the A.

terreus AT01 or ΔgedK fermentation cultures and verified by LC-HRMS and NMR

analyses. Emodin  (6), physcion  (7), aloe-emodin  (14), emodin-8-glucoside (15)

chrysophanol (16) and  rhein (17) were purchased from Solarbio  (China). The

Escherichia coli DH5α was used for plasmids construction, storage, and isolation and

it was grown at 37 °C in LB medium supplemented with appropriate antibiotics.



1.2 General DNA manipulation

Fast-digest restriction endonucleases (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) and Pro ligation-

free  cloning  kit  (abm, Canada) were used  for  construction  of  vectors. PCR

amplifications were performed using PrimeSTAR GXL DNA Polymerase (Takara,

Japan) according to recommended protocols. Plasmid extraction from E. coli, DNA gel

extraction, PCR products purification and fungal genomic DNA extraction were carried

out using Plasmid Mini Kit I, Gel Extraction Kit, Cycle Pure Kit and Fungal DNA Kit,

respectively, and  all kits were purchased from Omega (USA). The gateway BP

recombination was carried out according to standard instructions using Gateway® BP

Clonase™  II (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). The primers synthesis and DNA

sequencing services were supplied by TsingKe Biotech (Beijing, China).



1.3 Disruption of gedE, gedF and gedK in A. terreus

The pDONRTM221 plasmid (Invitrogen, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) was digested

by KspA I (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA), and ligated with pTRA (Pyrithiamine-

resistant gene) fragment which was amplified from pPTR II plasmid (Takara, Japan)

using primers ptrA F and ptrA R, to generate pDONR-pTRA. DNA fragments around 1

                                              1

[SI physical page 6]
kb upstream and downstream of gedE, gedF and gedK genes were amplified from the

AT01 genomic DNA using corresponding primer pairs as follows: gedE upstream, gedE

up F and gedE up R; gedE downstream, gedE down F and gedE down R; gedF upstream,

gedF up F and gedF up R; gedF downstream, gedF down F and gedF down R; gedK

upstream, gedK up F and gedK up R; gedK downstream, gedK down F and gedK down

R.  The  gedE  upstream/downstream,  gedF  upstream/downstream  and  gedK

upstream/downstream fragments were cloned into pDONR-pTRA in pairs by gateway

BP recombination to produce knockout vectors pDONR-pTRA-gedE, pDONR-pTRA-

gedF and pDONR-pTRA-gedK, respectively. The knockout cassettes were amplified

with PCR primer pairs gedE PCR F/ gedE PCR R for gedE, gedF PCR F/ gedF PCR R

for gedF, and gedK PCR F/ gedK PCR R for gedK, respectively. Subsequently, the

polyethylene glycol (PEG) mediated protoplast homologous recombination strategy

was employed to in-frame deletion of gedE, gedF or gedK in A. terreus AT01 as

previously described1. Transformants were selected on CD solid medium (3 g/L NaNO3,

2 g/L KCl, 1 g/L KH2PO4, 0.5 g/L MgSO4·7H2O, 0.02 g/L FeSO4·7H2O, 10 g/L glucose

and 1.2 M sorbitol) supplement with pyrithiamine (0.1 mg/L, Sigma). Primer pairs gedE

An F/ M13F and gedE An R/ M13R were used to perform colony PCR to verify correct

ΔgedE mutants with Phire Plant Direct PCR Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA)

according to standard instructions. Following the same procedures, ΔgedF mutants

were verified with primers gedF An F/ M13F and gedF An R/ M13R, while primers

gedK An F/ M13F and gedK An R/ M13R were used to validate correct ΔgedK variants.

All primers were listed in Table S2.



1.4 Strain fermentation and metabolites analysis

In order to analyze the metabolites change, the validated variants were individually

inoculated into 100 mL LPM medium (glucose 9 g/L, sucrose 10 g/L, milk powder 10

g/L, yeast extract 1 g/L, peptone 1 g/L, sodium acetate 1 g/L, KH2PO4 0.04 g/L, PEG-

400 0.5 g/L, MgSO4·7H2O 0.1 g/L, bean pulp 5 g/L, sodium citrate 2 g/L and CaCO3
                                              2

[SI physical page 7]
1.5 g/L) and cultivated at 30 oC with rotary shaking at 180 rpm in 500 mL flasks for 6-

8 days. The fermentation cultures were individually extracted by adding the same

volume of methanol and dichloromethane mixture (vol: vol=1: 1) three times and

centrifuged (20,000 ×g) for 15 min. The organic phase was evaporated by nitrogen flow

and  redissolved  in  the same volume  of  methanol. High  performance  liquid

chromatography (HPLC) analysis was carried out on an Agilent 1260 infinity HPLC

system (Agilent Technologies, USA) equipped with an ultraviolet detector using a BDS

HYPER GIL C18 reverse-phase column (Thermo, 5 μm, 250×4.6 mm, USA). The

gradient biphasic solvent system including acetonitrile and ddH2O+0.1% trifluoroacetic

acid was selected to separate compounds. The program was as follows: 30% acetonitrile

for 10 min and linear gradient 30-100% acetonitrile over 10 min at a flow rate of 1

mL/min. The detection wavelength was 340 nm.



1.5 Construction of E. coli expression vectors

The ORFs of gedF and gedK (Table S1) were amplified from the A. terreus AT01

genome (no introns for these genes) using primes 28b-gedF-N F/28b-gedF-N R and

28b-gedK-N F /28b-gedK-N R, respectively. The pET28b plasmid was double digested

by Nde I / Hind III, and then gedF and gedK fragments were individually cloned into

the digested vector with Pro ligation-free cloning kit (abm, Canada), to generate 28b-

gedF and 28b-gedK plasmids. All constructed vectors were confirmed by DNA

sequencing (TsingKe Biotech, China).



1.6 Expression and purification of GedF and GedK proteins

The plasmids 28b-gedF and 28b-gedK were individually transformed into E. coli

Codon  Plus  (DE3)-RIPL,  and  transformants  were  selected on  the LB  plate

supplemented with kanamycin (50 μg/mL). A single colony carrying 28b-gedF or 28b-

gedK was inoculated into 10 mL TB medium including kanamycin (50 μg/mL),

chloramphenicol (33 μg/mL), and streptomycin (50 μg/mL), and then cultivated at

                                              3

[SI physical page 8]
37 °C overnight. Subsequently, the seed culture reinoculated (vol: vol = 1: 100) into 1

L TB medium including 5% glycerol and antibiotics mentioned above, shaking

incubated  at  37  oC  until  OD600  reached  0.6.  Next,  0.2 mM  isopropyl-D-

thiogalactopyranoside (IPTG) was added into the culture to induce gene expression at

16 oC with rotary shaking at 150 rpm for another 20 h. The cells were harvested by

centrifugation at 8,000 ×g, 4 oC for 30 min and lysed by ultrasonication (5 s on/5 s off)

for 30 min on ice. The GedF and GedK proteins were purified by Ni-NTA affinity

chromatography as described earlier2.



1.7 In vitro enzymatic assays of GedF and GedK

The GedF standard in vitro enzymatic assay was performed in 100 μL of reaction buffer

(20 mM Tris-HCl, 10% glycerol, pH 7.4) containing 2 μM enzyme, 200 μM compound

1, and 1 mM NADPH (Solarbio, China) at 28 oC for 5 min. The cocatalyzed conversion

assay of 1 with GedF and GedK was carried out in the presence of 2 μM GedF, 2 μM

GedK, 200 μM compound 1, 20 μM FAD (Solarbio, China) and 1 mM NADPH in 100

μL of the reaction buffer at 28 oC for 3 min. All reactions were quenched by mixing

with the same volume of methanol and centrifuged (20,000 ×g) for 15 min. The

supernatants were applied for HPLC analysis as mentioned above.



1.8 18O-labeling reactions of desmethylsulochrin (2) with 18O2, and 18O2 and 16O2

mixture

For 18O-labeling reaction of 2 with 18O2, a stream of high purity nitrogen was bubbled

through solution A (100 μL 20 mM Tris-HCl, pH 7.4, 5 μM GedF and 5 μM GedK) and

solution B (100 μL ddH2O, 200 μM compound 1 and 1 mM NADPH) for 30 min to

thoroughly remove the atmospheric O2. Subsequently, the 97% 18O2 gas (Shanghai

research institute of chemical industry Co. Itd) was bubbled through solution A for 5

min. Next, the solution B was mixed with solution A in an anaerobic chamber to initiate

the reaction. For 18O-labeling reaction of 2 with 18O2 and 16O2 mixture, a stream of
                                              4

[SI physical page 9]
nitrogen was bubbled through penicillin bottle for 10 min to remove the atmospheric

O2. Subsequently, the 97% 18O2 gas was bubbled through penicillin bottle for 5 sec and

then 0.6 mL atmospheric O2 was injected into the bottle, too. Next, both solution B and

solution A were injected into aforementioned penicillin bottle to initiate the reaction.

The 16O2 control assay was performed in atmosphere environment. All of the mixture

was incubated at room temperature for 5 minutes and quenched by adding the same

volume of methanol. After vigorously shaking and high-speed centrifugation (20,000

×g) for 10 min, 5 μL of supernatant was subjected to LC-HRMS analysis.



1.9 18O-labeling assay with H218O

Solution A (50 μL 20 mM Tris-HCl, pH 7.4, 200 μM compound 1 and 1 mM NADPH)

and solution B (50 μL 20 mM Tris-HCl, pH 7.4, 2 μM GedF and 2 μM GedK) were

individually prepared and lyophilized overnight to thoroughly remove the water, and

then 50 μL H218O (98 atom % 18O, Tenglong Weibo Technology, China) was added into

the lyophilized solution A and B, respectively, to redissolve the residues. Next, the

solution A and B were mixed together and incubated at 28 oC for 30 min. The control

assay was also performed using H2O instead of H218O. The reactions were terminated

and analyzed as described above.



1.10 Enzymatic preparation of questin hydroquinone (4) on a large scale

The O2 in Tris-HCl buffer (20 mM, pH 7.4) was removed by a stream of high purity

nitrogen bubble for 30 min. The GedF-catalyzed conversion of 1 into 4 was prepared

in an anaerobic chamber and scaled up to 6 mL including 14 μmol compound 1, 42

μmol NADPH and 0.3 μmol enzyme. The reaction was stirred under a nitrogen

atmosphere and incubated at room temperature for 15 min. After that, 4 mL of CDCl3

was added into the reaction mixture and shaken for 2 min to extract compound. The

organic phase was concentrated by high purity nitrogen flow and directly subjected to

NMR analysis.

[SI physical page 10]
1.11 Enzymatic preparation of 2 on a large scale

The 20 mL scaled-up GedF and GedK mediated conversion of 1 (3 mg) into 2 assay

was carried out at 28 °C for 60 min and extracted by the same volume of ethyl acetate

three times. Subsequently, the organic phase was collected, evaporated, and redissolved

in methanol. The compound 2 was purified by semi-preparative HPLC (Waters,

XBridge BEH C18 OBDTM Prep Column, 130Å, 5 μm, 10 × 150 mm) with a biphasic

solvents (solvent A: acetonitrile, solvent B: ddH2O + 0.1% trifluoroacetic acid). The

solvents were delivered at 2 mL/min under a gradient elution program: 0 min 30% A,

13 min 30% A, 18 min 100% A, 21 min 100% A, 25 min 30% A. The wavelength of

detection was 340 nm. All  fractions containing 2 were  collected together and

evaporated by a Rotavapor R-3 rotary evaporator (Buchi, Switzerland) and N2 blowing.

Finally, ~1.5 mg 2 with > 95.0% purity was prepared.



1.12 The reactions of emodin (6) or questin (1) catalyzed by GedF (Na2S2O4) and

GedK

The 6 (or 1) ring-opening reaction catalyzed by GedF and GedK was performed as

described above. Specifically, for the Na2S2O4 and GedK reaction, 1 mg of 6 (or 1) was

dissolved in 0.5 mL THF and 0.5 mL H2O, and then sufficient Na2S2O4 was added into

the mixture and shaken until saturation3. Subsequently, 5 μL 6 (or 1) reduction product

was added into 100 μL 20 mM Tris-HCl buffer (pH7.4) containing 2 μM GedK and

incubated at 28 oC for 5 min. All reactions were quenched by methanol precipitation

and centrifuged (20,000 ×g) for 10 min. The supernatants were applied for further

HPLC and LC-HRMS analyses.



1.13 Identification of flavin cofactor bound to GedK

The purified GedK protein (20 mg/mL, 100 μL) was precipitated by boiling 10 min,

mixing with the same volume of methanol or 16 M urea. The sediments were removed

by high-speed centrifugation (20,000 ×g) and the supernatants were subjected to HPLC

                                              6

[SI physical page 11]
analysis.



1.14 Biotransformation of 1 analogues with GedF and GedK

The reaction assays were carried out in 100 μL of the reaction buffer containing 2 μM

GedF, 2 μM GedK, 1 mM NADPH and 100 μM substrate (compound 1, 5, 6, 7, 13, 14,

15, 16 or 17) at 28 oC and terminated by adding the same volume of methanol as

indicated time period.



1.15 LC-HRMS analysis

The LC-HRMS analysis was carried out on a Waters symmetry column (4.6 × 150 mm,

RP18) or Agilent C18 column (Eclipse Plus C18 RRHD, 2.1 × 50 mm). The negative

or positive mode electrospray ionization with a linear gradient of 10-100% acetonitrile

in ddH2O with 0.1% formic acid over 20 min, followed by 100% acetonitrile for 5 min,

at a flow rate of 0.5 mL/min. The high resolution mass spectra (HRMS) was recorded

on a Dionex Ultimate 3000 coupled to a Bruker Maxis Q-TOF spectrometer or an

Agilent 1290 infinity II coupled to a 6545 LC/Q-TOF.

The 1H, 13C and 2D NMR spectra of compound 1, 2, 4, 5 and 13 were obtained using

DMSO-d6 or CDCl3 as solvent, on a Bruker Avance III 600 MHz spectrometer with a 5

1.17 Phylogenetic analysis of GedF-like reductases and GedK-like dioxygenases

The coevolution of GedK-like dioxygenases and GedF-like reductases was analyzed

with MdpL (C8VQ61.1) and MdpK (C8VQ62.1) from Aspergillus nidulans, TpcI

(XP_751383.1)  and TpcG  (XP_751381.1)  from  Aspergillus  fumigatus, AgnL3

(A0A411PQN8.1) and AgnL4 (A0A411PQN7.1) from Paecilomyces variotii, DmxR6
                                              8

[SI physical page 13]
(QCL09097.1) and DmxR7 (QCL09098.1) from Cryptosporiopsis sp. 8999, AflY

(Q6UEF1.1) and AflX (Q6UEF2.1) from Aspergillus parasiticus SU-1, respectively.

The phylogenetic analysis of GedK-like dioxygenases, type I BVMOs and other well-

studied dioxygenases was conducted with the dioxygenases TDO (PDB/5TI9_A, Homo

sapiens),  DpgC  (PDB/2NP9_A,   Streptomyces   toyocaensis)   and  BphC-1

(PDB/1HAN_A,  Paraburkholderia  xenovorans);  Type   I  BVMOs  AFL838

(PDB/5J7X_A, Aspergillus flavus), CHMO (PDB/4RG3_A, Rhodococcus sp.), PAMO

(WP_011291921.1,  Thermobifida   fusca),  PeniC  (QDO73504.1,   Penicillium

griseofulvum) and PTI (WP_010984425.1, Streptomyces avermitilis); GedK and its

homologs MdpL, DmxR6, AgnL3, AflY and TpcI. MAFFT online version was used in

this study in order to evaluate L-INS-I and auto modes of alignment4, based on the

alignment sequences, the phylogenetic unrooted tree was constructed by NJ-Conserved

sites (16 AAs). The different colors of clades were painted by iTOL online service5.
