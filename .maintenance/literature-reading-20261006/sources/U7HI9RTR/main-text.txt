ABSTRACT: The C-10−C-4a bond cleavage of anthraquinone is believed to be a crucial step in fungal seco-anthraquinone
biosynthesis and has long been proposed as a classic Baeyer−Villiger oxidation. Nonetheless, genetic, enzymatic, and chemical
information on ring opening remains elusive. Here, a revised questin ring-opening mechanism was elucidated by in vivo gene
disruption, in vitro enzymatic analysis, and 18O chasing experiments. It has been conﬁrmed that the reductase GedF is responsible for
the reduction of the keto group at C-10 in questin to a hydroxyl group with the aid of NADPH. The C-10−C-4a bond of the
resultant questin hydroquinone is subsequently cleaved by the atypical cofactor-free dioxygenase GedK, giving rise to
desmethylsulochrin. This proposed bienzyme-catalytic and dioxygenation-mediated anthraquinone ring-opening reaction shows
universality.
A
nthraquinones are a class of ubiquitous natural products
in fungi and plants with diverse bioactivities, including
anticancer, antiviral, antioxidative, and anti-inﬂammatory
activities, and they have been clinically used as a laxative.1
Cleavage of the C-10−C-4a bond of the anthraquinone ring is
recognized as a key step for initiating the structural
rearrangement required to obtain numerous structurally
diverse seco-anthraquinone products, such as xanthones,
ergochromes, and grisandienes.2−4 Although isotopic labeling
studies, particularly by Vederas and Simpson,5−7 have
investigated fungal seco-anthraquinone biosynthesis in detail,
the anthraquinone ring-opening mechanism remains ambig-
uous.
Questin (1), a crucial anthraquinone intermediate in geodin
biosynthesis, has long been proposed to undergo classic
Baeyer−Villiger (BV) oxidation to give the ring-opening
product desmethylsulochrin (2).8 According to seminal work
of Sankawa, a BV monooxygenase (BVMO) catalyzes the
oxidation of 1 to give the ring-expanded lactone 3, and
subsequently, the seven-membered ring lactone is hydrolyzed
to 2 (Scheme 1A). Moreover, the ring-opening system of 1 is a
combination of an oxygenase, a hydrolase, and an electron
transfer protein.9 To decipher the mechanism of anthraqui-
none ring opening, the enzymatic conversion leading from 1 to
2 in Aspergillus terreus was studied by in vivo gene disruption, in
vitro enzymatic analysis, and 18O isotope labeling. Thus, a
novel bienzyme-catalytic and dioxygenation-mediated anthra-
quinone ring-opening mechanism was proposed (Scheme 1B).
In previous studies, tpcF, tpcG, and tpcI were proposed to be
the genes leading to ring opening from 1 to 2 in Aspergillus
fumigatus.10,11 The glutathione S-transferase-like protein gene
gedE, oxidoreductase gene gedF, and putative BVMO gene
gedK were identiﬁed from the geodin biosynthetic cluster in A.
terreus using tpcF, tpcG, and tpcI as probes, respectively. To
further clarify the functions of these genes, a homologous
recombination strategy was applied to construct single
knockout mutants of A. terreus AT01, generating the
inactivated mutants ΔgedE, ΔgedF, and ΔgedK (Figure S1).
HPLC and LC-HRMS analyses of fermentation cultures of
variants led to the interesting ﬁnding that both 1 and questinol
(5) accumulated in the ΔgedF and ΔgedK strains, but ΔgedE
and the native strain AT01 did not diﬀer signiﬁcantly (Figures
1A and S2). Compound 5 was structurally conﬁrmed to be a
hydroxylated derivative of 1 generated by an unidentiﬁed
oxidase12 (Figure 1B). Hence, we reasoned that both GedF
and GedK were long-sought enzymes that participated in the
anthraquinone ring cleavage of 1 and that GedE was not
involved. Notably, HPLC and LC-HRMS analyses showed that
an unknown product with an m/z value of 287.0915 [M + H]+
was detected in the fermentation cultures of only ΔgedK, not
ΔgedF (Figures 1A and S3). Due to the instability of this
product during extraction and puriﬁcation, its authentic
structure was temporarily unclear. However, the similarity
between the UV spectra of this product and emodin
hydroquinone 8b, a reduced product of emodin (6) generated
by Na2S2O4 (Figure S4),13 suggested that this compound
might be questin hydroquinone (4). Therefore, we surmised
that 1 was ﬁrst reduced to 4 by GedF, and then 4 served as the
substrate of GedK to produce 2.
To prove this hypothesis, in vitro enzymatic assays of GedF
and GedK were carried out successively. First, the ORF of gedF
was revised for its N-truncated annotation in the A. terreus
NIH2624 genome through protein sequence alignment using
TpcG as an entry (Figure S5 and Table S1). Next, the gedK

Subsequently, the in vitro activities of GedF and GedK were

predicted, compound 1 was completely converted into 2 in the
presence of GedF and GedK in 3 min (Figure 1C, viii).
Interestingly, the transformation also proceeded without

an anaerobic chamber, extracted by chloroform-d, and directly
subjected to NMR analysis. Although most of 4 was oxidized
back to 1 during the preparation process, a comparison of the
1H NMR data for the product and substrate 1 indicated that a
set of new proton signals was present in 4 (δH 5.48, 6.47, 6.69,
6.94, 7.04). Moreover, the key HMBC correlations from H-10
to C-4 and C-5 and from H-4 and H-5 to C-10 conﬁrmed that
the carbonyl group at C-10 in 1 was reduced to methine in 4
(Figures S10−S12, Table S3). Taken together, these in vivo
and in vitro results revealed that GedF was an anthraquinone
reductase responsible for transforming a cyclic ketone (in 1)
into a cyclitol group (in 4) at C-10, and then the C-10−C-4a
bond of 4 was cleaved by GedK, giving rise to 2 (Scheme 1B).
The 1 ring-opening reaction has long been considered a
classic BV oxidation reaction catalyzed by the BVMO GedK,8
but all of our foregoing ﬁndings showed otherwise. BVMOs are
a class of ﬂavoproteins that transform ketones into esters or
cyclic ketones into lactones by using molecular oxygen and the
cofactors ﬂavin and NAD(P)H.14 However, our study
illustrated that the ring-opening reaction of 1, cocatalyzed by
GedF and GedK, also occurred without FAD (Figure 1C, ix).
Strikingly, 1 was not reduced to two hydroquinone tautomers
by Na2S2O4 as 6 and physcion (7) did, which may be due to

To further decipher the catalytic mechanism for this atypical
bienzyme-catalyzed anthraquinone ring cleavage, isotope-
labeling experiments were conducted with
18O2 or H2
18O.
Interestingly, when 1 was converted into 2 via GedF and GedK
catalysis in the presence of 18O2, the m/z value of the product
was 323.0902 [M + H]+, 4 Da larger than that of 2 generated
in the atmosphere (319.0816, [M + H]+), while both native
and +4 species but no +2 species were observed when an in
vitro assay was performed in a 16O2 and 18O2 mixture (Figure
2). Moreover, no molecular weight change was detected when
the reaction was performed in H2
18O (319.0812, [M + H]+)
(Figure S18). These results indicated that both oxygen atoms
incorporated into 2 originated from the same O2 molecule and
ruled out two successive “monooxygenase” steps which were
proposed according to the in vivo
18O chasing results of
tajixanthone biosynthesis in Aspergillus variecolor.6,7 Hence, we
deduced that the ring cleavage of 4 is a dioxygenation catalyzed
by the dioxygenase GedK.
Dioxygenases are a large family of enzymes incorporating
both atoms of O2 into organic substrates.18 Here, based on the
reported catalytic mechanisms of two cofactor-independent
dioxygenases, HOD and DpgC,19,20 a likely bienzyme-catalytic
and dioxygenation-mediated anthraquinone ring-opening
mechanism was proposed. First, compound 1 is reduced to 4
by GedF using NADPH as an electron donor. Second,
deprotonation at C-10 in 4 generates conjugated anion
intermediate 8, and then single electron transfer (SET) takes
place from the anion to triplet oxygen to form conjugated
radical 9 and a superoxide anion. Subsequently, a covalent
bond can be formed between these two radicals to generate
peroxide intermediate 10, which is likely followed by a Michael
addition reaction generating the cycloperoxide anion transition
state 11.21−23 Finally, the bond rearrangements in 11 result in
peroxide bond breaking, giving rise to 12, and the protonation
of 12 yields end product 2 (Scheme 2).
Having characterized the likely mechanism of the 1 →4 →
2 transformation, we next evaluated the substrate promiscuity
of GedF and GedK. Similar GedF and GedK reactions were
performed using diﬀerent 1 analogues, and the reaction
products were identiﬁed by LC-HRMS and quantiﬁed by
HPLC peak area. Indeed, 5 and 1 were the most active
substrates tested, followed by 6, ω-hydroxyemodin (13), 7,
aloe-emodin (14), emodin-8-glucoside (15), and chrysophanol
(16), but this bienzyme-catalytic system did not show any
activity with rhein (17) (Figures 3A and S19).
Comparative phylogenetic analysis revealed that a number of
GedF and GedK homologues coevolved in diverse fungal seco-
anthraquinone biosynthetic clusters (Figure 3B) that shared
highly conserved residues and sequence similarity with each
other (Figure S20, S21). Moreover, Rossmann-fold NAD(P)
binding domains with the canonical sequence GXXGXXG
were found in all GedF-like reductases24 (Figure S20), and
their optimal electron donor was NADPH rather than NADH
(Figure S22). Phylogenetic analysis showed that GedK and its
analogous enzymes were clustered into one clade, while type I
BVMOs and other well-studied dioxygenases (e.g., DpgC)
were clustered into another two evolutionary clades, but the
GedK-like dioxygenases evolved closer to dioxygenases than
the BVMO PeniC (Figure 3C). Hence, we conﬁrmed that the
GedK-like enzymes were a class of unique cofactor-free

In summary, we revised the long-proposed anthraquinone
BV oxidative ring-opening hypothesis and extended the
seminal work by Mueller and Townsend.13,25 An intriguing
bienzyme-catalytic and dioxygenation-mediated anthraquinone
ring-opening mechanism was proposed. Speciﬁcally, 1 is
reduced to 4 by the reductase GedF in the presence of
NADPH. Subsequently, the C-10−C-4a bond of the hydro-
quinone ring in 4 is cleaved by the atypical dioxygenase GedK,
giving rise to 2. Additionally, the broad substrate speciﬁcities
and the wide homologue distributions of GedF and GedK
suggest the universality of the anthraquinone ring-opening
mechanism proposed here.
Based on the above conﬁrmed anthraquinone ring-opening
mechanism and some evidence obtained from parallel
systems,12,13,26−28 a likely pathway from 6 to monodictyphe-
none can be proposed. The GedF-like reductases might be
bifunctional enzymes catalyzing reduction of both 6 and 16 to
their hydroquinone forms. And the GedK-like dioxygenases
function in the ring cleavage of chrysophanol hydroquinone b
to generate monodictyphenone (Figure S23). Interestingly, the
GedK-catalyzed ring opening is highly speciﬁc at the C-10−C-
4a bond of 4, while the NsrF participating in neosartorin
biosynthesis catalyzed ring opening at either the C-10−C-10a
or C-10−C-4a bond of chrysophanol hydroquinone b (Figure
S24).28
The elucidation of the ring cleavage mechanism of
anthraquinone not only contributes to completely solving the
long mystery of key steps in fungal seco-anthraquinone
biosynthesis but also provides new enzymatic components,
namely, a reductase and a dioxygenase, to synthetic biology
module libraries of natural products.
