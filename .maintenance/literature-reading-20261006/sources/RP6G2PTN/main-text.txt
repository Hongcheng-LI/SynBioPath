Keywords:                                          Eupenifeldin, a bistropolone meroterpenoid, was ﬁrst discovered as an antitumor agent from the fungus
Tropolonic sesquiterpene                              Eupenicillium brefeldianum. We also isolated this compound and a new congener from a strain of Phoma sp.
Phoma sp.                              (CGMCC 10481), and evaluated their antitumor eﬀects. Eupenifeldin showed potent in vitro anti-glioma activity.
Eupenifeldin                                       This tropolone-humulene-tropolone meroterpenoid could be originated from two units of tropolone orthoqui-
Humulenol
                                           none methides and a 10-hydroxyhumulene moiety via hetero-Diels-Alder reactions. To explore the biosynthesis
Biosynthetic gene cluster
                                                     of this class of tropolonic sesquiterpenes, the genome of a eupenifeldin-producing Phoma sp. was sequenced and
                                                 analyzed. The biosynthetic gene cluster of eupenifeldin (eup) was identiﬁed and partially validated by genomic
                                                       analysis, gene disruption, and product analysis. A nonreducing polyketide synthase EupA, a FAD-dependent
                                           monooxygenase EupB, and a non-heme Fe (II)-dependent dioxygenase EupC, were identiﬁed as the enzymes
                                                   responsible for tropolone formation. While the terpene cyclase EupE of an unknown family was characterized to
                                                   catalyze humulene formation, and a cytochrome P450 enzyme EupD was responsible for hydroxylation of hu-
                                              mulene. This study sheds light on the biosynthesis of eupenifeldin, and paves the way to further decipher its
                                                   biosynthetic pathway.
