ABSTRACT: The biosynthetic gene cluster of the fungal meroterpenoid chrodrimanin B (4) was discovered in Penicillium
verruculosum TPU1311, and the complete biosynthetic pathway of 4 has been elucidated by heterologous reconstitution of its
biosynthesis in Aspergillus oryzae, as well as by in vitro characterizations of selected enzymes. The present study has identiﬁed
the polyketide synthase that produces 6-hydroxymellein (3) and also provided a biosynthetic platform of chrodrimanins for
further bioengineering.
F
ungal meroterpenoids comprise a large number of
structurally diverse natural products that possess a wide
range of biological activities.1 Most of the fungal meroterpenoids
are derived from both the polyketide and terpenoid pathways,1a
and these polyketide−terpenoid hybrid molecules have
provided clinically useful pharmaceutical drugs, such as
mycophenolic acid,2 as well as several promising drug leads.3
The identiﬁcation and characterization of the biosynthetic gene
cluster of pyripyropene A in 20104 accelerated the biosynthetic
studies on this class of natural products by numerous research
groups, which have solved many biosynthetic mysteries and
discovered a number of fascinating enzymes.1b However, the
reported studies are biased toward compounds derived from a
limited number of polyketides, most of which are generated by
nonreducing and partially reducing polyketide synthases
(PKSs).5 Thus, a biosynthetic study on a fungal meroterpenoid
with a nonterpenoid portion, which is not found in the others
with known biosynthetic genes, would facilitate our deeper
understanding of fungal meroterpenoid biosynthesis and could
provide genetic tools to artiﬁcially design meroterpenoid
pathways.
Chrodrimanin A (1) and verruculide A (2) are fungal
meroterpenoids with strong inhibitory activities against protein
tyrosine phosphatase 1B (PTP1B), and therefore, they could
potentially be developed into drugs for the treatment of type 2
diabetes or obesity (Figure 1A).6 Despite the remarkable
biological activities of these natural products, the molecular
bases for the biosyntheses of 1 and 2 have never been
investigated. From a biosynthetic point of view, the common
nonterpenoid moiety of 1 and 2 should be derived from the
polyketide 6-hydroxymellein (3), but interestingly, no PKS has
been discovered that produces 3. Thus, the elucidation of the
biosyntheses of 1 and 2 would provide new insight into the
mechanism for the diversity generation in fungal meroterpenoid
pathways. Furthermore, chrodrimanin B (4), which appears to
be the end product of the pathway involving 1 and 2, did not
exhibit the PTP1B inhibitory activity,6 while 4 functions as a
potent blocker of insect GABA-gated chloride channels.7
Therefore, the biosynthetic study could also contribute to the
selective and eﬃcient production of desired bioactive molecules
by metabolic pathway bioengineering.
To discover the biosynthetic gene cluster of chrodrimanin B
(4), we initially performed the whole genome sequencing
analysis of Penicillium verruculosum TPU1311, which reportedly
produces 4 and its analogues, including 1 and 2.6 By analogy to

a farnesyltransferase, an epoxidase, and a terpene cyclase to
provide the backbone structure of 4. The search for a genomic
region with genes encoding these enzymes led to the discovery
of a putative biosynthetic gene cluster of 4 with ten genes, which
was designated as the cdm cluster (Figure 1B and Table S1;
DDBJ/EMBL/GenBank accession number: LC422696). The
cdm cluster encodes the highly reducing PKS CdmE, and the
Pfam analysis suggested that the PKS possesses the KS-AT-DH-
MT-ER-KR-ACP domain architecture. However, further
analysis of its sequence indicated that the DH and MT domains
are inactive due to mutations of their catalytic residues,8 as
consistent with the required reactions to synthesize 6-
hydroxymellein (3). The ER domain could be active, but it
should not aﬀect the PKS chemistry due to the inactive DH
domain. The gene cluster also encodes the UbiA-like
prenyltransferase CdmH, the FAD-dependent monooxygenase
(FMO) CdmI, and the membrane-bound terpene cyclase
CdmG, which are expected to be responsible for the generation
of the ﬁrst cyclized intermediate, 3-hydroxypentacecilide A (5)
(Figure 1C). Five potential tailoring enzymes are also encoded
by the cdm cluster, among which the acetyltransferase CdmC
appears to be involved in the very last step of the biosynthesis, to
convert 1 into 4. The short-chain dehydrogenase/reductase
(SDR) CdmF is highly homologous to the known 3-OH
dehydrogenases found in the fungal meroterpenoid pathways,9
and therefore, CdmF would oxidize the C-3 hydroxyl group of 5
or its analogue to the ketone functionality. The three oxidative
enzymes, CdmA, CdmD, and CdmJ, seem to be engaged in the
oxidative decorations in the late-stage biosynthesis, but their
exact functions were not predictable at this stage.
We sought to prove the predicted early stage biosynthesis
leading to 5, by heterologously expressing the relevant genes in
the fungus Aspergillus oryzae NSAR1.10 Initially, the PKS gene
cdmE was solely introduced to the A. oryzae strain, which led to
the production of the expected product 3 (Figure 2A, lane (i)).11
The absolute conﬁguration of 3 was determined based on the
structures of chrodrimanins and verruculides and comparison of
the speciﬁc rotation with the reported value.12 This result
demonstrates that CdmE is a previously undescribed PKS,
which serves as the 6-hydroxymellein synthase, although the
multistep syntheses of 3 have been proposed in other fungal
pathways.13 The coexpression of cdmE and the prenyltransferase
gene cdmH then produced the hydrophobic metabolite 6,
named verruculide C, in the mycelium (Figure 2A, lane (ii)),
conﬁrming the function of CdmH as the 6-hydroxymellein 5-
farnesyltransferase. Further introduction of the FMO gene cdmI
yielded the new product verruculide B (7)6 (Figure 2A, lane
(iii), the dihydroxy analogue of 6, which should be derived from
the hydrolysis of the epoxide 7′ formed by CdmI (Figure 1).
Finally, the four gene-expression system, including the terpene
cyclase gene cdmG, expectedly produced the pentacyclic
molecule 3-hydroxypentacecilide A (5) (Figure 2A, lane
(iv)),14 which established the biosynthetic route to the
backbone structure of chrodrimanin B (4). In the terpene

of the substrate into the chair-boat conformation, as proposed
for the AndB-catalyzed reaction (Figure 1C),9b although the
primary sequence of CdmG is much more similar to that of Pyr4

Next, we investigated the functions of the predicted tailoring
enzymes encoded by the cdm cluster. We ﬁrst focused on the
SDR CdmF, as the close homologues of CdmF are widespread in
fungal meroterpenoid pathways and most of them accept the
products of the terpene cyclases to perform the alcohol
oxidation reaction at the C-3 position.9 Thus, we reasoned
that CdmF also has a similar activity, utilizing 5 as its substrate,
and introduced cdmF into the A. oryzae strain producing 5. The
transformant harboring cdmF produced chrodrimanin C (8)
(Figure 2A, lane (v)), the dehydrogenated product of 5, proving
that CdmF functions as the 3-OH dehydrogenase.
The next step should be catalyzed by one of the three
oxidative enzymes, the Fe(II)/α-ketoglutarate (αKG)-depend-
ent dioxygenases CdmA and CdmD and the cytochrome P450
monooxygenase CdmJ, but we could not predict which one of
the three enzymes works next by only the bioinformatic analysis.
Therefore, the three oxygenase genes were individually
transferred to the ﬁve gene-expressing transformant constructed
above. As a result, only the introduction of the P450 gene cdmJ
provided two new metabolites (Figure 2A, lanes vi to viii)). The
major product 9 was identiﬁed as chrodrimanin H,15 while the
other one 10 was its 3-hydroxyl analogue, chrodrimanin F.15
Collectively, the P450 CdmJ accepts both 5 and 8 as substrates
and functions as the C-7 β-hydroxylase.
To elucidate the functions of the remaining two dioxygenases,
cdmA and cdmD were again individually expressed in the A.
oryzae strain producing 9, but contrary to our expectations,
neither transformant accumulated a detectable new product
(Figure 2A, lanes ix and x). Surprisingly, two new compounds
were detected upon the coexpression of cdmA and cdmD in the
same host strain (Figure 2A, lane xi). One of the new metabolites
was identiﬁed as chrodrimanin A (1),6 while the other was
determined to be chrodrimanin E (11),15 which seems to be the
direct precursor of 1. On the bases of the structures of 1 and 11,
the αKG-dependent dioxygenases CdmA and CdmD have
dehydrogenation activity between C-1 and C-2 and the β-
hydroxylation activity at C-7′. Interestingly, the removal of the
P450 gene cdmJ from this eight gene-expression system also
provided three new products, 2, 12, and 13 lacking the C-7
hydroxyl group (Figure 2A, lane xii), among which 2 was
identical to verruculide A.6 In contrast, compound 12 was found
to be the C-7 deoxy analogue of 1, while 13 possesses a C-1 α-
hydroxyl group and seems to be a shunt pathway product.
Compounds 12 and 13 were hereby designated as chrodrima-
nins T and U, respectively. Finally, the introduction of the
acetyltransferase gene cdmC successfully transformed 1 into the
end product chrodrimanin B (4) (Figure 2A, lane xiii),16 thus
accomplishing the total biosynthesis of 4 in A. oryzae.
Although the biosynthesis of chrodrimanin B (4) was
successfully reconstituted in a heterologous host, some
ambiguity remained regarding the functions of the two
dioxygenases CdmA and CdmD. To obtain more in-depth
insight into the catalyzes by these enzymes, both CdmA and
CdmD were puriﬁed from an E. coli expression system as His6-
tagged proteins for in vitro characterization. On the basis of their
sequence similarities to the known Fe(II)/αKG-dependent
dioxygenases involved in the fungal meroterpenoid pathways,
the puriﬁed enzymes were subjected to in vitro enzymatic assays,
in a similar manner to the previous reports.9d,e,17 Initially,
chrodrimanin C (8) was reacted individually with CdmA and
CdmD, and interestingly, both reactions provided products in an
enzyme-dependent manner (Figure 3A). CdmA converted 8
into verruculide A (2) with the C−C double bond at C-1/C-2,
as well as chrodrimanin U (13), while CdmD yielded the new
product 14, which possessed the β-hydroxyl group at C-7′ and
was named verruculide D (Figure 3F). The coincubation of
CdmA and CdmD with 8 generated chrodrimanin T (12), as
expected (Figure 3A). Collectively, these observations indicate
that CdmA and CdmD serve as the C-1/C-2 dehydrogenase and
the C-7′ β-hydroxylase, respectively. Given the co-occurrence of
2 and the 1α-hydroxylated product 13, CdmA would initially
abstract the α-oriented hydrogen at C-1 to perform the
dehydrogenation reaction (Figure 3F). Meanwhile, 13 was
expectedly not converted into 12 when coincubated with both
CdmA and CdmD (Figure S3), which conﬁrms that 13 is a shunt
pathway product. To investigate the intermediacy of 2 and 14 in
the chrodrimanin B pathway, 2 and 14 were respectively
incubated with CdmD and CdmA. As a result, only 2 was
transformed into 12 (Figure 3B,C), suggesting that 14 is not a
pathway intermediate and that CdmA must function before
CdmD. Finally, the in vitro enzymatic assay with chrodrimanin

The in vivo reconstitution of the biosynthesis and the in vitro
analysis of the two dioxygenases indicate that there are multiple
pathways to synthesize the end product chrodrimanin B (4)
from the ﬁrst cyclized intermediate 3-hydroxypentacecilide A
(5) (Scheme 1), although it is diﬃcult to predict which pathway
is mainly utilized in P. verruculosum. In these proposed pathways,
the SDR CdmF, the αKG-dependent dioxygenases CdmA and
CdmD, and the P450 CdmJ function as a 3-OH dehydrogenase,
a C-1/C-2 dehydrogenase, a C-7′ β-hydroxylase, and a C-7 β-
hydroxylase, respectively. However, the substrate speciﬁcities of
these enzymes do not seem to be very strict, and therefore, the
synthesis of 4 can be achieved by several diﬀerent pathways. It is
intriguing that the dioxygenases CdmA and CdmD did not
solely work in vivo. This phenomenon may have occurred
because they need to interact with each other to be catalytically
functional at their physiological concentrations. This should be
further conﬁrmed in future studies. The last step of the
biosynthesis is proposed to be performed by the acetyltransfer-
ase CdmC. It is possible that CdmC is also a promiscuous
enzyme, but given that CdmD works after CdmA and that the
acetylated form of 12 has not been isolated before or obtained in
this study, we reasoned that the acetylation mainly occurs to 1.
In summary, we have identiﬁed the biosynthetic gene cluster
of the fungal meroterpenoid chrodrimanin B (4) and
successfully reconstituted its biosynthesis in the heterologous
fungus A. oryzae, which enabled the eﬃcient syntheses of several
bioactive natural products and provided plausible biosynthetic
routes leading to 4. The biosynthetic study revealed the PKS
CdmE, which synthesizes 6-hydroxymellein (3) as its product.
Although 3 can be generated in two steps, involving a
nonreducing PKS and a reductase,13 CdmE is the ﬁrst PKS
that produces 3 without the aid of any other enzymes, thus
broadening the repertoire of the fungal PKSs. Taken together,
our present study not only provided a biosynthetic platform of
the chrodrimanin-type molecules but also indicated the
importance of focusing on a compound with an unexploited
scaﬀold to discover new biosynthetic logic.
