Biosynthesis of Dothideomins Reveals a Fungal P450 That

ABSTRACT: Dothideomins are antibacterial bis(anthraquinone)
polyketides isolated from the endophytic fungus Dothideomycetes sp.
BMC-101, featuring a unique 6/6/6/5/6/6/6 heptacyclic scaffold
imbedded with a tricyclo[5.2.2.0.4,8]undecane core. Although the
structures and antibacterial potential are attractive, the biosynthesis
process and the formation of a heptacyclic scaffold, especially the
tricyclo[5.2.2.0.4,8]undecane cage-like core, are unclear. Here, we
elucidated the biosynthesis of dothideomins C and D encoded by a
dot gene cluster through heterologous expression, in vivo feeding
experiments, and in vitro biochemical assays. Our findings reveal an
enzyme cascade involved in the conversion of the precursor emodin
into dothideomins. Specifically, the cytochrome P450 monooxygenase
DotG is shown to solely catalyze the unprecedented formation of triple
C−C bonds and construct the tricyclo[5.2.2.0.4,8]undecane-embedded
skeleton. This study enhances the comprehension of the P450 enzyme-controlled formation of complex natural products.
■INTRODUCTION
Natural products possessing diverse structures and significant
biological activities serve as essential sources of drug lead
compounds.1 Anthraquinones (AQs) are characterized by a
9,10-anthracenedione with a tricyclic core structure (A, B, and
C rings), exhibiting a wide range of pharmacological
activities2,3 and biologically originating from a common
polyketide precursor, emodin (1).4,5 Dimeric AQs, also
known as bisanthraquinones (BQs), consist of two identical
or different anthraquinone monomers linked through C−C or
C−O bonds. Including simply dimerized by one or two cross-
links, such as skyrin (11),6 cladofulvin,7,8 and (−)-rubroskyr-
in,9 much more complicated cage-like structures were also
formed by three or more cross-links, such as (+)-rugulosin A
(12),9,10 (+)-cytoskyrin,11 (+)-rugulin,12 purpurogenone,13−15
and deoxypurpurogenone (Figure 1 and Figure S1).16
Additionally, BQs usually exhibit more potent biological
activities than their respective monomers, demonstrating a
diverse array of properties, including cytotoxic,17 antimicro-
bial,18−20 insecticidal,21 anti-influenzal,22 and anti-HIV23
activities.
Although BQs are continuously of significant interest to
chemical synthesis due to their unique congested polycyclic
system and impressive bioactivity, the biosynthesis has been
largely enigmatic so far. Recently, the elegant studies on the
biosynthesis of rugulosin A (12)24 and beticolin 125,26 showed
that the cytochrome P450 monooxygenase (P450) catalyzes
the crucial dimerization reaction. In the biosynthetic process of
rugulosin A (12), the P450 RugG catalyzed the key 5,5′-
dimerization of 1, and then, the cage-like structure was formed
by an additional aldo-keto reductase (AKR) RugH-catalyzed
ketone reduction and a spontaneous intramolecular Michael
addition.24
In 2022, we isolated four antibacterial cage-like BQs,
dothideomins A−D, featuring a unique 6/6/6/5/6/6/6
heptacyclic or 6/6/6/5/6/3/6/6 octocyclic scaffold (Figure
1).19 Distinguished from those in other BQ skeletons (Figure
1), the embedded tricyclo[5.2.2.0.4,8]undecane core motif in
dothideomins is dimerized by emodin (1) and an A-ring
opening and C-8 lost monomer (Scheme 1). Notably, most of
the ring-opening reactions of AQs occurred in the B-ring
typically catalyzed by Baeyer−Villiger monooxygenase or
dioxygenase.4 The enzyme responsible for cleaving the A-ring
of AQs has not been reported.4 In addition, dimerization of

■RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Identification of the Dothideomin Biosynthetic Gene
Cluster. To identify the biosynthetic gene cluster (BGC) of
dothideomins, the original producer Dothideomycetes sp. BMC-
101 was sequenced. Inspired by the biosynthetic progress of
rugulosin A (12), we analyzed the genome sequence of
Dothideomycetes sp. BMC-101 by employing rugG (accession
number: MZ286349)24 as a query, which led to the discovery
of a gene cluster designated as dot (Figure 2A). The dot gene
cluster is highly homologous to rug, encoding a total of 11
proteins (DotA, DotB, and E−M). Except for the additional
SDR gene dotM in dot cluster, all of the other 10 proteins,
nonreducing polyketide synthase (nrPKS, DotA), β-lactamase-
type thioesterase (DotB), anthrone oxygenase (DotE),
decarboxylase (DotF), cytochrome P450 monooxygenase
(DotG), aldo-keto reductase (DotH), MFS-type transporter
(DotJ), and three transcriptional factors (DotI, DotK, and
DotL), exhibit a high sequence identity with those in rug (54−
82% amino acid sequence identity (Figure 2A and Table S4).
Referring to the biosynthetic pathway of rugulosin A (12), four
enzymes (DotA, DotB, DotE, and DotF) encoded by the dot
gene cluster are probably responsible for the generation of
precursor 1 (Scheme 1). However, it is still unknown what are
the functions of other enzymes and how dothideomins
synthesized.
Considering the difficult genetic manipulation, long
fermentation period (4 weeks), and low yield (1.6−2.6 mg/
L) in the original strain, the dot cluster was expressed in
Aspergillus nidulans A1145 (AN). Compounds 9 and 10 were
obtained from the transformant harboring the entire functional
gene (AN-dotABEFGHM) (Figure 2B, trace (v)), and their
structures were fully confirmed by mass spectrometry (MS)
and NMR data (Table S9 and Figures S53−S66). This result
evidenced that the dot cluster is responsible for the
biosynthesis of dothideomins.
Elucidation of the Dothideomins C (9) and D (10)
Biosynthetic Process in A. nidulans. To investigate the
cascade reactions involved in the assembly line of 10, we
attempted a stepwise gene expression in A. nidulans A1145.
According to the predicted function and reported biosynthesis
of AQs, we first expressed the four genes dotABEF in A.
nidulans (AN-dotABEF), which lead to the isolation of the
major product 1, and three minor ones citreorosein (2),
emodic acid (3), and 7-hydroxyemodin (4) (Figure 2B, trace
(ii)). Compounds 1−4 were identified by 1H and 13C NMR
data (Table S6 and Figures S15−S26).27−30 Since the
transformant AN-dotABEF lacks additional oxidoreductases,
compounds 2−4 were inferred to be the byproducts attributed
to the oxidation of 1 performed by the endogenous enzymes of
A. nidulans, which was confirmed by in vivo feeding
experiments (Figure 3, trace (ii)).
To explore the pathway for the conversion of 1 into 10, the
P450 gene dotG, presumed to encode the dimerase, was

Compound 8 (named dothideomin A1) was purified and

precursor to 9 (Scheme 1). These results indicate that the
P450 DotG is powerful and mediates the formation of various
structures starting from 1, including the unusual cage-like core
in dothideomin.
To identify the functions of the remaining AKR DotH and
SDR DotM, we coexpressed the gene dotH with dotABEFG,
which resulted in the production of 9 and trace amount of 10
(Figure 2B, trace (iv)). Comparing the products of AN-
dotABEFGHM, dotM was proved to form 10 from 9 (Figure
2B, traces iv and (v)). These results revealed that DotH likely
catalyzes the stereoselective reduction upon the C-6 carbonyl
group of 8 to form 9, and DotM achieves the final product 10
by stereoselective reduction upon the C-6′ carbonyl group in
9. Besides, we also noticed that a trace amount of 12 could be
detected in the AN-dotABEFGH and AN-dotABEFGHM
strains (Figure 2B, traces iv and (v)), indicating that DotH
can also exert the same catalytic function as RugH.
Our in vivo results in A. nidulans demonstrated that the
P450 DotG is responsible for mediating the A-ring cleavage of
1 and the formation of tricyclo[5.2.2.0.4,8]undecane core
skeleton (Scheme 1). Contrastingly, in the biosynthesis of
rugulosin A (12), the corresponding P450 RugG is only able to
catalyze the formation of one C−C bond, resulting in skyrin
(11) in A. oryzae.24 The production of the cage-like structure
in 12 required further participation of both the P450 RugG
and the AKR RugH. To check the function of RugG and
eliminate the influence from heterologous hosts, we searched
and identified a highly homologous gene cluster (Tp_rug) to
rug from the fungus Talaromyces sp. HDN1820200 (producer
of 12 in our lab)33 and expressed the Tp_rug gene cluster in A.
nidulans (Figure 2A and Table S5). Consistent with the
literature, the expression of Tp_rugABEF in A. nidulans
resulted in the production of precursor 1, and the addition
of Tp_rugG led to the formation of 11 (Figure 2B, traces vi
and vii). Further coexpression of Tp_rugH with Tp_rugABEFG
is required to the formation of the cage-like structure 12
(Figure 2B(vii) and Figures S70−S72; Table S10). These
results indicated that Tp_rugG and Tp_rugH possess functions
analogous to those of their orthologues RugG and RugH in
vivo, albeit using different heterologous expression hosts.
Meanwhile, comparative analysis of the fermentation of the dot
and Tp_rug gene clusters in A. nidulans (Figure 2B) suggested
that the two homologous P450s (RugG and DotG) perform
distinct functions in the biosynthesis of cage-like BQs.
Determination of the Function and Enzymatic
Mechanism of DotG. The phylogenetic analysis indicated
that DotG is closely related to the P450 enzymes catalyzing the
dimerization of natural products, including RugG, NsrP,
AacuE, DmxR5, and ClaM (Figure S2). Further multiple
sequence alignment revealed that DotG shares moderate
sequence identity to Tp_RugG (58%), RugG (58%),24 NsrP
(40%),34 AacuE (48%),35 DmxR5 (40%),36 and ClaM (35%)37
(Figure S3). However, our in vivo results indicate that the
P450 DotG is capable of converting 1 into 8, involving not
only the expected dimerization reaction but also a rare A-ring
cleavage and decarboxylation of 1 (Scheme 1). To clarify the
function of DotG, we attempted to obtain purified protein
from Escherichia coli but failed due to its membrane-bound
nature (Figure S4). Therefore, a transformant expressing dotG
or Tp-rugG individually in A. nidulans was constructed and
substrate 1 was fed. The results clearly showed that
transformant AN-dotG generated 5−8 and 11 by feeding 1
(Figure 3, trace (iii)). Conversely, transformant AN-Tp_rugG
mainly yielded 11 when fed with 1 (Figure 3, trace (iv)).
Further feeding experiments are performed to verify whether
compounds 2−7 and 11 are related to the production of 8. We
fed these compounds to strain AN-dotG, and the results
showed that 2 can be converted to 3 under the catalysis of
endogenous oxidase in A. nidulans, while there are no new
compounds detected and the quantity of compounds 3−5, 7,
and 11 stay the same indicating that they are not utilized
(Figure S5A,B). Therefore, we conclude that 2−4 are
byproducts attributed to the oxidation of 1 by the endogenous
enzymes of A. nidulans (Figure S5E). When 6 was added to a
blank medium or fed to the AN-wild type, 7 was successfully
detected in the extracts (Figure S5B, traces i−iv), demonstrat-
ing that 6 possibly spontaneously formed 7 through a Friedel−
Crafts acylation reaction (Figure S5F). In addition, 5−7 as well
as a new trace compound 5a with a MW of 298 were detected
in a 6 day solution of compound 8 dissolved by methanol
(Figure S5C,D), indicating that these compounds were
degradation products of 8 (Figure S5F).

The above results indicate that P450 DotG solely converts 1
to the modified cage-like BQ 8 can be by forming the
tricyclo[5.2.2.0.4,8]undecane core structure. Based on the
results, the reaction mechanism of the P450 DotG is
speculated as (1) DotG catalyzes the formation of radical
intermediates 1a′ and 1b′ (Scheme 1); (2) 1a′ and
1b′undergo the radical-mediated addition reaction to produce
intermediate 1a. DotG then catalyzes the Baeyer−Villiger
oxidation reaction like SmP450-239 and AusI,40 inserting an
oxygen atom between the C-8′ and C-8′a of 1a to create the
lactone ring in 1b. 1b then could be transformed into 1c via
hydrolysis; (3) 1c undergoes an intramolecular Michael
addition reaction catalyzed by DotG and decarboxylates to
form 1d, which then continues to undergo an intramolecular
Michael addition reaction catalyzed by DotG to form modified
caged BQ 8 (Scheme 1). To further understand the
mechanism of DotG-mediated the tricyclo[5.2.2.0.4,8]-
undecane core formation, we next carried out density
functional theory (DFT) calculations on the key steps based

Determination of the Functions of DotH and DotM.
Following the functional elucidation of DotG, we attempted to
unravel the catalytic functions of DotH and DotM. DotH is a
member of AKR, phylogenetically close to RugH and
Tp_RugH (Figures S9 and S10). When 1 was fed with the
strain AN-dotH, no additional products were observed (Figure
6A, trace (iii)), suggesting that DotH is unable to modify the
monomer 1. However, feeding 1 with the transformant AN-
dotGH led to the production of 9−12 compared to the AN-
dotG strain (Figure 6A, trace (iv)), suggesting that DotH likely
catalyzes C-6 of 8 to form 9. In order to further support the
biochemical role of DotH, the intron-free DotH was cloned
from the cDNA and heterologously expressed in E. coli BL21
(DE3) (Figure S11A). Incubation of 8 with 10 μM purified
DotH and 2.0 mM NADH led to the yield of 9, excluding 7,
which is a spontaneous product of 8 (Figure 6B and Figure
S11B). The above results confirmed that DotH is responsible
for the stereoselective reduction at the C-6 carbonyl position of
8 to yield 9.
DotH shares 67% identity with RugH (Tp_RugH), which
catalyzes the C-6 reduction of intermediate b and triggers the
spontaneous Michael addition reaction in the biosynthesis of
12 (Scheme 1). To confirm that DotH and RugH have the
same function, we constructed chimeric expression strains by
combining the P450 genes (dotG and Tp_rugG) and AKR
genes (dotH and Tp_rugH). Consistent with the literature
reports, feeding 1 with AN-Tp_rugGH can yield 12 (Figure
6A, trace vii) and replacing Tp_rugH with dotH (AN-
Tp_rugG-dotH) also led to the production of 12 (Figure 6A,
trace ix). The results suggested that DotH can adopt the
product of Tp_RugG as a substrate for the reduction reaction
consistent with RugH. In contrast, the strain AN-dotG-
Tp_rugH exhibited the identical metabolic profile as AN-
dotG when fed 1 (Figure 6A, trace (v)), indicating that
Tp_rugH could not adopt the products of DotG. These results
demonstrated that although both enzymes catalyze the
reduction of the carbonyl group at the C-6 position, DotH
exhibits a greater potential than RugH to adopt both simple
and cage-like BQs (Scheme 1).
Suggested by the heterologous experiments (Figure 2B,
traces iv and (v)), DotM can convert 9 to 10. The multiple
sequence alignment showed that DotM belongs to the
“classical” SDR subfamily and possess the typical motif
TGXXXGXG for binding redox cofactors NAD(P)+ or
NADPH (Figure S12).41 To confirm the function of DotM,
9 was fed with the transformant harboring dotM individually or
as an empty vector. The results showed that both trans-
formants were capable of producing 10, while the expression of
DotM exhibits a much higher yield of 10, indicating that DotM
performs the final step of stereoselective reduction (Figure
6C,D).
Antimicrobial Activity Assays. The antimicrobial activ-
ities of the isolated compounds were tested against the
pathogenic bacteria E. coli, Acinetobacter baumannii, Klebsiella
pneumoniae, Salminella sp., Staphylococcus aureus, methicillin-
resistant S. aureus T144 (MRSA), Enterococcus faecalis, and
vancomycin-resistant E. faecium CAU369 (VREfm), as well as
the pathogenic fungus Canidia albicans. Compounds 9−12
were active against S. aureus and MRSA with minimum
inhibitory concentrations (MICs) ranging from 0.5 to 16 μg/
mL (Table S11). Also, compounds 9, 10, and 12 displayed
antibacterial activities against E. faecalis and VREfm with MICs
spanning from 1 to 16 μg/mL. Conversely, compounds 1−7
were inactive.
■CONCLUSIONS
In this study, we uncover and characterize the biosynthetic
gene cluster responsible for producing the unprecedented cage-
like BQ dothideomins C (9) and D (10). The biosynthetic
pathway was elucidated through heterologous expression, in
vivo feeding, and in vitro biochemical experiments indicating
that three enzymes (P450 DotG, AKR DotH, and SDR DotM)
are crucial for converting the common precursor emodin (1)
into the structurally complex compounds 9 and 10.
Specifically, the versatile P450 enzyme DotG exhibits the
ability to catalyze the formation of 8 with a tricyclo-
[5.2.2.0.4,8]undecane core cage structure from 1. This involves
not only a dimerization reaction but also an unprecedented A-
ring opening and a decarbonylation reaction. Subsequently, the
AKR DotH and SDR DotM enzymes facilitate a two-step
reduction process to yield 9 and 10. Compared with the
biosynthesis of (+)-rugulosin A (12), our results highlight the
significant role played by P450 and AKR enzymes in
determining the assembly pattern of BQs, with P450 being
particularly influential. Furthermore, we have isolated three
new compounds (6−8) from heterologous strains, of which
compound 7 possesses a unique 6/6/6/5/6/6 hexacyclic
scaffold. In conclusion, the evaluation of the dothideomin
biosynthesis enables us to characterize a powerful P450 that is
responsible for forming the uncommon cage motif. This
research not only underscores the importance of P450 in the
biosynthesis of BQs but also enhances our understanding of
how nature produces complex and diverse natural products.
