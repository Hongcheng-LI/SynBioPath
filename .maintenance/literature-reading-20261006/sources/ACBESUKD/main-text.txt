Discovery and Biosynthetic Interrogation of a Cobalt-Containing
Terpene-Polyketide-Nonribosomal Peptide Tribrid with
Anticoronavirus Activity

ABSTRACT: The structural complexity of natural products arises from the complexity
of their biosynthetic pathways and directly contributes to their biological activities.
Guided by this principle, we screened 500 microbial extracts using high-throughput
screening to identify structurally novel inhibitors of coronavirus main protease, 3CLpro.
We discovered and structurally characterized a new scaffold, cobaltribin, the only
isolated cobalt-containing natural product except vitamin B12 cobalamins. We further
analyzed cobaltribin analogs to expand this unique class of anticoronavirus compounds.
Furthermore, evidence from our bioinformatic, isotope labeling, and gene deletion
analyses suggested that cobaltribin is a tribrid natural product resulting from an
unprecedented terpene-polyketide (PK)-nonribosomal peptide (NRP) trihybridized
biosynthesis. Notably, cobaltribin inhibits live severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) replication in
infected Vero E6 cells without toxicity to the host, demonstrating its promise as a therapeutic candidate against SARS-CoV-2.
■INTRODUCTION
The vast structural diversity of microbial natural products is
dependent on the equally diverse biosynthetic pathways and
enzymes that are responsible for producing these compounds.
Hybridization of different classes of natural products through
crosstalk among distinct biosynthetic gene clusters (BGCs)
further expands the structural diversity and holds great
potential for discovery of novel natural products with unique
biological activities.1−3 In this study, we report a novel scaffold
arising from an unprecedented terpene-PK-NRP tribridization.
This compound, named cobaltribin (1), also represents the
only cobalt-containing microbial secondary metabolite except
vitamin B12. Furthermore, we show 1’s inhibitory activity
against 3-chymotrypsin-like protease (3CLpro), the main
protease essential for the replication of coronaviruses, as well
as its direct inhibition of live SARS-CoV-2 replication.
Altogether, we report the identification and characterization
of 1, a first-of-its-kind, cobalt-containing tribrid natural product
that holds promise as a 3CLpro-targeting anticoronavirus
therapeutic.
■RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Bioactivity-Guided Discovery and Structure Elucida-
tion of Cobaltribin (1). We screened 500 microbial extracts
for 3CLpro inhibitory activity1 and prioritized 50 of these
extracts (Table S1) to further identify potentially novel
metabolites against 3CLpro. Guided by mass spectrometry
(MS) and a high-throughput 3CLpro-inhibiting assay, we
identified five classes of bioactive compounds with diverse
scaffolds. The four classes (Figure S1), adipostatins,
asterriquinones, skyrins, and spirohexalines, were identified as
previously reported natural products with antiviral or
antibacterial activities.4−7 The remaining class, produced by a
soil actinomycete Streptomyces davaonensis DSM101723, was
prioritized for further investigation because the extract was
active (82.8% inhibition at 10 μg/mL) and could not be
dereplicated via tandem MS analysis. Thus, we isolated 3.1 mg
of the most abundant active compound of this class, cobaltribin
(1), from a scale-up culture of S. davaonensis DSM101723 in
20 L of SPM broth (40 × 0.5 L). We first used one- and two-
dimensional NMR to elucidate the chemical structure of 1
(Figure 1A and Figures S2−S6; see Supporting Information
Chemical Structure Elucidation for details). Briefly, three
structural motifs (parts A−C, Figure 1A) along with their
connectivity were determined: First, a 3-acyltetramic acid ring
system (part A, Figure 1A), as evidenced by key HMBC
correlations of H-7 with C-5, H-6 with C-4/C-5, and H-27
with C-2 (Figure S6 and Table S2), and reminiscent of the 3-
acyltetramic acid five-membered ring structure of the fungal

Furthermore, we obtained high-quality crystals of 1 (Figure
1B and Figure S7) from a methanol/water solvent system for
single-crystal X-ray diffraction. Surprisingly, 1 was found to be
a dimer, consisting of two identical monomers bridged by an
oxygen−cobalt coordination bond (CCDC 2159547, Figure
1B, and Tables S3−S11). The cobalt-incorporated dimeric
character of 1 likely explains the absence of obvious exact mass
corresponding to 1 in the MS data. The Co−O bond lengths
align with the Co atomic scattering factor in the X-ray data
(Figure 1B). Additionally, the ruby color of the crystals (Figure
1B), characteristic of cobalt and cobalt-containing compounds,
supports the presence of cobalt in 1. The elemental
composition of cobalt in 1 was further confirmed through
inductively coupled plasma MS (ICP-MS) (Figure S8) and MS
analysis (Figure S9). The X-ray single crystal diffraction
analysis also determined the absolute configurations of 1’s
stereocenters (Figure S7).
Interestingly, 1 consistently predominated in crude extracts,
even when cobalt was scarce or when competing metals
[Cu(II), Fe(II), Ni(II), and Mn(II)] were added in the
medium used for culturing S. davaonensis DSM101723 (Figure
S9). Additionally, the cobalt of 1 prepared in a methanol
solution could not be replaced by individually added metals
[Cu(II), Fe(II), Ni(II), and Mn(II)] (Figure S10), further
supporting the preference for Co(II) in forming 1. Also, the
genome of S. davaonensis DSM101723 encodes homologues of
well-known cobalt-specific transporters CbiMNQO9 (Table
S12), supporting the potential efficient uptake of cobalt from
medium into the bacterial cells. However, the strain lacks the
canonical cobalt chelatases, such as CbiK and CobNST,9,10
which are typically involved in vitamin B12 biosynthesis. This
suggests that the Co(II) incorporation could occur through
direct coordination, although the possible involvement of
additional or yet-unidentified chelatase-like proteins cannot be
excluded.
Cobaltribin (1) Inhibits Coronavirus 3CLpro and Live
SARS-CoV-2 Replication without Host Cell Toxicity.
Following the structural elucidation, we evaluated both in vitro
3CLpro inhibitory and live viral inhibitory activities of 1.
Compound 1 displayed dose-dependent inhibitory activity
against 3CLpro, with an IC50 value of 8.1 μM (Figure 2A),
which is comparable to previously reported inhibitors of
3CL.11,12 Next, we tested the efficacy of 1 in reducing the
replication of live SARS-CoV-2 in infected Vero E6 cells.13,14
We observed a 98% reduction in SARS-CoV-2 replication in
Vero E6 when treated with 10 μM of 1 (Figure 2B),
comparable to the antiviral drug remdesivir. Furthermore,
compound 1 at 20 μM showed no cytotoxicity to the host Vero
E6 cells (Figure 2C), indicating 1’s great safety profile and
potential for therapeutic development.

fragments at m/z 156.0647 ([M + H]+) (Figure 3D, fragment

acetyl extending from the lactam ring instead of the
unsaturated ethyl group of 1a. Group C displayed a conserved
lactam region as 1a, but the hydroxyl group on the terpene
moiety of 1a was substituted with either an isobutylene or an
isobutyl group (Figure 3D). Additionally, acyl chain lengths of
the analogs varied from C10 to C14 for group A, from C11 to
C13 for group B, and from C14 to C15 for group C (Figure 3C
and Figures S12−S14 and Table S13). It should be noted,
however, that further investigation is needed to confirm the
chemical structures of the predicted analogs of 1a identified
through MS/MS-based structural elucidation, as well as to
determine whether they naturally occur as cobalt complexes.

Guided by the feeding results, we performed a bioinformatic
analysis of the S. davaonensis DSM101723 genome using
antiSMASH 7.0,20 which led to identification of two BGCs
(BGC1 and BGC2, Figure S17) that encode both a NRP
synthetase (NRPS) and a SAM-dependent methyltransferase.
Double-crossover knockout of the NRPS of BGC1 in S.
davaonensis DSM101723 did not affect production of 1 (Figure
S17 and Tables S14 and S15), whereas knockout of the NRPS
gene in BGC2 completely abolished production of 1 (Figure
5A,B), suggesting that this NRPS gene (hereafter referred to as
cbtF) is essential for the biosynthesis of 1 and that BGC2
(hereafter referred to as cbt BGC) is related to the biosynthesis
of 1. Further analysis of the cbt BGC revealed the presence of
type I polyketide synthase (PKS)-encoding genes (cbtH-J)
(Figure 5A). Double-crossover deletion of each of these genes
in S. davaonensis DSM101723 resulted in the complete
abolishment of the production of 1 (Figure 5B), suggesting
that these three PKS genes were involved in the biosynthesis of
1. We also identified a highly promising terpene synthase
candidate located outside of the cbt BGC, TerF, for
biosynthesis of the terpene moiety of 1. Genome annotation
revealed six terpene synthase genes (terA−F) (Table S12), but
individual knockouts of five of them (terA−E) had no effect on
the production of 1 (Figure 5B and Figure S18). The
remaining candidate, TerF, shows the highest homology to
HpnD,19,20 a presqualene diphosphate synthase involved in
hopanoid (a triterpenoid) biosynthesis (Table S12). As HpnD
catalyzes the head-to-head condensation of two farnesyl
diphosphates, TerF is a strong candidate for assembling the
terpene moiety of 1 through the analogous head-to-head
condensation of two DMAPP units, consistent with both 1’s
chemical structure and isotope labeling results. Deletion of terF
rendered the strain nonviable; thus, further investigation is
needed to determine if the terpene moiety of 1 is derived from
terF or yet-unidentified enzymes.
Furthermore, we identified two genes in the cbt BGC
encoding putative modification enzymes including a cyto-
chrome P450 monooxygenase (cbtM) and a cyclase (cbtP)
(Table S12). We propose that the biogenesis of 1 may involve
an unusual head-to-head condensation of two isoprene units20
followed by CbtM-catalyzed epoxidation and then CbtP-
catalyzed cyclization between the terpene unit and the PK-
NRP intermediate (Figure 5C). Combining the results of our
feeding assays, bioinformatic analyses, and genetic knockout
experiments, we hypothesize that the three moieties of 1 result
from distinct biosynthetic pathways and unusual chemical
modifications, leading to the trihybridization of the unique
scaffold of 1 (Figure 5C).
While the deletion of the PKS genes abolished production of
1, it seems that the oxidation states of the PKS moiety in 1 do
not fully align with the annotated PKS domains. This
discrepancy suggests the possible involvement of additional
factors in biosynthesis of 1. Two putative PKS-associated
genes, cbtG (containing AT-ACP-E domains) and cbtK
(containing AT and ACP domains), may contribute unique
building blocks or influence the PKS chain elongation process.
Alternatively, the acyl/alkyl chain of 1 may be derived, in part,
from primary lipid metabolites, incorporating components
from cellular metabolism into the final product. More
biosynthetic studies are currently ongoing.
■CONCLUSIONS
In summary, we report the discovery and biosynthetic
interrogation of cobaltribin (1), a structurally unique natural
product that inhibits both coronavirus 3CLpro and live SARS-
CoV-2 replication. Cobaltribin (1) is a homodimeric
compound bridged through a cobalt coordination and, to
our knowledge, represents the only known cobalt-containing
natural product except vitamin B12 and its cobalamin
derivatives. Furthermore, our bioinformatic, isotope labeling,
and genetic knockout analyses suggest that cobaltribin (1)
originates from an unprecedented tribridization that integrates
nonribosomal peptide, polyketide/fatty acid, and terpene
biosynthetic pathways.
