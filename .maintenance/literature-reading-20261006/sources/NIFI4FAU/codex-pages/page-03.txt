Figure 2. Functional exploration of the D-alanyltransferases. (A) Substrate scope of AstC TE for acyl donors. Diverse acyl SNACs were employed in the
acylation of trienomycinol (1) catalyzed by AstC TE. The relative catalytic ratios of the acylated products were determined through the integration of
the LC-ESI-MS extracted ion current (EIC) peak areas, with D-alanyl loading set as 100%, respectively (Figure S4). (B) Substrate scope of AstC for
polyketide backbones. Using D-alanine as the acyl donor, two octaketide ansamycins (N-desmethyl-4,5-desepoxymaytansinol and 9-O-methyl DDM)
and two pentaketide ansamycins (microansamycin E and aminoansamycin A) were detected as the acyl acceptors, respectively, but maytansinol was
not. The hydroxyl groups used by D-alanylation are marked in pink. (C) Phylogenetic tree of homologs of AstC. The homologs in the phylogenetic tree
correspond to enzymes clustered with AstC in the SSN analysis, displaying >75% sequence similarity (Table S6). (D) X-ray crystal structure of SyAstC
A domain complexed with 5′-O-[N-(D-alanyl) sulfamoyl] adenosine (D-Ala-SA) (PDB ID: 8WWW, Table S7).
