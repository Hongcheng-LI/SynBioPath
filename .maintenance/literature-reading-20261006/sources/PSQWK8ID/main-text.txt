ABSTRACT: Rogersonins C−F (1−4), four unprecedented adenine-polyketide hybrids featuring a rare 9H-imidazo[2,1-i]purine
(1,N6-ethenoadenine) moiety, were isolated from an Ophiocordyceps-associated fungus, Clonostachys rogersoniana. Their structures
were elucidated primarily by NMR experiments. The absolute configurations of 1−4 were assigned by a combination of the modified
Mosher method, chemical degradation, electronic circular dichroism (ECD) calculations, and X-ray crystallography using Cu Kα
radiation. Compound 3 downregulated the expression of PD-L1 protein in MDA-MB-231 and A549 cells, but did not show
detectable effect on mRNA transcription of the PD-L1-encoding gene CD274.
C
ancer immunotherapy has been recognized as one of the
most promising strategies in cancer therapy.1,2 Pro-
grammed cell death protein 1 (PD-1, also known as CD279)
and its naturally occurring ligand (PD-L1; CD274) are
transmembrane receptors involved in negative regulation of
the activated T cells.3 Aggressive cancers including breast
cancers and nonsmall cell lung cancer (NSCLC), are known to
overexpress PD-L1, which allows tumor cells to escape
immune attack by suppressing the adaptive immune system.4
Therefore, disruption of the PD-1/PD-L1 complex at the
cancer cell-T cell interface has become an attractive approach
for cancer immunotherapy.4 Despite the proven efficacy of PD-
1/PD-L1 monoclonal antibodies, small molecule inhibitors
have attracted much attention due to their different
pharmacological properties from monoclonal antibodies.5
In an ongoing search for new bioactive secondary
metabolites from the rarely studied fungi inhabiting unique
environments,6 a strain of Clonostachys rogersoniana, isolated
from Ophiocordyceps sinensis, was subjected to a chemical
investigation, leading to the isolation of antibacterial p-
terphenyls, and epipolythiodioxopiperazines (ETPs) with
potent in vitro and in vivo antitumor activity.7−10 In addition,
the biosynthetic gene cluster (BGC) for the dimeric ETP,
deoxyverticillin A (ver) was also identified in Clonostachys
rogersoniana,11 and four unusual nonribosomal peptide-
polyketide hybrids including rogersonins A and B, two unique
imidazolone N-oxide-incorporating indole alkaloids, were
isolated from the gene disruption mutants.12,13 Since HPLC-
MS analysis of an EtOAc extract prepared from the solid-
substrate fermentation cultures of C. rogersoniana revealed the
presence of secondary metabolites that were significantly
different from those isolated previously, the fungus was
refermented on a larger scale using the same solid fermentation
approach. Subsequent chemical investigation of an EtOAc
extract afforded four new 9H-imidazo[2,1-i]purine (1,N6-
ethenoadenine)-incorporating adenine-polyketide hybrids,
which we named rogersonins C−F (1−4). Details of the

■RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Rogersonin C (1) was assigned a molecular formula of
C33H39N5O11 (17 degrees of unsaturation) on the basis of
HRESIMS and the NMR spectroscopic data (Table 1).
Analysis of its NMR data revealed the presence of two
exchangeable protons (δH 4.43 and 4.27, respectively), four
methyl groups, six methylene units, seven aliphatic methines
including six heteroatom-bonded, 11 aromatic or olefinic
carbons with six protonated, four carboxylic carbons (δC 169.6,
168.6, 165.0, and 164.4 respectively), and one ketone carbon
(δC 206.2). In addition, its
1H NMR spectrum displayed
resonances for two singlets at δH 8.99 (1H, s) and 8.37 (1H, s),
respectively. These resonances, together with the presence of
five nitrogen atoms in 1 as deduced by HRESIMS, implied that
the possible presence of an adenine moiety.14 This deduction
was subsequently confirmed by relevant HMBC correlations.
The 1H−1H COSY NMR data of 1 showed five isolated
spin-systems of C-12−C-17 (including C-25), C-20−C-26, C-
1′−C-14′, C-4′−C-9′ (including C-15′), and C-12′−C-16′
(Figure 1). The HMBC correlations from H-2 to C-4 and C-
24, and from H-6 to C-4 and C-23 confirmed the presence of
an adenine moiety (rings A and B), while those from H-6 to C-
8, and from H2-9 to C-8 and C-21, plus the chemical shift
considerations for C-8 (δC 113.8) and C-21 (δC 146.7),
revealed the presence of an imidazole unit (ring C). The above
results established the structure of an 9H- imidazo[2,1-i]purine
(1,N6-ethenoadenine) core. In turn, the HMBC cross-peaks
from H2-9 and H-12 to C-10, H-16 and H-20 to C-18, and
from H3-26 to C-21 established a macrolide unit (ring D),
which fused to the 1,N6-ethenoadenine moiety at C-8 and C-
21. Similarly, the structure for the second macrolide (ring E)
was also proposed, and attached to N-3 based on the HMBC
correlations from H-1′ to C-2 and C-4. Considering their
chemical values, the remaining two exchangeable protons were
assigned to the two free hydroxy groups located at C-15 (δC
69.3) and C-7′ (δC 70.3), respectively. Collectively, the gross
structure of 1 was proposed as shown.
The relative configuration of 1 was deduced by analysis of
1H−1H coupling constants (Table 1) and ROESY correlations
(Figure S6). The C-16/C-17 and C-8′/C-9′ olefins were
assigned the E-geometry based on the large coupling constants
of 15.9 and 15.7 Hz observed for H-16/H-17 and H-8′/H-9′,
respectively. A ROESY correlation between H-1′ and H-12′
revealed their spatial proximity. However, the relative
configuration for other stereogenic centers in macrolide units
could not be assigned due to their conformational flexibility.
The absolute configurations of C-12, C-15, C-4′ and C-7′ in
1 were assigned by using the modified Mosher method.15,16
Teatment of 1 with (R)- and (S)-MTPA Cl afforded the (S)-
(1a) and (R)-MTPA (1b) esters, respectively. The difference
in chemical shift values (Δδ = δS −δR) for the diastereomeric
esters 1a and 1b was calculated to assign the 15S and 7′S
absolute configuration (Figure 2). Methanolysis of 1 using
NaOMe−MeOH afforded the major products 5 and 6 in a
ratio of 1:2, indicating that diol 8 is the precursor for the
formation of both macrolide units (Figure 3), and C-4 in 8 has
the 4S absolute configuration (corresponding to C-15 and C-7′
in 1). Subsequent treatment of 6 with (R)- and (S)-MTPA Cl
afforded the (S)- (6a) and (R)-MTPA (6b) esters,
respectively. The difference in chemical shift values (Δδ = δS
−δR) for the diastereomeric esters 6a and 6b was calculated,
and the Δδ values for H3-8 (−0.163) and H-4 (+0.106) were
consistent with the 7S absolute configuration (corresponding
to C-12 and C-4′ in 1; Figure S35). However, deviations were
observed for the calculated Δδ values of H2-5 and H2-6 (Figure
S35), as that found in cordybislactone, a 14-membered
bislactone isolated Cordyceps sp. BCC 49294.17 Due to the
anisotropic effect of aryl ring in the MTPA ester, the shielding
effects for β-protons in a secondary alcohol are stronger than
those for α-protons, especially when a linear aliphatic chain
adopts zigzag conformation.17 In this case, the 7-O-MTPA
group should contribute to the positive Δδ values calculated
for H2-5 and H2-6, while the presence of 4-O-MTPA group
rendered a negative Δδ values calculated for H2-6.

deduced by comparison of the experimental and simulated
ECD spectra calculated using the time-dependent density
functional theory (TDDFT).18 The ECD spectra of four

field followed by reoptimization at the B3LYP/6-31G(d) level
to afford the lowest energy conformers (Figure S38). The
overall calculated ECD spectrum of each configuration was
then generated according to Boltzmann weighting of the
conformers. The experimental ECD curve of 1 correlated well

Compounds 1−4 were first tested for cytotoxic effects
against tumor cell lines, MDA-MB-231and MCF7 (human
breast cancer cells), and A549 (human nonsmall cell lung
cancer cells; NSCLC), but all were inactive compared to the
positive control cisplatin (Table S1).9 1−4 were evaluated for
PD-L1 protein-regulating effect by detecting the PD-L1
expression on the surface of MDA-MB-231 cells.21 MDA-
MB-231 cells were incubated with phycoerythrin (PE)
antihuman PD-L1 antibody after treatment with 1−4, and
the fluorescence intensity was measured (Figure S40).
Compound 3 (5 and 10 μM) was found to significantly
downregulate expression of the surface PD-L1 compared to the
negative control DMSO (Figure 6A). Western blotting and
real-time quantitative PCR analyses confirmed the down-
regulating effects in both MDA-MB-231 and A549 cells
(Figure 6B), but did not show detectable effect on mRNA
transcription of the PD-L1-encoding gene CD274 (Figure
S41).
Compounds 1−4 are new adenine−polyketide hybrids with
unique structural features, which could be generated from
adenine and a 14-membered macrolide via Michael addition
(Scheme S1). Nucleosides and their bases, possessing an
exocyclic amino group and a neighboring endocyclic nitrogen,
are liable to form ethenonucleosides with an additional
imidazole ring.22 Among the eight basic ribonucleosides and
2′-deoxyribonucleosides, cytidine and adenine derivatives can

regulate PD-L1 expression in tumor cells, it did not show
detectable effect on mRNA transcription of the PD-L1-
encoding gene CD274 (Figure S41). Considering complicated
mechanisms regulating PD-L1 expression at post-transcrip-
tional, translational, and post-translational levels in tumor
cells,35 the pharmacological effect of 3 on PD-L1 regulation
warrants further investigation.
■EXPERIMENTAL SECTION
General Experimental Procedures. Optical rotations were
measured on a JASCO P2000 automatic digital polarimeter, and
UV data were obtained on a Thermo Scientific Evolution Pro UV−vis
spectrophotometer. IR spectra were recorded using a PerkinElmer
Spectrum Two spectrophotometer. ECD spectra were acquired on a
JASCO J-815 spectrometer. 1H and 13C NMR data were acquired
with Varian NMR system 600 spectrometers using solvent signals
(acetone-d6: δH 2.05/δC 29.8, 206.1; DMSO-d6: δH 2.50/δC 39.52) as
references. The HSQC and HMBC experiments were optimized for
145.0 and 8.0 Hz, respectively. HRESIMS data were obtained on a
Thermo Fisher Scientific Orbitrap Explorision 120 mass spectrometer.
All MS experiments were performed in the positive ion mode. HPLC
analysis and separations were performed using an Agilent 1260
instrument equipped with a variable-wavelength UV detector.
Fungal Material. The culture of C. rogersoniana (XZC04-CC-
302) was isolated from a sample of O. sinensis collected in Linzhi,
Tibet, People’s Republic of China, on March first, 2004. The isolate
was identified based on its ITS sequence (GenBank Accession No.
KY618084).7,11 The fungal strain was cultured on slants of potato
dextrose agar at 25 °C for 7 days. Agar plugs were cut into small
pieces (about 0.5 × 0.5× 0.5 cm3) under aseptic conditions, and 25
pieces were used to inoculate in five 500 mL Erlenmeyer flasks, each
containing 200 mL of media (0.4% glucose, 1% malt extract, and 0.4%
yeast extract). The final pH of the media was adjusted to 6.5, and the
flasks were sterilized by autoclave. Five flasks of the inoculated media
were incubated at 25 °C on a rotary shaker at 220 rpm for 2 days to
prepare the seed culture. Fermentation was carried out in 60 Fernbach
flasks (500 mL) each containing 40 g of rice. Distilled H2O (60 mL)
was added to each flask, and the contents were soaked overnight
before autoclaving at 15 psi for 30 min. After cooling to room
temperature, each flask was inoculated with 5.0 mL of the spore
inoculum and incubated at 25 °C for 9 days.
Extraction and Isolation. The fermentation material was
extracted repeatedly with EtOAc (4 × 5.0 L), and the organic solvent
was evaporated to dryness under vacuum to afford 14.6 g of extract.
The extract was fractionated by silica gel vacuum liquid chromatog-
raphy (VLC) using petroleum ether−EtOAc−MeOH gradient
elution. The fraction (300 mg) eluted with 9:1 EtOAc−MeOH was
further separated by Sephadex LH-20 column chromatography (CC)
eluting with MeOH, and the resulting subfractions were combined
and purified by semipreparative RP HPLC (Agilent Zorbax SB-C18
column; 5 μm; 9.4 × 250 mm; 25% CH3CN in H2O for 80 min; 3
mL/min) to afford 1 (13 mg, tR 72.0 min). The fraction (210 mg)

Computational Details.18 Conformational analyses for 1 and 2
were performed via the MOE version 2009.10 (Chemical Computing
Group, Canada) software package with LowModeMD at the OPLS3
force field. The OPLS3 conformational analyses were further
optimized using TDDFT at the B3LYP/6-31G(d) basis set level in
MeOH with the SMD model. The stationary points have been
checked as the true minima of the potential energy surface by
verifying they do not exhibit vibrational imaginary frequencies. The
100 lowest electronic transitions were calculated for compounds 1
and 2. The rotational strengths of each electronic excitation were
given using both dipole length and dipole velocity representations.
The ECD spectrum was simulated in SpecDis1.70 using a Gaussian
function. The overall ECD spectra were then generated according to
Boltzmann weighting of each conformer. The systematic errors in the
prediction of the wavelength and excited-state energies are
compensated for by employing UV correlations. All quantum
computations were performed using the Gaussian 09 package.40
Cell Culture and Reagents. The human breast cancer cell lines
MDA-MB-321 and MCF-7 were grown in Dulbecco’s modified
Eagle’s medium (DMEM) and human NSCLC cell line A549 cells
were cultured in DMEM/F-12 medium. All the culture media were
supplemented with 10% (v/v) fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% (v/
v) penicillin/streptomycin. Cells were cultured at 37 °C in an
incubator supplied with 5% CO2.
MTT Assay. MTT assays were performed as previously described.9
Briefly, cells were seeded into 96-well plates at a density of 5 × 103
cells/well for 24 h, and were the exposed to different concentrations
of test compounds. After incubation for 72 h, cells were stained with
25 μL of MTT solution (5 mg/mL) for 25 min. Finally, the mixture of
medium and MTT solution was removed and 75 μL DMSO was
added to dissolve formazan crystals. Absorbance of each well was
measured at 544 nm (test wavelength) and 690 nm (background)
using the Multi-Mode Microplate Reader. Background was subtracted
from the absorbance of each well. Three duplicate wells were used for
each concentration, and all the tests were repeated three times.
Detection of PD-L1 Expression by Flow Cytometry and
Immunoblotting.21 Cells were treated with DMSO or 3 for the
indicated time. For the detection of cell-surface PD-L1, cells were
digested with Accutase (#40506ES60, Yeasen) and stained with PE
antihuman PD-L1 antibody (#329705, BioLegend) in PBS
supplemented with 2% (v/v) FBS. After being incubated in the
darkness for 40 min at 4 °C, cells were then subjected to the
NovoCyte flow cytometer (Agilent). Data were calculated by FlowJo
X software. For the Western blotting analysis, cells were collected and
lysed with RIPA lysis buffer (#CW2333, Cowin Biotech)
supplemented with protease inhibitor cocktail (#87785, Thermo
Fisher Scientific) on ice. Equal loading was subjected to 10% SDS-
PAGE and Western blotting analysis. Antibodies were as follows: PD-
L1 monoclonal antibody (#66248-1-Ig, Proteintech), GAPDH
polyclonal antibody (#AP0063, Bioworld), goat antirabbit IgG (H +
L)-HRP conjugate (#1706515, Biorad) and goat antimouse IgG (H +
L)-HRP conjugate (#1706516, Biorad). Quantitation of the individual
bands was carried out using the ImageJ software.
RNA Extraction and Real-Time PCR Analysis.21 QIAzol lysis
reagent (#79306, QIAGEN) was used to extract RNA, and cDNA was
