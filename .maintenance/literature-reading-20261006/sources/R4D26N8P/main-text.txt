ABSTRACT: Fungal-derived natural products continue to play a
pivotal role in the discovery of drug agents for human, veterinary, and
general agricultural use. The fungus Neodidymelliopsis negundinis
presents a significant saprobic ascomycete whose metabolites
remained hitherto unstudied. Herein we report the isolation of
eight unprecedented secondary metabolites named neodidymellio-
sides A and B (1 and 2), neodidymelliol A (3), and neodidymellioic
acids A−E (4−8) produced by the submerged cultures of the fungus.
Compound 1 proved to be the most active compound, with IC50
values ranging between 4.8 and 8.8 μM against KB3.1 (cervix), PC-3
(prostate), MCF-7 (breast), SKOV-3 (ovary), A431 (skin), and A549
(lung) cell lines. Compound 1 revealed significant inhibition of
Staphylococcus aureus and Candida albicans biofilms.
F
ungi continue to be valuable producers of pharmacolog-
ically significant molecules including antibiotics, immuno-
suppressants, and antihypercholestrolemics. The fungi account
for about 148,000 described species spanning the globe
(Species Fungorum, 2021; http://www.speciesfungorum.org/
), with Ascomycota cosmopolitan in terrestrial and aquatic
habitats, and playing a pivotal role in various ecosystems, being
the most abundant.1
The genus Neodidymelliopsis (Didymellaceae) accounts for
die-back (phytopthera) in plant hosts.2 Despite the losses
incurred due to the invasion of crops by plant pathogenic
fungi,3 recent efforts to harness these fungi as starting points
for biological control agents discovery have been advanced.4,5
Hyde et al. originally reported our current study species N.
negundinis from Russia in 2018,6 followed by a recent isolation
in Italy.7 To the best of our knowledge, one novel polyketide,
(2Z)-cillifuranone, rapidly isomerized to its 2E counterpart,
and four known fungal metabolites, taiwapyrone, xylariolide D,
pachybasin, and N-(5-hydroxypentyl) acetamide, have been
reported from the genus Neodidymelliopsis.8 In this study, we
report eight previously undescribed polyhydroxy-isoprenoid
metabolites (1−8) isolated from the submerged cultures of N.
negundinis. Herein, we describe the chemical and biological
characterization of the isolated metabolites.

Compound 2 was purified as a white amorphous solid. The
molecular formula of 2 was established as C51H94O12 indicating
five degrees of unsaturation based on its HRESIMS data.
Compound 2 revealed one additional degree of unsaturation
compared to 1. The 1H and 13C NMR spectral data of 2
(Table 1) exhibited a close resemblance to 1 apart from the
presence of a methine sp3 carbon at δC 86.0 (C-34) directly
correlated to a proton at δH 3.80 (t, J = 7.3 Hz, H-34) and an
unprotonated sp3 carbon at δC 84.5 (C-35) that revealed key
HMBC correlations (Figure 1) to two singlet methyl groups at
δH 1.15 (H3-37) and δH 1.19 (H3-36), respectively. A literature
search of 2 revealed a close similarity to SCH 60065, a
polyhydroxy-isoprenoid glucopyranoside comprising a terminal
epoxide at C-34 and C-35 accounting for the additional degree
of unsaturation in 2 compared to 1, which was reported as a
neurokinin inhibitor isolated from Acremonium sp.9 The
1H−1H COSY spectrum of 2 (Figure 1) revealed a spin
system from H-34 to two diastereotopic methylene groups at
δH 1.76/δH 1.68 (H2-32) and δH 1.92/δH 1.81 (H2-33),
indicating that the epoxide is present at C-34/C-35 of the
aglycone part. Unlike the known metabolite SCH 60065,9 the
sugar moiety in 2 was determined to be a β-D-mannopyranosyl
residue, as in compound 1, based on comparing chemical shifts
and coupling constants (J values) of 1H and 13C NMR spectral
data of the sugar moiety in 2 (Table 1) to those reported in the
literature.11−14 The HMBC spectrum of 2 (Figure 1) revealed
key correlations from the anomeric proton at δH 4.51 (d, J =
1.0 Hz) to a hydroxylmethylene carbon at δC 65.7 assigned as
C-1, indicating that the β-D-mannopyranosyl moiety is
attached via an O-glycosidic linkage to C-1 of the aglycone.

hence it was identified as a previously undescribed
polyhydroxy-isoprenoid metabolite that was named neo-

Compound 4 was isolated as a white amorphous solid, and
its molecular formula was determined as C45H84O8, indicating
four degrees of unsaturation based on HRESIMS data. The 1H
and
13C NMR spectral data of 4 (Table 2) revealed the
presence of three olefinic protons at δH 5.65 (d, J = 1.3 Hz; δC
118.9) and at δH 5.13 (m, overlapped; δC 124.7) assigned to
H-2 and H-6 that revealed in the 1H−1H COSY spectrum
(Figure 2) key correlations to two allylic methyl groups at δH
2.13 (d, J = 1.3 Hz, H3-38; δC 19.6) and δH 1.62 (br s, H3-39;
δC 16.1). In addition, a third olefinic proton appeared at δH
5.12 (m, overlapped; δC 126.0) revealing key COSY
correlations to two allylic methyl groups at δH 1.68 (d, J =
1.3 Hz, H3-36; δC 26.1) and δH 1.62 (br s, H3-37; δC 17.9),
suggesting their presence as a terminal dimethyl allyl moiety.
The HMBC spectrum of 4 (Figures 2, S25) from the
deshielded olefinic methyl group (H3-38) to two unprotonated
sp2 carbons indicated one carboxylic acid group at δC 170.3
and one olefinic carbon at δC 160.0 together with a methine
sp2 carbon at δC 118.9 (C-2), indicating their existence as an
α,β-unsaturated carboxylic acid functionality that accounts for
the two additional degrees of unsaturation compared to 3.
Aside from these characteristic spectral features, both 1H and
13C NMR spectral data of 4 were in a close resemblance to
those obtained for compound 3 and those reported in the
literature.9 Hence, compound 4 was confirmed to be a
previously undescribed polyhydroxy-isoprenoid carboxylic
acid, which was trivially named as neodidymellioic acid A.
Compound 5 was obtained as a white amorphous solid, and
its HRESIMS data revealed a protonated molecule and a
sodium adduct at m/z 787.6277 [M + H]+ and m/z 809.6095
[M + Na]+, respectively. Thus, the molecular formula of 5 was
determined to be C45H86O10, indicating three degrees of
unsaturation. The 1H and 13C NMR spectral data of 5 (Table
3) revealed a close coherence to those of 3 (Table 2) except in
the disappearance of a hydroxymethylene group ascribed to
H2-1 in 3 that was replaced by a terminal carboxylic acid group
at δC 171.3 (C-1) forming an α,β-unsaturated carboxylic acid
functionality with C-2 (δC 118.0) and C-3 (δC 158.4) that was
also elucidated in 4. Based on these results, compound 5 was
confirmed to be a previously undescribed polyhydroxy-
isoprenoid carboxylic acid that was named as neodidymellioic
acid B.

Compound 7 was isolated as a white amorphous solid.
HRESIMS of 7 revealed a protonated molecule and a sodium
adduct at m/z 769.6194 [M + H]+ and m/z 791.6004 [M +
Na]+ that established its molecular formula to be C45H84O9, as
for compound 6, indicating four degrees of unsaturation. The
1H and 13C NMR spectral data of 7 (Table 4) revealed a close
similarity to those of the aglycone moiety in 1. A main
difference in 7 is the presence of an olefinic methine proton at
δH 5.67 (br s) directly correlated via the HSQC spectrum to an
olefinic sp2 carbon at δC 119.1 (C-2) instead of an olefinic
proton in 1 at δH 5.35 (tdd, J = 6.5, 2.6, 1.3 Hz; δC 121.5)
assigned for H-2. The same proton (H-2) in 7 revealed key
HMBC correlations (Figure 2) to a carboxylic acid carbon at
δC 172.2 (C-1), a deshielded unprotonated sp2 carbon at δC
156.8 (C-3), and an olefinic methyl carbon at δC 18.7 (C-38).
These results confirmed that compound 7 is a previously
undescribed polyhydroxy-isoprenoid carboxylic acid derivative,
and it was named as neodidmeyllioic acid D.
Compound 8 was obtained as a white amorphous solid, and
its HRESIMS revealed a protonated molecule and a sodium
adduct at m/z 785.6140 [M + H]+ and m/z 807.5949 [M +
Na]+, respectively. Thus, the molecular formula of 8 was
determined to be C45H84O10, indicating four degrees of
unsaturation similar to compounds 6 and 7 despite having
an additional oxygen atom in its molecular formula. This close
similarity in the molecular formulas of compounds 6−8 was
also obvious in their 1H and 13C NMR spectral data (Tables 3,
4), which revealed the presence of an epoxide and an α,β-
unsaturated carboxylic acid moiety in 8 similar to compounds
6 and 7, respectively. A main difference in 8 was found through
the presence of an additional oxygenated aliphatic methine at
δH 3.27 (dd, J = 10.5, 1.7 Hz, H-10) that was directly
correlated via the HSQC spectrum to an sp3 carbon (δC 77.8).
The same proton also unveiled in the 1H−1H COSY (Figure
2) clear spin system correlations to a diastereotopic methylene
group at δH 1.33/δH 1.41 (H2-9) extending to a second
diastereotopic methylene group (δH 2.02/δH 2.26, H2-8). To
further determine the position of this oxygenated aliphatic
methine in 8, its HMBC spectrum (Figure 2) was recorded,
which displayed key correlations from the olefinic proton at δH
5.18 (m, H-6) and the oxygenated aliphatic methine proton at
δH 3.27 to a methylene sp3 carbon at δC 37.5 assigned to C-8,
and hence the additional methine proton was confirmed to be
at C-10. Based on these findings, compound 8 was elucidated
as a previously undescribed polyhydroxy-isoprenoid carboxylic
acid derivative and named neodidmeyllioic acid E.

■EXPERIMENTAL SECTION
General Experimental Procedures. Ultraviolet−visible (UV/
vis) spectra were obtained using a UV−vis spectrophotometer (UV-
2450, Shimadzu, Kyoto, Japan). Optical rotations were determined
using a MCP 150 polarimeter at 20 °C (Anton-Paar Opto Tec
GmbH, Seelze, Germany). High-resolution electrospray ionization
mass spectra (HRESIMS) were acquired with an Agilent 1200 Infinity
Series HPLC-UV system (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA,
USA) utilizing a C18 Acquity UPLC BEH column (2.1 × 50 mm, 1.7
μm: Waters, Milford, MA, USA, solvent A: H2O + 0.1% formic acid;
solvent B: acetonitrile (ACN) + 0.1% formic acid, gradient: 5% B for
0.5 min increasing to 100% B in 19.5 min, maintaining 100% B for 5
min, flow rate 0.6 mL min−1, UV/vis detection 190−600 nm)
connected to a time-of-flight mass spectrometer (ESI-TOF-MS,
Maxis, Bruker, Billerica, MA, USA) (scan range 100−2500 m/z, rate 2
Hz, capillary voltage 4500 V, dry temperature 200 °C). The Smart
Formula algorithm of the Compass Data Analysis software (Bruker,
version 4.4) was used to determine the molecular formulas of the
detected compounds. NMR spectra were recorded with an Avance III
500 spectrometer (Bruker, Billerica, MA, USA, 1H NMR: 500 MHz,
and 13C NMR: 125 MHz) or an Avance III 700 spectrometer (Bruker,
Billerica, MA, USA, 1H NMR: 700 MHz, and 13C NMR: 175 MHz)
dissolving compounds in deuterated methanol-d4.
Fungal Specimen. Neodidymelliopsis negundinis (MFLUCC18-
0083) was isolated from dead twigs and branches of Acer negundo and
deposited at the Institute of Excellence in Fungal Research, Mae Fah
Luang University, Thailand. The fungus was characterized morpho-
logically and genetically in a separate study.6 The GenBank accession
numbers from β-tubulin, ITS, LSU, and RPB2 molecular loci can be
retrieved as MG564164, MG564165, MG564163, and MG564166.2
Fungal Culture Preparation, Fermentation, and Extraction
of Metabolites. N. negundinis (MFLUCC18-0083) was cultured in
yeast malt medium (YMG) (10 g/L malt extract, 4 g/L yeast extract,
4 g/L D-glucose, and 20 g/L agar in deionized water) plates with
adjusted pH to 6.3 and incubated at 24 °C. After 7 days, five plugs of
fungal colonies were obtained using a cork borer and further placed
into 20 × 500 mL Erlenmeyer flasks, each containing 250 mL of
Zucker-Malz (ZM) medium broth (5 g/L molasses, 5 g/L oatmeal, 4
g/L saccharose, 4 g/L mannitol, 1.5 g/L D-glucose, 1.5 g/L CaCO3,
0.5 g/L edamin, 0.5 g/L (NH4)2SO4, and 20 g/L agar in deionized
water and pH adjusted at 7.2). The cultures were incubated at 23 °C
on a rotary shaker at 140 rpm for 14 days. Upon harvesting, mycelial
and supernatant parts were separated, and the supernatant part was
extracted in triplicate by ethyl acetate in a ratio of 1:2. The ethyl
acetate phase was separated and filtered through anhydrous Na2SO4
to remove remaining water. The mycelial part was soaked in 500 mL
of acetone and placed in an ultrasonic bath at 40 °C for 30 min. The
solutions obtained from ethyl acetate and acetone parts were
evaporated under vacuum, yielding total supernatant and mycelial
extracts of 1.3 g and 480.2 mg, respectively.
Isolation and Physicochemical Properties of 1−8. The
mycelial total extract was divided into two halves. One half (240
mg) was dissolved in MeOH and subjected to preparative reverse
phase HPLC (PLC 2020, Gilson, Middleton, WI, USA). As stationary
phase, Nucleodur C18 column (250 × 21.2 mm, 5 μm, Macherey-
Nagel, Düren, Germany) was used, while the mobile phase consisted
of solvent A, deionized water with 0.1% formic acid, and solvent B,
MeCN with 0.1% formic acid (v/v). Purification of the crude extracts
was performed by using a linear gradient elution of 50−100% solvent
B at a flow rate of 30 mL/min over 90 min followed by holding an
isocratic elution at 100% solvent B for 10 min to afford two fractions,
P1 (20.4 mg, tR = 51.2−59.6 min) and P2 (23.7 mg, tR = 67.2−71.4
min).
Fraction P2 (23.7 mg) was further subjected to preparative HPLC
equipped with a Nucleodur C18 column (250 × 21.2 mm, 10 μm,
Macherey-Nagel, Düren, Germany), while the mobile phase consisted
of solvent A, deionized water with 0.1% formic acid (v/v), and solvent
B, MeCN with 0.1% formic acid (v/v). Purification of the crude
extracts was performed by using a linear gradient elution of 40−100%
solvent B at a flow rate of 20 mL/min over 45 min and then holding
the gradient at 100% solvent B for 10 min to afford 1 (1.9 mg, tR =
28.0 min).
Fraction P1 (20.4 mg) was further subjected to preparative
reversed phase HPLC (PLC 2020, Gilson, Middleton, WI, USA)
implementing a Luna C18 column (250 × 21.2 mm, 10 μm,
Phenomenex, Aschaffenburg, Germany) and a gradient elution
consisting of solvent A, deionized water with 0.1% formic acid (v/
v), and solvent B, MeCN with 0.1% formic acid (v/v). Purification of
the crude extracts was performed by using a linear gradient elution of
30−100% solvent B at a flow rate of 20 mL/min over 60 min followed
by holding the gradient at 100% solvent B for 10 min to afford 2 (1.4
mg, tR = 52.0 min).
Due to the complexity of compounds, the rest of the mycelial total
extract (200 mg) was subjected to column chromatography using a
Diaion HP-20 stationary phase. The mobile phase consisted of solvent
A, deionized water, and solvent B, MeOH. Purification of the crude
extracts was performed by using a gradient elution of 150 mL of
mobile phase ratio consisting of 100:0, 70:30, 50:50, 30:70, and
0:100, respectively, to afford five fractions (S1−S5). Fraction S5 was
obtained by eluting with 100% methanol (31.3 mg). Fraction S5 was
further subjected to preparative HPLC (PLC 2020, Gilson,
Middleton, WI, USA) using a Luna C18 column (250 × 21.2 mm, 5
μm, Phenomenex, Aschaffenburg, Germany), and the gradient elution
was conducted using a mobile phase of solvent A, deionized water
with 0.1% formic acid (v/v), and solvent B, MeCN with 0.1% formic
acid (v/v), in a linear gradient of 10−100% solvent B at a flow rate of
20 mL/min over 70 min and continued as an isocratic elution at 100%
solvent B for 10 min to afford subfractions S5-F3 (8.7 mg, tR = 52.3−
56.2 min) and S5-F13 (9.9 mg, tR = 68.4−74.6 min).
Both subfractions S5-F3 and S5-F13 were purified with a
semipreparative HPLC (Agilent Technologies 1200 Infinity Series)
equipped with an XBridge C18 column (250 × 10 mm, 5 μm, Waters,
Eschborn, Germany) and applying a gradient elution using solvent A,
deionized water with 0.1% formic acid (v/v), and solvent B, MeCN
with 0.1% formic acid (v/v). Purification was performed by a linear
gradient elution of 30−100% solvent B at a flow rate of 3 mL/min for
60 min and then eluting in an isocratic mode at 100% solvent B for 10
min. Three compounds were obtained as follows: 5 (0.9 mg, tR = 29.4
min), 3 (0.6 mg, tR = 30.7 min), and 8 (0.5 mg, tR = 33.0 min) were
purified from the subfraction S5-F3. Using the same separation
procedure for subfraction S5-F13 afforded three other compounds: 7
(0.5 mg, tR = 45.4 min), 6 (0.6 mg, tR = 50.0 min), and 4 (0.7 mg, tR =
53.3 min).

Cytotoxicity Assay. In vitro cytotoxicity (IC50) assessments were
carried out on the isolated compounds based on the MTT (3-(4,5-
dimethylthiayol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyltetrazolium bromide) test in 96-
well plates, against the two cell lines L929 (mouse fibroblasts) and
KB3.1 (human endocervical adenocarcinoma). For those featuring
significant antiproliferative properties, further investigation was
conducted against prostate cancer (PC3), breast cancer (MCF-7),
ovarian cancer (SKOV-3), epidermoid carcinoma (A431), and human
lung adenocarcinoma (A549) cells. Epothilone B served as the
positive control. Following 5 days of incubation, we determined the
minimum concentration of the tested compounds required to achieve
50% growth inhibition, expressed as IC50 values in accordance with
our previously established methods.18,19
Antimicrobial Assay. The antifungal and antibacterial activities
(minimum inhibition concentration (MIC) in μg/mL) of the isolated
compounds were determined in serial dilution assays against a panel
of bacteria including Bacillus subtilis (DSM 10), Mycobacterium
smegmatis (ATCC 700084), and Staphylococcus aureus (DSM 346) as
Gram-positive in addition to Acinetobacter baumannii (DSM 30008),
Chromobacterium violaceum (DSM 30191), Escherichia coli (DSM
1116), and Pseudomonas aeruginosa (PA14) as Gram-negative. In
addition, the isolated compounds were also assessed against Candida
albicans (DSM 1665), Mucor hiemalis (DSM 2656), Rhodotorula
glutinis (DSM 10134), Schizosaccharomyces pombe (DSM 70572), and
Wickerhamomyces anomalus (DSM 6766) as described previously.19,20
Biofilm Inhibition Assay. Biofilm inhibition assays were
conducted according to the methodology previously described,18,21
targeting the inhibition of biofilm formation in Pseudomonas
aeruginosa (PA 14), Staphylococcus aureus (DSM 1104), and Candida
albicans (DSM 11225). In brief, P. aeruginosa and S. aureus were
cultured in LB (Luria−Bertani broth) and CASO medium at 37 °C
for 18 h, respectively, in a shaker at 120 rpm after being thawed from
a −20 °C stock solution. The culture’s optical density at 600 nm
(OD600) was measured and adjusted to a 0.1 McFarland standard in
M63 minimal medium supplemented with necessary nutrients and
CASO medium with 4% glucose for P. aeruginosa and S. aureus,
respectively. Microtiter plates were loaded with 150 μL of the
bacterial suspension containing serially diluted test compounds (250−
2.0 μg/mL) and incubated for 24 h at 37 °C. The evaluation of
biofilm inhibition was performed by staining the biofilm with 0.1%
crystal violet (CV). After staining, the plates were washed, and the
biofilm was dissolved in 95% ethanol. The absorbance of the dissolved
biofilm was measured at 530 nm using a plate reader. The negative
control involved methanol (2.5%) while myxovalargin A and
microporenic acid A concentrations ranging from 2.0 to 250 μg/mL
were used as the positive control for P. aeruginosa and S. aureus,
respectively.
C. albicans was cultured in YPD (yeast extract peptone dextrose)
medium for 18 h at 30 °C and 120 rpm after thawing from a stock
solution stored at −20 °C. The culture was adjusted to 0.05
McFarland standard in RPMI 1640 medium. Microtiter plates were
loaded with 150 μL of the yeast suspension and incubated for 90 min
at 37 °C. Following the initial incubation, the medium was replaced
with fresh RPMI medium containing serially diluted test compounds
(250−2.0 μg/mL) for a 24 h incubation at 37 °C. Biofilm inhibition
assessment involved CV staining, washing, and measuring the
absorbance at 610 nm. Farnesol was the positive control, and
methanol (2.5%) was the negative control. All experiments were
performed three times, and the standard deviation (SD) was
maintained at or below 20% for S. aureus and 10% for P. aeruginosa
and C. albicans.
Statistical Analysis. The data obtained from the biofilm
inhibitory assay were analyzed on GraphPad Prism 9 (GraphPad
Software, San Diego, CA, USA). Differences between samples and the
control group were determined by a two-tailed Student’s t-test at the p
< 0.01 level of statistical significance. Data are displayed as the mean
± SEM.
