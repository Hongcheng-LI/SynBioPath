Supporting Information
ABSTRACT: A new cytochalasin dimer, verruculoid A (1), three
new cytochalasin derivatives, including 12-nor-cytochalasin F (2),
22-methoxycytochalasin B6 (3), and 19-hydroxycytochalasin B (4),
and 20-deoxycytochalasin B (5), a synthetic product obtained as a
natural product for the ﬁrst time, together with four known
analogues (6−9), were isolated and identiﬁed from the culture
extract of Curvularia verruculosa CS-129, an endozoic fungus
obtained from the inner fresh tissue of the deep-sea squat lobster
Shinkaia crosnieri, which was collected from the cold seep area of
the South China Sea. Structurally, verruculoid A (1) represents the
ﬁrst cytochalasin homodimer containing a thioether bridge, while
12-nor-cytochalasin F (2) is the ﬁrst 12-nor-cytochalasin
derivative. Their structures were elucidated by detailed inter-
pretation of the NMR spectroscopic and mass spectrometric data. X-ray crystallographic analysis and ECD calculations conﬁrmed
their structures and absolute conﬁgurations. Compound 1 displayed activity against the human pathogenic bacterium Escherichia coli
(MIC = 2 μg/mL), while compounds 4, 8, and 9 showed cytotoxicity against three tumor cell lines (HCT-116, HepG-2, and MCF-
7) with IC50 values from 5.2 to 12 μM. The structure−activity relationship was brieﬂy discussed.
C
old seeps are speciﬁc environments with a high
concentration of methane and low temperatures caused
by seaﬂoor geological activities.1,2 Microbes in cold seeps adapt
to the unique habitat by gene regulation and metabolic
pathways.3,4 However, research about secondary metabolites
from cold-seep-derived microbes has rarely been described. So
far, only two papers reporting secondary metabolites of cold-
seep-derived fungi have been published.5,6 Endozoic fungi can
interact with their host to adapt to special circumstance in the
cold seep and thus are considered to be special and signiﬁcant
resources of unique secondary metabolites.7 Therefore,
investigation of secondary metabolites from cold seep endozoic
fungi is warranted.
The deep-sea squat lobster Shinkaia crosnieri is the dominant
species with a large biomass in the cold seep environment of
the South China Sea. In continuing research on the discovery
of novel secondary metabolites from marine-sourced fungi with
a wide variety of habitats,8,9 the endozoic fungus Curvularia
verruculosa CS-129, obtained from the deep-sea cold seep squat
lobster S. crosnieri, was cultured and chemically investigated.
Natural products isolated from the species of the fungal genus
Curvularia mainly include alkaloids, polyketides, quinones, and
terpenes, and these compounds exhibited anti-inﬂammatory,
antilarval, antimicrobial, antioxidative, and cytotoxic activ-
ities.10 However, only two papers describing the isolation of
vinblastine11 and a 14-membered resorcylic acid lactone
(f152A1)12 were reported from the species C. verruculosa. In
the present work, four new cytochalasin derivatives (1−4) and
a new natural product (20-deoxycytochalasin B, 5), together
with four known analogues, namely, cytochalasin F (6),13
cytochalasin B6 (7),14 cytochalasin B (8),15 and deoxaphomin
(9),16 were isolated and identiﬁed from C. verruculosa CS-129.
Cytochalasins, biosynthesized by a polyketide synthase and
nonribosomal peptide synthetase pathway, showed complex
structural features and potent biological activities.17 Details of
the isolation, structure elucidation, antibacterial activities, and
cytotoxicities of compounds 1−9 are described herein.

■RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
The EtOAc extract of C. verruculosa CS-129 cultured on solid
rice medium was fractionated and puriﬁed by a combination of
column chromatography including Lobar LiChroprep RP-18,
silica gel, Sephadex LH-20, and semipreparative HPLC, to
yield compounds 1−9.
Verruculoid A (1) was initially isolated as a white
amorphous solid, and its molecular formula was assigned as
C58H72N2O10S by HRESIMS. Analysis of its 1H and 13C NMR
data (Table 1) revealed the presence of two methyls, one
oleﬁnic and six aliphatic methylenes, 14 methines (seven
oleﬁnic), and six nonprotonated carbons (one oxygenated sp3-
hybridized, one keto, and two ester or amide carbonyls).
Detailed analysis of the NMR data suggested that compound 1
is a symmetrical cytochalasin dimer linked by a central bridging
sulfur atom. Subsequent interpretation of COSY and HMBC
data (Figure 1) revealed that the cytochalasin monomer in 1
closely resembled cytochalacin B6 (7), a cytochalasin derivative
identiﬁed from a fungal species of the genus Phoma.14
However, signals for a methylene group at δH 2.52/2.45 and
δC 29.0 (CH2-22) in cytochalasin B6 (7)14 were replaced by a
methine (δH/δC 3.97/43.8, CH-22) in 1 (Table 1), indicating
that the two monomers were coupled at C-22 and C-22″
through a sulfur atom.
NOE correlations from H-8 to H-4, H-5, and H-14 and from
H-14 to H-16 indicated the cofacial orientation of these
protons, while correlations from H-3 to H-7 and H3-11 placed
these groups on the opposite face (Figure 2). However, no
obvious NOE correlations could be observed around C-9 and
C-22, so that the relative conﬁgurations at these two
stereogenic centers could not be determined. After many
attempts, suitable crystals were obtained by dissolving the
sample in MeOH−H2O (95:5) and slowly evaporating the
solvents by keeping the sample in a refrigerator. The structure
and absolute conﬁguration (AC) of compound 1 was therefore
conﬁrmed by X-ray crystallographic analysis using Cu Kα
radiation, and its AC was assigned as 3S, 4S, 5S,7S, 8S, 9S, 16R,
22S, 3″S, 4″S, 5″S, 7″S, 8″S, 9″S, 16″R, and 22″S with a Flack
parameter of 0.040(9) (Figure 3). Compound 1 represents the
ﬁrst example of a cytochalasin-type homodimer containing a
thioether bridge.
12-nor-Cytochalasin F (2) was also obtained as a white
amorphous powder, and the molecular formula was assigned as
C28H35NO5 by HRESIMS, requiring 12 degrees of unsatura-
tion. Detailed analysis of the NMR data of compound 2
indicated that its structure is similar to cytochalasin F (6),13
which was isolated from a fungal species of the genus
Geniculosporium. However, the methyl signals resonating at
δH/δC 1.21/19.6 (CH3-12) in 6 were missing in that of 2, while
the nonprotonated carbon signal at δC 57.4 (C-6) in 6 was
replaced by a methine group resonating at δH/δC 2.91/54.1 in 2
(Tables 2 and 3), indicating that compound 2 is 6-
desmethylcytochalasin F. The planar structure of compound
2 was further supported by the COSY and HMBC correlations
(Figure 1).
The relative conﬁguration of compound 2 was determined
by a NOESY experiment (Figure 2). NOE correlations from
H-3 to H-7 and H3-11 and from H-6 to H3-11 indicated the
cofacial orientation of these protons, while correlations from
H-14 to H-8 and H-16, from H-8 to H-5, from H-4 to H-10b,
and from H-22 to H-16 and H-20 showed these groups were
on the opposite face. Based on the previous reports, most
cytochalasins share the same steric conﬁgurations due to their

Some cytochalasins exhibited antibacterial activities against
human pathogens such as Staphylococcus aureus and Klebsiella
pneumoniae as reported.22,23 Compounds 1−9 were assayed for
their antibacterial activities mainly against aquatic pathogens,
and compound 1 showed potent activity against the human
pathogenic bacterium Escherichia coli with an MIC value 2 μg/
mL, equal to the positive control chloromycetin. Compounds 4
and 6 exhibited inhibitory activity against the aquatic
pathogenic bacterium Edwardisella ictarda with the same
MIC value of 8 μg/mL (the MIC value of the positive control
chloromycetin was 0.5 μg/mL). Compound 8 showed
inhibitory activity against the aquatic pathogenic bacterium
Vibro parahemolyticus with an MIC value of 4 μg/mL
compared to the positive control chloromycetin (MIC 2 μg/
mL). The MIC values of other compounds were larger than 32
μg/mL. These results indicated that the methyl group at C-6
(2 vs 6) and the hydroxy groups at C-19 and C-20 (5 vs 4 and
8) could increase the antibacterial activity.
Compound 8 could reduce the clonogenicity on U937
human monocytic leukemia cells in the literature report.24

(Table 4). Compounds 4, 8, and 9 exhibited cytotoxicity

■EXPERIMENTAL SECTION
General Experimental Procedures. General experimental
procedures were the same as previously reported8,9 except that the
ECD spectra were measured on a Chirascan CD spectrometer
(Applied Photophysics Ltd.).
Fungal Material. The fungal strain Curvularia verruculosa CS-129
was isolated from the fresh tissues of Shinkaia crosnieri, which was
collected from the cold seep environment of the South China Sea, in
July 2018. The fungal strain was identiﬁed as Curvularia verruculosa
according to its ITS region sequence, which is the same (100%) as
that of Curvularia verruculosa (KJ748697.1). The sequence
information on the fungus was deposited in GenBank with the
accession no. MW784843. The strain is preserved at the Key
Laboratory of Experimental Marine Biology, Institute of Oceanology,
Chinese Academy of Sciences (IOCAS).
Fermentation, Extraction, and Isolation. For chemical
investigations, fresh mycelia of C. verruculosa CS-129 were grown
on PDA medium with seawater at 28 °C for 5 days and were then
inoculated into 125 × 1 L Erlenmeyer ﬂasks with solid rice medium
(70 g rice, 0.1 g corn syrup, 0.3 g peptone, 0.1 g methionine, and 100
mL naturally sourced and ﬁltered seawater that was obtained from the
Huiquan Gulf of the Yellow Sea near the campus of IOCAS) and
statically cultured for 30 days at room temperature. After incubation,
the fermented rice substrate was extracted three times with EtOAc.
The combined EtOAc extracts were ﬁltered and evaporated under
reduced pressure to yield an organic extract (80 g), which was
subjected to vacuum liquid chromatography (VLC) eluting with
diﬀerent solvents of increasing polarity from petroleum ether (PE) to
MeOH to yield nine fractions (Frs. 1−9). Fr. 5 (15 g), eluted with
PE−EtOAc (2:1), was puriﬁed by column chromatography (CC)
over Lobar LiChroprep RP-18 with a MeOH−H2O gradient (from
10:90 to 100:0) to aﬀord seven subfractions (Fr.5.1−5.7). Fr. 5.6 was
further puriﬁed by preparative TLC (plate: 20 × 20 cm, developing
solvents: CH2Cl2−MeOH, 40:1) and by Sephadex LH-20 (MeOH)
to obtain compound 5 (11.0 mg). Fr. 5.7 was further puriﬁed by CC
on Si gel eluting with a CH2Cl2−MeOH gradient (from 150:1 to
80:1) and Sephadex LH-20 (MeOH) to obtain compound 7 (21.1
mg). Fr. 6 (18.6 g), eluted with PE−EtOAc (1:1), was puriﬁed by
reversed-phase CC over Lobar LiChroprep RP-18 with a MeOH−
H2O gradient (from 10:90 to 100:0) to yield three subfractions (Fr.
6.1−Fr. 6.3). Fr. 6.2 was further puriﬁed by CC on Si gel eluting with
a CH2Cl2−MeOH gradient (from 100:1 to 20:1) and Sephadex LH-
20 (MeOH) to obtain compound 6 (6.1 mg). Fr. 6.3 was fractionated
by CC on Sephadex LH-20 (MeOH) and then by semipreparative
HPLC (Elite ODS-BP column, 10 μm; 20 × 250 mm; 80% MeOH−
H2O, 10 mL/min) to aﬀord compound 2 (2.0 mg, tR 18.0 min). Fr. 7
(16.5 g), eluted with CH2Cl2−MeOH (20:1), was puriﬁed by CC

X-ray Crystallographic Analysis of Compounds 1 and 3.25
Colorless crystals of compounds 1 and 3 were obtained from the
solution of mixed MeOH−H2O. Crystallographic data were collected
on a Bruker Smart-1000 diﬀractometer equipped with graphite-
monochromatic Cu Kα radiation (λ = 1.541 78) Å at 297(2) K. The
data were corrected for absorption by using the program SADABS.26
The structures were solved by direct methods with the SHELXTL
software package.27,28 All non-hydrogen atoms were reﬁned
anisotropically. The H atoms connected to C atoms were calculated
theoretically, and those to O atoms were assigned by diﬀerence
Fourier maps. The absolute structures were determined by reﬁnement
of the Flack parameter.29 The structures were optimized by full-matrix
least-squares techniques.
Crystal data for compound 1: C58H72N2O10S, fw = 989.23,
monoclinic space group P2(1), unit cell dimensions a = 18.3429(9)
Å, b = 7.2409(4) Å, c = 20.7773(11) Å, V = 2757.7(3) Å3, α = γ =
90°, β = 92.114(2)°, Z = 2, dcalcd = 1.191 mg/m3, crystal dimensions
0.220 × 0.200 × 0.180 mm, μ = 0.988 mm−1, F(000) = 1060. The
10 082 measurements yielded 9009 independent reﬂections after
equivalent data were averaged. The ﬁnal reﬁnement gave R1 = 0.0412
and wR2 = 0.1171 [I > 2σ(I)]. The absolute structure parameter was
0.040(9).
Crystal data for compound 3: C30H39NO6, fw = 509.62,
monoclinic space group P2(1), unit cell dimensions a = 13.2416(5)
Å, b = 7.3330(2) Å, c = 14.5866(5) Å, V = 1410.24(8) Å3, α = γ =
90°, β = 95.330(2)°, Z = 2, dcalcd = 1.200 mg/m3, crystal dimensions
0.160 × 0.150 × 0.120 mm, μ = 0.669 mm−1, F(000) = 548. The 4563
measurements yielded 3145 independent reﬂections after equivalent
data were averaged. The ﬁnal reﬁnement gave R1 = 0.0686 and wR2 =
0.1796 [I > 2σ(I)]. The absolute structure parameter was 0.3(2).
Computational Section. Conformational searches were per-
formed via molecular mechanics using the MMFF method in
Macromodel software, and the geometries were reoptimized at the
B3LYP/6-31G(d) PCM/MeOH level using Gaussian 09 software to
obtain the energy-minimized conformers.30 Then, the optimized
conformers were subjected to calculations of the ECD spectra using
TDDFT at the BH&HLYP/TZVP, CAM-B3LYP/TZVP, and PBE0/
TZVP levels. The solvent eﬀects of the MeOH solution were
evaluated at the same DFT level using the SCRF/PCM method.
Antimicrobial Assays. The antimicrobial activities against eight
aquatic pathogens (Aeromonas hydrophilia QDIO-1, Edwardsiella
ictarda QDIO-10, E. tarda QDIO-2, Micrococcus luteus QDIO-3,
Pseudomonas aeruginosa QDIO-4, V. anguillarum QDIO-6, V. harveyi
QDIO-7, and V. parahemolyticus QDIO-8) and one human
pathogenic bacterium (Escherichia coli EMBLC-1) were determined
by a serial dilution technique using 96-well microtiter plates.31 The
aquatic pathogenic strains and human pathogenic bacterium were
obtained from IOCAS (Institute of Oceanology, Chinese Academy of
Sciences). Tested compounds and positive control (chloramphenicol)
were dissolved in DMSO to give a stock solution.
Cytotoxicity Assay. The cytotoxic activities of the isolated
compounds against the three tumor cell lines HCT-116 (human
colorectal cancer cell line), HepG-2 (human hepatocarcinoma cell
line), and MCF-7 (human breast cancer cell line) were determined
according to previously reported methods.32 Brieﬂy, tumor cells were
plated into a 96-well plate and were then treated with test compounds
at corresponding concentrations after seeding overnight. After 72 h,
25 μL of MTT solution (5 mg/mL) was added into each well. The
supernatant was removed after a certain incubation period, and 75 μL
of DMSO was then added to dissolve formazan crystals. Absorbance
of each well was read at 544 nm (test wavelength) and 690 nm
(background). Cisplatin was used as positive control.
