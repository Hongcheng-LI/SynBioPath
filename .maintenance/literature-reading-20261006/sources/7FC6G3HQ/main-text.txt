  Introduction


   Depsides represent a  large  class  of  ester-linked polyketide
   natural products, in which two or more hydroxybenzoic acid
   derivatives are condensed between phenol and carboxylic acid
   moieties to form ester linkages referred to as a depside bond
   (see Figure 1 for representative examples).[1–7] While predom-
    inantly found in lichens,[8,9] depsides are also produced in some
   higher plants[10–12] and free-living fungi.[13] As a class of natural
   products, depsides have garnered interest due to their diverse
   range of biological activities such as anticancer, antiviral, and
    antibacterial.[14–16] Structural variation in depsides can arise as a
    result of different number of repeating units, and the alkyl
   substitutions in the benzoic acid monomers. The biosynthesis
   of  depsides  has been  investigated  in  recent  years, and
   formation of the 2,4-dihydroxybenzoate moiety is derived from
   the  function  of a fungal nonreducing  polyketide synthase
   (NRPKS). Formation of the depside bond is also attributed to
   the NRPKS, although proposed mechanisms and responsible
   domains vary.[3,5,17–21]
       O-glycosylated depsides, such as exophillic acid and aqua-
    statin, which contain aryl ether linked glucose and galactose,
    respectively,  have  been  isolated  from  fungi  (Figure 1).[3,4]
