ABSTRACT: Five cyclopenta[d]pyrano[4,3-b]pyran-1,7(6H)-dione 6/6/5-fused tricyclic ring system containing metabolites
peniapyrones A−E (1−5), and four previously undescribed cyclopenta[4,5]furo[3,2-c]pyran-1-one 6/5/5-fused tricyclic ring system
containing compounds peniapyrones F−I (6−9), were isolated from the endophytic Penicillium brefeldianum F4a. Their structures,
including absolute configurations, were determined through spectroscopic analysis and quantum chemical calculations. Peniapyrones
D (4) and E (5) were a pair of diastereoisomers. Compounds 1, 3, and 5−9 showed cytotoxic activity against AsPC-1, CRL-2234,
and MCF-7 cancer cell lines. Compounds 1, 3, 6, 8, and 9 inhibited the Kirsten rat sarcoma viral oncogene homologue (KRAS)
mutant AsPC-1 cell line.
B
ased on Global Cancer Statistics, an estimated 10 million
cancer-related deaths and 19.3 million new cases occurred
globally in 2020,1 and there is a need to develop novel and
efficient anticancer drugs. Endophytic fungi have been
explored extensively for the isolation of anticancer drugs for
some time.2 Since 1993, many anticancer drugs have been
detected in endophytic fungi, including taxol (paclitaxel),3
camptothecin,4 hypericin,5 and podophyllotoxin.6 Plant
diversity has shaped a rich and unique diversity of endophytic
fungi, and they are considered a unique habitat for strains with
the potential to produce cytotoxic secondary metabolites and
are thus sustainable contributors to the discovery of anticancer
lead compounds.7−9
The genus Penicillium has been one of the most successful
and fruitful in terms of producing the capability of cytotoxic
secondary metabolites.10,11 In our previous study, three
undescribed brefeldin A analogues and a unique penipente-
none A, along with five known brefeldin A analogues which
potently inhibited KRAS mutant cancer cells, were isolated
from the endophytic Penicillium brefeldianum F4a.12 As part of
our continued endeavor to investigate the chemical diversity of
compounds displaying cytotoxic activity, peniapyrones A−I
(1−9; Chart 1), unprecedented 6/6/5-fused tricyclic com-
pounds and previously undescribed 6/5/5-fused tricyclic α-
pyrone derivatives, were isolated from P. brefeldianum F4a.
Structurally, compounds 1−5 possessed a cyclopenta[d]-
pyrano[4,3-b]pyran-1,7(6H)-dione carbon skeleton and com-
pounds 6−9 possessed a cyclopenta[4,5]furo[3,2-c]pyran-1-
one carbon skeleton. Herein, we present their isolation,
structure elucidation, and cytotoxic activity.

correlations of H2-10/C-8, H-11/C-8, and H2-14 (two olefinic

(±)-Peniapyrone I (9) was isolated as a colorless and
transparent oil. The UV spectrum of 9 was the same as that of
6, displaying a characteristic absorption peak at 293 nm
corresponding to a 4-hydroxy-2-pyrone moiety.13,14 Based on
(+)-HRESIMS, the molecular formula of 9 was assigned as
C12H12O4, with 7 degrees of unsaturation. Analysis of the
NMR data (Table 2 and Figure 1) revealed a close structural
relationship between 9 and 6. The primary distinctions were
observed within unit C, characterized by the presence of a
keto-carbonyl group. The HMBC correlation of H-11/C-9 and
the 1H−1H COSY correlation of H2-10/H-11, as well as the 7
degrees of unsaturation of 9, further confirmed that the keto-
carbonyl group was positioned at C-9 of 9 (Figure 1).
Additionally, the relative configuration of 9 was confirmed as
(7S*,11S*) through analysis of the NOESY spectrum (Figure
2). The barely measurable optical rotation implied that 9 was
obtained as a racemate mixture. Subsequently, 9 was analyzed
by chiral HPLC-DAD to two main chromatographic peaks,
which could be confirmed as a pair of optically pure
enantiomers, (+)-9 and (−)-9 (Figure S78). Regrettably,
there was an insufficient quantity of 9 for chiral separation.
Compounds 1−9 were assessed for in vitro cytotoxic effects
on three human cancer cell lines: AsPC-1 (human metastatic
pancreatic adenocarcinoma), CRL-2234 (human hepatocellu-
lar carcinoma), and MCF-7 (human breast carcinoma).
Compounds 1, 3, and 5−9 showed potential cytotoxic
activities against the AsPC-1, CRL-2234, and MCF-7 cell
lines. In addition, compounds 1, 3, 6, 8, and 9 (IC50 = 1.5−2.9
μM) displayed higher inhibition on AsPC-1 cells than the
positive control dasatinib (IC50 = 3.3 μM), and compounds 1,
3, and 5−9 (IC50 ranges from 0.2 to 9.9 μM) showed more
inhibition on CRL-2234 and MCF-7 cells than the positive
controls dasatinib and gemcitabine (IC50 > 10 μM) (Table 3).
Comparing the cytotoxic activities among compounds 1−4, it
was found that C-12 substituted groups in the 6/6/5-fused
tricyclic α-pyrones had a great effect on the inhibitory activity
against different tumor cells. Interestingly, the incorporation of
a methoxy group or ring D at C-12 significantly decreased the
cytotoxic activity. Conversely, the presence of a hydroxy or
ethoxy group at C-12 exhibited enhanced cytotoxic effects.
Moreover, compound 5 exhibited more significant cytotoxic
activities than compound 4, suggesting that the chirality played
an important role in the growth inhibition on cancer cells.
Comparing the cytotoxic activities among compounds 6−8, it
was inferred that C-8 substituted groups in the 6/5/5-fused
tricyclic α-pyrones had a great effect on the cytotoxicity.
Particularly, due to the presence of a methylene group at C-8,
compound 8 displayed over 3−23 times activity more than
compound 7. The above structure−activity relationship will
provide the basis for the structure optimization design of
peniapyrones in the future.
Most notably, AsPC-1 cells are Kirsten rat sarcoma viral
oncogene homologue (KRASG12D) mutant cancer cells and
KRAS represents a pivotal therapeutic target for malignant
tumors.15 Over the past three decades, only two KRAS
inhibitors were approved by the FDA, AMG 510 and
MRTX849, both for KRASG12C.16,17 However, a KRASG12D
inhibitor has not yet been discovered. Thus, compounds 1, 3,
6, 8, and 9 are prospective lead compounds for driving the
progression of new anticancer drugs for the treatment of
KRASG12D mutated tumors.
