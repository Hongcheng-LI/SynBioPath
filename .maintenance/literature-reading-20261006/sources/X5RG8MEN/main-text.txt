Genome Mining and Targeted Discovery of Twistane-Containing
Angucyclinones from Marine Streptomyces phaeochromogenes XSA2

ABSTRACT: Genome mining of a type II PKS biosynthetic gene
cluster from marine Streptomyces phaeochromogenes XSA2 resulted
in the discovery of 12 angucyclinone derivatives, including nine
new phaeomycins A−I (1−9) and three known analogs (10−12).
Phaeomycin A (1) contains a unique phthalide moiety connected
to a rare twistane skeleton that has not been previously identified
in natural products. The chemical structures of the isolated
compounds were established via extensive spectroscopic and X-ray
crystal-lographic analyses. A plausible biosynthetic pathway for 1−
2 was proposed based on bioinformatic analysis.
A
ngucyclinones, the aglycones of angucyclines, which are
commonly produced by actinomycetes, constitute a large
group of polycyclic aromatic polyketide natural products with
complex chemical structures and excellent biological activ-
ities.1,2 Typical angucyclinones are biosynthesized by type II
polyketide synthases (T2 PKSs) and characterized by a parent
benz[a]anthracene core. Additional tailoring enzymes catalyze
post-PKS modifications, enriching the structural diversity of
these molecules by decorating or reconstructing the basic
framework.3,4 Notably, redox enzyme−mediated oxidative C−
C bond cleavage and carbon skeleton rearrangement are
important contributors to the generation of atypical
angucyclinone derivatives with novel architectures.5,6 Recent
advances in genome mining technologies combined with
individual or comprehensive targeted activation strategies [e.g.,
“OSMAC” (one strain many compounds), promoter engineer-
ing, and heterologous expression] have greatly enhanced the
efficient discovery of natural products.7−10
We previously reported multiple angucyclinone-type poly-
ketides featuring highly oxygenated scaffolds and diverse
pharmacological properties during our exploration of bioactive
metabolites from marine actinomycetes.11 Building upon these
findings and seeking novel structural variants, we performed a
systematic bioinformatic screening of our in-house actino-
mycete genome library, identifying a homologous T2 PKS
biosynthetic gene cluster (BGC) in Streptomyces phaeochromo-
genes XSA2. Heterologous expression combined with the
OSMAC strategy facilitated the discovery of 12 angucyclinone-
originating polyketides. Phaeomycins A and B (1 and 2)
represent a new type of atypical angucyclinones possessing an
unprecedented C-ring-opened core skeleton, which is charac-
terized by an unusual C-7−C-12a linkage.
Through genome sequencing and bioinformatic annotation
of S. phaeochromogenes XSA2, we identified a T2 PKS BGC
sharing 53.1%, 45.4%, 40.3% and 34.9% sequence similarity
with the BGCs of lugdunomycins,12 thioangucyclines,13
saccharothrixins11 and rubiginones,14 respectively (Figure
S1). All reference compounds were classified within the
angucyclinone/angucycline families. Comparative genomic
analysis of the characterized BGCs revealed the presence of
the phm BGC, which spans a 28.7 kb DNA fragment that
includes 28 open reading frames (Figure 1a and Table S1).
The phm BGC encodes “minimal PKSs” PhmA2−A4, cyclase
PhmA1, ketoreductase PhmA5, aromatase/cyclase PhmA6, α/
β hydrolase PhmH, and oxidoreductases PhmO1−OX (Figure
1a). Besides the genes that are functionally similar to those in
the homologous BGCs, the phm BGC harbors three
phylogenetically distinct genes (phmO7, phmO8, and
phmOX) with low similarity or uncharacterized functions,
suggesting the potential involvement of the phm BGC in the
biosynthesis of structurally novel secondary metabolites.
To target obtain the phm BGC corresponding products, we
implemented a combinatorial approach employing OSMAC
cultivation and heterologous expression. S. phaeochromogenes
XSA2 was cultured in four different fermentation media, with
the metabolites in SM6 medium displaying similar UV
absorption profiles as saccharothrixins (Figure S2).11 To

Preliminary assessment based on molecular weight discrep-
ancies with saccharothrixins11 strongly suggested the structural
novelty of these products.
To isolate the target products, we conducted a 40 L scale-up
fermentation of S. phaeochromogenes XSA2 with SM6 medium
(Table S2). The crude extract was subjected to sequential
normal-phase silica gel and semipreparative HPLC guided by
LC-MS/MS, yielding 12 angucyclinone derivatives (Figure 1c),
including nine new phaeomycins A−I (1−9) and three known
analogs (10−12). Structural elucidation was performed via
comprehensive spectroscopic analysis [high-resolution electro-
spray ionization mass spectrometry (HRESIMS) and NMR]
complemented with a literature comparison.
Phaeomycins A and B (1 and 2) were obtained as yellowish
powders, with the molecular formulas C19H20O6 and C19H22O6
established by HRESIMS analysis, yielding [M + H]+ ions at
m/z 345.1325 and 347.1486, respectively. Analysis of the 1D
NMR and HSQC data (Table 1) of 1 revealed 19 carbon
resonances, one ketone carbonyl (δC 214.3), one ester
carbonyl (δC 169.3), six olefinic carbons (δC 152.9, 132.5,
131.1, 128.6, 121.1, and 115.6), five methines with two
oxygenated signals (δH/C 5.91/80.4 and 3.62/73.3), two
quaternary carbons (δC 75.5 and 52.9), three methylenes (δC
37.7, 34.0, and 33.6), and one methyl (δH/C 1.20/22.5). The
six olefinic carbons were easily assigned to the 1,2,3-
trisubstituted benzene ring, and the presence of a phthalide
moiety was confirmed by the HMBC correlations from H-11
(δH 7.28) to C-12 and H-7 to C-7a/C-8/C-11a/C-12 (Figure
2). The presence of the rare twistane fragment was established

C bond between C-7 and C-12b, as evidenced by the key

only difference being the presence of a decalone group in 2
instead of a twistane unit, which were confirmed by the COSY
and HMBC correlations (Figure 2). Moreover, the absolute
configuration of 2 was determined to be same as that of 1
based on their shared biosynthetic origin. Compounds 1 and 2
represent a new class of atypical angucyclinones with an
unprecedented carbon skeleton that has not been previously
identified in angucyclinone-originating derivatives.
The molecular formulas of the remaining new compounds
(3−9) were determined by HRESIMS spectral analysis (Figure
S4). The 1D NMR data of 3−9 (Tables S5−S9) indicated the
presence of a highly oxygenated angucyclinone core backbone,
which were similar to that of our previously reported
saccharothrixins.11 The COSY and HMBC correlations of
3−9 (Figure S5) were thoroughly elucidated to construct their
planar structures. The known compounds elmycin B (10),16
emycin A (11),17 and ochromycinone (12)17,18 were
confirmed by comparison of the MS and NMR spectra with
literature data. The absolute configuration of 3 was identified
by X-ray crystallographic analysis (Figure 3). The remaining
new compounds (4−9) were determined to be the same as
compounds 1−3 and the coisolated known compounds based
on their coexistence after fermentation and shared biosynthetic
origin as well as NOESY and ECD spectra analysis (Figure
S6).
To the best of our knowledge, the twistane architecture in 1
has not been previously discovered in natural products and the
unprecedented C-7−C-12a linkage in 1 and 2 constitutes a
novel structural feature within angucyclinone derivatives,
indicating an extraordinary novel biosynthetic pathway and
enzyme catalytic mechanism. Based on bioinformatic analysis
of the phm BGC and the coisolated molecules, a plausible
biosynthetic pathway for 1−2 was proposed (Figure 4). The
phm BCG, comprising the genes phmA1−A6, which encode a
cyclase (PhmA1), ketoacyl synthase (PhmA2), chain length
factor (PhmA3), acyl carrier protein (PhmA4), ketoreductase
(PhmA5), and aromatase/cyclase (PhmA6), was hypothesized
to generate the early intermediate UWM6.1 Heterologous
expression of these core genes in S. albus J1074 led to UWM6
production, supporting this hypothesis (Figure S8). Subse-
quent oxidative transformation of UWM6 yielded emycin A
(11), which underwent spontaneous oxidation to form
ochromycinone (12), as evidenced by their coisolation.
The formation route for 1 and 2 was proposed through
twice C−C bond cleavage and new C−C bond formation,
starting from the precursor (11 or 12) (Figure 4). PhmO7
(homologue of SimC7), which catalyzes C-7 keto conversion
to a hydroxyl group,20 and PhmOX (homologue of LugOV),
which is predicted to introduce an oxygen atom between C-6a
and C-7 and then trigger C-ring cleavage,19 could direct the
initial C-ring opening between C-6a and C-7, leading to the
formation of 8. Then, the novel skeleton of 1 and 2 could be
created from 8 through a series of reactions, including
secondary Baeyer−Villiger (B−V) oxidation could be catalyzed
by PhmO8 (homologue of SpiH1) to generate the C-ring−
opened product,21 inducing secondary C−C bond cleavage
between C-12 and C-12a; intramolecular esterification
between C-7 and C-12; and once or twice aldol reactions,
causing new C−C bond formation between C-7 and C-12b
and C-2 and C-6, respectively. However, the specific functions

Antioxidant, antitumor, anti-inflammatory (lipopolysacchar-
ide-induced nitric oxide (NO) production in adherent cells),
and antibacterial bioactivity assays were conducted with 1−8.
Compounds 6 and 7 at a concentration of 3 mg/mL exhibited
antioxidant activity, with ·OH clearance rates of 28.14% and
41.49%, respectively (Figure S9a). Compound 8 exhibited
cytotoxic activity against human cancer cell lines MKN-45 and
5637, with IC50 values of 7.22 and 7.90 μM, respectively
(Table S10). Compounds 6 and 8 suppressed NO generation
at a concentration of 20 μM, which was nontoxic to
macrophage line RAW264.7 (Figure S9b). None of the tested
compounds exhibited appreciable antibacterial activity against
the tested strains.
In conclusion, we identified 12 angucyclinone derivatives,
including nine new phaeomycins (1−9), via genome mining of
a T2 PKS BGC from S. phaeochromogenes XSA2, which
encodes multiple oxidoreductases. Compounds 1 and 2
represent the first examples of angucyclinone-originating
derivatives with unique C-ring cleavage, forming a phthalide
architecture connected to a rare twistane or decalone moiety,
respectively, through an unprecedented C-7−C-12a linkage.
Biological activity screening revealed that 7 exhibited
antioxidant activity and 8 exhibited antitumor and anti-
inflammatory activities. A preliminary biosynthetic pathway
for the phaeomycins was hypothesized based on bioinformatic
analysis and the coisolated compounds. More experiments are
underway to verify the functions of the genes comprising the
phm BGC and biological activities of 1 and 2. The findings of
the study expand the existing structural diversity of natural
products and highlight the unlimited metabolic potential of
type II PKSs.
