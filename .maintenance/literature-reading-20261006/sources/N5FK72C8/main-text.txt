A Selenium-for-Oxygen Swapping Reaction Catalyzed by the Radical
S‑Adenosyl Methionine Enzyme AbmM

ABSTRACT: Selenium is an essential element to organisms; however, few enzymes are known to catalyze the carbon−selenium
bond formation. While several carbon−sulfur bond-forming enzymes have been engineered to install carbon−selenium bonds by
using paired electron chemistry, the potential of utilizing radical chemistry to facilitate C−Se bond formation is underdeveloped.
Herein, the native sulfur-inserting radical S-adenosyl methionine (SAM) enzyme AbmM involved in the biosynthesis of albomycin δ2
is shown to catalyze a selenium-for-oxygen swapping reaction converting the furanose of cytidine 5′-diphosphate to a 4′-hydroxy-4′-
selenofuranose diphosphate. The dephosphorylated 4′-seleno product can be further processed by downstream biosynthetic
enzymes AbmG and AbmH to yield a 4′-selenoheptose nucleoside. These results establish an enzymatic approach to synthesize 4′-
seleno sugars in a radical-mediated manner and provide a likely route to prepare selenium-containing analogues of albomycin δ2 for
drug discovery. This work also demonstrates that [Fe4Se4] clusters can support radical SAM redox chemistry and that the radical
SAM cluster in AbmM can function as the chalcogen donor.
S
elenium (Se) is a chalcogen essential to the physiology of
living organisms.1,2 While many selenium-containing
metabolites have been discovered, natural enzymes known to
catalyze formation of carbon−selenium bonds remain
scarce.3−6 Given the similarity of selenium chemistry to that
of sulfur, it has been possible to employ C−S bond-forming
enzymes to construct C−Se bonds. For example, ubiquitin-like
small sulfur carrier proteins have been applied to the synthesis
of L-selenocysteine (1), selenothiamine monophosphate (2),
and selenochuangxinmycin (3) using paired electron chemistry
(Figure 1A).7 In contrast, the potential of using radical
chemistry to facilitate C−Se bond formation is under-
developed. Previously, the radical S-adenosyl methionine
(SAM) enzyme BioB was demonstrated to be able to catalyze
the production of selenobiotin (5) in a radical-mediated
selenium insertion manner when the enzyme was depleted of
Fe and S followed by reconstitution with FeCl3 and Na2Se to
reconstitute an auxiliary [Fe2Se2]Aux
2+ cluster which serves as
the selenium donor (4 →5, Figure 1B).8 In this work, the
radical SAM enzyme AbmM, which catalyzes a sulfur-for-
oxygen-swap reaction as its biosynthetic function, is shown to
also catalyze the analogous selenium-for-oxygen swapping
reaction.
AbmM is a radical SAM enzyme that catalyzes sulfur
incorporation into cytidine 5′-diphosphate (CDP, 6) to form
the 4′-thiofuranose product (8) during the biosynthesis of
albomycin δ2 (Figure 2).9 Previous mechanistic studies have
shown that the 5′-deoxyadenosyl radical (5′-dA·) generated
from the reductive cleavage of SAM abstracts the 4′H atom of
CDP to form a C4′ radical (10) and 5′-deoxyadenosine (5′-
dA).9 The radical then propagates from C4′ to C1′ via C1′−O
bond cleavage (10 →11).9,10 Evidence was also acquired
revealing the subsequent attack by the resulting C1′ radical
(11) at a bridging sulfur in the radical SAM [Fe4S4]RS cluster
as opposed to the C-terminal auxiliary [Fe4S4]Aux cluster to
forge the C1′−S bond (11 →12). Intramolecular cyclization
then yields the thiofuranose product (12 →8),9 which after
dephosphorylation produces the key intermediate 9 in the
biosynthesis of albomycin δ2. Therefore, the [Fe4S4]RS cluster
of AbmM plays a dual role in the catalysis: mediating the
reductive cleavage of SAM as well as serving as the sulfur
donor.

Treating AbmM with the iron chelator 1,10-phenanthroline
(PT) and sodium dithionite has been shown to selectively
remove bound [Fe4S4]RS clusters and leave the auxiliary
[Fe4S4]Aux clusters intact.9 Subsequent reconstitution with
Na2
34S and FeCl3 led to the enzyme containing labeled
[Fe4
34S4]RS clusters and unlabeled [Fe4S4]Aux clusters. This
difference in the susceptibility to chelation by the two [Fe4S4]
clusters of AbmM also raises the possibility of selectively
replacing the sulfide components of the [Fe4S4]RS cluster with
selenide. If the resulting [Fe4Se4]RS cluster continues to be able
to both facilitate reductive cleavage of SAM and serve as a
selenium atom donor, then it may be possible to mediate the
conversion of CDP (6) to the selenated product 4′-
selenofuranose (13) (Figure 3A). To assess this possibility,
the as-isolated AbmM was first treated with PT and sodium
dithionite on ice packs overnight under anaerobic conditions
to remove the [Fe4S4]RS cluster. The resulting protein,
designated AbmM-PT, was then incubated anaerobically with
8-fold FeCl3 and Na2Se on ice packs for 3 h to reconstitute the
[Fe4Se4]RS cluster. The resulting enzyme was designated as Se-
AbmM.
The activity of Se-AbmM was tested by incubating 100 μM
enzyme with 0.4 mM CDP, 0.6 mM SAM, and 2 mM sodium
dithionite in 40 mM 4-(2-hydroxyethyl)-1-piperazineethane-
sulfonic acid (HEPES) buffer (pH 7.5) at room temperature
under anaerobic conditions for 0.5 h. The reaction mixture was
subsequently treated with calf intestinal alkaline phosphatase
(CIP) to dephosphorylate any nucleotides and thereby
facilitate characterization by HPLC-UV and LCMS. As a
result, a new species eluting at 12.5 min was observed along
with ca. 30% substrate consumption and the formation of 5′-
deoxyadenosine (5′-dA), whereas the control reaction with
AbmM-PT did not produce either 5′-dA or the native
thioproduct 9 and did not show any consumption of the
substrate (Figure 3B). The presence of 5′-dA derived from
SAM suggests that the reconstituted Se-AbmM is capable of
mediating the reductive cleavage of SAM. Furthermore, the
species eluting at 12.5 min was characterized by an m/z (calcd
for C9H14N3O5Se+ [M + H]+: 324.0094, found: 324.0098) and
an isotopomer distribution pattern consistent with the
selenated product 14 (Figure 4A,B). The specific activities of
Se-AbmM were 0.21 ± 0.02 min−1 and 0.26 ± 0.02 min−1 with
respect to the formation of 14 and the reduction of SAM. In
comparison, specific activities of 0.17 ± 0.01 min−1 and 0.22 ±
0.02 min−1 were measured for wild-type AbmM-catalyzed
sulfur incorporation and SAM reduction, respectively. These
results imply that the observed rates of product formation in
the selenium insertion reaction are comparable to those in the
native sulfur incorporation reaction.
A large-scale reaction was next performed in order to isolate
sufficient product for NMR characterization. The 1H NMR
spectrum of this product exhibited two well-resolved sets of
peaks consistent with an equilibrated mixture of major and
minor selenohemiacetal epimers (Figure 4C).9 Indeed, 2D
exchange spectroscopy (EXSY) suggested that the two sets of
proton signals are interchangeable (Figure S3). As anticipated,
no resonance consistent with 4′H was detected for either

During the biosynthesis of albomycin δ2, the dephosphory-
lated AbmM product (9) is converted to 21/23 by the
multifunctional kinase AbmG through sequential phosphor-
ylation at 4′−OH followed by elimination of the diphosphate
(Figure 5A).11 To test whether the 4′-seleno substrate 14
could also be processed by AbmG, 0.3 mM 14 was incubated
with 4.8 μM AbmG, 100 mM KCl, 5 mM MgCl2, and 2.5 mM
ATP in 50 mM HEPES (pH 7.5) buffer at room temperature
anaerobically. HPLC analysis of the reaction mixture showed
that 14 was converted to the monophosphorylated product
(16, calcd for C9H13N3O8PSe−[M−H]−401.9612, found:
401.9619), then the diphosphorylated intermediate (18, calcd
for C9H14N3O11P2Se−[M−H]−481.9275, found: 481.9279),
and finally the aldehyde hydrate (24, calcd for C9H14N3O5Se+
[M + H]+ 324.0094, found: 324.0094) (Figures 5B and S7).
Therefore, AbmG is capable of accepting 14 to form 4′-
selenonucleoside 22/24.
The next step in the albomycin biosynthetic pathway
involves AbmH-catalyzed transaldolation between 21 and L-
threonine to produce 25 (Figure 6A).12 In order to determine
whether the aldehyde 22 containing the selenofuranose would
also be recognized as a substrate for the transaldolation
reaction, an AbmG-AbmH coupled reaction was conducted
containing 0.4 mM 14, 40 mM KCl, 5 mM MgCl2, 2.5 mM
ATP, 5 mM L-threonine, 20 μM AbmG, and 20 μM AbmH at
room temperature under anaerobic conditions overnight. To
facilitate HPLC characterization, the reaction mixture was
treated with CIP and then NaBH4 to convert ATP and residual
aldehyde 22 to adenosine and 27, respectively. The results
shown in Figure 6C demonstrate that the AbmG aldehyde
product is converted to a new product eluting at 3.3 min in the
presence of AbmH, which displays an exact mass consistent
with the anticipated AbmH product (26, calcd for
C11H17N4O6Se+ [M + H]+ 381.0308, found: 381.0302)
(Figure S8). Hence, AbmH is also capable of recognizing the
selenoanalogue of its native substrate to generate a 4′-
selenoheptose product (26) (Figure 6B).

To date, only a few carbon−selenium bond-forming
enzymes have been discovered as shown in Figure 7, where
selenophosphate (28) serves as a common substrate in these
transformations and its selenium is incorporated into
selenocysteinyl tRNA (29),3 2-selenouridine tRNAs (30),4 or
1-seleno-N-acetylglucosamine (31)5,6 via paired-electron
chemistry. In contrast, the results reported herein represent
an alternative tactic based on a radical-mediated approach to
introduce selenium into secondary metabolites. It was shown
that AbmM activity can be recovered using selenide in place of
sulfide during reconstitution of the enzyme following selective
chelation of the radical SAM cluster.9 The resulting enzyme
(Se-AbmM) is capable of catalyzing a selenium-for-oxygen
swapping reaction to form a 4′-selenonucleotide. This result
demonstrates that AbmM can also tolerate [Fe4Se4]RS clusters
to catalyze the reaction with similar activity and suggests that
the [Fe4Se4]RS cluster serves as the selenide donor that
undergoes complete or partial degradation upon C−Se bond
formation. This mechanism would be consistent with the
proposal that the [Fe4S4]RS cluster is the sulfur donor during
the AbmM-catalyzed reaction.9,13
Moreover, the dephosphorylated Se-AbmM product can be
accepted by the downstream biosynthetic enzymes AbmG and
AbmH to yield a 4′-selenoheptose. Whether additional steps in
the albomycin δ2 biosynthetic pathway also accept 4′-
selenosubstrates remains under current investigation; however,
the present study has paved the way for the enzymatic
preparation of a selenoanalog of albomycin δ2. More
importantly, these results continue to demonstrate the
versatility of radical SAM enzymes and, in particular, are
consistent with the previous report8 that [Fe4Se4] clusters are
equally capable of supporting radical SAM chemistry.
