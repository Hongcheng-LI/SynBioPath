ABSTRACT: Twelve new cytochalasins, phomopchalasins D−O
(1−3, 5−12, and 14), including one brominated (2) and two
iodinated cytochalasins (3 and 6), together with six known
analogues (4, 13, and 15−18) were isolated from the mangrove-
derived fungus Phomopsis sp. QYM-13 treated with 3% NaBr or 3%
KI in potato liquid medium. Their structures and absolute
conﬁgurations were established by extensive spectroscopic analysis
(1D and 2D NMR, HRESIMS), electronic circular dichroism
calculations, and a single-crystal X-ray diﬀraction experiment.
Compounds 3 and 6 represent the ﬁrst iodinated cytochalasins.
Compounds 2, 15, 17, and 18 exhibited signiﬁcant cytotoxicity
against human cancer cell line MDA-MB-435 with IC50 values
ranging from 0.2 to 8.2 μM.
N
atural halogenated compounds are commonly isolated
from marine organisms, exhibiting diverse bioactivities
including cytotoxic, anti-inﬂammatory, antimicrobial, antiviral,
and antioxidant activities.1,2 Approximately 6000 halogenated
compounds have been discovered from natural sources.3,4
Chlorinated or brominated compounds account for most of the
halogenated metabolites. Iodinated compounds are much more
rare than chlorinated or brominated ones.2,5 Moreover, natural
iodinated molecules have mainly been isolated from algae and
sponges, such as tasihalides A and B from a cyanobacterium and
a red alga6 and iodinated acetylenic acids from the marine
sponges Suberites mammilaris and Suberites japonicus.7
An OSMAC (one strain many compounds) approach has
been widely applied to the discovery of new metabolites from
microorganisms. In this strategy, silent gene clusters can be
activated through various methods, including alteration of
medium composition, cocultivation, addition precursors, and
enzyme inhibitors.8,9 For example, adding NaBr in the medium
led to the isolation of the brominated azaphilones penicilones G
and H from Penicillium janthinellum HK1-610 and bromophi-
lones A and B from Penicillium canescens 4.14.6a.11 Inspired by
this approach, NaBr or KI was added to the culture medium of
the mangrove-derived fungus Phomopsis sp. QYM-13 in an
attempt to obtain halogen-substituted metabolites. In order to
select a suitable salinity, a preliminary experiment of the strain
was performed at three concentrations (0.3%, 3%, and 6%) of
NaBr/KI (Figure S85). Subsequently, the EtOAc extracts of the
diﬀerent fermentation broths were subjected to UPLC-MS
analysis (Figure S86). A follow-up fermentation of the optional
medium (3% NaBr or 3% KI) led to the isolation of 12 new
cytochalasins, phomopchalasins D−O (1−3, 5−12, and 14),
including one bromide (2) and two iodide (3 and 6) derivatives.
Herein, we describe details of the isolation, structure
elucidation, and cytotoxic activities of all compounds.

Compound 2 was isolated as a white solid. The molecular
formula was determined as C30H38BrNO4 based on the
HRESIMS data, and the bromine isotope pattern at m/z
556.20593 [M + H]+ and 558.20386 [M + 2 + H]+ (a ratio of
1:1) showed the presence of one bromine atom in the molecule.
Comparing the chemical shifts at C-14 (δH 3.47 and δC 77.0 in 1;
δH 5.12 and δC 62.1 in 2) indicated the hydroxy group in 1 was
replaced by a bromine in 2. The COSY and HMBC correlations
(Figure 1) further conﬁrmed the two-dimensional structure.
The absolute conﬁguration of 2 was determined as 3S,
4R,5S,7S,8R,9R,13R,14R,16R,20S,21R by comparing the exper-
imental ECD spectra between 1 and 2 (Figure 3).
Compound 3 was isolated as a colorless oil with the molecular
formula of C30H38INO4 deduced from its positive HRESIMS
ion at m/z 604.19225 [M + H]+. The similarities between the
1D NMR data (Tables 1 and 2) of 1 and 3 indicated that the
structure of 3 closely resembled 1. The deduction was further
conﬁrmed by extensive analysis of their 2D NMR data. The
slight diﬀerences of the chemical shifts at H-14 (δH 3.47 for 1, δH
5.30 for 3) and C-14 (δC 77.0 for 1, δC 49.6 for 3) showed an
iodine atom substituted at C-14 in 3. Analysis of the NOESY
correlations (Figure 2) and the experimental electronic circular
dichroism (ECD) spectrum (Figure 3) suggested 3 had the same
absolute conﬁguration as 1.
The bromine and iodine in compounds 2 and 3 replaced the
hydroxy group in 1, and the conﬁgurations of compounds 2 and
3 were the same as those of 1. Incubation of compound 1 with
NaBr or KI under various conditions did not result in the
formation of 2 or 3. The results suggest that 2 and 3 originate
from enzyme-catalyzed pathways rather than as products of
chemical reactions (see the Experimental Section)
Compound 5 was obtained as a colorless oil. The molecular
formula was determined to be C30H37NO4 based on HRESIMS
data. The 1H NMR data (Table 1) of 5 showed four methyl
groups, one phenyl group, and an exomethylene group. The 13C
NMR data (Table 2) of 5 highlighted the presence of 30 carbon
resonances, including four methyls, four methylenes (one
oleﬁnic), 16 methines (six oleﬁnic), and six nonprotonated
carbons. The 1H and 13C NMR data of 5 were similar to the data
for the known compound cytochalasin J3 (4),13 except for the
presence of an acetyl group (δC 170.2, 21.2) in 5. The HMBC
correlation (Figure 1) from H-21 to C-24 indicated the acetyl
group was located at C-21. The analysis of the NOESY
correlations (Figure 2) and the experimental ECD spectrum
(Figure 3) showed the absolute conﬁguration of 5 was identical
to that of 4.

Compound 8 showed a molecular formula of C30H37NO4 as
deduced from its (+)-HRESIMS data. The similar 1H and 13C

The molecular formula of compound 9 was assigned as
C30H39NO6 based on its (+)-HRESIMS data. Comparison of
the 1D and 2D NMR data (Table 2 and 4) of 9 and 18 showed
that the Δ19 double bond in 18 was oxidized to an epoxide in 9,
as inferred by the COSY and HMBC correlations (Figure 1).
The NOESY correlations (Figure 2) of H-16/H-19 indicated
the epoxide was in the β-orientation. The absolute conﬁguration
of 9 was determined by the similar experimental ECD curve
(Figure 6) of 9 to that of 18.
Compound 10 was assigned a molecular formula of
C28H35NO4 based on its positive HRESIMS data. The 1 H
and 13C NMR data (Tables 3 and 5) suggested that 10 was
structurally similar to 9. The main diﬀerence was the
disappearance of the acetyl group at C-21, and the hydroxy
group at C-21 was oxidized to a ketone carbonyl, as conﬁrmed
by the HMBC correlations from H-19 and H-20 to C-21.
Additionally, the HMBC correlations (Figure 1) from H3-12 to
C-6 and C-7 and from H3-11 to C-6 indicated that the Δ6(12)
double bond and C-7 alcohol had been replaced by a Δ6 double
bond. The relative conﬁguration of 10 was established by
NOESY cross-peaks (Figure 2). The identical experimental and
calculated ECD curves (Figure 7) ascertained its absolute
conﬁguration.
Compound 11 had the molecular formula C28H35NO3
according to the HRESIMS data. The two-dimensional structure
of 11 was similar to 10 by comparison of their NMR data
(Tables 4 and 5). The distinction was the disappearance of the
hydroxy group at C-18 in 11, which was conﬁrmed by the COSY
correlations (Figure 1) of H-17/H-18(/H3-23)/H-19/H-20
and the HMBC correlations (Figure 1) from H3-23 to C-17 and
C-19. The relative conﬁguration of 11 was the same as 10 based
on their similar NOESY correlations (Figure 2). Its absolute
conﬁguration was assigned by comparing the experimental and
calculated ECD spectra (Figure 8).
Compound 12 was isolated as a colorless oil. The molecular
formula C28H37NO3 was deduced from its HRESIMS data. The
NMR data (Tables 4 and 5) of 12 were closely related to those of
17, with the diﬀerence being the absence of the exomethylene
protons and an oxygenated methine and the presence of a new
methyl (δH 1.71) and one oleﬁn proton (δH 5.35). Thereafter,
the HMBC correlations (Figure 1) from H3-12 to C-6 and C-7
and from H3-11 to C-6 indicated the existence of a double bond
between C-6 and C-7. The relative conﬁguration of 12 was
deduced from the NOESY correlations (Figure 2). The absolute
conﬁguration was identical to 18 based on similar ECD spectra
(Figure 6).
Compound 14 was puriﬁed as a colorless oil with a molecular
formula of C28H37NO4 based upon the HRESIMS data. The 1H
and 13C NMR data (Tables 4 and 5) of 14 showed close
resemblances to those of 12. By analyses of the HMBC spectrum
(Figure 1), the correlation from H2-12 to C-7 and C-7 indicated
the methyl at C-12 in 12 was oxidized to a hydroxymethyl group
in 14. The relative and absolute conﬁgurations of 14 were
consistent with those of 12 by the similar NOESY correlations
(Figure 2) and ECD curves (Figure 6).

■EXPERIMENTAL SECTION
General Experimental Procedures. Optical rotations were
measured on a PerkinElmer 341 instrument at 25 °C. Melting points
were recorded on a Fisher-Johns hot-stage apparatus. UV spectra were
measured in MeOH using a Shimadzu UV-2700 spectrophotometer.
ECD data were obtained on a Chirascan CD spectrometer (Applied
Photophysics). IR spectra were recorded on a Nicolet iS 10 FT-IR
spectrometer with KBr disks. A Bruker Avance 500 spectrometer (1H
500 MHz, 13C 125 MHz) in chloroform-d was used for the 1D and 2D
NMR data collection. Chemical shifts were referenced to tetrame-
thylsilane (TMS) at 0.00 ppm, residual solvent at H 7.26 for 1H NMR
spectra, and C 77.1 ppm for 13C NMR spectra. All HRESIMS data were
obtained on an Agilent G6230 Q-TOF mass spectrometer. Silica gel
(200−300 mesh, Qingdao Marine Chemical Factory) and Sephadex
LH-20 (Amersham Pharmacia) were used for column chromatography
(CC). Silica gel plates (Qingdao Huang Hai Chemical Group Co., G60,
F-254) were used for thin-layer chromatography (TLC). Semi-
preparative HPLC was performed on an Agilent 1260 liquid
chromatography system with an XSelect CSH C18 column (5 μm,
4.6 × 250 mm).
Fungal Material. The fungus Phomopsis sp. QYM-13 was isolated
from healthy leaves of Kandelia candel, which were collected in June
2017 from the South China Sea, Dongzhai Harbor Mangrove Nature
Reserve Area, Hainan Province, China. The strain was identiﬁed as a
Phomopsis sp. (GenBank No. MZ672030) by a BLAST search, which
showed 99% identity to the sequence of a Phomopsis sp. (GenBank No.
EU236706.1). The strain is preserved in the School of Chemistry, Sun
Yat-sen University.
Fermentation, Extraction, and Isolation. The fungus Phomopsis
sp. QYM-13 was cultivated on potato dextrose agar (PDA) for 5 days.
The mycelia of the strain were inoculated into 500 mL of potato
dextrose broth (PDB) for 3 days to prepare the seed culture. Then, it
was inoculated into 100 × 1000 mL Erlenmeyer ﬂasks each containing
200 mL of PDB liquid medium (3 g of potato extract, 20 g of glucose, 30
g of NaBr or KI in 1 L of H2O) for 30 days at room temperature under
static conditions.
After fermentation (adding 3% NaBr), the mycelia and broth were
separated. The mycelia were extracted with MeOH three times, and the
broth was extracted with EtOAc. Then the solution of MeOH extract
from mycelia and the EtOAc extract from the broth were concentrated
under reduced pressure, respectively. Subsequently, the total residue
(21.2 g) was subjected to silica gel CC (200−300 mesh silica) using
gradient elution with petroleum ether/EtOAc (9:1 to 1:9) to aﬀord six
fractions (Fr. A−F). Fr. B was applied to Sephadex LH-20 CC
(CH2Cl2/MeOH, v/v, 1:1) to give three fractions (Fr. B1−B3). Fr. B1
was subjected to silica gel CC (CH2Cl2/MeOH, v/v, 98:2) to yield
compounds 1 (12.8 mg) and 18 (20.0 mg). Fr. C was chromatographed
on Sephadex LH-20 CC (100% MeOH) to aﬀord three fractions (Fr.
C1−C3). Fraction C1 was puriﬁed by semipreparative reversed-phase
