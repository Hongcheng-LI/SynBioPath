ABSTRACT: Flavonoid apiosides are widely distributed in cereals,
fruits, vegetables, and medicinal herbs and play critical roles in
human health. Their facile and efficient synthesis has been a hot but
challenging topic in the fields of both organic chemistry and
biosynthesis. However, very few apiosyltransferases (ApiGTs) have
been reported thus far. Here, we report the first flavonoid
apiosyltransferase (CaApiGT) capable of catalyzing the 2″-O-
apiosylation of flavonoid 3-O-glycosides in chickpea (Cicer
arietinum). Moreover, we identify PcApiGT from parsley
(Petroselinum crispum), which catalyzes the 2″-O-apiosylation of
flavonoid 7-/4′-O-glycosides. To dissect the mechanisms under-
lying their different sugar acceptor selectivity, we obtain 10
complex crystal structures of CaApiGT and PcApiGT with
resolutions ranging from 1.55 to 2.65 Å, including CaApiGT/UDP, 6 ternary structures of CaApiGT/UDP/sugar acceptors,
PcApiGT/UDP, and 2 ternary structures of PcApiGT/UDP/sugar acceptors. Structural analyses, theoretical calculations, and site-
directed mutagenesis indicate that flavonoid 3-O-glycosides and 7-O-glycosides exhibit a T-shape and streamline shape, respectively,
and fit the active pockets of CaApiGT and PcApiGT. Moreover, the sugar acceptor selectivity of these two apiosyltransferases is
determined by a key α-helix. In CaApiGT, this α-helix contains multiple polar amino acids, particularly a threonine residue at its end.
Using this α-helix motif as a marker, we further characterize four apiosyltransferases from Leguminosae plants that exhibit functional
similarity to CaApiGT. This work unravels detailed sugar acceptor selectivity mechanisms of plant apiosyltransferases and provides
efficient biocatalysts for the synthesis of flavonoid apiosides.
■INTRODUCTION
Glycosylation is a frequent and important postmodification
reaction that profoundly impacts the properties of drug basic
scaffolds,1 such as bioactivity, solubility, and bioavailability.2,3
Notably, glycosylation reactions involving apiose,4 digitoxose,5
fucose,6 and quinovose7 moieties have attracted considerable
attention due to their distinctive structural and functional
features. Apiose is a natural pentose with a unique branched
chain. A wide array of apiosylated natural products have been
reported from plants,8 thus far. Particularly, flavonoid apiosides
are widely present in cereals,9 fruits,10 vegetables,11,12 and
medicinal herbs,13 and they possess various biological
activities. For instance, liquiritin apioside (liquiritigenin 4′-O-
apiosyl-(1 →2)-glucoside) from Glycyrrhiza uralensis shows
antitussive activity,14 and apiin (apigenin 7-O-apiosyl-(1 →2)-
glucoside) from parsley could alleviate hyperuricemia.15
Although flavonoid apiosides are widely distributed in the
plant kingdom and exhibit significant bioactivities, very few
apiosyltransferases responsible for the biosynthesis of apiosides
have been reported. In 2023, our group reported the first
flavonoid apiosyltransferase (GuApiGT)4 from the medicinal
plant G. uralensis, which demonstrated high efficiency in
catalyzing the 2″-O-apiosylation of flavonoid 7-O- or 4′-O-
glycosides and high selectivity toward uridine diphosphate
apiose (UDP-Api) as sugar donor (Figure 1A). Additionally,
we identified four other ApiGTs that exhibited similar
functions to GuApiGT. In the meanwhile, Osbourn’s group
reported
the
first
triterpenoid
apiosyltransferase
(UGT73CY2)16 from Quillaja saponaria, though it could not
utilize flavonoids as substrates. While flavonoid 3-O-
apiosylglycosides represent the largest group of flavonoid
apiosides in plants, no apiosyltransferases have been reported
to accept flavonoid 3-O-glycosides. Moreover, the selectivity
toward sugar acceptors remains poorly understood, whereas

Chickpea (Cicer arietinum) is an annual plant of the
Leguminosae family.17 It is a popular food worldwide with
an annual global production exceeding 17 million tons.18 It
exhibits various benefits to human health,19 and could be
helpful to metabolic syndromes, diabetes, and inflammation.
Chickpea contains abundant flavonol apiosides, with kaempfer-
ol as the aglycone. One major compound is kaempferol 3-O-
apiosyl-(1 →2)-glucoside, also named astragalin 2″-O-
apioside.
In this study, we report the first flavonoid 3-O-glycosyl
apiosyltransferase, CaApiGT from chickpea (Figure 1B), which
exhibits high selectivity in catalyzing the 2″-O-apiosylation of
flavonoid 3-O-glycosides, but shows poor catalytic activities
toward flavonoid 7-O- or 4′-O-glycosides. Moreover, we
determined eight ternary complex structures of CaApiGT
and PcApiGT (an analogue of GuApiGT) bound to different
flavonoid 3-O- or 7-O-glycosides, respectively. Combined with
theoretical calculations and site-directed mutagenesis, we
demonstrate that the sugar acceptor selectivity of these two
types of flavonoid apiosyltransferases is primarily governed by
a key α-helix in the active pocket (Figure 1C).
■RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Molecular Cloning and Functional Characterization
of CaApiGT. Based on our previous research,4 we found that
apiosyltransferases from leguminous plant possess a unique 45-
amino-acid PSPG box. Thus, we obtained two candidate genes
(UGT79B90 and UGT79B91) from the transcriptome of C.
arietinum. Subsequently, these two sequences were cloned and
expressed, and the enzymes were purified using His-tag affinity
chromatography (Figure S1 and Tables S1 and S2). To
characterize function of the enzymes, we introduced the UDP-
apiose/UDP-xylose synthase (UAXS) of Arabidopsis thaliana
into the enzyme catalysis system to supply the sugar donor
UDP-Api,20−23 which is commercially unavailable (Figure 2A).
In the presence of UGT79B91, astragalin (1, kaempferol 3-O-
glucoside) could be efficiently converted to a new product 1a
(Figure 2B). According to liquid chromatography mass
spectrometry (LC/MS) analysis, 1a showed an [M −H]−
ion at m/z 579 in the MS spectra, which is 132 Da greater than
that of 1 (Figure 2C). We purified 1a from scaled-up
enzymatic reactions, and identified its structure as astragalin
2″-O-apioside by 1D and 2D NMR spectroscopic analyses
(Figures S2−S7). The 13C NMR spectrum showed additional
signals at δC 108.3 (C-1‴), 76.3 (C-2‴), 78.9 (C-3‴), 73.8 (C-
4‴), and 63.8 (C-5‴), which were characteristic for an apiosyl
residue.24 In the heteronuclear multiple bond correlation
(HMBC) spectra, the long-range correlation between H-1‴
and C-2″ indicated the apiosyl moiety was attached to 2″-OH
of the glucose residue. UGT79B91 was named CaApiGT
(GenBank accession number OR372735, Table S2). CaApiGT
represents the first reported apiosyltransferase capable of
catalyzing the apiosylation of flavonoid 3-O-glycosides.

investigated by using 1 as the sugar acceptor. CaApiGT
showed its maximum activity at pH 8.0 (50 mM NaH2PO4−

UDP or uridine monophosphate (UMP) may play an
important role in stabilizing the structure of CaApiGT, as they
are present in all of the crystals and their locations are highly
overlapped (Figures 4B and S37). This result also indicates
that the sugar donor may bind to the active pocket before the
sugar acceptor enters for the glycosylation reaction.36−38 In all
of the crystals, CaApiGT shows very similar conformations,
except that one α-helix (F198S199V200R201S202Y203) in CaA-

that the introduction of an extra rhamnosyl moiety in 5
occupies the space of water molecules to break the water-
mediated interactions among F198, S199, and S202 (Figure
S38).
Mechanisms for the Regio-Selectivity and Substrate
Promiscuity of CaApiGT. To interpret the reason why
CaApiGT regioselectively catalyzes the 2″-O-apiosylation, we
analyzed the obtained complex crystal structures. The
conserved catalytic dyad (His-Asp) acts as a key factor in
initiating the glycosylation reaction of UGTs, and the distances
between His19 of CaApiGT and 2″-OH of astragalin (1),
isoquercitrin (2), hyperoside (7), rutin (5), quercetin 3-O-
xyloside (10), and guaijaverin (11) are 2.6−2.7 Å (Figure 4C).
The short distance allows the formation of hydrogen bonds
between H19 and 2″-OH, but not the other hydroxyl groups.39
As a result, H19 and D116 of CaApiGT could initiate the
deprotonation of 2″-OH and attack the anomeric carbon of the
apiosyl group of UDP-Api to generate apiosides.

series of flavonoid 3-O-glycosides, we examined the inter-
actions of CaApiGT with different sugar acceptors (1, 2, 5, 7,
10, 11) in the complex crystal structures.40 Take the structure

Mechanisms for Substrate Preference among Quer-
cetin 3-O-Glycosides. Substrates 2, 5, 7, 10, and 11 are all 3-
O-glycosides of quercetin. While CaApiGT showed high
catalytic efficiency toward all these substrates, it exhibited
the preference order of glucoside (2) > galactoside (7) > 6″-O-
rhamnosylglucoside (5) > xyloside (10) > arabinoside (11),
when a low amount of enzyme was added into the catalytic
system (Figures 5A and S43). Molecular simulations were
utilized to assess the reactive capacity of various sugar
acceptors (Figure 5B). We collected 20,000 states and
calculated the quantity that meets the reaction requirements,
namely, the O−C1 distance is below 3.4 Å and the O−C1−O1
angle is between 125 and 155° 4,35 (Figure S44). Moreover, we
also calculated the binding free energies by the alchemical free
energy method to evaluate the binding ability of various sugar
acceptors (Figure 5C). All the computational results were
consistent with the experimental preference order.
We noticed that glucose and xylose (with 4″α-OH)
glycosides showed higher conversion rates than their isomeric
galactose and arabinose glycosides (with 4″β-OH), respec-
tively. We analyzed the structures of CaApiGT/UDP/7 and
CaApiGT/UDP/11, and found that 4″-OH of both 7 and 11
are close to C4 of Phe192 (Figures 5D and S45). Then we
constructed the F192Y mutant, and the hydroxyl group of
tyrosine could form a hydrogen bond with 4″-OH of the sugar
moiety to stabilize the acceptor. As expected, the conversion
rates for 11 and 7 increased by 4.8-fold and 1.3-fold,
respectively (Figures 5A and S43). Furthermore, a total of
11,496 reasonable near-attack conformations were identified
from 20,000 snapshots of the F192Y mutant for acceptor 11,
which facilitate the reaction (Figure S46).
Mechanisms for Sugar Donor Selectivity of Two
Types of Apiosyltransferases. Importantly, CaApiGT
selectively catalyzes apiosylation of flavonoid 3-O-glycosides,
while GuApiGT only accepts flavonoid 7- or 4′-O-glycosides as
sugar acceptors. To dissect mechanisms for the sugar acceptor
selectivity of these two apiosyltransferases, we tried to obtain
the complex crystal structure of GuApiGT but failed after
many attempts. Fortunately, we discovered an apiosyltransfer-
ase PcApiGT (UGT94AX3, GenBank accession number
PQ656105) from parsley41 (Petroselinum crispum, Figures
S47−S50), which showed very similar functions as GuApiGT
(Figures 3 and S51−S66). Furthermore, we obtained the
crystal structures of PcApiGT/UDP (2.65 Å), PcApiGT/

bonds with 7-OH of the acceptor. Therefore, we proposed that
the α-helix might be used as a marker to distinguish the
function of apiosyltransferases.

■CONCLUSIONS
In this work, we identified the first flavonoid 3-O-glycosyl
apiosyltransferase CaApiGT. This function was different from
those of all previously reported flavonoid O-apiosyltransferases,
including GuApiGT, which catalyzes the 2″-O-apiosylation of
flavonoid 7- or 4′-O-glycosides. We further identified PcApiGT
from parsley, which has functions similar to those of
GuApiGT. Taken together, CaApiGT and PcApiGT/GuA-
piGT represent two major types of flavonoid O-apiosyltrans-
ferases. These enzymes enable the rapid and efficient
biosynthesis of flavonoid O-apiosides, demonstrating signifi-
cant potential for future drug discovery and production.
This work deepens the mechanistic understanding of
apiosyltransferases. In our previous study, we elucidated the
structural basis for the high sugar donor selectivity toward
UDP-apiose. Here, we further revealed mechanisms for the
different sugar acceptor selectivities of the two types of
apiosyltransferases. Through crystal structure analysis, the-
oretical calculations, and site-directed mutagenesis, we
demonstrate that their distinct substrate preferences arise
from differences in the binding pocket geometry: CaApiGT
features a T-shaped pocket that accommodates flavonoid 3-O-
glycosides, whereas PcApiGT has a streamlined-shaped pocket
to fit flavonoid 7-/4′-O-glycosides. Notably, a key α-helix
(P140-T147) governs CaApiGT’s pocket conformation, and its
mutagenesis alters sugar acceptor selectivity. The coexistence
of these two types of apiosyltransferases may provide
important clues for plant evolution.
