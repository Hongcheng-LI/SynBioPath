ABSTRACT
Four phenazineswere isolated from the Taibai mountain-derived 
Streptomyces xanthophaeus A54, among which Taibphenazine A (1) 
and Taibphenazine B (2) are new compounds. Comprehensive 
spectroscopic analyses, including HRESIMS, NMR, ECD calculations, 
and optical rotation value, were employed to elucidate the struc­
tures of these compounds. Taibphenazine B showed considerable 
anti-influenza A virus activity (IC50=27.07 μM), which was similar to 
the positive control ribavirin (IC50=24.20 μM).
1.  Introduction
Phenazine natural products featuring nitrogen-containing heterocyclic are mostly 
produced by Streptomyces and Pseudomonas (Price-Whelan et  al. 2006; Pierson and 
Pierson 2010). To date, hundreds of phenazines have been identified, encompassing 
diverse biological activities, including antitumor, antifungal, antibacterial, antimalarial, 
antiparasitic, and antineuroinflammatory properties (Laursen and Nielsen 2004; Liang 
et  al. 2017; Bauman et  al. 2019; Gao et  al. 2024).
Mount Taibai (Mt. Taibai), the main peak of the Qinling Mountains in China, is renowned 
for its unique ecological environment and rich biodiversity. In our search for novel bio­
active natural products, we isolated many Streptomyces strains from the soil samples

2.  Results and discussion
The 55 L large-scale fermentation broth of S. xanthophaeus  A54 in MA medium was 
extracted by EtOAc. A vacuum evaporator was used to dry the extract at a reduced 
pressure. Fractionation was conducted via a stepped-gradient ODS column chroma­
tography and subsequent purification on a C18 semipreparative HPLC to afford com­
pounds 1–4 (Figure 1). These compounds displayed characteristic phenazine UV 
absorption at approximately 250 and 365 nm (Figure S1).
Compound 1, a yellow amorphous powder, was assigned its chemical formula 
C18H16N2O5 by HRESIMS analysis (Figure S2), necessitating 12 degrees of unsaturation. 
The 1H NMR data of compound 1 (Table S1, Figure S3) displayed one methyl [δH 0.85 
(d, J = 7.2 Hz, 3H, H-3′)], one methoxyl [δH 4.00 (s, 3H, H-12)], two methines [δH 6.10 
(d, J = 8.0 Hz, 1H, H-1′), 2.90 (m, 1H, H-2′)], and six aromatic protons [δH 8.24 (d, J = 6.6 Hz, 
1H, H-2), 8.45 (d, J = 8.8 Hz, 1H, H-3), 8.01 (m, 1H, H-4), 8.08 (m, 1H, H-7), 8.16 (d, 
J = 8.0 Hz, 1H, H-8), 8.02 (m, 1H, H-9)]. The 13C NMR spectrum (Figure S4) reveals six 
aromatic nonprotonated carbons resonating at δC 139.8 (C-1), 132.0 (C-4a), 141.2 (C-5a), 
142.9 (C-6), 142.6 (C-9a), and 141.1 (C-10a), suggesting the presence of a 1,6-disubstituted 
phenazine nucleus) (Mitova et  al. 2008; Yang et  al. 2011). This was further confirmed 
by 2D NMR analysis, including COSY, HMBC, and HSQC spectra (Figure S5–S7).1H−1H 
COSY correlations (Figure S8) of δH 6.10 (d, J = 8.0 Hz, 1H, H-1′)/2.90 (m, 1H, H-2′)/0.85 
(d, J = 7.2 Hz, 3H, H3-3′) together with HMBC correlations (Figure S8) from δH 0.85 (d, 
J = 7.2 Hz, 3H, H3-3′) to δC 176.4 (C-4′) and δC 68.8 (C-1′), of δH 2.90 (m, 1H, H-2′) to δC 
68.8 (C-1′) and δC 142.9 (C-6), and of δH 6.10 (d, J = 8.0 Hz, 1H, H-1′) to δC 142.9 (C-6) 
and δC 129.1 (C-7) revealed that a 3-hydroxy-2- methylbutanoic acid fragment was 
attached to C-6. Moreover, the HMBC correlations (Figure S8) of δH 8.24 (d, J = 6.6 Hz, 
1H, H-2) and δH 4.00 (m, 3H, H3-12) to δC 167.0 (C-11) disclosed that a methyl formate 
moiety was at C-1 of the phenazine, confirming the compound 1’s planar structure. 
The 3J coupling constants was used to confirm the relative configurations 
of C-1′ and C-2′ in compound 1. The observed coupling constants of 8.0 Hz suggested 
an unlike-configuration (R,S or S,R) at C-1′ and C-2′ (Yang et al. 2011, 2012; Kunz et al. 
2014; Liang et  al. 2017). Compared with other phenazines (Yang et  al. 2011, 2012;

Kunz et al. 2014; Liang et al. 2017), a negative Cotton effect at around 250 nm (Figure 
S9) and the negative optical rotation value (-32) shown that compound 1 processed 
the 1′S,2′R configurations, which were further confirmed by the ECD calculations (Figure 
S10). Thus, compound 1 was named as Taibphenazine A, a novel phenazine analogue.
Compound 2, a yellow amorphous powder, was assigned a molecular formula of 
C18H16N2O5 based on HRESIMS data (m/z 341.1159 [M + H]+ and m/z 703.2049 [2M + Na]+; 
Figure S11). Detailed NMR spectroscopic comparisons between compounds 1 and 2 
confirmed that the planar structure of 2 was the same as Taibphenazine A (1) and 
is an isomer of compound 1 (Figure S12–S18). The relative configurations of C-1′ and 
C-2′ in compound 2 were assigned by analysing the 3J coupling constants. The 
observed coupling constants of 4.0 Hz indicated a like-configuration (S,S or R,R) at 
C-1′ and C-2′ (Yang et  al. 2011, 2012; Kunz et  al. 2014; Liang et  al. 2017). The 1′R, 
2′R absolute configuration of 2 was conclusively assigned through its positive optical 
rotation value (+22), a positive Cotton effect (Figure S9), and ECD calculations (Figure 
S19). Therefore, compound 2 was named as Taibphenazine B, a new analogue of 
phenazines.
Compound 3 was identified as Nexphenazine A (Wang et al. 2022), while compound 
4 was proved to be Subphenazine A (Gao et al. 2024) according to their HRESIMS data, 
NMR spectra, CD data (Figure S9), and optical rotation values. From the biosynthetic 
logic, compounds 3 and 4 might be methylated products of compounds 1 and 2, respec­
tively, further supporting the absolute configurations of compounds 1 and 2 as 1′S, 2′R.
Compounds 1–4 were evaluated for bioactivity against the growth of three 
Gram-positive bacterial strains (Enterococcus faecium CCARM 5203, Enterococcus faecalis 
CCARM 5172, Staphylococcus aureus CCARM 3090), four Gram-negative bacterial strains 
(Salmonella typhimurium CCARM 8250, Pseudomona aeruginosa ATCC BAA-2108, 
Escherichia coli CCARM 1009, Klebsiella pneumoniae ATCC 13883) and seven fungal 
pathogens of Fusarium graminearum, Trichothecium roseum, Candida albicans CMCC(F) 
98001, Alternaria, Colletotrichum gloeosporioides, Phytophthora capsica and Valsa mali. 
However, compounds 1–4 displayed no inhibitory activity against the tested strains.
Compounds 1–4 were also evaluated for their anti-influenza activity against the 
A/Puerto Rico/8/34 (H1N1; PR/8) virus. As shown in Table 1, Taibphenazine B (2) showed 
considerable anti-H1N1 activity (IC50=27.07 μM), compare to the positive control ribavirin 
(IC50=24.20 μM). In contrast, compounds 1, 3, and 4 did not show anti-H1N1 activity, 
with IC50 values over 100 μM. Comparative analysis of these structures, methylation at 
the carboxyl group obviously has negative effect on anti-H1N1 activity. Interestingly, 
the configuration of the hydroxy group at C1′ is also critical for the activity.

3.  Experimental
3.1.  General experimental procedures
Ultraviolet (UV) and circular dichroism (CD) spectral data were obtained via a Chirascan 
spectrometer (Applied Photophysics, Ltd., Leatherhead, Surrey, U.K.). Optical rotations 
(ORs) were conducted by an Autopol III automatic polarimeter (Rudolph Research 
Analytical, NJ, U.S.A.). All NMR experiments were measured on a Bruker Avance III 
spectrometer. HRESIMS was acquired on an Agilent 6546 LC/Q-TOF spectrometer 
(Agilent Technologies, Inc., California, U.S.A.).
3.2.  Cultivation and fermentation
The S. xanthophaeus  A54 strain (Accession number: CP177298) was isolated in 2022 
from soil samples of Taibai Mountain, China. The soil from Taibai Mountain was dis­
solved in physiological saline, and then the strain was isolated and purified using 
dilution plating procedure. The strain was cultured on a plate of MS Agar Medium 
(20 g agar, 20 g hexitol, 30 g soybean meal) at 30 °C. The fermentation medium was 
MA media (10 g dextrin, 4 g meat extract, 20 g maltose, 2 g soybean meal, 2 g yeast 
extract, 4 g peptone, pH 7.0).
3.3.  Extraction and purification
S. xanthophaeus A54 was cultured on MS Agar Medium at 30 °C for 7 days, and then 
it was transferred to TSB medium and was incubated for 2 days at 30 °C 220 rpm to 
give the seed broth. 12 mL seed broth was transfered to a 2000 mL Erlenmeyer flask 
containing 600 mL of MA liquid medium. Subsequently, the liquid medium (55 L) 
was cultured at 30 °C for 7 days (220 rpm). Following fermentation, EtOAc was used 
to triple-extracted the broth supernatant. 19.3 g of crude extract was obtained by 
vacuoconcentrating the mixed organic phases. To eliminate nonpolar components, 
the extract underwent liquid-liquid partitioning between n-hexane and 90% meth­
anol. This 11 fractions (Fr.1 − Fr.11) were obtained by chromatographing this MeOH 
layer on a silica gel column using stepwise elution of ether-ethyl acetate 
(100:0 − 100:100, v/v) and CH2Cl2-CH3OH (100:1 − 100:32, v/v). Fr.7-1 − Fr.7-11 was 
obtained by separating Fraction 7 (4.6 g) on a C18 column using a gradient of 
CH3OH − H2O (30%−100%, v/v). MeCN-H2O (35:65, v/v) was used in semipreparative 
HPLC to further purify Fraction 7-2 (416 mg), yielding 1 (7.3 mg) and 2 (1.8 mg). 
Fr.4-1 − Fr.4-13 was obtained by separating Fraction 4 (1.4 g) on a C18 column using 
a gradient of CH3OH − H2O (30%−100%, v/v). MeCN-H2O (45:55, v/v) was used in 
semipreparative HPLC to further purify Fraction 4-6 (52 mg), yielding 3 (40.7 mg) and 
4 (10.3 mg).

3.4.  Antibacterial and antifungal assay
Compounds 1–4 was tested for antibacterial activity by agar diffusion test against 
seven multidrug resistant (MDR) bacterial and seven pathogenic fungi strains as pre­
viously described (Hou et  al. 2018).
3.5.  Antivirus activity assay
Compounds 1–4 were tested for anti-influenza activity against the A/Puerto Rico/8/34 
(H1N1; PR/8) virus by the cytopathic effect (CPE) inhibition assay (Senthilraja and 
Kathiresan 2015; Hou et  al. 2018). Ribavirin obtained from Macklin (China) was used 
as a positive control. Before assess antiviral activity, cytotoxicity profiles of compounds 
1–4 and the positive control ribavirin were determined in Madin–Darby canine kidney 
(MDCK) cells using a CCK-8 assay.
3.6.  Computational method
ECD calculations were conducted using the previously reported methodologies (Hou 
et  al. 2020).
4.  Conclusion
In conclusion, two new phenazines, named as Taibphenazines A (1) and B (2), together 
with two known phenazines of Nexphenazine A (3) and Subphenazine A (4), were 
obtained from the fermentation broth of Streptomyces xanthophaeus A54, originating

siderable anti-H1N1 activity equivalent to the positive control ribavirin.
