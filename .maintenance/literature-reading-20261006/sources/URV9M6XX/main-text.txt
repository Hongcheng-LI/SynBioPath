ABSTRACT: The structural groups of 2-oxindole and tricyclic 3a-
hydroxy-hexahydropyrrolo-[2,3-b]indole (HO-HPI) are important
pharmacophores. Chemical synthesis of complex alkaloids contain-
ing a 2-oxindole or HO-HPI moiety, especially the latter one, has
been a long-standing challenge. Herein, we characterized the P450
enzyme AfnD, and its homologue proteins, HmtT, ClpD, KtzM,
and LtzR, as cyclopeptide 2-oxindole and HO-HPI monooxyge-
nases (cpOPMOs) that could introduce a 2-oxindole or HO-HPI
moiety into the tryptophan-containing cyclopeptides in a pH-
dependent manner. A universal catalytic mechanism was proposed
for the five cpOPMOs, in which two conserved residues, Asp and
Ser (Thr for LtzR), were proposed to divergently open the epoxide
intermediates, thereby forming a 2-oxindole or HO-HPI moiety.
Based on this, we constructed ten Asp or Ser/Thr mutants of cpOPMOs, which could synthesize cyclopeptides with an HO-HPI or
2-oxindole structure, selectively, under appropriate reaction conditions. All of the ten cpOPMO mutants exhibited high substrate
promiscuities and usually performed well with cyclopeptides that are structurally similar to their native substrates. Overall, our work
discovers a group of intriguing P450 enzymes, the cpOPMOs, and provides a powerful enzymatic toolkit for the selective synthesis of
HO-HPI- or 2-oxindole-containing cyclopeptides.
■INTRODUCTION
Alkaloids that contain 2-oxindole or tricyclic 3a-hydroxy-
hexahydropyrrolo-[2,3-b]indole (HO-HPI) structures are
fertile sources for drug development.1−3 Currently, over one
hundred structurally diverse alkaloids featuring 2-oxindole have
been discovered from organisms of varied kingdoms, e.g., the
3-substituted oxindole derivatives, violacein (1) from bacte-
rium and costinone A (2) from plant,4−7 as well as the spiro-
oxindole derivatives, citrinadin A (3) from fungus, and
orbicularisine (4) from animal (Figure 1A).8−10 They display
fascinating bioactivities, such as antifungal, antitumor,
antioxidant, neuroprotective, and tyrosine kinase inhibition.
Specifically, four natural product mimic drugs with a 2-
oxindole moiety have been clinically approved (Figure S1),
including sunitinib (anticancer), nintedanib (for treating
idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis), ziprasidone (neuroleptic
agent), and ropinirole (for treating Parkinson’s disease).11
For HO-HPI-containing alkaloids, more than 70 compounds
have been isolated from different natural sources.1,12 They
exhibit remarkable structural diversity and a broad spectrum of
bioactivities such as antibacterial, antifungal, antitumor,
antiviral, and insecticidal. Most of those alkaloids feature a 2-
substituted HO-HPI moiety, which can be divided into syn-cis
and anti-cis diastereomers according to the stereochemistry of
C2, C3a, and C8a (Figure S2).13 More than 50 alkaloids
contain the syn-cis diastereomer, including alboflavusin B1
(AFN B1, 5) from bacterium and melicopteline C (6) from
plant;14−16 and about a dozen alkaloids contain the anti-cis
diastereomer (e.g., okaramine C (7) from fungus) (Figure
1A).17 In addition, there are some alkaloids possessing the
HO-HPI moiety with an achiral C2, e.g., flustraminol B (8)
from Flustra foliacea and cruciferane from Isatis indigotica
(Figure S2).18,19 Of note, compound 6 that possesses antiviral
activity was isolated from Melicope pteleifolia, which is applied
as traditional Chinese medicine for the treatment of cold and
rheumatoid arthritis.20

such, enzymes that can form 2-oxindole or HO-HPI were
investigated intensively and considered biocatalysts for the

The reaction can be accomplished by flavin-dependent

dioxygenase for auxin oxidation (DAO) and the tryptophan

been solved, including a dioxygenase AtDAO1 that can convert
indole-3-acetic acid to 2-oxindole-3-acetic acid and two FMOs,
CtdE, and PhqK, participating in the biosynthesis of 21R-

was proposed to shape the spiro-oxindole structure by
oxidizing paraherquamide L to form an indole-2,3-epoxide
intermediate and then opening the epoxide ring via a
semipinacol-type rearrangement (Figure S3). A similar
catalytic mechanism was proposed for the other characterized
FMOs and the TDO-like MarE.25 Taking advantage of the
high substrate promiscuity of PhqK, it was used as a catalyst to
introduce the spiro-oxindole structure into a number of
halogenated malbrancheamides.28 For the cytochrome P450
monooxygenases, two of them, FtmG and CyaH, have been
characterized enzymatically.29,30 FtmG was proposed to
hydroxylate the substrate via a radical reaction and then to
form the spiro-oxindole of spirotryprostatin B by a subsequent
semipinacol-type rearrangement, while a carbocation-involving
mechanism was proposed for CyaH in cyanogramide biosyn-
thesis (Figure S3).

cytochrome P450 proteins (Table S2). Among them, only
three FMOs, BvnB, AspB, and NotB, have been identified by

D) could be shaped by the following intramolecular epoxide

produce syn-cis HO-HPI-containing cyclohexapeptides (albo-

In this work, we first verified AfnD as an HO-HPI-forming
P450 enzyme that can convert pre-AFN A1 (12) to 9. During
this process, we noticed that AfnD preferred to convert 12 to
AFN C1 (13) with a 2-oxindole moiety under acidic
conditions. Surprisingly, all of the four AfnD homologues,
including HmtT, LtzR, and ClpD (from the chloptosin (14)
producer) and KtzM (from the kutzneride 1 (15) producer)
(Figure 1B),40−42 could generate both 9 and 13 in a pH-
dependent manner. Therefore, the five P450 proteins were
termed cyclopeptide 2-oxindole and HO-HPI monooxygenases
(cpOPMOs). Based on the predicted model of the HmtT-pre-
AFN A1 complex, we identified two key residues S75 and D73
that could direct the formation of HO-HPI and 2-oxindole,
respectively, and this mechanism is shared by all of the five
cpOPMOs. Given that all of the five cpOPMOs exhibited high
substrate promiscuities, a set of cpOPMO mutants were
constructed and successfully applied in the selective synthesis
of HO-HPI- or 2-oxindole-containing cyclopeptides.
■RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
AfnD Can Form Both HO-HPI and 2-Oxindole
Structures. In a previous work, we deleted the afnD gene
in Streptomyces alboflavus sp. 313 and observed the
accumulation of 12.14 To verify AfnD is the P450 enzyme
shaping the HO-HPI structure of 9, we expressed the N-His6-
tagged AfnD in Escherichia coli BL21 (DE3), purified the
protein by affinity chromatography (Figure S5), and incubated
it with 12 in the presence of NADH and a pair of ferredoxin/
ferredoxin reductase (SelFdx1499/SelFdR0978) from Synecho-
coccus elongatus PCC 794243 at pH 7.5. HPLC-MS detection
revealed that AfnD converted 12 into a pair of products. The
major product was confirmed to be 9 by HR-ESI-MS analysis
and comparing with the authentic standard (Figures 2A and
S6), verifying AfnD as the P450 enzyme that forms the HO-
HPI motif of 9. HR-ESI-MS analysis of the minor product,
termed AFN C1 (13, m/z [M + H]+ 757.3548, calcd
757.3547), suggested that it is an isomer of 9 (Figure S6).
To obtain enough 13 for structural elucidation, we tested the
AfnD reaction at varied assay conditions and found that the
production ratio of 9 and 13 was pH dependent. Production of
9 was preferred under alkaline condition (pH 8.0), while 13
was the major product under acidic condition (pH 6.0)
(Figure 2B). Compound 13 was then prepared by a scaled-up
enzymatic synthesis at pH 6.0 and assigned as a 2-oxindole-
containing cyclohexapeptide by NMR and MS/MS analyses
(Table S5, Figures S7 and S8), which demonstrated that AfnD
is a bifunctional P450 monooxygenase that can form both HO-
HPI and 2-oxindole structures (Figure 2C).

To check whether 9 and 13 can interchange to each other,
we tested their stabilities under different conditions and
confirmed that neither of them can be converted to the other
one spontaneously or by AfnD (Figures S9 and S10), which
implied that AfnD forms the two products divergently by
irreversible reactions. We further checked the fermentation
broths of different S. alboflavus strains and observed the
production of 13, together with 9, in the wild-type strain, but
not in the ΔafnD mutant, suggesting that AfnD can oxidize 12
to 9 and 13 under natural conditions (Figure S11).
Characterization of the AfnD-Like P450 Proteins with
12. As depicted in Figure 1B, AfnD and its four homologue
proteins (the in vivo characterized HmtT and LtzR and the
uncharacterized ClpD and KtzM) are all from the biosynthetic
pathways of cyclohexapeptides with both HO-HPI and
hexahydropyridazine moieties. The protein sequence identities
among the five P450s are as high as around 60% (Figure S12),
indicating that they all belong to the CYP107 family. We tested
the four AfnD homologues using 12 as a substrate and
observed that all four P450s could convert 12 to 9 and 13 with
different efficiencies (Figure 2A). We also showed that none of
the HmtT, ClpD, KtzM, and LtzR could catalyze the
interconversion of 9 and 13 (Figure S10). Subsequently, we
assayed these enzymes under acidic (pH 6.0), neutral (pH
7.0), and alkaline (pH 8.0) conditions, which showed that, in
all cases, the production ratios between 9 and 13 changed in
pH-dependent manners as that of AfnD (Figure S13),
indicating that the five P450 enzymes share a common
catalytic mechanism. The structures of the five groups of
cyclohexapeptides, AFNs, himastatins, chloptosins, kutzner-
ides, and petrichorins are quite different; e.g., all of the six
residues of 15 and five residues of the himastatin monomer are
different from those of 9 (Figure 1B), suggesting that the
native substrates of those P450s are structurally varied. While,
all of them could recognize 12, implying that these P450
enzymes have high substrate promiscuities and are of
considerable potential to be applied as biocatalysts in
synthesizing cyclopeptides containing an HO-HPI or 2-
oxindole moiety. Since all of the five P450 proteins can
catalyze the formation of 2-oxindole and HO-HPI in
cyclopeptides, we named them cpOPMOs.

molecular docking to build the HmtT-pre-AFN A1 complex
model to investigate its catalytic mechanism. The overall
structure of HmtT adopts a typical prism-shaped fold,
comprising 12 α helices and 14 β strands, with the heme
prosthetic group situated at the center, serving as the active
site. In the HmtT-pre-AFN A1 complex, the substrate,
compound 12, is posed in the active site channel, positioning
the 6-Cl-L-Trp residue near the heme group within a suitable
distance (3.9 Å) for the oxidative reaction occurring (Figure
3A,3B). For most P450 enzymes, the oxygen activation process
involves a highly conserved Thr residue in I-helix.44,45 In the
case of HmtT, the I-helix residue T236 is approximately 4.5 Å
from the heme iron (Figure S14A,B). When it was mutated to
Ala, the HmtT T236A mutant exhibited significantly reduced
catalytic activity in generating 9 and 13 (Figure 3C),
suggesting that HmtT follows the classical oxygen activation
process of P450 enzymes. Upon the binding of 12 to the active
site of HmtT, the ligand water of the heme iron is displaced.
An electron from the redox partner proteins reduces the ferric
iron to ferrous, which binds to oxygen and gains another
electron to form the peroxoferric intermediate (Figure S14C).
The ferric peroxide forms a hydrogen bond network with T236
and a potential water molecule and abstracts two protons from
T236, generating a high-valent iron-oxo transient intermediate,
Compound I (Cpd I) to oxidize 12 (Figure S14D).
An epoxide intermediate has been proposed for most of the
enzymatic reactions that form HO-HPI or 2-oxindole
structures.28,46−48 Hence, we assumed that substrate 12
could be oxidized to an indole epoxide intermediate by
HmtT via radical reactions involving Compound II (Cpd II)
formation (Figures 3D and S14C). The docking analysis
revealed that S75 and D73, two residues situated on the
opposite sides of the indole group (Figure 3B), could be
involved in opening the epoxide ring and directing the
formation of 9 and 13. At neutral conditions (pH 7.0), HmtT
produced more 9 than 13 (at a ratio of about 4:1). When we
mutated D73 to Ala and assayed the mutant at pH 7.0, about
97% of the products were 9; on the contrary, when S75 was
mutated to Ala, HmtT S75A produced 13 as the major product
(about 72%) at pH 7.0 (Figure 3C). Based on the results, we
proposed that, upon the indole epoxide intermediate
formation, the hydroxyl group of S75 and the carboxyl group
of D73 will compete in binding the oxygen atom of the epoxide
ring (Figure 3D). In route 1, the hydroxyl group of S75 is
hydrogen bonded to the epoxide, facilitating the scission of the
epoxide ring at the C8a side to form a hydroxyiminium adduct,
which is electrophilic and easy to be attacked by N1
intramolecularly to form the HO-HPI unit of 9. A water
molecule located near S75 (2.9 Å) and N1 of 6-Cl-L-Trp (2.5
Å) may form a hydrogen bond network with S75 and N1,
facilitating the formation of 9. In route 2, the carboxyl group of
D73 is hydrogen bonded to the epoxide, promoting the
transfer of a proton from C8a to C3a by 2,3-hydride migration
and opening the epoxide ring at the C3a side to form 13. The
functions of S75 and D73 were also supported by the facts that
HmtT was inclined to generate 9 under alkaline condition (the
carboxyl group of D73 is predominantly dissociated, no longer
competes with S75) and produce 13 under acidic condition
(D73 competes with S75) (Figure S13). In addition, when the
HmtT assay was performed in deuterated solvent, no
deuterium was incorporated into 9 or 13, which also supported
the proposed mechanism (Figure S15). Both the HmtT D73N
and the HmtT D73A-S75A double mutant generated 9 as the
predominant product (about 97%) at pH 7.0, further
demonstrating the critical role of D73 in forming the 2-
oxindole structure (Figure 3C). To check the possible
influence of D73 protonation on the reactions, we carried
out the assays of HmtT and its mutants under different
conditions (Figure S16). Unlike HmtT, which converted 12 to
13 as the major product under acidic condition, both of the
two D73 mutants (D73A and D73N) generated 9 as the major
product even under acidic condition, indicating that the
protonation state of D73 is important to 2-oxindole formation.
We also noted that when the reaction condition was changed
from neutral to alkaline, the major product of HmtT S75A was
shifted from 13 to 9, which may add another piece of evidence
for the role of D73 protonation.
Five CpOPMOs Share a Common Catalytic Mecha-
nism. As mentioned above, HmtT, AfnD, ClpD, KtzM, and
LtzR share around 60% protein sequence identities. Sequence
alignment of the five cpOPMOs revealed that the three key
residues (D73, S75, and T236 in HmtT) are well conserved,
except that, in the case of LtzR, the Ser residue is replaced by a
characteristically similar T75 residue (Figure S17A). We
constructed the structural models of AfnD, ClpD, KtzM, and
LtzR with AlphaFold2 using HmtT as a template. Structural
alignment showed that the five P450 enzymes are very similar,
and comparison of the four predicted structures of cpOPMOs
with that of HmtT resulted in root-mean-square deviations
(RMSDs) less than 1 Å (Figures S17B,C). In all five cpOPMO
structures, the conserved I-helix Thr residue is suited within an
appropriate distance to heme for oxygen activation (Figure
S17D). The subsequent molecular docking analyses of AfnD,
ClpD, KtzM, and LtzR revealed that 12 could also enter their
active site channels, with the Asp and Ser (T75 for LtzR)
residues close to C8a and C3a of the indole group of 6-Cl-L-
Trp, respectively (Figure 4A). Overall, the in silico analyses
implied that the five cpOPMOs have a common catalytic
mechanism.
To study the catalytic mechanism of AfnD, we conducted
point mutagenesis of the three conserved residues individually
and obtained AfnD mutants D74A, S76A, and T237A. As
predicted, the catalytic efficiencies of the T237A mutant
dropped significantly, suggesting that T237 is important to the
oxygen activation process of AfnD. At neutral conditions (pH
7.0), AfnD converted 12 to 9 and 13 at almost equal
efficiencies, while the D74A and S76A mutants generated 9
(about 95%) and 13 (about 89%) as the major products,
respectively, verifying that the two residues direct the
formation of HO-HPI and 2-oxindole structures in AfnD as
well as in HmtT (Figure 4B). In addition, we constructed the
D73 and S75 (T75 in the case of LtzR) mutants of the other
three cpOPMOs, including ClpD D73A and S75A, KtzM
D73A and S75A, and LtzR D73A and T75A. As predicted, all
three D73A mutants generated 9 as the major products, while
the two S75A mutants and LtzR T75A generated 13 as the
major products (Figure 4B). Taken collectively, the results
supported that all of the five cpOPMOs share a common
catalytic mechanism.

Selective Synthesis of Cyclopeptides with an HO-HPI
or 2-Oxindole Structure. Subsequently, we proceeded to
test the applicability of those ten cpOPMO mutants in the
synthesis of cyclopeptides containing an HO-HPI or 2-
oxindole structure (Figure 5). In a previous study, we isolated
a brominated AFN A1 congener 16HPI (the product of 16
containing an HO-HPI structure and the following compounds
were named after this) from S. alboflavus sp. 313,49 suggesting
that AfnD could take the brominated pre-AFN A1 (16) as a
substrate. When the ΔafnD mutant was fermented in the
production medium supplemented with NaBr, the production
of 16 was detected (Figure S19). We isolated 16, verified its
structure by MS and NMR analyses (Table S6 and Figure S20)
and incubated it as a substrate with the cpOPMO mutants
individually. As predicted, all five cpOPMO Asp mutants,
AfnD D74A, HmtT D73A, ClpD D73A, KtzM D73A, and
LtzR D73A, generated 16HPI as the major products (over 93%)
at pH 7.0, with AfnD D74A performing the highest specific
activity (11.30 ± 1.68 U/mg, U = μmol/min) (Figure S21).
While, the other five cpOPMO mutants, AfnD S76A, HmtT
S75A, ClpD S75A, KtzM S75A, and LtzR T75A, generated
16OXI (the product of 16 containing a 2-oxindole structure and
the following compounds were named after this) (Figure S22),
as the dominant product at pH 6.0 (Figure S21). ClpD S75A
could synthesize 16OXI with high specific activity (11.94 ± 0.92
U/mg) and 99% ratio (Figure 5). Compound 17 was detected
and isolated from the ΔafnD mutant (Figure S19). It possesses
almost the same structure as 12, except for a replacement of
the D-valine at R1 position by the D-allo-isoleucine (Table S7,
Figures S23 and S24). Under neutral conditions, all of the five
cpOPMO Asp mutants could convert 17 to 17HPI as the major
product (over 86%), with AfnD D74A displaying the highest
specific activity (8.26 ± 0.97 U/mg) (Table S8 and Figures
S25−S27). Under acidic conditions, the other five cpOPMO
mutants generated 17OXI as the major product, and ClpD S75A
exhibited the best catalytic efficiency (specific activity, 17.18 ±
1.34 U/mg; 99% ratio) (Figure 5).
Compared with 12, only one residue is changed in
compounds 16 and 17. It was not surprise that the AfnD
mutants could recognize them. Notably, all of the other
cpOPMO mutants could also take them as substrates,
especially ClpD S75A outperforming AfnD S76A in synthesiz-
ing 16OXI and 17OXI, encouraging us to further explore the
catalytic potential of these cpOPMO mutants. We synthesized
compound 18, in which residues R1, R3, and R6 were kept the
same as 12, while residues R2, R4, and R5 were designed based
on the structure of himastatin monomer (Table S9, Figures 5,
S28, and S29). Significantly, all five cpOPMO Asp mutants
could convert 18 to 18HPI as the major product (over 94%) at
pH 7.0, and ClpD D73A showed a relatively high catalytic
efficiency (specific activity, 1.92 ± 0.29 U/mg). For the
preparation of 18OXI, HmtT S75A surpassed the other
cpOPMO Ser mutants with a specific activity of 1.58 ± 0.24
U/mg and 91% product ratio at pH 6.0 (Figures 5, S30, and
S31).
Next, we tested the necessity of the unusual piperazic acid
(Piz) residue, which is present in AFNs and all of their natural
product analogues with an HO-HPI moiety, in the recognition
of cyclopeptide substrates by cpOPMOs. We synthesized
cyclohexapeptides 19−22 containing no Piz residue (Tables
S10−S13, Figures S28 and S32−S35). Compounds 19 and 20
are structural mimics of compounds 12 and 17, with all of their
Piz residues being replaced by pipecolic acids (Figure 5). They
could be efficiently converted to 19HPI and 20HPI by ClpD
D73A or AfnD D74A and to 19OXI and 20OXI by ClpD S75A
(Figures S36−S39). Moreover, all of the ten cpOPMO
mutants showed certain catalytic activities toward 19 and 20,
demonstrating that the Piz residue is not necessary to
cpOPMOs. Compound 21 is an analogue of 19 with its R2
residue being changed to L-valine. ClpD D73A and ClpD S75A
could convert 21 to 21HPI and 21OXI efficiently (Figures S40
and S41). Of note, the two mutants of HmtT could synthesize
21HPI and 21OXI at high efficiencies (Figure S40), which
reminded us that the himastatin monomer has a L-valine-like
residue (L-hydroxyisovaleric acid) at the R2 position (Figure
1B). Compound 22 was designed based on the structure of the
left monomer of the heterodimeric 11, by replacing its two
different Piz residues to pipecolic acids (Figure 5). It turned
out that LtzR D73A was the best catalyst to synthesize 22HPI,
and HmtT S75A converted 22 to 22OXI with relatively high
efficiency (Figures 5, S42, and S43).
To further explore the substrate promiscuities of those
cpOPMO mutants, we synthesized cyclopeptides 23, 24, 25,
and 26 with five, four, seven, and eight residues, respectively
(Tables S14−S17, Figures S28 and S44−S47). Although the
cpOPMO mutants could not catalyze the latter three
substrates, to our delight, KtzM D73A and LtzR D73A were
able to convert 23 to 23HPI, and AfnD S76A, HmtT S75A, and
ClpD S75A successfully converted it to 23OXI, with more than
99% production ratios (Figures 5, S48, and S49).
Taken together, our results suggested that the cpOPMO
mutants possess impressive substrate promiscuities and
catalytic specificities. Each of them could recognize all eight
tested cyclohexapeptides. Furthermore, some of the mutants
were capable of taking cyclopentapeptide as a substrate. And,
the cpOPMO Asp mutants always generated the HO-HPI-
containing products predominantly, while the cpOPMO Ser/
Thr mutants preferred to synthesize the 2-oxindole-containing
cyclopeptides. Some of the mutants with broad substrate
spectra and high catalytic efficiencies are potent catalysts for
the synthesis of cyclopeptides with an HO-HPI or 2-oxindole
moiety, e.g., AfnD D74A for the preparation of HO-HPI-
containing cyclopeptides and ClpD S75A for the preparation
of 2-oxindole-containing cyclopeptides. We also noted that the
cpOPMO mutants tended to perform well with cyclopeptides
that are structurally similar to their native substrates.
Therefore, those different cpOPMO mutants may serve as a
set of potent catalysts in the synthesis of various HO-HPI- or
2-oxindole-containing cyclopeptides.

both HO-HPI and 2-oxindole structures in a pH-dependent
